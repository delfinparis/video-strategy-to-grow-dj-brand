## The production recommendation: Option 2 (hybrid), shot in two pieces

Zillow offered three production models. Option 2 is the right one, and the reason isn't workflow preference.

Option 3 (their crew travels to Chicago and films on-site) produces a video that *looks* like a Zillow production, because it is one. The entire value D.J. carries into this is that his content doesn't look like it came from Zillow. A crew, a stabilizer, and a 24-hour turnaround edit produce something visibly different from every other video on his grid, and the audience reads that difference before it reads the content. Option 1 gives up the back-end amplification setup for no reason. Option 2 keeps the selfie-stick walk-and-talk format the audience already trusts and takes Zillow's help on captions, whitelisting, and paid distribution, which is where their help is actually worth something.

**Then shoot it in two pieces**, per the production note in NF-067:

1. **Now, before the ruling:** shoot the spine (hook, story, turn, payoff, loop-back) plus *both* ten-second ruling branches, back to back, same location, same shirt.
2. **On ruling day:** drop the matching branch into the timeline and post.

This is the whole reason to build the asset in advance. It means the video goes out the same day the ruling lands even if that day is a bad filming day, which is the difference between being in the conversation and being three days late to it.

## Guardrails (enforced in NF-067, enforce again in the edit)

Zillow's terms say content will not make legal claims, reference trial proceedings, or assign blame. That is a **lower heat register than D.J.'s entire existing coverage of this case.** NF-012 called the board composition "a conspiracy in the annual report." NF-062 ran at heat 5. NF-067 runs at heat 2.5 on purpose.

**Do not dial it up in the edit, and do not improvise upward on camera.** The specific things that stay out:

- No characterization of who was right or wrong.
- No reference to testimony, filings, hearing content, or trial proceedings.
- No naming MRED, Compass, Rebecca Jensen, or Robert Reffkin as bad actors.
- The word "lawsuit" stays out of the spoken script. "The Chicago listing ruling" is the neutral referent.
- Friction points at the silent listing agreement, which is a documentation gap, not a party to anything.

**If the ruling doesn't go Zillow's way, the register gets steadier, not sharper.** Branch B is written that way. The temptation on a bad-ruling day is to go hot in Zillow's defense. That's the version that reads as a mouthpiece.

## Prompts in Zillow's brief that need reframing before D.J. answers them

Several of the hero questions assume D.J. is a practicing agent with clients. He isn't, and answering them straight breaks the protected voice signature ("I've never practiced real estate. But I've interviewed 700 people who have.") in front of the largest audience this content will ever reach. Reframes are in `soundbite-bank.md`.

| Zillow's prompt | Problem | Reframe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "As an agent, what is your #1 priority when working with a client?" | D.J. holds a license but has never represented a buyer or seller. | Answer from the archive or from the brokerage seat, not from a transaction he didn't do. |
| "How you're supporting clients through it" | Same. He supports agents, and agents support clients. | "How I'm supporting three hundred agents through it." |
| "What does today's ruling mean for your clients?" | Same. | "What it means for our agents and the people they represent." |
| "Why did you set up a direct feed with Zillow? What was that process like?" | **Highest-value question in the brief** and the only one nobody else can answer. But it needs verification first. | **VERIFY BEFORE ANSWERING:** did Kale Realty actually set up the direct feed or the MLS Grid PDAP backup feed, and who did it? If yes, this is the strongest sixty seconds in the package. If D.J. only *covered* how to do it rather than doing it, say that instead. Do not blur the two. |

The consumer-facing "finish the sentence" prompts ("The best home search starts with...") are answerable but weak in his voice, because his audience is agents, not buyers. `soundbite-bank.md` answers them in a form that works for Zillow's quote cards without turning D.J. into a consumer-brand spokesperson.

## The one strategic thing to decide before saying yes

The May strategy record named the risk plainly: *"the reason Zillow wants D.J. is that he is not Zillow. The day the content reads as 'what Zillow wants me to say,' the credibility, and therefore the amplification value, evaporates."*

This collaboration is the highest-exposure version of that risk so far. It's a whitelisted, paid-boosted, collab-posted video about a ruling in a case Zillow is a party to. Everything before this was D.J. covering a story that happened to help Zillow. This is Zillow commissioning coverage on the day of their own ruling.

That does not make it a bad idea. It makes it a decision rather than a default. Three things keep it on the right side:

1. **The video is agent-facing, not consumer-facing.** Zillow's prompt list leans consumer ("what should buyers feel"). NF-067 tells Chicago listing agents to fix a page in their listing presentation. That's content D.J. would have made without Zillow, and it's also the content Zillow already paid to boost to 171K, so it's not a compromise.
2. **The paid-partnership label goes on before publish, not after.** FTC matter and a trust matter with his own audience.
3. **The take survives either ruling.** Branch B is a real branch, not a softer version of Branch A. If it can only be said when Zillow wins, it shouldn't be said.

Compass and Anywhere are roughly thirty-five percent of the Chicago market. The version of this that survives contact with that audience is the version where D.J. is talking about listing agreements, not about who won.
