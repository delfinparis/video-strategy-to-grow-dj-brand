# Soundbite Bank -- Zillow Collab, Ruling Day

Zillow's brief asks for footage they can cut into quote cards, carousels, and Stories. That's a different asset than the reel, and it should be shot as a separate block, not carved out of NF-067.

**How to shoot this:** after the reel is in the can, stay in the same setup and run these as single clean takes. One question, one answer, stop, reset. Ten to thirty seconds each. Don't chain them. Zillow's editors need clean heads and tails on every clip or the quote cards get ragged.

**Don't memorize these.** Read the answer, put the card down, say it in your own words. Zillow's brief says "not scripted," and they're right about that. These exist so you're not composing a legal-adjacent answer on camera.

**Guardrails still apply.** No characterization of who was right, no reference to testimony or filings, no naming anyone as a bad actor. Full list in [`README.md`](README.md).

## Block 1 -- Works either way (shoot these first, they're the backbone)

**Q: If a client texted you asking, "What's going on with MRED and Zillow?" what would you say?**

> I'd tell them the honest version. Two companies disagreed about where listings should show up, a judge weighed in, and none of that changes what you should expect from the person representing you. You should know where your home is being marketed. You should be able to ask that question and get a straight answer. That was true before any of this and it's true today.

**Q: What's one thing you hope buyers feel after hearing this news?**

> That they're not missing something. The worst feeling in a home search is the suspicion that there's a house out there you never got to see. Buyers shouldn't have to wonder whether they're looking at the whole market or somebody's version of it.

**Q: What's your message to someone starting their home search today?**

> Ask your agent one question. Where are you looking for me, and where aren't you? Not because anybody's hiding anything. Because you should know how your search actually works. The buyers who ask that question end up better represented than the ones who don't, every single time.

**Q: What's one thing every buyer deserves when searching for a home?**

> To know what they're not seeing. Everybody focuses on what shows up in the search results. The thing that costs a buyer a house is the listing they never knew existed.

**Q [REFRAMED -- see README]: As an agent, what's your number one priority when working with a client?**

> I've never practiced real estate. I've interviewed seven hundred people who have. And the ones at the top all do the same unglamorous thing. They tell the client the part the client didn't ask about. Not the good news, not the pitch, the part that's going to matter in three weeks. That's the whole job.

**Q: Finish the sentence. "The best home search starts with..."**

> Knowing what's out there. Not a strategy, not a pre-approval letter, just an honest picture of what's actually for sale. Everything else in the process is built on top of that one thing, and if it's incomplete, so is every decision after it.

**Q: Finish the sentence. "The best agent will ensure..."**

> That you never find out about a house too late. That's it. That's the measure.

**Q: Complete the sentence. "Buyers deserve..."**

> A market they can see. Not a curated one, not a version, the market.

**Q: Complete the sentence. "Every home search should..."**

> Start with everything and narrow from there. Most of them start already narrowed and nobody tells the buyer.

**Q: Complete the sentence. "My job is easiest when..."**

> The seller already knows the answer before they think to ask the question.

**Q: Complete the sentence. "The best decisions happen when..."**

> People have the full picture and enough time to sit with it. Take either one away and you get a decision somebody regrets.

**Q: Complete the sentence. "The future of real estate should..."**

> Be more transparent than the version I walked into twelve years ago. It already is. That's the direction, and I don't think it reverses.

## Block 2 -- If the ruling goes Zillow's way

Tone: grounded, forward momentum. Not celebratory. A win lap in this footage ages badly and it's the clip that gets screenshotted by the thirty-five percent of this market that works at Compass or Anywhere.

**Q: What was your first reaction when you heard the decision?**

> Relief, mostly, and not for the reason people assume. Chicago agents have spent three months not knowing what to tell their sellers. That uncertainty was the expensive part. Today they can answer the question.

**Q [REFRAMED]: What does today's ruling mean for the agents you work with?**

> It means the answer to "can buyers find my house" is the same answer it was last year. For a listing agent, that's the entire conversation. The ruling is a headline. The conversation with the seller is the job.

**Q: Why do you think this matters beyond just Chicago?**

> Because every market is having a version of this conversation, and Chicago's the one that went first. Agents in other cities are watching what their sellers get told here, and they should be.

**Q: Why is it important for homes to stay visible?**

> Because the seller decides. That's the part that gets lost. A seller who wants their house in front of the most people possible should be able to get that, and a seller who wants it quiet should be able to get that too. What matters is that it's their call and they made it on purpose.

**Q: What gives you confidence moving forward?**

> Agents ask better questions than they did a year ago. I get asked about syndication and portal display now by people who'd never used those words before. That's not a small thing. An informed agent is a hard agent to make a decision for.

## Block 3 -- If the ruling doesn't go Zillow's way

Tone: steady and reassuring. The temptation on a bad-ruling day is to go hot in Zillow's defense. That's the clip that turns D.J. into a mouthpiece. Don't.

**Q: What would you tell clients who are confused by today's news?**

> That a court ruling and your house are two different things. The ruling is about how companies exchange data. Your house is about what your agent does with it. Those got tangled together in the headlines and they shouldn't be.

**Q: What's one thing buyers should focus on instead of headlines?**

> Whether their agent can tell them where their search is coming from. Not the portal, not the brand, the actual source. A buyer who knows that is in good shape no matter what any court decides.

**Q: What advice do you have for someone starting their search today?**

> The same advice as last week. Ask where your agent is looking and where they're not. That question got more useful today, not less.

**Q: What gives you confidence even after today's ruling?**

> Because transparency doesn't run on a court schedule. Sellers have started asking where their home shows up. Buyers have started asking what they're not seeing. You don't un-ask those questions. That's the part I'd bet on.

**Q [VERIFY BEFORE ANSWERING -- see README]: Why did you set up a direct feed with Zillow? What was that process like?**

> **DO NOT ANSWER THIS UNTIL VERIFIED.** This is the strongest question in Zillow's brief, because it's operational and nobody else can answer it. It's also the easiest one to get wrong on camera.
>
> Confirm first: did Kale Realty actually set up a direct feed or the MLS Grid PDAP backup feed, who did it, and when? Or did D.J. only publish a video explaining how other brokerages could do it?
>
> If Kale did it, this is sixty seconds of real operational detail and it should lead the package. If D.J. only covered it, the honest answer is "I didn't set one up, I spent a week explaining to other brokers how to," which is still a good answer and is also true. Blurring the two is the kind of thing that gets caught at 171K views.
