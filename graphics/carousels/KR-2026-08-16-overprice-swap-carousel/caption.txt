"Let's start high and see" is the most expensive sentence in a listing appointment.

A home priced above the comps doesn't get a chance to prove itself. It sits. Then the price cut that follows reads as a red flag instead of a correction, because the buyers who watched it sit already assume something is wrong with it. The buyers who would have paid full price are already under contract somewhere else.

The fix isn't arguing with the seller about the number. It's showing the math before anyone agrees to one: the three closest sold comparables, and a 14-day check-in on the calendar from day one, so the pricing conversation happens on your terms instead of after the showings stop.

Price it to win the first two weeks. That's the only pitch that matters.
