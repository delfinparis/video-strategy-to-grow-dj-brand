Alex Maldonado, a REALTOR with Circle Real Estate in Los Angeles, had a buyer who'd saved her
whole down payment and was still about $4,000 short on closing costs. That gap is where most
first-time buyer deals quietly die.

His fix stacks two sources instead of leaning on one. "We're getting them lender credits while at
the same time writing into the contract that the seller gives the seller credits," he said.
"That's basically what we do to get them covered."

He builds the credit into the offer price itself, so the seller's net proceeds don't take the
hit. When a credit-heavy offer risks looking weak, he adds 3% earnest money to show commitment,
and works with a lender who can close escrow in 21 days, which matters most to a seller who needs
the cash fast.

The down payment was never the obstacle. The conversation about closing costs, had early instead
of late, is.
