This week NAR voted down a proposed rule that would have required agents to disclose when a neighborhood or property type isn't their specialty. Their reason why is exactly backwards.

It came from NAR's own Professional Standards Committee at the midyear meetings in D.C. The committee chair called it an ethical duty. The Executive Committee sent it back. One argument against it: admitting you don't know something would make a client lose trust in you.

In twelve years interviewing top producers on the Keeping It Real Podcast, I've never met one who pretended to know every market in the city. Chicago is fifty different markets. Most agents think admitting a gap loses the deal. The best ones know it's what earns the client for life.

Slide 7 is the move for the next lead that lands somewhere you don't know cold.
