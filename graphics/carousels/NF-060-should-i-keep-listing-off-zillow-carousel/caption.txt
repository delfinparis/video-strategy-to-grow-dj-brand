A seller is going to text you this week asking if they should keep their home off Zillow. Here's the honest answer, backed by the numbers.

Zillow's Chief Industry Development Officer, Errol Samuelson, testified under oath this week. A new listing gets almost 180 views the day it hits the market. By day five, that drops to about 60.

That's not just Zillow talking. Bright MLS and Drexel University analyzed over a million transactions. On-MLS homes sold for about 17.5% more than comparable off-MLS homes, roughly $54,000 for a typical seller.

Off-MLS gets pitched as control and privacy. What sellers don't hear is the price. Fewer buyers see it. Fewer bids. A winning bid from a smaller pool.

Off-MLS is a service to the brokerage. On-MLS is a service to the seller.
