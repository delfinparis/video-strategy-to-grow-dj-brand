Cotality's chief economist, Selma Hepp, told HousingWire this week that 80 to 90 percent of new home sales now require a mortgage rate buydown to close. Builders cut the median new home price 5.8 percent year over year to $393,700 in August, and buyers still needed the buydown to qualify.

That's the real competition a resale listing is up against right now. Not a lower price. A lower payment.

If you're pricing a resale listing against new construction, the comparison isn't sticker price to sticker price. It's your seller's price against the builder's price plus a buydown. Bring a seller-paid rate buydown into the conversation before you bring another price cut, and show every buyer the payment at that rate, not the listed one.
