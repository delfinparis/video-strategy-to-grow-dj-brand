"Who set the price? Did you, or did an agent, based on comps?"

That's the question Bill Melnick asks every time he takes over a listing that didn't sell. He's a
luxury agent in Litchfield County, Connecticut, with over $144 million in career volume, and the
listing that tests this hardest is the one he's best known for: tennis legend Ivan Lendl's estate,
first listed in 2014 at just under $20 million with another agent, sold in 2024 for $12 million
under Bill, still the county record.

His read on the hard part: it's never the marketing. It's getting a seller to accept that the
first number was wrong. So he doesn't argue it himself. He asks who set it, then points to how
long the house has sat as the market's own verdict. "The market is speaking. It's not me."
