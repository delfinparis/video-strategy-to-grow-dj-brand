NAR publishes two different first-time buyer numbers, and most agents have only been quoting one.

Twenty-one percent is the November Profile survey. Record low, tracked since 1981, the number every headline ran.

Twenty-nine percent is the July 2026 existing home sales report, released August 11. That's the share of everything that actually closed last month.

Both are NAR. Both are correct. They count different people. The 21 asks buyers if this was their first home ever. The 29 uses mortgage data, anyone who hasn't owned a home in three years, including someone buying alone again after a divorce or a death.

The mortgage definition calls them first-time buyers. Your marketing calls them nothing. Slide 4 has three ways to find that gap in your own database.
