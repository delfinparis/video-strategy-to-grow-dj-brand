## 1. Taking a listing at a price the comps don't support, because another agent already agreed to it and you don't want to lose the appointment

**ST-0001** | Pricing and listing intake | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **Realtor.com**

**Who is complaining:** Sellers after the fact, and buyer's agents who watch the listing go stale

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Three agents pitch. Two give an honest number, the third buys the listing at fifty thousand higher to flatter the seller. Then no showings, no offers, and thirty days later the let's discuss an adjustment calls begin. Then another. Then another.

**The angle on the fix:** Put the reduction schedule in writing up front so a cut is a plan instead of a panic

**The swap:** Price to the comps and be willing to lose a listing you can't price honestly. If a seller insists on testing high, put the reduction schedule in the listing agreement on day one, so the cut is a plan rather than a monthly argument.

**Hook (starting point; strip it back to the thing agents do):** "The agent who quoted you the highest price didn't do you a favor. They bought your listing, and you'll pay it back at closing with interest."

## 3. Vanishing during the week before closing

**ST-0017** | The slow fade | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.0 | receipt: **Redfin**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers at peak emotional investment
