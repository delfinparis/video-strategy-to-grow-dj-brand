## 1. [TIP] Taking a listing at the seller's number

- **Hook:** You took the listing at the seller's number.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Bank:** ST-0001 | target: self | receipt: confirmed (Realtor.com, Jun 2026 — Joel Berner, senior economist: homes at week 4 close 1.8% above monthly average relative to asking; homes still on market at week 18 close 1.3% below — a gap of more than 3 points)
- **Looks like:** Three agents pitch. Two give the honest number, the third buys the listing at fifty thousand over to flatter the seller. No showings, no offers, and thirty days later the price-cut calls begin. Then another. Then another.
- **The fix:** Price to the comps. If the seller insists on testing high, put the reduction schedule in the listing agreement day one — so the cut is a plan, not a monthly argument.
- **Angle:** Put the reduction schedule in writing up front so a cut is a plan instead of a panic
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: agents who buy a listing by quoting high make the seller pay for it at closing.

## 2. [TIP] Leaving a material defect off the disclosure form

- **Hook:** You told your seller to skip the flood disclosure.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Bank:** ST-0010 | target: sideways | receipt: confirmed (Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77/55 — seller who knowingly violates is liable for actual damages and court costs; 24 mandatory disclosure items; continuing obligation to supplement before closing under Section 30)
- **Looks like:** Seller mentions the basement takes water every spring. Agent says don't put that on the form, repaint the water line, mark unaware. Buyers move in, first storm hits, four inches ruin the finished basement they were promised was dry.
- **The fix:** Guide sellers to fix or disclose — get a bid, credit the buyer, or state it in writing. Every time. NAR Article 2 is explicit: latent material defects are not confidential.
- **Angle:** A defect disclosed and priced in is a closed deal; hidden, it is a rescission or a judgment
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: hiding a defect flips the liability, and Illinois has a statute for it with a real dollar penalty.

## 3. [TIP] Getting paid when clients use your inspector

- **Hook:** You get paid when clients use your inspector.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Bank:** ST-0052 | target: sideways | receipt: confirmed (RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. 2607 — fine up to $10,000 or up to one year in prison, or both; joint and several liability at three times the settlement service charge)
- **Looks like:** Agent says use my inspector, my lender, my title company. Warm and helpful. What the client never hears: the agent gets a per-referral fee or has a stake in the affiliated title company.
- **The fix:** Put any affiliated-business arrangement in writing before the referral. Better: give three independent options. "I get nothing if you use them. I recommend them because they're good."
- **Angle:** Recommend on merit, disclose every dollar, and hand them three independent options
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: undisclosed referral fees from vendors you recommend are a federal crime under RESPA, with triple-damage civil liability.
