## 1. [TIP] You get paid when clients use your vendors

**ST-0052** | Conflicts of interest | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. 2607**

- **Hook:** You get paid when clients use your vendors.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Looks like:** Agent warmly recommends their inspector, lender, and title company -- but never mentions the per-referral payment they receive for each one.
- **The fix:** Disclose any affiliated-business arrangement in writing before the referral. Give clients three independent options. Say plainly: "I get nothing if you use them. I recommend them because they're good."
- **Angle:** An undisclosed kickback turns the recommendation into a conflict. Disclosing it or removing it is what makes the agent trustworthy.
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: the agent taking undisclosed vendor kickbacks, the federal law that bans it, and the two-sentence fix.

## 2. [TIP] You don't know your own neighborhood

**ST-0056** | Market competence | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.0 | receipt: **NAR 2026 Member Profile via HousingWire**

- **Hook:** You don't know your own neighborhood.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Looks like:** Buyer asks about property taxes, school district lines, or last year's reassessment -- and the agent reaches for their phone or says they'll get back to them. The buyer quietly notes it.
- **The fix:** Build one page per neighborhood and know it cold: price per square foot, days on market, school boundaries, reassessment cycle, zoning quirks. Answer in one sentence, no phone.
- **Angle:** Knowledge on demand is the credential buyers actually test for. A one-page neighborhood sheet is the system.
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: the agent who freezes on a basic neighborhood question, why buyers lose trust instantly, and the one-page fix.
