## 2. [TIP] You get paid by the lender you recommend

- **Hook:** You get paid by the lender you recommend.
- **Verdict:** That's really stupid. Here's why.
- **Bank:** ST-0052 | target: sideways | receipt: confirmed (RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. 2607)
- **Looks like:** "Use my guy for the lender, the inspector, and the title company." Warm and helpful. What the client never hears: the agent gets a per-referral payment, a marketing fee, or a stake in the affiliated company.
- **The fix:** Put any affiliated-business arrangement in writing before the referral and give the client three independent options. Say it plainly: "I get nothing if you use them. I recommend them because they're good."
- **Angle:** Recommend on merit, disclose every dollar, hand them three independent options
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: federal law requires disclosure of any payment for a referral, and hiding it carries a triple-damages penalty.

- **Bank:** ST-0001 | target: self | receipt: confirmed (Realtor.com, Joel Berner, 2026-06-11 — homes priced right sell 1.8% above asking; homes at 18 weeks close 1.3% below, a 3-point gap)
- **Looks like:** Three agents pitch. Two give an honest number. The third buys the listing at fifty thousand higher. Then no showings, no offers, and thirty days later the price-reduction calls begin.
- **The fix:** Price to the comps and be willing to lose a listing you can't price honestly. If the seller insists on testing high, put the reduction schedule in the listing agreement on day one — a plan, not a monthly argument.
- **Angle:** Put the reduction schedule in writing up front so a cut is a plan instead of a panic
- **Fits 30s?** One idea: overpricing costs the seller money at closing and costs you the repeat business.
