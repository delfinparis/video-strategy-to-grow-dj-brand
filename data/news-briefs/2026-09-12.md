## 1. Leaving a material property fact out of the disclosure and hoping nobody asks

**ST-0010** | Duty and disclosure | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, and the E&O carrier

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Seller mentions the basement takes water every spring. Agent says don't put that on the disclosure, repaint the water line and mark unaware. Buyers move in, first storm hits, four inches ruin the finished basement they were promised was dry.

**The angle on the fix:** The question that finds the thing the seller wasn't going to mention

**The swap:** Ask the seller one question before the disclosure goes out: 'What would you want to know if you were buying this house?' Then write down whatever they pause on.

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, citation corrected. 765 ILCS 77/35 sets the mandatory disclosure form, which carries 24 numbered items (the bank said 23) and a continuing obligation to supplement before closing under Section 30. The remedy is 765 ILCS 77/55, verified separately and quoted: a seller who knowingly violates the Act or discloses information they know to be false 'shall be liable in the amount of actual damages and court costs, and the court may award reasonable attorney's fees incurred by the prevailing party.' Note attorney's fees are discretionary ('may award'), not automatic. The bank previously cited only the 77/35 form page, which contains no remedy language. (Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77, 2026)

> **Flag:** Legal-exposure entry. Cite the state disclosure statute and a real filed case, not a general 'you could get sued.'

## 2. Running down the agent on the other side of the deal in front of your own client

**ST-0013** | Offers and negotiation | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **NAR 2026 Code of Ethics, Article 15 and SOP 15-2/15-3**

**Who is complaining:** Clients who lose confidence in the whole deal, and the agent being run down

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Feels like being on the client's team. That listing agent is an idiot, the seller's clearly cheap and difficult, this'll be a nightmare. Said right before two sides have to cooperate to close, and the client quietly wonders what you say about them.

**The angle on the fix:** Your client is not hearing an opinion about that agent, they are hearing what you'll say about them

**The swap:** Next time the other agent does something that deserves it, say one sentence to your client: 'I'll handle it.' Then handle it. The complaint goes to the managing broker, not to the person you're representing.

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, citation corrected. Article 15 verbatim: 'REALTORS shall not knowingly or recklessly make false or misleading statements about other real estate professionals, their businesses, or their business practices.' The online/social-media reach comes from SOP 15-2, which extends the duty to statements repeated 'in person, in writing, by technological means (e.g., the Internet), or by any other means,' and SOP 15-3, which adds a duty to clarify or remove false statements by others on electronic media the REALTOR controls. The bank previously cited the case-interpretations page, which carries neither the Article text nor the social-media language. (NAR 2026 Code of Ethics, Article 15 and SOP 15-2/15-3, 2026)

> **Flag:** Verify the NAR Code of Ethics Article 15 wording (false or misleading statements about competitors) before citing it on camera.

## 3. Calling a windowless or closetless room a bedroom in the listing

**ST-0032** | The listing itself | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **2024 International Residential Code, Section R310 (emergency escape and rescue openings) and R304/R303**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, appraisers, lenders, and eventually a plaintiff's attorney

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** A finished-basement fourth bedroom with no window. A six by seven nook listed as a bedroom because it has a door. A three-bed where the third is a converted attic with no egress and no heat vent.

**The angle on the fix:** Count the way an appraiser will, and use the honest word instead

**The swap:** No legal egress plus heat source means den, flex room, office, or bonus room. Never bedroom. If it is close, say easily converted to a fourth bedroom instead of miscounting.

**Hook (starting point; strip it back to the thing agents do):** "That room you're calling a bedroom? If a buyer can't climb out the window in a fire, it's not a bedroom, and now you own that lie."

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, re-sourced from the blog to the code. A legal bedroom needs an egress opening with a minimum net clear opening of 5.7 sq ft, at least 24 inches of opening height and 20 inches of opening width; a minimum floor area of 70 sq ft with at least 7 feet in any horizontal direction; and heat capable of maintaining 68 degrees (a space heater does not count). Say '2024 IRC', and note local amendments can differ. (2024 International Residential Code, Section R310 (emergency escape and rescue openings) and R304/R303, 2024)
