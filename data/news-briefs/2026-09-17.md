## 1. Leaving a material property fact out of the disclosure and hoping nobody asks

**ST-0010** | Duty and disclosure | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, and the E&O carrier

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Seller mentions the basement takes water every spring. Agent says don't put that on the disclosure, repaint the water line and mark unaware. Buyers move in, first storm hits, four inches ruin the finished basement they were promised was dry.

**The angle on the fix:** The question that finds the thing the seller wasn't going to mention

**The swap:** Ask the seller one question before the disclosure goes out: 'What would you want to know if you were buying this house?' Then write down whatever they pause on.

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, citation corrected. 765 ILCS 77/35 sets the mandatory disclosure form, which carries 24 numbered items (the bank said 23) and a continuing obligation to supplement before closing under Section 30. The remedy is 765 ILCS 77/55, verified separately and quoted: a seller who knowingly violates the Act or discloses information they know to be false 'shall be liable in the amount of actual damages and court costs, and the court may award reasonable attorney's fees incurred by the prevailing party.' Note attorney's fees are discretionary ('may award'), not automatic. The bank previously cited only the 77/35 form page, which contains no remedy language. (Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77, 2026)

> **Flag:** Legal-exposure entry. Cite the state disclosure statute and a real filed case, not a general 'you could get sued.'

## 2. Taking undisclosed kickbacks from recommended inspectors, lenders, or title companies

**ST-0052** | Conflicts of interest | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. 2607 (prohibition also at 12 CFR 1024.14)**

**Who is complaining:** Clients, the CFPB, and federal prosecutors

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Use my guy for the lender, the inspector, and the title company. Warm and helpful. What the client never hears is that the agent gets a per-referral payment, a marketing fee, or a stake in the affiliated title company.

**The angle on the fix:** Recommend on merit, disclose every dollar, and hand them three independent options

**The swap:** Put any affiliated-business arrangement in writing before the referral and let the client choose freely. Better: give three independent options and say it plainly. 'I get nothing if you use them, I recommend them because they're good.'

**Hook (starting point; strip it back to the thing agents do):** "When your agent says use my guy, ask one question. Do you get paid if I do? Federal law says they have to tell you."

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, citation corrected. The prohibition is 12 CFR 1024.14: 'No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback or other thing of value pursuant to any agreement or understanding' for referrals of settlement services. The PENALTIES are not in the CFR section the bank cited; they are 12 U.S.C. 2607(d), verified and quoted: (d)(1) 'shall be fined not more than $10,000 or imprisoned for not more than one year, or both'; (d)(2) joint and several liability 'in an amount equal to three times the amount of any charge paid for such settlement service.' Say the statute, not the CFR, when the penalty is spoken. (RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. 2607 (prohibition also at 12 CFR 1024.14), 2026)

## 3. Taking a listing at a price the comps don't support, because another agent already agreed to it and you don't want to lose the appointment

**ST-0001** | Pricing and listing intake | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **Realtor.com**

**Who is complaining:** Sellers after the fact, and buyer's agents who watch the listing go stale

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Three agents pitch. Two give an honest number, the third buys the listing at fifty thousand higher to flatter the seller. Then no showings, no offers, and thirty days later the let's discuss an adjustment calls begin. Then another. Then another.

**The angle on the fix:** Put the reduction schedule in writing up front so a cut is a plan instead of a panic

**The swap:** Price to the comps and be willing to lose a listing you can't price honestly. If a seller insists on testing high, put the reduction schedule in the listing agreement on day one, so the cut is a plan rather than a monthly argument.

**Hook (starting point; strip it back to the thing agents do):** "The agent who quoted you the highest price didn't do you a favor. They bought your listing, and you'll pay it back at closing with interest."

**Verdict (beat two, verbatim, every script):** "That's really stupid. Here's why."

**Receipt:** Homes closing around the four week mark sell 1.8% above the monthly average relative to asking; homes still on market at 18 weeks close 1.3% below. Realtor.com frames the gap as more than 3 percentage points. Published 2026-06-11, senior economist Joel Berner. (Realtor.com, 2026)
