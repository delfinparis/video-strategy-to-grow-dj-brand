## 1. Going radio silent after the contract is signed, as if the job ended at acceptance

**ST-0002** | Responsiveness and communication | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **NAR**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, sellers, and the agent on the other side of the deal

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Four-minute replies while they're shopping. Then the ink dries and 'did the inspector send anything back?' sits unanswered for two days. You've trained the client to open every text with 'sorry to bug you again.'

**The angle on the fix:** The published response standard: tell the client your response window out loud at signing, so silence becomes a breach they can name

**The swap:** At the signing table, say the window. 'You'll hear from me every Friday, and inside four hours on anything urgent.' Then put a recurring Friday task in your phone before you leave the table.

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** Responsiveness ranks among the qualities buyers value most; Gen X and Boomers rank it above process knowledge. (NAR, 2026)

## 2. Leaving a material property fact out of the disclosure and hoping nobody asks

**ST-0010** | Duty and disclosure | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.7 | receipt: **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, and the E&O carrier

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Seller mentions the basement takes water every spring. Agent says don't put that on the disclosure, repaint the water line and mark unaware. Buyers move in, first storm hits, four inches ruin the finished basement they were promised was dry.

**The angle on the fix:** The question that finds the thing the seller wasn't going to mention

**The swap:** Ask the seller one question before the disclosure goes out: 'What would you want to know if you were buying this house?' Then write down whatever they pause on.

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** Illinois 765 ILCS 77 requires disclosing known material defects on the 23-item form, and under 765 ILCS 77/55 a violating seller is liable for actual damages plus court costs and attorney's fees. (Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, 765 ILCS 77, 2026)

> **Flag:** Legal-exposure entry. Cite the state disclosure statute and a real filed case, not a general 'you could get sued.'

## 3. Listing a property with no showing instructions, no working lockbox, and a phone number nobody answers

**ST-0011** | Showings and access | target: **sideways** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **Virtuance**

**Who is complaining:** Buyer's agents, and sellers who never learn the showings didn't happen

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Buyer in town for one afternoon, eight homes. Seven have lockboxes and book in ten minutes. The eighth is call to show. Voicemail, text, wait, callback four hours later, seller can only do Thursday. Buyer flies home Wednesday. Never shown.

**The angle on the fix:** A showing that never happened looks exactly like a house nobody wanted, and the seller can't tell the difference

**The swap:** Open your own active listings in the MLS as if you were a buyer's agent. If you can't figure out how to get in within thirty seconds, neither can they.

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** The typical Chicago home takes about 47 days to sell. (Virtuance, 2026)
