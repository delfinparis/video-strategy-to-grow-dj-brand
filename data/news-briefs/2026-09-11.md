## 1. The part-timer who hides the day job and cannot keep pace with the transaction

**ST-0030** | Showings and access | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.0 | receipt: **NAR 2026 Member Profile via HousingWire**

**Who is complaining:** Clients who discover the constraint by losing a house to it

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** A nine-to-five they don't mention. Showings only after 6pm, calls to voicemail all day, offer deadlines landing while they're unreachable at their desk. The buyer loses a house because the offer couldn't go in until 8pm, and never knew why.

**The angle on the fix:** Disclose it up front and build real business-hours coverage, so the client never discovers it by losing

**The swap:** If you're part-time, say so at the first meeting and build actual coverage, a licensed partner or TC who works business hours. Never let a client find out by losing a house.

**Hook (spoken, sharpen at build time):** "Your agent has a full-time job they never told you about. That's why you keep losing houses. Ask the question."

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, figures corrected and re-sourced. The typical NAR member completed 10 transaction sides in 2024, holding steady from 2023. Among agents working fewer than 20 hours a week, 57% earn under $25,000 a year and 29% earn $25,000-$49,000 -- say '57%', not 'mostly'. Median REALTOR gross income was $58,100 in 2024, up from $55,800. The bank previously pointed at a DataDigest article that does not carry these figures. (NAR 2026 Member Profile via HousingWire, 2026)

## 2. Talking buyers into waiving the inspection to make the offer look cleaner

**ST-0049** | Cutting corners on protection | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **NAR REALTORS Confidence Index, September 2024**

**Who is complaining:** Buyers, holding a five-figure repair bill

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** To win this we need to waive inspection, sellers hate contingencies. First-timers agree because the agent said so. Two months later the sewer line collapses or the knob-and-tube shows up behind the drywall. No contingency, no leverage, no recourse, and the bill is theirs.

**The angle on the fix:** The information-only inspection is the middle path buyers do not know exists

**The swap:** Offer the option nobody told them about: an information-only or pass-fail inspection. Walk from major defects, agree not to nickel-and-dime the small stuff. Or shorten the window instead of deleting it. Reserve a true waiver for buyers who can self-insure a five-figure surprise.

**Hook (spoken, sharpen at build time):** "Your agent wants you to waive the inspection to look serious. Here's the forty thousand dollars that decision can cost you."

**Receipt:** VERIFIED 2026-09-10, claim narrowed to what survives. USABLE: only about 18% of buyers waived the inspection contingency (NAR REALTORS Confidence Index, September 2024) -- so waiving is not the norm the pressure implies. DROPPED: the '$11,222 in hidden repairs' figure does not appear on the cited page at all. NOT FOR CAMERA: the 86%-of-inspections-find-an-issue and $14,000-saved figures are real on the page but both trace to Porch.com, a home-services marketplace, and do not meet Rule 1's source bar. (NAR REALTORS Confidence Index, September 2024, 2024)

## 3. Taking a listing at a price the comps don't support, because another agent already agreed to it and you don't want to lose the appointment

**ST-0001** | Pricing and listing intake | target: **self** | heat ceiling 4.3 | receipt: **Realtor.com**

**Who is complaining:** Sellers after the fact, and buyer's agents who watch the listing go stale

**What it looks like (Act 2, pre-written):** Three agents pitch. Two give an honest number, the third buys the listing at fifty thousand higher to flatter the seller. Then no showings, no offers, and thirty days later the let's discuss an adjustment calls begin. Then another. Then another.

**The angle on the fix:** The seller's side of it: what to say when the seller tells you another agent promised more

**The swap:** Ask one question back. 'Did they show you the three closest comps, or did they show you a number?' Then show yours.

_1 other open angle(s) banked on this practice._

**Receipt:** Homes closing around the four week mark sell 1.8% above the monthly average relative to asking; homes still on market at 18 weeks close 1.3% below. Realtor.com frames the gap as more than 3 percentage points. Published 2026-06-11, senior economist Joel Berner. (Realtor.com, 2026)
