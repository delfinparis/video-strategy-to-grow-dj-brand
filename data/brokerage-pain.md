# Brokerage pain: the Broker Problems bank

Standing candidates for the Broker Problems lane (Tue/Thu videos, plus the Tuesday
broker-problem comparison carousel). Strategy: [`../docs/strategy/2026-08-18-why-agents-leave.md`](../docs/strategy/2026-08-18-why-agents-leave.md).
Build rules: [`../docs/series/broker-problems-standard.md`](../docs/series/broker-problems-standard.md).

Seeded 2026-08-18. The calendar has pointed at this file since the July goal reset; it is
now real.

**These are candidates, not scripts.** Nothing here ships without re-verification at build
time. A figure that was right in July may be wrong by October, and half of what follows is
marked `receipt: needed` on purpose -- an entry with no sourceable number can still run, but
only in a flavor that does not require one.

---

## The qualifying test

A candidate belongs in this bank only if it clears all four:

1. **Is it about the arrangement between an agent and their brokerage?** If it is about a
   practice, a belief, or an incentive anywhere else in the business, it is a take. Send it
   to [`sacred-cows.md`](sacred-cows.md).
2. **Has the viewer normalized it?** The lane's whole mechanic is moving something from
   background to foreground. If agents already complain about it out loud, it produces
   agreement, not recognition, and agreement does not convert.
3. **Can it be said without a defensible counter-explanation existing?** For the "doesn't
   make sense" flavor this is strict: if there is a good reason the agent simply has not been
   told, that is a Value Giveaway explaining the reason, not a Broker Problem.
4. **Does it stand with the agent?** Friction points at the arrangement. Never at the agent
   for having accepted it. They accepted it because it was normal, and because nobody in the
   room had an incentive to flag it.

## What the entries are for

**The objective is to make the agent explore other options.** Every entry below is judged on
whether it can produce that, not on whether it is annoying.

The seed that works is **"I have never once checked whether this is normal,"** never "my
brokerage is bad." The first is undefendable and costs nothing to resolve. The second is
arguable, and defending six years of their own judgment is cheaper for the agent than
reconsidering it. **Seed ignorance of the market, not inadequacy of the firm.**

Every entry carries a **rung**, which is what its close can honestly ask for:

- **Rung 1** -- check your own situation. Costs nothing, produces recognition.
- **Rung 2** -- find out what normal is. Costs nothing, produces exploring. **This is the
  engine, and across any four weeks at least half the scripts should land here.**
- **Rung 3** -- have one conversation. Small social cost, highest intent, one script in four.

An entry that can only ever support rung 1 is a fine entry and a weak one. Note it as such.

## The heat ceiling

**3.5 default. Heat 4 only in a week the take lane surrenders its slot** (Rule 9.2 caps heat
4-5 at one post across the entire schedule, and the Wednesday take normally spends it).

**Heat 5 is banned permanently and is not rationed.** No named brokerage, franchise,
coaching program, or software product. The rule is **no identifiable target**, not "no name
typed" -- a knowing wink carries the same exposure with less punch, and D.J. is a brokerage
president quoted in trade press, not an anonymous account. The opposite failure is worse for
this lane: "some brokerages could do better" is safe, says nothing, and earns no comment. Go
maximally specific about the practice while the target stays a category.

**Ship test:** could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? If yes, rewrite.

## The Kale rule

**No Broker Problem video mentions Kale.** Not at the end, not in one line, not "at my
brokerage we do it differently." Reach dies and, worse, the comment mechanic dies -- nobody
outs their own brokerage underneath a competitor's ad. The Kale answer lives in the DM Ana
sends after Jennica flags the commenter. Be right in public, be Kale in private.

## Flavor A: The Slow Leak (money)

*Costs small enough that they never trigger a decision. Always annualized, always converted
to a thing the agent would have bought instead. Hook families 6 and 4. Heat 3.5.*

### A1. The fee your brokerage charges your client, that you defend and never see
**The pain:** an administrative or transaction fee added to your buyer's or seller's closing
statement. Your client asks you what it is at the table. You are the one who has to answer
for it, and not a dollar of it reaches you.
**Receipt: VERIFIED 2026-08-18, with a stated limit.** Consumer Policy Center, Stephen Brobeck
and Wendy Gilch, *"Junk" Fees Charged to Both Home Sellers and Buyers*, published 2026-07-28.
Administrative fees typically **$400 to $600**, with a full range of **$200 to more than
$2,000**; the report estimates roughly **$2 billion a year** in aggregate consumer cost. Agent
estimates collected in the report put it at **30% of listing agents and 60-75% of buyer
agents** charging one.
**The limit, and it must be honored:** the authors state the work is "based largely on
anecdotal evidence" drawn from agent interviews, social media, and prior trade reporting. The
$2B figure and the 30% / 60-75% splits are **estimates, not measurements.** Say "a report out
last month estimates," never "studies show." Do not put the $2B number in a hook.
https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/07/28/junk-brokerage-fees-cost-consumers-2b-annually-report-says
**Angles open:** (1) the fee you have to explain and do not receive; (2) it hits both sides of
the same deal at some firms; (3) your client learns about it at the closing table, which makes
it your credibility, not the brokerage's.
**Rung:** 3 (ask what it pays for, in writing), with a rung-2 variant available -- find out what the fee is at other firms in your market before you ask.
**Status:** open. Strongest money entry in the bank. Fresh, national, and it reframes a fee as
a *reputation* cost rather than a wallet cost, which is the angle nobody runs.

### A2. The cap you have never once hit
**The pain:** the cap is sold as the headline benefit and it is priced for the top decile.
Everyone pays into a structure whose best feature they will never reach.
**Receipt: not needed.** This one is the agent's own arithmetic, run on camera against their
own number. Do not attach an industry statistic to it -- the power is that they compute it
themselves in eight seconds.
**Supporting context (not spoken as a stat):** Consumer Federation of America, 2024, found
**49% of agents sold one or zero homes** that year. Already logged in
[`../docs/recruiting-objections.md`](../docs/recruiting-objections.md).
**Angles open:** (1) run your own cap math on camera; (2) the cap is a benefit for the person
you are being asked to become, not the person you are.
**Rung:** 2. The arithmetic is rung 1; the close has to push to "and find out what a cap looks
like at three other firms," or this entry stops at a shrug.
**Status:** open.

### A3. The technology fee for the platform you opened twice
**The pain:** a mandatory monthly charge for a CRM, website, or suite that the agent does not
use, cannot opt out of, and did not choose.
**Receipt: NEEDED.** Every figure I could find for monthly technology fees traces to brokerage
recruiting blogs and comparison-site content, not to a primary survey. **Do not put a dollar
range on camera until a real source exists.** Run this as annualized arithmetic on a number
the *viewer* supplies ("whatever your number is, times twelve") rather than a claimed range.
**Angles open:** (1) times twelve, and what that buys; (2) you are paying for a tool whose
real customer is the brokerage's recruiting page.
**Rung:** 2. Since no source exists for a market range, the close *is* the research: "go find
out what other firms charge for the same thing." The missing receipt turns into the ask.
**Status:** open, receipt-constrained.

### A4. Six years, and you have never renegotiated anything
**The pain:** tenure without a single review of the terms. Not a fee -- an absence.
**Receipt: VERIFIED 2026-08-18.** NAR 2025 Member Profile: median tenure with current firm is
**six years**; **28%** have been with their firm twelve years or more.
https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-show-strong-commitment-to-profession-amid-market-headwinds-new-nar-report-finds
**Angles open:** (1) six years of terms nobody revisited; (2) your production changed and your
agreement did not; (3) the renegotiation conversation, scripted -- **careful, that version is
a Value Giveaway, not a Broker Problem.**
**Rung:** 2 or 3.
**Status:** open. Pairs naturally with A2.

### A5. Dues nobody ever explained to you
**The pain:** association and MLS dues the agent pays without ever having been told what they
are for or why membership is mandatory through the brokerage.
**Receipt: FIRST-PARTY, verified 2026-08-18.** A guest on Keeping It Real, describing his own
brokerage: *"my broker never told me that. He just told me you have to be a part of the
association. And he never really explained all the background behind it... that's a lot of
problem with our industry. We don't really educate everybody on how it works."*
**Sourcing note:** the guest attribution needs to be pinned to a named episode before this
runs. The quote is in the transcript archive at
`keeping-it-real-content-system/data/transcripts/`; identify the episode and guest before
build, or paraphrase without attributing.
**Angles open:** (1) the bill you pay and cannot explain; (2) what the dues actually buy, told
straight -- **that version is a Value Giveaway.**
**Rung:** 1. Weak on the ladder -- it produces a shrug unless paired with a market question.
**Status:** open, attribution-constrained.

## Flavor B: The Thing That Doesn't Make Sense (logic)

*Arrangements whose only available explanation is that they benefit someone other than the
agent. Highest comment volume of the three flavors, because the viewer resolves the tension by
answering the question in the reply box. Hook families 4 and 7. Heat 3.5, occasionally 4.*

### B1. Your reviews are not yours
**The pain:** the testimonials an agent spends a decade collecting live on a platform the
brokerage licenses. Leave, and they do not come with you.
**Receipt: FIRST-PARTY, VERIFIED 2026-08-18.** Sara DeWulf, top producer, on *Keeping It Real*,
episode published **2023-10-31**, in her own words: *"I had, my previous brokerage, they use
testimonial tree, and when I switched I lost all of those. So I went to my Google page and I
think I had 11 reviews, which is absolutely ridiculous for doing this for 13 years."* She
rebuilt to roughly six or seven hundred afterward.
**Why this is the best entry in the bank:** it is D.J.'s own show, so there is no re-verifying
a third party's number; it is a specific human with a specific number; the loss is
irreversible in a way a fee is not; and every listing agent watching will immediately wonder
where *their* reviews live, which is the recognition mechanic working exactly as designed.
**Heat check:** the platform is named in the quote. **Do not say the product name on camera.**
"The review platform my brokerage paid for" carries the same meaning with none of the
exposure. Naming it is heat 5 and it is banned.
**Angles open:** (1) the reviews; (2) the wider version -- reviews, database, listing photos,
your own domain: run the ownership audit; (3) the two questions to ask before you collect one
more testimonial.
**Rung:** 2. Used in BP-001 -- the export button is rung 1, and the line that does the real work
is "whether that's how it works everywhere, or how it works where you are."
**Status:** open. **Recommended first script in the lane.**

### B2. Ownership audit: what of yours is on their system
**The pain:** database, marketing assets, listing photography, the transaction archive, the
email address on ten thousand business cards. Most agents have never asked which of these they
can export.
**Receipt: not needed.** This is a checklist, not a claim. Do not invent a statistic about how
many agents can export their CRM.
**Angles open:** (1) the four-item audit, three of them unpacked (Rule 10.4 intentional
incompleteness -- the fourth arrives in the replies); (2) the one email to send your broker.
**Rung:** 2. The audit is only interesting against a comparison.
**Status:** open. Sequence this *after* B1; B1 is the story that makes anyone care about the
audit.

### B3. The pitch you got is not the onboarding you got
**The pain:** the recruiting conversation described mentorship, leads, and training. Week
three, the calendar is empty.
**Receipt: NEEDED for any figure.** Runs fine as a scene with no number.
**Angles open:** (1) read the recruiting page you were pitched from, out loud, against your
actual last ninety days; (2) the questions that would have caught it -- **that version is a
Value Giveaway.**
**Rung:** 2, and unusually strong for the objective -- "go read the recruiting page of any firm,
including one you'd never join, and see what they promise" is exploring, dressed as an audit.
**Status:** open.

### B4. Their revenue from you goes up as their time to you goes down
**The pain:** the more you produce, the more the brokerage earns from you, and the less
attention you get -- because attention is triaged toward whoever is at risk of leaving or
whoever is new.
**Receipt: NEEDED.** No clean source exists for this and it will read as a claim without one.
**Careful:** this sits close to a takes-lane argument (an incentive, not an arrangement).
Qualifying test 1 is marginal. If it drifts toward "who profits," move it to
[`sacred-cows.md`](sacred-cows.md).
**Status:** parked pending a receipt.

### B5. Company leads go to the people who need them least
**The pain:** the distribution rule rewards production, so the agents who most need volume get
the least of it, and the ones who need it least get first refusal.
**Receipt: NEEDED.** Do not assert a distribution pattern as fact without a source (Rule 8.5 --
do not narrate a hidden mechanism as fact). Runs as a question, not a claim: "ask how yours
are routed, and whether anyone will tell you."
**Angles open:** (1) the one question about lead routing that most brokerages will not answer
in writing.
**Rung:** 3.
**Status:** open, must run interrogative.

### B6. Training scheduled for when you are working
**The pain:** the Tuesday 10am class, in the hours a working agent is with clients or on the
phone.
**Receipt: not needed.** Recognition, not statistics.
**Angles open:** (1) count how many sessions this year you could physically have attended;
(2) "available" versus "attendable."
**Rung:** 1, liftable to 2 ("find out when anybody else in this market runs theirs").
**Status:** open.

## Flavor C: The Silence (support)

*The moment you needed your broker and nobody picked up. Scene lane, not stat lane. Runs at
heat 3 because recognition does the work. Hook families 1 and 5. **At least twice a month** --
this is the flavor that converts and the one a fee-focused lane will quietly stop making.*

### C1. Ten times in thirteen years
**The pain:** the total volume of contact between an agent and the person whose name is on
their license.
**Receipt: VERIFIED 2026-08-18, with a stated limit.** Mike DelPrete, *"Research Study: What
Agents Really Want in a Brokerage,"* published **2025-08-21**. A participating agent: *"I might
have only communicated with my broker 10 times in 13 years."*
https://www.mikedp.com/articles/2025/8/21/research-study-what-agents-really-want-in-a-brokerage
**The limit, and it must be honored:** **ten** in-depth interviews, conducted June 2025, with
agents who had 5+ years of experience and had left a brokerage within the prior six months,
annual sales $3.5M-$35M. **This is a quote from one agent in a ten-person qualitative study.
It is never a percentage and never "research shows."** The honest framing is the honest one:
"a study came out last year that interviewed ten agents who had just left. One of them said
this." That framing is *stronger* than a fake statistic, because it is a person.
**Angles open:** (1) the quote, cold, then count your own; (2) the inverse -- when did your
broker last call you when nothing was wrong.
**Rung:** 1. Used in BP-002. Deliberately left at rung 1 -- this entry runs on recognition and a
market question would break the register.
**Status:** open. **Recommended second script in the lane.**

### C2. The Saturday the deal went sideways
**The pain:** the inspection blows up, the lender goes quiet, the other agent stops
responding -- on a weekend -- and there is no one to call.
**Receipt: not needed.** Scene.
**Angles open:** (1) the scene, told straight, ending on "who did you call"; (2) what
"full support" actually has to mean to be a real promise -- **overlaps the existing
[`BROKER-support-when-you-need-it-carousel.md`](../scripts/carousels/BROKER-support-when-you-need-it-carousel.md);
check that deck before drafting so the video and carousel are not the same argument.**
**Status:** open.

### C3. Nobody noticed your bad quarter
**The pain:** production dropped for three months and no one at the brokerage said a word.
**Receipt: not needed.** Scene. Supporting context, not spoken as a stat: DelPrete's departed
agents described leadership inaccessibility and absence of belonging as the drivers, above
splits and technology.
**Heat check:** this one gets close to sad. It is the emotional/identity lane (Rule 10.7), and
it is allowed to be -- but it needs a payoff that is a reframe, not a rescue, and it must not
end on Kale (see the Kale rule above).
**Status:** open.

### C4. The platform that became a second job
**The pain:** a required brokerage system that consumes the working week without returning
anything.
**Receipt: VERIFIED as a quote, UNUSABLE as spoken.** DelPrete's study, same source as C1,
quotes an agent: *"KW Command felt like a full-time job."*
**Heat check -- this is a hard block.** The quote names a product and a franchise. Quoting it
verbatim is heat 5 and is banned outright. The usable version carries no name: "the system my
brokerage required took more time than it gave back." If the script cannot land without the
brand, the script does not run.
**Rung:** 2.
**Status:** open, name-stripped only.

### C5. You left, and nobody called
**The pain:** the exit that produced no conversation. Not a retention failure at the end -- a
relationship that was never there.
**Receipt: not needed.** Scene.
**Careful:** D.J. runs a brokerage that people also leave. This entry cuts both ways and he
should be comfortable being asked about it in the replies before it ships. **Flag to D.J.
before build.**
**Rung:** 1.
**Status:** open, D.J. sign-off required.

## Flavor E: Run The Math With Me (arithmetic, performed)

*D.J.'s own register, supplied 2026-08-18. Heat 4, rung 2, hook families 3 and 6. The structure
is a rehearsed confrontation -- "if I were an agent, I'd walk into my managing broker's office
and do this out loud" -- with the arithmetic performed rather than presented, a fast list of
foreclosed purchases, a one-clause concession, a pre-empted objection answered with a checkable
number, and a close on irony. Six moves, documented in the standard. Reference: BP-005.*

**Needs the week's heat-4 slot.** See the standard's case for raising the Rule 9.2 cap; until
D.J. rules on it, this flavor runs only when the take lane yields.

### E1 / A6. Twenty percent of every check, annualized
**The pain:** the split is known as a percentage and has never once been converted into a
dollar figure with a year attached. Percentages do not feel like money. $18,000 does.
**Receipt: VERIFIED by direct query 2026-08-18.** Illinois IDFPR professional license database,
Socrata dataset `pzzh-kp68` at data.illinois.gov. Active licenses, Chicago IL address:

| | |
|---|---|
| Real Estate Broker Corporation | 735 |
| Real Estate Limited Liability Firm | 738 |
| Real Estate Broker Partnership | 7 |
| **Brokerage companies, total** | **1,480** |
| Licensed Real Estate Managing Brokers | 3,125 |
| Licensed Real Estate Brokers | 11,414 |

**The limit, and it is a hard one.** Spot-checking the Chicago corporation names returns
property management firms, commercial shops, and at least one industry data cooperative.
**These 1,480 are not all one-person residential sales brokerages and no script may say they
are.** The provable claim is that 1,480 licensed brokerage companies exist and a viewer can
name about twelve. "1,480 solo agents" is false and the list is public.

**Live data.** The number moves. Re-run the query the morning of filming.

**What was cut.** D.J.'s original rant said "over 2,000 agents in Chicago who self sponsor."
IDFPR carries no self-sponsorship field and the figure is not verifiable as stated. 1,480 was
chosen over 3,125 managing brokers because it needs no interpretation to confirm.

**The arithmetic** ($300k x 2.5% = $7,500; 20% = $1,500; x12 = $18,000) is a worked example,
stated as hypothetical in the script, not a market claim. The 2.5% is an assumption; post
settlement there is no citable average and none should be invented. Chicago's real median runs
higher (roughly $381k-$420k across 2026 sources), which would put the annual figure near
$24,000 -- **the conservative version is deliberate**, because it cannot be called
cherry-picked and an agent with a higher average does the upgrade themselves.

**The named-exemplar move (added with D.J.'s 2026-08-18 revision).** The abstraction "1,468
other firms" is a statistic. Two people the viewer already respects is proof. The revised script
sets a sarcastic trap ("those people must be crazy, doing no deals at all") and springs it one
second later with names.

**Verified exemplars, both named positively:**
- **Grigory "Greg" Pekarsky** -- co-founded Vesta Preferred Realty (Chicago, 2009) with Brad
  Robbins; Managing Broker; $1B+ career sales; firm reports Illinois' #1 independent team by
  volume since 2021 (RealTrends). **Past Keeping It Real guest, 2025-01-28.**
- **Matt Laricy** -- Managing Partner of Americorp Ltd, fourth-generation Chicago family
  brokerage; head of the LARICY Team; reported #1 in Chicago for volume and transactions by the
  Chicago Association of REALTORS across multiple years. Not a past guest.

**The correction, and it is load-bearing.** D.J.'s rant called both men self-sponsored agents
with "no fancy brokerage brand." **Neither self-sponsors** -- both are principals of independent
firms they own or co-own. The true version is the better argument: they did not go without a
brokerage, **they decided a national brand was not worth what it costs and built their own.**
Say "Greg built his own firm, Matt runs his family's." Never say they self sponsor.

**Positive naming is not heat 5.** The banned move is naming a party **as wrong**. Naming
someone as right is the Sara DeWulf pattern from B1. **But nothing critical may be said about
either man or his firm, and neither may be compared to Kale** -- either move turns them into
props in an ad and burns two real relationships.

**Courtesy heads-up before air is a production requirement.** D.J. runs a competing brokerage
and is using two named competitors' principals to argue about splits. Everything said is
factual and flattering, so there is no exposure, but they hear it from him first. Greg is warm
(past guest); Matt is the one who most needs the text, and it plausibly converts into a booking.
**Do not tag either man in the post** -- tagging is a summons, not a citation.

**Rung:** 2. The close, "maybe something to think about," is the softest ask in the entire lane
and it is correct for the objective. Do not strengthen it.
**Angles open:** (1) BP-005 as written, the 20% split; (2) the same performance run on a
monthly fee structure instead of a split; (3) the same performance run on a cap. **Each new
angle needs its own exemplars** -- reusing Pekarsky and Laricy every time turns two people into
a bit.
**Status:** angle 1 used in BP-005. Angles 2 and 3 open.

### E2. Itemize what the split actually buys
**The pain:** the direct answer to the strongest objection BP-005 will draw -- that the split
buys E&O, compliance, a managing broker on the hook, transaction coordination, and the office.
**Receipt: not needed.** The script is the itemization, done live, with the agent invited to
total their own.
**Why it is a separate entry:** the reply to that objection is not a rebuttal, it is "good, now
write that list down and see if it comes to eighteen thousand." That is a whole video and it is
stronger than arguing.
**Rung:** 2.
**Status:** open. Run it two to three weeks after BP-005, off the actual comments.

## Flavor D: The Permission Slip (allowed-ness)

*The flavor that makes acting on the doubt feel allowed. The other three produce a doubt; an
agent sitting on a doubt still does nothing, because looking around feels like a small
betrayal of people who have been decent to them and nobody has ever told them otherwise.
Hook families 5 and 3. Heat 3. About monthly. Rule 10.7 register -- the payoff is a reframe,
not a tactic.*

**This flavor only works in D.J.'s mouth.** A brokerage president saying agents should be
looking is a position that costs him something, which is exactly why it carries. Anyone else
saying it is recruiting. That makes his credential load-bearing rather than promotional
(Family 3, The Defector), and this is the one place in the lane where saying "I run a
brokerage" is required rather than tolerated.

**The failure mode that kills the whole lane:** if a Permission Slip resolves to "and that's
why you should look at Kale," it stops being permission and becomes the pitch it was
pretending not to be -- and the viewer retroactively re-reads the previous month of Broker
Problems as a funnel. That costs more than any single video gains. **This flavor carries the
strictest version of the Kale rule.**

### D1. Looking is not disloyalty
**The pain:** the agent who is quietly unhappy and has never looked, because looking feels
like something you would not want your broker to find out you did.
**Receipt: not needed.** This is D.J.'s stated position.
**The spine:** every professional in every other industry knows what their market rate is. In
real estate an agent can spend a decade never once finding out, and the reason is that the
whole social architecture of a brokerage treats the question as a betrayal. It is not. It is
due diligence on your own business, and a firm that would punish you for asking has answered
the question.
**Rung:** 2.
**Heat check:** heat 3, and keep it there. This must never sound like a dare.
**Status:** open. **Recommended fourth script in the lane** -- run it once BP-001 through
BP-003 have established that D.J. names problems without selling anything.

### D2. I run a brokerage and my agents should be looking too
**The pain:** the suspicion that anyone raising these questions is recruiting.
**Receipt: not needed.** D.J.'s own position, stated plainly.
**The spine:** the strongest version of D1, and the most exposed. D.J. says out loud that his
own agents should know what the market offers, and that a brokerage that has to rely on
nobody checking is not a brokerage worth staying at. **Requires D.J.'s explicit sign-off:
it is a real commitment and agents will hold him to it.**
**Rung:** 2.
**Status:** open, D.J. sign-off required.

### D3. The year I should have left and didn't
**The pain:** the agent staying somewhere out of loyalty, inertia, or fear of the conversation.
**Receipt: FIRST-PARTY, needs D.J.** A confession-register script (Family 5) about a time D.J.
stayed too long somewhere, in any part of his career, and what it cost. **Cannot be written
without him.** Rule 1 forbids inventing the story and the story is the entire script.
**Rung:** 2.
**Status:** blocked pending D.J.'s material.

### D4. The exit conversation, and how much less it costs than you think
**The pain:** the imagined confrontation that keeps agents from even exploring. Objection #2 in
the map -- switching feels like starting over -- shows up first as fear of the *conversation*,
long before anyone runs the numbers.
**Receipt: not needed for the video.** The version with a script for the conversation is a
Value Giveaway, not a Broker Problem. **Careful:** this entry sits closest to the
kale-signaling lane of anything in the bank. It stays a Broker Problem only as long as it is
about the conversation being survivable, not about where the agent goes afterward.
**Rung:** 2.
**Status:** open, boundary-sensitive.

## Not yet in the bank: the two sources D.J. is feeding

Both were requested on 2026-08-18 and neither has landed. The bank is thinner than it should
be until they do.

**D.J.'s recruiting calls.** Thirty superlative calls a day, and the specific complaint an
agent volunteers on those calls is the highest-quality material available for this lane --
Chicago-specific, current, and in the agent's own words. It cannot be cited as a statistic
on camera, but it is exactly what the "doesn't make sense" flavor runs on. **Intake:** dump
raw notes; entries get written here with `receipt: first-party, unquantified`.

**Kale's own departures.** The `Left Kale Date` field exists in Close. Why agents left *Kale*
is the most honest and most dangerous material in this lane, and it is the one D.J. can speak
to without a source at all. **Not started.** Requires D.J.'s judgment on how much of it he
wants said out loud before any of it becomes an entry.
