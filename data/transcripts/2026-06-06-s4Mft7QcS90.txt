The FTC just got asked to investigate how Zillow sends you leads, and I'm the guy who spent all month defending Zillow. Two members of Congress wrote the FTC on May 29th. The question's simple. When a buyer fills out a form on a portal, do they know they're being handed to an agent who paid to be there? The letter calls it deceptive. People steered without being told who's getting paid. Now, when that lead closes, Zillow can take as much as 40% of your commission. The buyer never sees that number. They think they tapped the listing agent. They tapped a referral machine. I've backed Zillow in the Emred fight because the MLS should stay neutral, but this is different. This is about what the buyer doesn't know. So, there's two moves. First, do the math on a portal lead. 40% off the top isn't free, so know what each close really nets you. Second, be the transparent one. Tell every buyer how you get paid and who referred them. That's the exact thing regulators are watching now. It's just a letter. The FTC hasn't moved yet, but it is the right question. If your buyer knew who was getting paid, would they still pick you? Make sure the answer's yes.
