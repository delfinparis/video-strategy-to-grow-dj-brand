NAR's Executive Committee just blocked a rule that would require agents to disclose when they don't know a market or a property type. Took 2 years to draft it, 1 hour to kill it. So, the Professional Standards Committee spent 2 years building this. The rule was simple, if you don't have the knowledge or skill for a property type or a geographic area, you tell your client. NAR's Executive Committee debated it for an hour on June 17th and sent it back to the committee. Translation, it's dead. So, the post-sister consumer protection era is the worst possible moment for the trade association to kill a transparency rule its own committee drafted. The argument against was that new agents would be hurt. The argument for was that clients deserve to know. The clients heard you. &gt;&gt; &gt;&gt; So, here's what you do now. Be the agent who makes the disclosure NAR wouldn't mandate. At your next buyer or listing consult, say this. I haven't sold a condo in the last 12 months. If you want someone who has, I'll refer you. If you want me, here's how I'm going to get up to speed. That's the rule NAR voted not to make. It's still the right one.
