In 2026, real estate's top producers are losing something they didn't know they had, and by the time they notice, it's gone. In the last 18 months, Compass bought Anywhere Real Estate for $1.6 billion. That deal puts Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Sotheby's, and Corcoran under one roof. Now, this week, Real Brokerage announced an $880 million deal for Remax. Three companies, roughly 200,000 agents, most of the traditional brokerage market in America. Now, for a decade, if you were doing 50 million plus in annual volume, those brands paid you to switch. Six-figure signing bonuses, equity grants, marketing budgets bigger than most agents' entire commission income. &gt;&gt; &gt;&gt; That premium existed for one reason: the brands were competing. Coldwell Banker had to outbid Sotheby's. Remax had to outbid Compass. Well, that's over. Coldwell Banker can't outbid Sotheby's, they share an owner. Real won't outbid Remax, it's the same company. The bidding war that built top producer leverage just became a conversation between cousins. Cousins don't pay premiums. So, the big three do not have to fight for top talent anymore. They just have to wait them out.
