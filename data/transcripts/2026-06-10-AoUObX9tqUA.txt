Two MLSs have now picked a side in the Compass-Zillow fight, but they shouldn't have. An MLS is supposed to be a database, not a political actor. So, MRED in Chicago cut Zillow off in May until a judge ordered the feed restored. RealTracks in Nashville, 18,000 brokers across four states, threatened the same cut. Their deadline passed yesterday with negotiations still active. Compass CEO Robert Refkin publicly thanked them. That's the tell. When one party in a dispute thanks you publicly, you weren't neutral. When your MLS picks a side, every member broker gets conscripted into a fight that they never agreed to. Your sellers don't care about portal politics. They want their listing on every site possible. An MLS that decides to play favorites is making decisions for sellers the sellers never asked for. The cooperative model breaks when the cooperative starts taking sides. So, here's what you do right now. If you sit on an MLS committee, raise the neutrality question. If you don't, ask your broker to. The MLSs that stay neutral are the ones every member can keep trusting. The ones that pick sides, well, they've just set the precedent for the next MLS.
