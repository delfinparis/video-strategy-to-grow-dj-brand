The first time home buyer just turned 40. That's NAR's newest number and it's a record. And if you're still running the nervous rookie pitch, you're pitching somebody who doesn't exist. You know the pitch. Somebody built it for a 30-year-old. Explain earnest money, slow it down, point them at a starter home. But here's where it gets worse. These people aren't nervous. They're outbid. First-timers put 10% down on average. The repeat buyers beating them put down 23% and three in 10 pay full cash. So, most agents lead with how the process works, but the ones winning these buyers lead with the competition. &gt;&gt; &gt;&gt; Pull the last 10 sales in their price band. Show what each sold for, what they put down, and whether it was cash. So, first-time buyers are down to 21% of the market, which is the smallest share NAR has recorded since 1981. So, here's what you do now. Open the last email you sent a first-time buyer. If it led with a process explainer, rebuild it before your next showing. 40-year-olds don't need the process explained. They need to know what they're up against.
