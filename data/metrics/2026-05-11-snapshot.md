**Read:** every engagement/reach metric reversed direction. The Apr 27 narrative was "we lost the March viral peaks; need to see whether the pivot content rebuilds reach." Three weeks of pivot content later, the rebuild is underway. Apr 28 → May 10 is the cleanest read available - both endpoints are post-Apr-18 pivot, so the trend is not a window-methodology artifact.

The rolling 30-day window ending May 10 is now ~70% post-pivot content (Apr 21 - May 10 = 20 days of 30). At the 2026-05-21 capture the window will be ~100% post-pivot; at that point any read becomes fully decisional. **The current direction makes the 5/21 read look directional, not deciding.**

**The follower drop is a single-day platform event, not turnover.** Native IG followers tab shows: 45 follows, 163 unfollows, net -118 over Apr 11 - May 10. The native daily-net graph hovers near 0 for ~25 of the 30 days, then spikes down to roughly -96 on one day around May 5-7, then returns to flat. Subtracting that spike: the other 29 days produced ~67 unfollows and 45 follows = -22 net over 29 days = ~-0.75/day, essentially flat-with-slow-drift churn on a 7,500-follower base. **The pivot is not driving turnover; the audience is stable except for one platform event.**

Diagnosis: **Meta inactive/spam account cleanup.** Posts in the May 5-8 window were not controversial (D.J. confirmed), and a content-triggered unfollow normally leaves a multi-day tail rather than a clean one-day spike. The shape of the graph (perfectly flat on both sides, single-day cliff, then flat again) is the textbook signature of a Meta-side inactive-account purge - the platform periodically removes bots and dormant accounts at scale, which shows up as a sudden one-day drop in the dashboard's daily-net followers chart. No further action; treat as platform noise.

Within-window engagement metrics (+41% interactions, +37% profile visits, +25% accounts engaged) are unaffected. The accounts removed in the purge were inactive; they weren't the ones engaging.

**Content split:** Reels 69.4% of views and 69.3% of interactions; Posts 22.3% / 28.8%; Stories 8.3% / 2%. Consistent with the IIR reel form being the load-bearing format.
