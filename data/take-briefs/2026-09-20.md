## 1. "Your first year is supposed to be brutal."

**Section:** Career and identity | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) |
**Hook shape:** 4 (Cohort address + indictment + promise)

**Who profits:** brokerages whose model depends on churn

**The turn:** the difficulty is not a rite of passage, it is an unmanaged onboarding

**Hook (spoken):** "If this is your first year, your brokerage already budgeted for you to earn
eight thousand dollars before taxes. That's not a rite of passage. Here's how you beat the
number."

**The swap:** Instead of waiting on training that isn't coming, track one number every week:
appointments set, not hours logged. That number is the real onboarding your brokerage skipped.

**Loop-back:** "Your first year wasn't supposed to be brutal. It was supposed to be unmanaged, and
somebody else made that choice for you."

**Receipt:** NAR's **2026 Member Profile** (published June 25, 2026, measuring 2025 income):
agents with two years or less of experience earned a **median gross income of $8,000**, versus
$88,500 for agents with 16+ years in the business. This updates the $8,100 figure used in the
(unshipped) 2026-09-13 brief, which came from the prior year's edition -- re-verified per the
routine's rule that a stat right in April may be wrong by September. Same underlying story as the
in-repo `docs/recruiting-objections.md` entry, sharper number.

## 2. "The national brand gives you credibility with clients."

**Section:** Brokerage economics | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 2 (Sacred Cow) |
**Hook shape:** 2 (Verdict + cost)

**Who profits:** the brand charging for it

**The turn:** consumers rank brokerage brand near the bottom of what they actually care about

**Hook (spoken):** "Your brokerage's logo isn't why a buyer picked you. It's not even in their top
four reasons."

**The swap:** Instead of leading your listing presentation with the company name, put your last
three closings and the client reviews on the first slide. That's what's actually on the list.

**Loop-back:** "The brand on your sign was never the reason they called. You were."

**Receipt:** NAR's **2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers**: buyers' top reported reasons for
choosing their agent were experience (21%), honesty and trustworthiness (19%), and reputation
(15%). Brokerage brand or firm affiliation does not appear among the top reported reasons at all.
Note: I could not find a standalone published percentage for "brand of firm" itself in the public
summaries of this release (the full breakdown sits behind NAR's paid report); the receipt as
written leans on the confirmed top-four list and brand's absence from it, not an invented rank
position for brand. This replaces the caveat the bank flagged ("needs the current NAR Profile
citation") with the actual 2025 edition.

## 3. "AI is going to replace agents."

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 8 (Cohort Callout) |
**Hook shape:** 3 (Cohort reversal)

**Who profits:** doom content, and the vendors selling the cure for it

**The turn:** it replaces the parts of the job nobody was paying an agent for anyway

**Hook (spoken):** "For everyone convinced AI is coming for your job, you're panicking about the
wrong thing. It's not coming for the job. It's coming for the parts of the job you were never
paid for."

**The swap:** Instead of avoiding the tools out of fear, hand AI the CMA prep and the disclosure
paperwork this week. Spend the hour you get back on the one phone call a client actually
remembers.

**Loop-back:** "AI was never coming for your job. It was coming for the parts of it you were doing
for free."

**Receipt:** NAR's **2025 REALTORS® Technology Survey** (published September 18, 2025): roughly
two-thirds of agents said they embrace new technology primarily to save time, and 64% said their
motivation was to enhance the client's experience -- not to hand off the relationship work.
Reported as "two-thirds" / "66-67%" in NAR's own release and in HousingWire's coverage of it; I
did not find an exact single-decimal figure in the public summary, only that rounded range, which
is how the source itself reports it rather than an estimate I've added.

## 4. "You need a bigger CRM / more leads."

**Section:** Practice | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap / List) | **Hook shape:** 1
(Imperative + harm)

**Who profits:** lead vendors

**The turn:** the database an agent already owns is unworked

**Hook (spoken):** "Stop buying a bigger lead list. It's covering for a database you've never
worked."

**The swap:** Instead of a new lead source this week, pull fifty people from your phone you
haven't texted in a year and send one of them something small today. No ask attached.

**Loop-back:** "You don't have a leads problem. You have an unopened database, and a bigger list
won't open it either."

**Receipt:** one verified case, not a population stat -- flagged as such. Chicago agent Rachel
Kilmer, profiled by **Inman, June 21, 2026** (source script `NF-052`, carousel `EVERGREEN-002`):
grew her business **74% a year for five straight years** running a pop-by system on her existing
sphere, no paid leads. This is the same receipt already in-repo for this entry; re-checked and
still holds. Single case, said as a single case in the swap, not generalized into an industry
percentage.

## 5. "Post as often as you can."

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 7 (Forbidden) | **Hook shape:**
6 (Diagnosed loss)

**Who profits:** content-mill services selling volume

**The turn:** past a threshold the extra posts compete with each other

**Hook (spoken):** "You know why your reach keeps dropping? You're posting every single day, and
each new post is competing with the one you put up yesterday."

**The swap:** Instead of filling the feed, hold one steady day a week and don't miss it for six
months straight. Skip the days you don't have something worth saying.

**Loop-back:** "More posts was never the growth lever. Showing up the same day, every week, was."

**Receipt:** **Buffer's 2026 platform study**: accounts that posted consistently across 20 of 26
weeks earned roughly **+450% more engagement per post** than sporadic posters. Already in-repo,
`docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #3; re-checked, no caveat on this one, still holds.
