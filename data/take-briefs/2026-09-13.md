## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal. -- WEDNESDAY PICK

**Section:** Career and identity | **Slot:** Wed | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 4 (System
Indictment) | **Hook shape:** 4 (Cohort address + indictment + promise)

**Who profits:** brokerages whose model depends on churn

**The turn:** the difficulty is not a rite of passage, it is an unmanaged onboarding

**Hook (spoken):** "If this is your first year, your brokerage already budgeted for you to quit
before month twelve. Here's how you don't."

**The swap:** Instead of waiting on training that isn't coming, track one number every week:
appointments set, not hours logged. That's the real onboarding, and it's the one your brokerage
didn't build for you.

**Loop-back:** "Your first year wasn't supposed to be brutal. It was supposed to be unmanaged, and
that was somebody else's choice, not yours."

**Receipt:** NAR 2024 Member Profile: agents with two years or less of experience earned a median
$8,100 that year, and 62% of them made less than $10,000. Already in-repo,
`docs/recruiting-objections.md`, cited to NAR's 2024 Member Profile highlights (published
2024-07-10). Sanity-checked against the NAR 2025 Member Profile, which shows the share of members
with two years or less of experience falling further (18% to 15%) -- consistent with the same
story, not a contradiction of it.

## 5. You need a bigger CRM / more leads. -- MONDAY PICK

**Section:** Practice | **Slot:** Mon | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap / List) | **Hook
shape:** 1 (Imperative + harm)

**Who profits:** lead vendors

**The turn:** the database an agent already owns is unworked

**Hook (spoken):** "Stop buying more leads. You're paying to replace people who already trust
you."

**The swap:** Pull your last three years of past clients and call ten of them this week. No pitch,
just checking in.

**Loop-back:** "The lead you're chasing isn't a stranger you haven't bought yet. It's a client you
already closed and stopped calling."

**Receipt:** A Kansas City agent grew her business 74% a year for five years, largely without paid
leads, working her own sphere instead. Source: Inman, "How This Agent Grew Her Business 74% YOY,
Spending Next To Nothing," 2026-06-21. Already in-repo (EVERGREEN-002 / NF-052), status:
confirmed.

## 6. Post as often as you can. -- FRIDAY PICK

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Fri | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 5 (Confession) |
**Hook shape:** 2 (Verdict + cost)

**Who profits:** content-mill services selling volume

**The turn:** past a threshold the extra posts compete with each other

**Hook (spoken):** "I told agents to post every single day. I was wrong, and it cost some of them
their reach."

**The swap:** Pick a schedule you can actually keep for six months, even if it's two posts a week,
and hold it instead of chasing daily volume.

**Loop-back:** "It was never about posting more. It was about showing up the same weeks in a row,
and that's the one thing daily posting can't fake."

**Receipt:** Buffer's "State of Social Media Engagement 2026" study (100,000+ accounts, 26-week
window): creators who posted in 20 or more of those 26 weeks earned roughly 450% more engagement
per post than creators who posted in 4 weeks or fewer. Already in-repo,
`docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #3, cited to Buffer 2026. Re-checked live: current on
Buffer's own site as of this week.
