## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal. — Wed, this week's hardest swing

**Section:** Career and identity | **Heat:** 4.5 | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) | **Shape:** 4, cohort address plus indictment plus promise

**Hook (spoken):** "If you're a realtor in your first year, your brokerage doesn't lose a dime if you quit by month twelve. That's why nobody built you a plan. Here's how you build one yourself."

**Who profits:** Brokerages whose recruiting model depends on churn. Replacing a licensee who quits costs them nothing. Training one costs money this quarter.

**The receipt:** 15 percent of REALTORS nationally have two years or less in the business right now, the lowest share on record, and that group's median gross income was $8,000 in 2025, against a $59,200 overall median. Source: NAR's 2026 Member Profile (covering 2025 activity), released June 25, 2026. Confirmed today against NAR's own feature sheet, HousingWire's coverage, and the Florida Realtors oversample report, all three of which cite the same figures.

**The swap:** "If you're new, stop waiting on floor time and company leads. This week, text 20 people you already know: I just got my license, keep me in mind."

**Loop-back:** "Your first year isn't brutal because the business is hard. It's brutal because nobody built you an onboarding, and calling that a rite of passage is how they get away with not building one."

## 3. Use AI to write your listing descriptions. — Fri

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Heat:** 4.2 | **Hook family:** 3 (Defector) | **Shape:** 2, verdict plus cost

**Hook (spoken):** "The AI-written listing description you posted isn't free. It's costing you the one buyer who would have called."

**Who profits:** The tool vendor selling the one-click button. They don't lose the showing. You do.

**The receipt:** 68 percent of buyers say clear labeling of AI-generated content in a property listing is important or essential, and 37 percent say it should be mandatory. Source: Cotality's 2026 AI in Housing survey, press release dated April 2026, fielded Jan 29 to Feb 9, 2026 across the US, Canada, the UK, and Australia.

**Receipt correction, read this before using it:** last week's brief (2026-10-18) cited this same 68 percent figure as "68 percent of buyers want to be told when AI wrote something they're reading," attributed loosely to "Cotality, AI in Housing 2026." That description does not match what the survey actually measured. The Cotality survey contains two different 68 percent findings: one on AI labeling importance (used above, and it supports this take) and a separate, unrelated 68 percent on buyers who say they'd manually verify AI output. Last week's brief, and the in-repo note in `docs/ai-tells-field-guide.md` it drew from, pointed at the wrong one. The labeling figure above is the correct citation and it still supports the claim. Worth fixing the in-repo note so this doesn't get mis-cited again.

**The swap:** "Run the draft, then read it out loud. If it sounds like every other listing on the block, rewrite the first two sentences yourself before you post it."

**Loop-back:** "The tool never cost you the showing. The paragraph that sounded like everyone else's did."
