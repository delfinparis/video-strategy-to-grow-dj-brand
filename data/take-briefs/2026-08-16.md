## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal.

**Section:** Career and identity | **Slot:** Wed (heat 4, the week's only heat-4 post) | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) | **Hook shape:** 4 (Cohort address + indictment + promise)

**Hook (spoken):**
"If you're in your first two years, your brokerage already priced in that you'd make $8,100 and quit. Here's how you don't become that number."

**Who profits:** Brokerages whose model runs on churn. Every agent who washes out in year one gets replaced by two more new licensees paying the same desk fees and buying the same leads on the way out. Turnover is not a bug in that model. It's the business.

**The swap:** Ask your broker one direct question this week: what is the written 90-day plan for a brand new agent, who owns it, and how often do they check in. If there is no answer on paper, that is the real answer.

**Loop-back:** "Your first year wasn't brutal because the business is hard. It was brutal because nobody built you a plan, and that absence was the plan."

**Receipt:** NAR's 2024 Member Profile found agents with two years or less of experience earned a median $8,100 that year, and 62 percent made less than $10,000. (Source: NAR 2024 Member Profile Highlights, July 2024; already in-repo via `docs/recruiting-objections.md`, re-checked this cycle.)

## 2. Open houses are how you get buyers.

**Section:** Practice | **Slot:** Fri (heat 3.5) | **Hook family:** 8 (Cohort Callout) | **Hook shape:** 3 (Cohort Reversal)

**Hook (spoken):**
"For every agent who quietly stopped holding opens because 'they don't work,' you're right, and you're still leaving your best free lead source on the table."

**Who profits:** The listing agent who tells the seller "I'll find your buyer here" gets an easy yes on the listing appointment. The seller pays for a Sunday that was never built to produce their buyer.

**The swap:** Stop pitching the open house as "how we find your buyer." Pitch it as neighbor intelligence: capture every visitor's name and run the open for the room, not the door.

**Loop-back:** "You were right to stop believing open houses sell the house. You were wrong to stop holding them."

**Receipt:** NAR's Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers puts it at 4 percent of buyers finding their home through a yard sign or open house, versus 52 percent online. That gap has held across recent editions including 2025. (Source: NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, 2025.)

**Angle note:** This exact stat ran as the straight myth-bust in `EVERGREEN-003` (2026-07-08). This version earns its own slot by running the cohort-reversal shape and adding the who-profits turn the carousel never made, per the take-standard.md guidance on reusing a receipt with a genuinely different angle.

## 3. Use AI to write your listing descriptions.

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Slot:** Fri or Mon (heat 3.5) | **Hook family:** 7 (Forbidden) | **Hook shape:** 2 (Verdict + Cost)

**Hook (spoken):**
"Your AI-written listing description is the one piece of marketing on that house a buyer actually reads word for word, and one in three buyers thinks it should carry a warning label."

**Who profits:** The AI tool vendors selling a one-click description generator. It costs them nothing that every listing on the block ends up reading like every other one.

**The swap:** Run the draft, then rewrite the first two sentences yourself in your own voice before you post it. That is the part a buyer reads before deciding whether to book the showing.

**Loop-back:** "The tool didn't cost you the showing. The paragraph that sounded like everyone else's did."

**Receipt:** A 2026 Cotality buyer survey found 68 percent of buyers want AI-generated real estate content clearly labeled, and more than a third think it should be mandatory. The same survey found buyer confidence in AI for home search fell from 30 percent in 2025 to 16 percent in 2026. (Source: Cotality 2026 survey, reported by HousingWire, 2026.)

## 4. Ask for the comment.

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Mon (heat 3.5) | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap / List) | **Hook shape:** 1 (Imperative + Harm)

**Hook (spoken):**
"Stop writing 'comment below' on your posts. It's the reason fewer people are seeing them."

**Who profits:** Engagement-bait coaching, which measures a post's success by comment count instead of by what a comment actually buys you.

**The swap:** Delete the "comment below" line from your next caption. Replace it with one sentence that answers a real question, the kind one agent would actually send another agent.

**Loop-back:** "You didn't lose the comment by not asking for it. You were losing the post by asking."

**Receipt:** Meta's engagement-bait policy, in place since 2017 and still enforced, demotes posts that explicitly ask for comments, likes, or shares as a tactic. Metricool's 2026 Social Media Study confirms posts built around a "send this to someone" moment now outperform posts built around asking for a comment. (Source: Meta engagement-bait policy, 2017-present; Metricool 2026 Social Media Study. In-repo via `docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #4, re-checked this cycle.)

## 5. Post at exactly the right time or you miss the window.

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Mon alt (heat 3) | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap / List) | **Hook shape:** 5 (Paradox Verdict)

**Hook (spoken):**
"You're still checking the 'best time to post' chart before you hit publish, and it's costing you nothing but the time you could've spent on the caption."

**Who profits:** Scheduling tools selling a best-time chart as their headline feature. A chart is a cheaper product to ship than a good caption editor.

**The swap:** Stop checking the chart. Spend that five minutes tightening your first line instead, since that decides whether the algorithm keeps showing the post at all.

**Loop-back:** "You were never missing a window. You were spending the five minutes you had on the wrong five minutes."

**Receipt:** Buffer's own 2026 research calls posting time "not the secret sauce," and on Instagram roughly 70 percent of a post's views land in the first 72 hours regardless of the exact minute it goes up. (Source: Buffer 2026 research. In-repo via `docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #8, re-checked this cycle.)

## 6. Keep every video under 15 seconds.

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Fri alt (heat 3) | **Hook family:** 8 (Cohort Callout) | **Hook shape:** 5 (Paradox Verdict)

**Hook (spoken):**
"If you're still cutting every video down to 15 seconds, you're deleting the part that would have made someone watch to the end."

**Who profits:** Nobody specifically. It's a rule left over from a platform that doesn't run the algorithm anymore.

**The swap:** Stop timing your videos to a number. Time them to the point where the story or the tip is actually finished, then cut there.

**Loop-back:** "You didn't lose the viewer at fifteen seconds. You lost them the moment you cut the video before it earned the extra ten."

**Receipt:** Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts all now reach non-followers on videos running one to three minutes, as long as completion holds. Length is retention-relative, not fixed. (Source: TikTok newsroom, Meta Transparency Center, Hootsuite 2026. In-repo via `docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #9, re-checked this cycle.)
