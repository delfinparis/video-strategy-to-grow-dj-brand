## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal.

**Section:** Career and identity | **Slot:** Wed | **Heat:** 4.5 | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) | **Shape:** 4 (cohort address + indictment + promise)

**Who profits:** The brokerage that recruits on headcount and doesn't build a real onboarding
gets your dues whether you last a year or not. Churn is the model, not a side effect of it.

**The turn:** The difficulty isn't a rite of passage. It's an unmanaged onboarding that a
churn-dependent recruiting model has no incentive to fix.

**Hook (spoken):** "If you're in your first two years and barely covering rent, that's not a
rite of passage. It's a business model that profits when you quit before month twelve. Here's
how you don't."

**The swap:** Before you take your next listing or buyer appointment, ask your broker in
writing for a 90-day onboarding plan: a named mentor, an actual lead source, a call-review
schedule. If they can't produce one, that's the answer to why the math is built this way.

**Loop-back:** "Your first year isn't brutal because real estate is hard. It's brutal because
nobody built the first year to work."

**Receipt:** Agents with two years or less of experience had a median gross income of
**$8,000** in the NAR 2026 Member Profile (2025 production data), reported by HousingWire
and Mile High Title Guy's coverage of the same release. I could not reach NAR's own PDF
directly from here to re-confirm the exact figure first-hand -- flag this for a direct check
against nar.realtor before it airs. **Do not use the "87% of agents fail" or "71% sold zero
homes" figures anywhere near this script.** `docs/recruiting-objections.md` already flags both
as unverified industry legend that cannot be attributed to NAR, and a second search this
morning confirms it: no primary source exists for either number.

## 2. Ask a cold lead if they're still looking.

**Section:** Prospecting and follow-up | **Slot:** Mon (primary) | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap/List) | **Shape:** 1 (imperative + harm)

**Who profits:** Nobody. It's the path of least resistance, which is exactly why it's worth
calling out -- no villain to name, just a habit nobody updated.

**The turn:** The question invites a no and hands the lead an easy exit. Rebuilt on a
different receipt than the brief first offered: not a measured response-rate comparison (no
sourceable number exists for that), but the actual math on where sellers come from.

**Hook (spoken):** "Stop texting a cold lead 'are you still looking.' It hands them the
easiest way to say no."

**The swap:** Instead of pinging the cold lead, text three people in your actual database
today with something specific: a listing you saw, a rate move, a real "thought of you." That's
where the real conversion lives.

**Loop-back:** "The lead going cold was never the problem. The question you keep sending it
is."

**Receipt:** Roughly **two in three sellers** come from referral or repeat business with an
agent they've already worked with. NAR, *2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers*. Already
in-repo at `docs/recruiting-objections.md` rank 2; re-check against the current NAR Profile
release before shipping, same caveat the bank note carries.

## 5. Use AI to write your listing descriptions.

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Slot:** Fri (alternate) | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 7 (Forbidden) | **Shape:** 2 (verdict + cost)

**Angle check:** this entry already shipped once as `TAKE-001` (2026-08-19, Defector shape,
built on the 68%-want-disclosure stat). More than 8 weeks ago, so it's eligible again, but it
needs a genuinely different angle, not a rerun. Built below on a different number from the
same source that `TAKE-001` deliberately left unused.

**Who profits:** The vendor selling the one-click button doesn't lose the showing when the
copy sounds like everyone else's. You do.

**The turn:** Buyer trust in AI-assisted home search is falling, not holding steady, which
makes a generic AI description a worse bet than it was a year ago, not a neutral one.

**Hook (spoken):** "Your AI-written listing description isn't saving you twenty minutes. It's
landing in front of buyers who trust AI less every year."

**The swap:** Run the AI draft, then rewrite it so it sounds like you walked the house, not
like you prompted it.

**Loop-back:** "The draft was never the problem. Posting it unedited, while buyers trust the
tool less every year, is."

**Receipt:** US prospective buyers' trust in AI for home search fell from **30% to 16%** in
one year. Cotality, *AI in Housing 2026* survey (fielded Jan 29-Feb 9, 2026). This is the
second number from the same Cotality survey `TAKE-001` used for its 68%-disclosure figure --
`TAKE-001`'s own Data Source section flags this exact number as "verified but deliberately
unused," so it's a genuinely new receipt, not a repeat of the one already aired.

## 6. Stack hashtags for reach.

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Mon (alternate) | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 9 (Swap/List) | **Shape:** 6 (diagnosed loss)

**Who profits:** Nobody. Not a vendor play, just a habit that outlived the platform behavior it
was built for.

**The turn:** The tag stack is doing nothing the first 125 characters of a real caption
couldn't do better, and on Instagram it now measurably costs reach rather than adding it.

**Hook (spoken):** "You know why that post barely moved? Not the algorithm. The eleven
hashtags you stacked underneath it."

**The swap:** Delete the tag block. Put the actual search phrase an agent or a client would
type into the first line of the caption instead.

**Loop-back:** "The hashtags never buried your reach. They were standing in for the caption
you didn't write."

**Receipt:** Instagram posts *with* hashtags got **31-32% fewer views and about 33% fewer
interactions** than posts without. Metricool 2026 Instagram Study, 24M+ posts. In-repo at
`docs/myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md` #1, correlational (Metricool's own framing), not
proven causal -- say "posts with hashtags perform worse," not "hashtags cause the drop."
