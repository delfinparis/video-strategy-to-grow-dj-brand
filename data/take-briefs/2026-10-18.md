## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal. — Wed (this week's hardest swing)

**Section:** Career and identity | **Heat:** 4.5 | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) | **Shape:** 4, cohort address + indictment + promise

**Hook (spoken):** "If you're a realtor in your first year, your brokerage makes the same money whether you make it to year two or not. That's why nobody built you a plan. Here's how you make one."

**Who profits:** Brokerages whose recruiting model depends on churn. Replacing a licensee who quits costs them nothing; training one costs money this quarter.

**The receipt:** Only 15 percent of REALTORS nationally have two years or less in the business right now, the lowest share on record, and that group's median gross income was $8,000 in 2025. **Source: NAR's 2026 Member Profile** (covering 2025 activity), released June 25, 2026. Re-verified today against NAR's own feature sheet and the Florida Realtors oversample report, which both cite the same $8,000 and 15 percent figures directly from NAR. This is the same figure last week's brief sourced; still holds. **Do not let the script derive "85 percent fail" or any number NAR didn't publish** — the honest claim is the $8,000 and the 15 percent, stated plainly, with the argument built on top, not folded into it.

**The swap:** "If you're new, stop waiting on floor time and company leads. This week, text 20 people you already know: I just got my license, keep me in mind."

**Loop-back:** "Your first year isn't brutal because the business is hard. It's brutal because nobody built you an onboarding, and calling that a rite of passage is how they get away with it."

## 2. Stack hashtags for reach. — Mon

## 4. Open houses are how you get buyers. — Fri (option B)

**Section:** Practice | **Heat:** 4.3 | **Hook family:** 5 (Confession, via a diagnosed-loss opener) | **Shape:** 6, diagnosed loss

**Hook (spoken):** "You know why half the buyers who tour your open house never call you back? They'd already found the house online. You were just confirming it."

**Who profits:** The listing agent who wanted Sunday covered. "I'll find your buyer here" was never a promise to the buyer. It wins the pitch to the seller.

**The receipt:** 48 percent of buyers used an open house as an information source in their home search last year. **Source: NAR, *2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers*, Exhibit 3-2.** In-repo, verified 2026-08-18 against the report PDF; spot-checked today, no change. Only a small share actually found the home they bought that way, which is the gap the take argues, not a second stacked number.

**The swap:** "Stop running it to catch today's buyer. Run it to find your next seller, the neighbor who showed up just to see what it listed for, and follow up with that name this week."

**Loop-back:** "The open house never cost you buyers. It cost you the neighbor's name you didn't write down."

**Repeat note:** This belief shipped once before, as `TAKE-002-open-houses.md` (2026-08-21, cohort-reversal shape: "you were right they don't sell the house, you were wrong to stop"). That's more than 8 weeks ago, so it's eligible again, and this is a genuinely different angle: diagnosed-loss framing, a different who-profits read (the pitch, not the showing), and a swap that targets seller leads instead of "take every name." `EVERGREEN-003` (2026-07-08) also ran the 48% figure as a straight data-card carousel.
