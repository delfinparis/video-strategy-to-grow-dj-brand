## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal.

**Section:** Career and identity | **Slot:** Wed (the week's only heat-4) | **Heat:** 4 |
**Hook family:** 4, System Indictment | **Shape:** 4, cohort address + indictment + promise

**Who profits:** brokerages whose growth model depends on new-agent churn, the fees and dues
keep flowing whether or not the agent survives.

**Hook (spoken):** "If you're two years into real estate, the median agent like you made about
eight thousand dollars this year. That's not because you're bad at this. That's the business
model working exactly as designed. Here's how you break it."

**The swap:** Before signing with any brokerage, ask them to name your specific mentor and put
your first 90 days in writing. No answer means no plan, whatever they told you about culture in
the interview.

**Loop-back:** "Your first year isn't brutal because you're new. It's brutal because somebody
budgeted for you to quit."

**Receipt:** NAR 2026 Member Profile. Members with two years or less in the business earned a
median gross income of $8,000 this cycle, down slightly from $8,100 the year before, against
$88,500 for members with 16+ years. Reported by HousingWire, 2026 ("NAR 2026 member profile
shows Realtors more experienced"), corroborated by NAR's own newsroom release citing the same
2026 Member Profile. Re-verify the exact figure against the primary NAR Member Profile PDF at
build time if it has been published by then; HousingWire's number is a secondary citation of a
report NAR itself confirmed contains the 16+-year figure.

## 2. Open houses are how you get buyers.

**Section:** Practice | **Slot:** Fri, option A | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Hook family:** 8, Cohort
Callout | **Shape:** 3, cohort reversal

**Who profits:** the listing agent who wants Sunday coverage regardless of whether it produces a
buyer.

**Hook (spoken):** "For every agent still running Sunday open houses to find your buyer, you are
dead wrong. NAR says four percent of buyers ever find the home that way. Open houses are just
great at something else."

**The swap:** Run the open house for the seller relationship, not a buyer pipeline. Capture every
visitor as a warm contact and book the seller follow-up call that same afternoon instead of
counting the open house as lead generation.

**Loop-back:** "Open houses were never about finding you a buyer. The four percent already proved
that. What they've always found you is your next seller."

**Receipt:** NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, 2025. 4% of buyers found their home through a
yard sign or open house, 52% found it online. Already used in-repo as a straight myth-bust
carousel (EVERGREEN-003, 2026-07-08) and offered twice before in take briefs (2026-08-11,
2026-08-16) without being picked. This build needs to run the cohort-reversal shape above, not
the myth-bust structure, or it repeats EVERGREEN-003's angle rather than adding one.

## 3. Use AI to write your listing descriptions.

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Slot:** Fri, option B | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Hook family:** 7,
Forbidden | **Shape:** 1, imperative + harm

**Who profits:** the tool vendors selling speed, and it costs nothing to point at them because
this is D.J.'s own most common taught use case, not an attack on agents for using AI.

**Hook (spoken):** "Stop pasting the AI draft straight into the MLS. Right now thirty-seven
percent of listings are getting flagged as computer-written, and buyers can feel it before they
can prove it."

**The swap:** Keep the AI draft as an outline, then rewrite the one or two sentences only you
could know, the specific thing the seller told you about that kitchen. That is the line a buyer
remembers.

**Loop-back:** "The listing description is the one piece of writing a client actually reads.
Don't hand the only one that matters to a machine."

**Receipt:** Originality.ai analysis of over 15,000 rental and 56,000 sales listings in the first
two weeks of May 2026, reported by The Globe and Mail. 37% of home sales listings were flagged as
likely AI-written by Originality.ai's own detection tool. Note in the Data Source section at
build time that AI-detection tools are not 100% accurate and this is one vendor's model, not a
regulatory finding, name it as such rather than stating it as settled fact.

## 4. Ask for the comment.

**Section:** Marketing and social | **Slot:** Mon | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Hook family:** 9, Swap /
List | **Shape:** 1, imperative + harm

**Who profits:** engagement-bait coaching that sells CTAs as a growth hack.

**Hook (spoken):** "Stop typing 'comment below' on your posts. Meta has a name for that, and it's
demoting you for it."

**The swap:** Ask one real question tied to the specific post instead, something only a person who
actually watched it could answer. That earns the comment instead of begging for it.

**Loop-back:** "You didn't lose reach because the algorithm hates realtors. You lost it the day
you started asking for the comment."

**Receipt:** Meta's own Transparency Center, "Engagement Bait" content-distribution guidelines
(ongoing policy, current as of 2026). Meta names phrasing like "Comment if you..." as engagement
bait and states accounts that repeatedly rely on it receive progressively less organic
distribution. Flag for the build: Metricool's 2026 Instagram Study (24M+ posts) found posts with
comment-focused CTAs get 202.78% more raw comments, that is a different metric than reach and does
not contradict Meta's distribution policy, but do not conflate the two numbers in the script. Use
Meta's own policy as the one receipt (beat 3 is one number, said once); the Metricool comment-lift
figure is background, not the spoken stat.

**"Ask a cold lead if they're still looking."** (Prospecting and follow-up.) Searched for a
sourced comparison of closed yes/no lead-qualification questions versus open-ended alternatives

inferring a figure onto a claim it was not measuring. Dropped rather than stretched.

**"Go full-time or you're not serious."** (Career and identity, marked `evidence: research` in

agents (60+ hrs/week) earn a median around $144,400 against roughly $30,000 for agents under 20
hrs/week, and part-time agents skew toward the lowest production tiers. The counter-number the
take needs does not exist in the data found, so per the take-standard's own rule ("never invent
the counter-number"), the take does not ship this week.
