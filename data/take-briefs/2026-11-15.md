## 4. Switching means starting over. Your clients won't follow.

**Section:** Brokerage economics | **Slot:** Fri | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 8 (Cohort Callout) | **Shape:** #2, verdict + cost

**Who profits:** the brokerage that wants retention without earning it.
**The turn:** roughly two in three sellers come from referral or repeat business, and they chose the agent, not the brand.

**Hook (spoken):** "Your broker tells you clients won't follow you if you leave. That's not true, and it's the reason you're still underpaid."

**The swap:** Before your next split negotiation, count how many of your last ten clients came from a referral or a repeat, not a brokerage lead. Bring that number with you.

**Loop-back:** "Your clients were never going to follow the sign. They were already following you. Your broker just needed you to wonder."

**Receipt, re-verified today:** NAR's **2025** Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers: about two in three sellers found their agent through a referral or had used that agent before (66%, per [Virginia REALTORS' summary of the report](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/), Dec 2025). **Flag for D.J.:** NAR released the **2026** Profile this month (Nov 2026) and it supersedes the 2025 edition. Public summaries of the 2026 report confirm the same pattern holds -- friend/neighbor/relative referral is still sellers' #1 way of finding an agent, a prior agent is #2 -- but the exact combined 2026 percentage sits behind NAR's paid full report (store.realtor) and I could not independently confirm whether it is still ~66% or has moved. **Ship with the 2025 figure as written (it is a real, correctly-dated NAR number), or pull the 2026 member copy before filming if you want the freshest number.** Either way, cite the year you actually use.
