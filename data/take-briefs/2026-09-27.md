## 1. Your first year is supposed to be brutal. -- SURVIVED, assign Wed

**Section:** Career and identity | **Slot:** Wed | **Heat:** 4 | **Hook family:** 4 (System Indictment) | **Hook shape:** 4 (cohort address + indictment + promise)

**Who profits (from the bank):** brokerages whose model depends on churn

**The turn (from the bank):** the difficulty is not a rite of passage, it is an unmanaged onboarding

**Receipt (re-sourced, the bank note had no actual number):** NAR's own 2024 Member Profile: agents with two years or less of experience earn a median $8,100 a year, and 62 percent make under $10,000 in that window. [NAR 2024 Member Profile Highlights, 2024-07-10](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/documents/2024-nar-member-profile-highlights-07-10-2024.pdf) (already cited in-repo at `docs/recruiting-objections.md`; I did not find a newer NAR Member Profile that supersedes this figure).

**Hook (spoken):** "If you're in your first year of real estate, you are not failing. The system is, and here's what nobody told you to do about it."

**The swap:** "This week, ask your broker for the name of the agent assigned to actually mentor you. If there isn't one, that's your answer, and it's time to go find a brokerage that has one."

**Loop-back:** "Your first year isn't brutal because real estate is hard. It's brutal because nobody built you a floor to stand on."

## 4. AI is going to replace agents. -- DROPPED

**Section:** Tech and AI | **Receipt:** found a number, it measures the wrong thing

**Why dropped:** the only concrete figure I could find is Morgan Stanley Research's "37% of
real estate tasks can be automated." Checked the actual study before using it: that 37% is
built from 162 REIT and commercial real estate firms, $92B of labor costs, 525K employees, and
covers property management, leasing and CRE office/admin work, not solo residential agents'
listing and negotiation work. Using it to back a claim about working real estate agents would be
misapplying a commercial-real-estate labor study to a residential audience, which is worse than
having no number. Dropped rather than stretched.

**Loop-back:** "More posts was never the goal. Getting watched was, and you've been trading one for the other."
