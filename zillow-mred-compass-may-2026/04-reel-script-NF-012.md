# The MLS Just Picked a Side. Most Agents Missed It.

> **WOW: Mindset reframe + memorable one-liner. Reframe -- the Zillow lawsuit isn't a corporate-portal fight, it's the moment the MLS publicly stopped being neutral. One-liner -- "the MLS you pay dues to just told you, in writing, whose side it's on." Designed to make a non-Compass agent re-evaluate what their MLS actually does for them.**

**Pillar:** Industry Dynamics | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** Wednesday, May 13, 2026

## Shareable Moment
> "The MLS you pay dues to just told you, in writing, whose side it's on."

## Script (~60 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:05)
The Zillow lawsuit has two defendants. Everybody's writing about one of them. The other one is the whole story.

### THE TELL (0:05-0:25)
MRED. The Chicago MLS. The one that calls itself a neutral cooperative.

On May 6, MRED told Zillow to display one specific brokerage's private listings nationwide, or lose access to Chicago listings entirely. Not just in Cook County. *[~0:15 re-hook beat]* The whole country.

A regional MLS, in Chicago, demanding national concessions on behalf of its biggest member. Compass holds about a quarter of MRED's board seats. The feed company that delivered a parallel threat the same day is chaired by MRED's own CEO. This isn't a conspiracy in a back room. It's a conspiracy in the annual report.

### THE TWIST (0:25-0:38)
Here's where it gets stupid. In this one fight, the company whose incentives accidentally line up with the consumer is Zillow. And the party hiding houses from buyers is the MLS.

The cooperative. The neutral one. Read that twice. I had to.

### WHO LOSES (0:38-0:48)
Who loses? Anyone who isn't a Compass agent. Your listings go to a portal that just got threatened. Your buyers see incomplete inventory. And the MLS you pay dues to just told you, in writing, whose side it's on.

### CLOSE (0:48-0:60)
Three calls this week. One. Every active seller you have. Get ahead of the question before they read about it. Two. Everyone you sold a home to in the last two years. They're going to see headlines about Chicago listings and wonder what it means for their home value. Be the agent who called them, not the one they had to chase down. Three. Anyone in your sphere who's mentioned selling in the next year. The headline is your reason to call. The relationship is the point.

**Estimated Duration:** ~60 seconds (~270 spoken words)

- **Claim:** "Compass holds about a quarter of MRED's board seats."
  - Source: Chicago Agent Magazine 2026-05-12 summary of the complaint. Specifically: three Compass-affiliated seats out of approximately thirteen total, which the complaint frames as roughly 23 percent.
  - Status: confirmed. Spoken script uses "about a quarter" as the qualitative approximation of 23 percent to keep the line natural and avoid combining two numbers.

- **Claim:** "The feed company that delivered a parallel threat the same day is chaired by MRED's own CEO."
  - Source: Inman 2026-05-12 and HousingWire 2026-05-12. MLS Grid, the feed company that distributes MRED's data, threatened to terminate Zillow's access on May 6, the same day MRED issued its demand. MLS Grid's board is chaired by MRED CEO Rebecca Jensen.
  - Status: confirmed. Spoken script does not name MLS Grid or Rebecca Jensen, both names slow the line and add no value to a viewer who isn't a court reporter.
