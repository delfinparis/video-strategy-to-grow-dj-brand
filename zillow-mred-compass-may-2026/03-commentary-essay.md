# The MLS Just Died. Nobody Wants to Say It.

I read the Zillow complaint Tuesday night with a slice of pizza in one hand and my reading glasses fogging up. (I'm old. The glasses keep getting thicker. Pretty soon I'll need binoculars to read my own listing agreements.)

Everyone's calling this a Zillow versus Compass story. It's not.

The complaint has two defendants. Compass and MRED, the Chicago MLS. The Compass piece is the headline. The MRED piece is the whole story.

## The Fairy Tale

The MLS sells itself as a neutral cooperative. Member-owned. Fair. Equal access for the little guy and the big guy.

Have you ever actually read your MLS bylaws? Of course you haven't. I've been in this business twenty-plus years and I've read mine exactly once, and only because somebody sued my brokerage. (Spoiler: we won. Other spoiler: I didn't understand most of what I read.)

The cooperative pitch holds up exactly as long as the MLS doesn't get caught picking a side.

On May 6, MRED got caught.

## What They Actually Did

MRED told Zillow to display one specific brokerage's private listings everywhere in the country, or lose access to Chicago listings. Not in Cook County. Not in Illinois. The whole country.

A regional MLS, in Chicago, demanding national concessions on behalf of one specific brokerage.

Read that sentence again. I had to read it three times. I assumed I was misunderstanding it. I was not.

And the org chart? The feed company that delivered a parallel termination threat the same day is MLS Grid, and MLS Grid's board is chaired by MRED's own CEO, Rebecca Jensen. Compass holds three of MRED's roughly thirteen board seats, about 23 percent of the board. The MRED expansion deal also has Compass subsidizing membership costs for the first 100,000 Compass agents who join MRED nationally, which Zillow's complaint argues could triple MRED's size.

This isn't a conspiracy in a back room. It's a conspiracy in the annual report.

## Don't Make Zillow the Good Guy

I want to be careful here, because I know what you're thinking. "D.J. is rooting for Zillow." I'm not.

Zillow is not your friend. Zillow's whole business is selling your name back to you as a Premier Agent lead, and they need open listings because open listings are the bait. (You are the fish in this metaphor. I'm sorry. I don't make the rules.)

But here is the weird part. In this one specific fight, the company whose financial incentives accidentally line up with the consumer is Zillow. And the party hiding houses from buyers is the MLS.

We have arrived somewhere stupid.

## Compass Is the Honest One

This is going to make some of you mad. The most intellectually honest party in this entire lawsuit is Compass.

Compass has never pretended to be neutral. They built a private network. They told sellers, loudly, that asymmetric access produces better outcomes. They published the data. They put it on billboards. You can love or hate the three-phase strategy (I have opinions, we'll get there another day) but Compass is acting like a brokerage. That's a brokerage's job.

The party that's lying is MRED. The website still says industry-leading cooperative. The behavior says enforcement arm for one member.

## Quick Detour, Because I Have to Say This

I interview agents every week on the podcast. I'm going to tell you the conversation I keep having and you're going to tell me if you recognize it.

Agent (good agent, top producer, lovely person): "I just don't pay attention to the politics. I just focus on my clients."

Me, smiling, pretending this is a fine answer: "Yeah."

Me, in my head: "You are paying for the politics whether you pay attention or not."

I had a guy on last month, thirty-one years in the business, never been to an MLS board meeting in his life and not planning to start. Then he spent six minutes of the episode complaining that his listings keep getting flagged by Zillow.

Anyway. Back to the story.

## You Are the Inventory

Here's the part nobody in our industry wants to say out loud.

You think you're the customer. You're not.

Compass wants you in the building because the private network is worthless without thousands of agents to populate it. Zillow wants you on the platform because Premier Agent doesn't exist without you bidding on your own zip code. The MLS wants your dues because the cooperative needs members to point at when regulators ask questions.

In every one of those scenarios, your listings and your leads are the product. You are the supply chain.

I don't say this to depress you. (Although if you are depressed, I get it. I put down half a sleeve of Thin Mints reading the complaint and I'm not even a Compass agent.) I say it because the only way you stop being the supply chain is to know that you are one.

## Pick a Tribe. On Purpose.

There are three honest paths in 2026. Pick one.

One. Walled garden. Go to Compass, or whoever builds the next private network, because there will be a next one. You're betting that asymmetric access is the moat of the next decade. You trade independence for power.

Two. Portal volume. Stay public. Live with the rules. Optimize for lead flow. You trade margin for volume.

Three. Build your own. Brand, audience, direct relationships. You don't need a portal or a brokerage to validate you. (Hardest path. Also the only one with no landlord.)

Pick one. Be honest about why.

The path that doesn't exist anymore is the fourth one. The complain-and-wait one. The "I'm sure the MLS will protect us" one. That door shut on May 6 and most of you didn't even hear it close.

## What Happens Next

My prediction. Settles within twelve months. MRED walks back the territorial threat (because their attorney has seen what discovery is going to look like and is having trouble sleeping). Compass keeps its private network. Zillow keeps its standards. Everybody puts out a press release. Trade press moves on.

But the move is on the shelf now. Every MLS executive in America just learned that you can use rule-making power to extract concessions from a portal on behalf of your biggest broker. Somebody is going to try this again. The antitrust theory is briefed and ready.

Five years from now, the neutral cooperative MLS as we know it is gone. What replaces it is some mix of portal-direct deals, brokerage walled gardens, and a handful of MLSs that survive by becoming functionally franchises of their dominant broker. NAR will spend that whole period publishing white papers about cooperation while the cooperation evaporates underneath them.

## One Last Thing

If you remember nothing else, remember this.

The second defendant on the caption is the whole story. The first one is just the headline.

It's not Zillow. It's not Compass.

It's MRED. And every MLS in the country is watching how this plays.

Fin.
