# Drip 005: Lowball Play Follow-Up

**Triggered by:** Engagement on PB-001 (Here's the Play When You Get a Lowball Offer)
**Source script:** [`../../scripts/the-playbook/PB-001-lowball-offer.md`](../../scripts/the-playbook/PB-001-lowball-offer.md)
**Send within:** 24-48 hours of the engagement
**Best for:** Buyer's agents currently in deal flow, listing agents who hit low offers regularly, any agent who shares tactical content (suggests they want the craft as a discipline, not just motivation)

## Subject

Did the lowball play actually work?

## Body

[Name], thanks for the engagement on the lowball play this week. Real question -- did you end up using it, and if so, how did the other agent respond? I'm tracking which plays actually work in the field versus which ones sound right but fall apart in practice, and the early data is interesting.

I have a growing collection of plays like this one that I pull from the 700+ agents I've interviewed on the Keeping It Real Podcast. If you have 15 minutes this week, I'd love to trade notes -- you tell me what's been working for you, and I'll share a couple of plays I've been saving for specific scenarios you might be running into.

Here's my direct link: [CALENDLY_LINK]

-- D.J. Paris

## Rationale

- The "did you actually use it?" opener is the most engaging possible follow-up because it invites the recipient to share a war story. Most people want to tell someone about their last deal.
- Frames D.J. as a catalog-curator of plays, which is consistent with The Playbook series positioning and the "whole catalog" close of IS-003.
- "Trade notes" is peer-to-peer, matching the working-agent tone of the series itself.
- The implicit credibility anchor is the 700 interviews, which is the protected brand signature and the differentiator nobody else has.
- No recruiting pitch. The value is the plays, and the plays are shared on the call.
