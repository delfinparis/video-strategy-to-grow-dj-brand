# Weekly Mentor Success Story Collection

This document defines the system for collecting real anecdotes from Kale mentors and mentees on a weekly basis. The stories fuel future Kale-signaling content across all four content pillars, replacing the previous need to fabricate observations.

---

## Why This Exists

D.J. is VP of Business Development at Kale Realty. His job is not coaching or mentorship — those happen inside the mentorship program, which D.J. doesn't personally lead or sit in on. That created a content problem: every Kale-signaling script that tried to reference "what I saw in a mentorship session" was either false or had to be rewritten as secondhand observation.

The fix is structural, not editorial. Instead of D.J. inventing mentorship anecdotes or dancing around the framing, Kale's real mentors share real stories directly with D.J. every week. He reports what he's told, naming the person who told it. That's the Bartlett observer model applied to his own brokerage.

This is the strongest possible version of Kale-signaling content because every claim traces to a real person with a real quote, and D.J. is honestly reporting what he was told rather than pretending to have witnessed something he didn't.

---

## The Weekly Collection Protocol

### Who collects: D.J. Paris (or delegated to a Kale ops person)

### When: Every Friday, 15-20 minutes

### How: One of three methods, pick whatever fits

**Method A: Async form submission (lowest friction)**

Create a simple Google Form or Notion form with these fields:

1. **Your name** (mentor or mentee)
2. **Role** (mentor / mentee / both)
3. **The moment** — In 2-3 sentences, what was the specific conversation or decision you want to share? Be concrete. Not "I helped her with her business," but "Last Tuesday we talked through whether she should take an overpriced listing from her sphere, and here's what we landed on."
4. **The insight** — What was the one thing either of you learned or decided that you think other agents would benefit from hearing?
5. **Can D.J. share this publicly?** (yes / yes with my name changed / no, just for internal use)
6. **If yes, can D.J. quote you by name?** (yes / prefer first name only / prefer anonymous but attributed as "a Kale mentor / mentee")

Send the form link in the weekly mentor newsletter or Slack channel every Friday morning. Give a 48-hour window to respond. Collect submissions, cross-reference against the script calendar.

**Method B: 15-minute voice memo round-robin (richest content)**

Once per week, pick 2 mentors and 2 mentees from the program rotation. Send them a 30-second ask via Slack or text: *"Hey, got 90 seconds for a voice memo? Just tell me the story of a conversation you had this week with your mentor/mentee that stuck with you. No editing, no preparation, just what happened. I'll use it for content."*

Receive the voice memos. Transcribe them (Otter, Whisper, or any transcription tool). The transcripts become source material for the script pipeline.

This method produces the richest content because voice memos capture natural language, unexpected details, and real emotion. The downside is it requires ongoing engagement from mentors and mentees, so it only works if D.J. rotates the ask so nobody gets asked twice in the same month.

**Method C: Monthly mentor meetup with story prompt (highest volume, lowest D.J. effort)**

If Kale already runs a monthly mentor meetup, add a 10-minute block to the end of the meeting: *"Before we break, everyone — share one conversation you had with your mentee this month that stuck with you. 60 seconds each."*

Record the segment (with consent). Transcribe the 10 minutes. That's ~10-15 real stories per month without any individual ask required. Mentors enjoy sharing wins, so the social dynamic in the room actually helps rather than hurts.

### Recommended: Method A for the first 30 days

Start with the async form. It's the lowest friction and it respects mentors' time. If form completion rates are high (> 50%), stay with it. If rates are low (< 30%), switch to Method B or C for richer content pull.

## The Story Inventory

Once stories start flowing, log them in a simple Notion database or spreadsheet with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Date received | When the story came in |
| Contributor name | Who told the story |
| Role | Mentor / mentee / both |
| Story summary | 2-3 sentence condensation |
| Insight | The thing someone could learn from it |
| Share permission | Public / first name only / anonymous attributed / internal only |
| Quote permission | Can D.J. use a direct quote? |
| Used in script | If used, which script number |
| Used on | Date script was published |
| Status | New / In use / Used / Declined |

Target inventory: 8-12 active stories at any given time, with fresh additions every Friday. This gives D.J. enough content runway that no single week of Kale-signaling content is blocked on waiting for stories.

## How Stories Feed the Content Pipeline

The mentor stories become source material for scripts across all four pillars:

### Inside the Industry Type A (Access) — Primary use case

Replaces the old "I sat in on a session" framing with honest secondhand reporting:

```
"Last week one of our mentors at Kale, [name with permission], told me
about a conversation she had with her mentee. [Story]. And the thing
that stuck with me was [insight]."
```

That's 100% honest. D.J. is reporting what he was told, by whom, and what he took from it. Same emotional hook as a fabricated first-person observation, stronger because it names a real person and gives them credit.

### Agent Tip of the Day — Kale variant

A mentor tells D.J. about a specific tactic that worked for their mentee. The Agent Tip script features the mentor by name (with permission), describes the tactic, and credits the source. Cross-brokerage credibility because a real Kale mentor said it.

### The Playbook — Scenario variant

A mentee describes a specific difficult situation they navigated with mentor guidance. The Playbook script uses that real scenario as the template, credits the mentee (with permission), and walks through the play.

### Inside the Industry Type B (Synthesis) — Pattern over time

Over a quarter of collected stories, patterns emerge. D.J. reports those patterns qualitatively: *"After collecting 50 mentor stories at Kale this quarter, I noticed something. Almost every breakthrough conversation starts with the same kind of question."* That's a real synthesis from a real dataset, not a fabrication.

## Editorial Standards Integration

Every story used in a script must pass the same Rule 1 check as any other claim:

- **Permission documented:** The contributor gave explicit permission for the specific use case (public quote, anonymous attribution, etc.)
- **Quote accuracy:** If quoting directly, the quote matches the source transcript or form response word-for-word
- **Paraphrase honesty:** If paraphrasing, the paraphrase preserves the contributor's actual meaning, not a reshaped version
- **Named when possible:** Real names with permission are always stronger than anonymous attribution. Default to "first name + role" (e.g., "One of our mentors, Sarah, told me...") rather than "a mentor at my brokerage."

Stories that don't have permission for public use can still feed D.J.'s own understanding and influence qualitative observations, but cannot be quoted or specifically referenced in published content.

## Rollout Plan

### Week 1 (this week)

1. Draft the async form in Google Forms or Notion. Use the field list in Method A above.
2. Announce in the Kale mentor channel: *"Starting this Friday, I'm collecting mentor success stories for content. 3 questions, 5 minutes. I'll credit you by name if you say yes."*
3. Set up the Notion story inventory database.

### Week 2

1. Send the form link Friday morning in the mentor channel.
2. Follow up Monday morning with everyone who didn't respond — not pushy, just a single reminder.
3. Log whatever comes in to the story inventory.
4. Start using the first real story in a new IA-004 variant or a future Kale-signaling script.

### Week 3 onward

1. Weekly rhythm: Friday form send, Monday reminder, story logging, cross-reference with upcoming script calendar.
2. After 30 days, evaluate form completion rate. If below 30%, switch to Method B (voice memo round-robin) or C (monthly meetup capture).

### Success metric

**After 90 days, at least 50% of Kale-signaling scripts in the content calendar are anchored to a named, real story from a Kale mentor or mentee.** Below that, the story pipeline is not producing enough. Above that, the pipeline is healthy and scripts can be scheduled weeks in advance with confidence.

## Relationship to IA-004

IA-004 in its current form (2026-04-14 rewrite) uses honest secondhand observation framing ("I talk to our mentors and mentees constantly... here's what they all tell me"). This is the fallback version that works before the story collection system is producing real anecdotes.

**Once the story collection system is running (likely within 2-3 weeks), a new IA-004 variant should be written that anchors to a specific named story from a real Kale mentor.** The secondhand-observation version will still be available, but the named-story version is stronger.

Format example for the named-story version:

```
"One of our mentors at Kale, [Name], told me about a conversation she
had with her mentee last week. The mentee had an overpriced listing
on her desk -- sphere client, emotional seller, a bad price. And the
mentee wasn't asking whether to fix the price. She was asking whether
to take it at all.

The mentor didn't teach her a technique. She walked her through a
single decision: what would you regret in six months if you take this,
and what would you regret in six months if you don't.

That is not training. That's what real mentorship looks like.

Most brokerages sell training. At Kale, we run an actual mentorship
program -- 25 mentors, close to 100 mentees, voluntary, real
schedules, real deals on the table. And this is the kind of thing I
hear from them every single week."
```

Every detail in that script traces to a real conversation reported by a real mentor. No fabrication. Strongest possible version of the Kale-signaling hook.
