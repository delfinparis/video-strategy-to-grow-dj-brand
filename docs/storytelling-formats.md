# Storytelling Formats -- the story-shape layer

Ten first-person story shapes, rated for fit against D.J.'s brand and rewritten to survive the
universal standards. This is the layer the repo was missing: it answers **"whose story is this and
what shape does it take,"** which is a different question from the three layers that already exist.

| Layer | Doc | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Rule 10 / [`hook-matrix-cheatsheet.md`](hook-matrix-cheatsheet.md) | How do I open the first spoken line? |
| Structure | [`series/viral-3-act-spine.md`](series/viral-3-act-spine.md) | How do I build hook -> story -> payoff? |
| Series | [`series/`](series/) + [`content-pillars.md`](content-pillars.md) | What topic am I covering? |
| **Story shape** | **this doc** | **Whose story is it, and what arc does it run?** |

These are orthogonal. A Lesson From Others (format 10) can open on a Confession hook (family 5),
run the full 3-act spine, and ship inside Inside the Industry. Picking a story shape does not
replace any of the other three choices.

**Provenance:** adapted from the Storytelling Template Database in Kallaway's Short Form Academy
(reviewed July 2026). It is a creator-economy document written to sell a program, aimed at consumer
creators. The taxonomy is sound. Roughly half the formats and nearly all of the CTA guidance do not
survive contact with a B2B recruiting brand that bans engagement asks. What follows is the version
that does. The rejections are documented so nobody re-litigates them in six months.

---

## Precedence: this doc loses every conflict

The source material has no sourcing discipline, no fabrication guardrail, and its CTAs are the exact
asks Rule 4 bans. So:

1. **Rule 1 (never fabricate) beats every template here.** See "The mad-lib trap" below. This is the
   single biggest risk in adopting this doc.
2. **Rule 4 (no engagement asks) voids every CTA in the source.** "Comment LEARN," "follow for
   more," "follow to watch," "comment YES," "save it," "comment below" -- all banned, no exceptions.
   Every CTA in this doc has been rebuilt on Rule 10.4 (passive comment engineering) and Rule 10.5
   (the implicit follow trigger).
3. **The 0:00 spoken scroll-stop binds.** The source opens its origin-story template with "Hi I'm
   [name], and X years ago I was a normal guy." That is throat-clearing. captions.ai builds captions
   from audio, so the first spoken line carries the whole hook. Every format here opens on tension.
4. **Rule 7 (length) beats story ambition.** Several of these formats want room the format does not
   have. If the arc pushes past the cap, compress the arc.
5. **Rule 5 (no em dashes, no AI-speak).** The source templates are full of em dashes. Stripped.

## The constraint that governs this entire doc: D.J. is not a practitioner

Every format in the source is first person. D.J. holds a real estate license but **does not practice
and has never represented a buyer or a seller.** His authority comes from 700+ podcast interviews,
a seat in NAR's 2026 influencer program, and running a 250-agent brokerage. That is a protected
voice signature (Rule 6), and two sibling standards state the rule harder because first-person
copy violates it by default:

- [`sales-workflow/ai-event/WEBINAR-EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md`](../../sales-workflow/ai-event/WEBINAR-EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md):
  *"Any first-person story that involves D.J. performing an agent action is banned."*
- [`kale-agent-advantage/docs/editorial-standards.md`](../../kale-agent-advantage/docs/editorial-standards.md):
  *"If you're not sure whether a first-person story works for D.J., default to attribution."*

**This is the highest-risk interaction in the whole doc.** A first-person format library is exactly
the artifact that produces "I sat down with a nervous buyer and..." A story that needs the narrator
to have taken a listing appointment, sat with a buyer, worked a Sunday open house, or sent the
follow-up text as an agent is **not D.J.'s story**, no matter how well the format fits.

**Which stories D.J. can own in first person:** the broker's chair, the podcast host's chair, the
recruiter's chair, the operator's chair. Hiring, retention, what 700 interviews taught him,
recruiting calls (his actual daily work), building the brokerage, being wrong about how the
business works. That is a real and under-mined territory, not a consolation prize.

**When the story is an agent action, attribute it** (order of preference, from the webinar standard):
a real named Kale agent, an anonymous-but-real Kale agent, a named podcast guest, or D.J.'s
pattern-level observation across agents. Never a first-person reenactment.

This is the structural reason **Format 10 (Lesson From Others) rates highest** in the table below.
It is not a workaround, it is the format purpose-built for a narrator whose credibility comes from
who he has listened to rather than what he has personally closed. When a story needs a
practitioner, Format 10 gives it to the practitioner and keeps D.J. in the chair he actually
occupies.

Formats 1, 2, 6, and 8 carry the most exposure here. Each entry below states its practitioner
boundary.

## The three traps

### 1. The mad-lib trap (the serious one)

The source hands you a menu of dramatic details to pick from:

> "It got so bad that I [insert lowest moment: 'couldn't sleep' / 'almost gave up' / 'was completely
> broke']."

Choosing a low point off a list is fabrication with extra steps. That is the precise failure mode
behind IS-002 and PB-001: reaching for the detail that made the line better but was not true. Rule 1
and Rule 8.2 (specificity must be earned) override the story instinct every time.

**The gate, applied to every blank in every template below:** if the detail is not something D.J.
would say under oath, on a podcast, with the person in the room, it does not go in the script. A
real story with a flat middle beats an invented story with a great one. If the true version has no
low point, the format is wrong for that material -- pick a different format.

Formats 1, 4, 6, and 7 apply the most pressure here, because the source's own advice is to maximize
the gap, make the challenge grand, and add high-intensity roadblocks. Ignore that advice. Report
what happened.

### 2. The CTA trap

Every CTA block in the source is a Rule 4 violation. Replacements:

| Source CTA (banned) | What we run instead |
| --- | --- |
| "Comment LEARN for the full breakdown" | Intentional incompleteness (10.4). Unpack three of four things fully. The fourth pulls the comment without an ask. |
| "Follow for more" / "Follow to watch" | Implicit follow trigger (10.5). "This is one of the swaps I keep a running list of." Informational, not directive. |
| "Comment YES if you think I can do it" | Nothing. Cut it. Land the loop-back instead (Rule 9.1). |
| "If this resonated, save it" | Nothing. Family 9 exact-words payloads get saved without being asked. |
| "Who taught you the most? Comment below" | The defensible line (10.4). State a position worth backing up and let the agreement arrive on its own. |

### 3. The proof trap

Format 2's own secret sauce is that the proof must be visually undeniable, or the audience discounts
the whole video. Same applies to 7 and any adapted 4. D.J.'s usable proof tokens are real and should
be the only ones used: KIR at nearly 10M downloads, 700+ episodes, a 250-agent brokerage, NAR 2026
real estate influencer, named press. Every one of those has a receipt. Nothing else gets claimed on
screen, and nothing gets rounded up (Rule 1).

## The adopted formats

Each entry gives the arc, the fit, the default hook family, and templates already scrubbed for
Rules 1, 4, and 5. Templates are starting points, not fill-in-the-blanks. Rewrite them into D.J.'s
actual sentence.

### 2. Personal Learning / Epiphany -- adopt, the workhorse

**Arc:** proof -> where you were before -> the insight -> the step-by-step -> loop-back.
**Fit:** tactical lane, any series. This is the closest shape to what the repo already ships, which
is why it is the easiest adoption. The difference from a standard Playbook script is that the
**result comes first** and the teaching is reverse-engineered from it.
**Default hook family:** 6 (Named Stakes). Family 5 (Confession) for the backstory beat.
**Length:** standard cap (Rule 7).

**Practitioner boundary:** the result in the hook has to be a result D.J. actually produced, which
means an operator, podcast, or recruiting result. Not "here's how I got the listing." If the
strongest proof belongs to an agent or a guest, the script is a Format 10, not a Format 2.

The one demand the source makes that we should honor: **the solution has to be non-obvious.** If the
proof is strong and the method is generic, the audience files the result as luck and the video is
worth nothing. Proof plus obvious method is the failure state.

- **Hook (proof):** "We did [real, sourced result] in [real timeframe]. The method is not the one
  everybody guesses."
- **Backstory:** "For [real period] I was doing [the thing that did not work], because I believed
  [the old belief]."
- **The insight:** "The mistake was [specific mistake]. The fix was [specific fix]."
- **Breakdown:** "Three moves. First [step]. Then [step]. Then [step]." (Unpack two fully, leave the
  third a half step short -- 10.4.)
- **Close:** "Here's what you do now" (Rule 4), then loop back to the number in the hook.

### 10. Lesson From Others -- adopt, highest value

**Arc:** the person -> their situation -> the lesson in their words -> how D.J. applied it -> what
changed.
**Fit:** this is the format D.J. is uniquely positioned to own, and the repo is under-using it.
700+ KIR episodes is a lesson library nobody else in Chicago real estate has. The Kale mentor-story
protocol in [`mentor-stories-collection.md`](mentor-stories-collection.md) is a second feed.
**Default hook family:** 6 (Named Stakes) or 3 (Defector).
**Series fit:** Inside the Industry (IS), Podcast Promo, Chicago Agent Spotlight.

**Why it outperforms a straight tip for a recruiting brand:** it positions D.J. as the student and
curator rather than the expert selling expertise. That is the strongest possible pass on the Rule
10.3 recruiting test -- an agent evaluating Kale respects the person who platforms other people's
wisdom more than the person who claims all of it.

**The binding constraints, and they are not optional:**

- Name the person and the episode. A vague "someone told me once" wastes the format's entire
  advantage. The source is right that the other person has to feel like a real character.
- Consent and attribution rules from [`mentor-stories-collection.md`](mentor-stories-collection.md)
  bind on any Kale mentor or mentee story: share-permission and name-permission are separate
  questions and both get asked.
- The lesson lands as a **direct quote or a specific moment**, not a paraphrased takeaway. Quotes
  are high-fragility facts (Rule 8.3) -- pull from the transcript, do not reconstruct from memory.
- Check the promo registry before airing a KIR lesson. Across 700+ episodes the same tip repeats.

Templates:

- **Hook:** "[Named person] told me one thing that changed how I [specific area]. I have repeated it
  to every agent I have hired since."
- **Situation:** "When we recorded, they had just [real, verifiable context]."
- **The lesson:** "They said, quote, '[exact line from the transcript].'"
- **Application:** "I took that and [specific thing D.J. actually did]. Here's what changed."
- **Close:** the action, then the loop-back. No "comment below."

### 1. Hero's Journey -- adapt, identity lane only

**Arc:** problem -> pain -> failed attempts -> the thing that worked -> the result.
**Fit:** the identity lane (Rule 10.7), roughly once every couple of weeks, never as a tactical
default. The source's secret sauce is right -- the format lives or dies on whether the viewer feels
the pain as their own.
**Default hook family:** 5 (Confession) or 4 (System Indictment).

**Practitioner boundary -- this format's biggest risk.** The Hero's Journey wants a personal
struggle, and the nearest-to-hand struggle in this industry is an agent's. D.J. cannot be the hero
of an agent-practice arc because he has never lived one. The arcs he can own are the broker's, the
podcast host's, and the recruiter's. If the draft has him grinding out cold calls to *sellers*,
sweating a closing, or losing a listing, it is the wrong narrator. Hand it to a named agent or a
guest and run it as Format 10.

**The honest constraint:** D.J.'s real arc is not rags-to-riches, and performing one would fail the
Rule 10.3 recruiting test on contact. The version that works is smaller and true: a specific thing
he was wrong about for years, what it cost, what he tried that did not work, and the change. The
"failed attempts" beat is the one that earns the format, and it is the one most likely to get
invented. Only list attempts that actually happened.

- **Intro:** "For [real period] I [the real problem], and I did not know how to fix it."
- **Inflection:** "It cost me [the real, specific cost]." (Not a menu pick. The actual cost.)
- **Rising action:** "I tried [real attempt], then [real attempt]. Neither one moved it."
- **Climax:** "What finally worked was [the actual change]."
- **Falling action:** "Since then, [the real result]."
- **Resolution:** the reframe, not a pitch.

### 3. About Me / Origin Story -- adopt once, then pin it

**Arc:** normal life -> the trigger -> the epiphany -> the change -> the purpose.
**Fit:** one build, pinned to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and used as the LinkedIn featured video.
Revisit annually. This is a real gap: new profile visitors currently have no single video that
explains why D.J. does this.
**Default hook family:** 5 (Confession) or 7 (Forbidden).

**The one change from the source:** it opens its template with "Hi I'm [name], and X years ago I was
a normal guy." Do not do that. The 0:00 scroll-stop is mandatory even on a pinned video, and the
name goes in the second sentence, not the first.

- **Intro:** open on the tension, then place yourself. "[Tension line]. I run a brokerage of 250
  agents, and [the thing that does not fit the picture]."
- **Conflict:** "Then [the real trigger event]."
- **Epiphany:** "That is when I understood [the realization]."
- **Change:** "So I [the actual thing D.J. did]. Today, [current state]."
- **Purpose:** what the whole thing is for, in one line, aimed at the agent watching.

Because it is pinned rather than fed, this one can run slightly long. It is the only format in this
doc with that exemption.

### 9. Personal Update -- adopt, low frequency

**Arc:** tease -> context -> the news -> the reason.
**Fit:** identity lane, community building. The source is right that these are not authority plays.
They are the format that makes an audience feel like insiders, which matters for a recruiting brand
where the relationship precedes the decision by months.
**Default hook family:** 7 (Forbidden) or 5 (Confession).
**Frequency:** only when there is real news. A manufactured update is worse than no update.

- **Hook:** "Something changed in how we [do the thing], and I want to explain why."
- **Context:** "For the last [real period] we were [the previous state]."
- **The update:** "As of [real date], [the change]. It was not an easy call, because [the real
  tension]."
- **Rationale:** "[The actual reason]." Then the loop-back. No ask.

### 8. Day In The Life -- adapt, production-gated

**Arc:** identity and context -> morning -> the real work and its complication -> wind down.
**Fit:** strong follower driver and it doubles as recruiting proof. An agent considering Kale seeing
what the person running it actually does all day is a recruiting asset no script can replicate.
**Default hook family:** 8 (Cohort Callout) or 1 (Mirror).

**Practitioner boundary:** this is a *broker's* day, and that is the whole point. Do not stage agent
activities to fill it. The honest version (recruiting calls, agent problems landing on his desk,
a podcast record, brokerage operations) is more differentiated than another agent-day video anyway,
because nobody else is filming from that chair.

**The real constraint is production, not editorial.** This is a multi-clip shoot across a full day,
not a selfie-stick walk-and-talk, so it does not fit the daily build and the
[`pattern-interrupt-cheatsheet.md`](pattern-interrupt-cheatsheet.md) selfie-stick moves mostly do not
apply. Treat it as a planned shoot, a handful of times a year, not a lane.

The source's secret sauce applies: it has to contain something genuinely unexpected. A day of
predictable meetings is not a video. The 30 cold calls before 10am is.

### 6. Challenge -- adapt, one at a time, must be true

**Arc:** the challenge and stakes -> the rules -> the journey with real obstacles -> resolution or
cliffhanger.
**Fit:** exactly one honest version exists today, and it is the daily call volume. A bounded,
documented, genuinely-attempted challenge is the strongest proof-of-work a recruiter can show,
because it is the same activity he is asking agents to do.
**Default hook family:** 6 (Named Stakes).

**Practitioner boundary:** recruiting calls are D.J.'s actual job, so a challenge built on them is
first-person-legal. A challenge built on agent prospecting activity is not. Keep the challenge
inside the work he really does.

**Ignore the source's secret sauce entirely.** "Make the challenge extremely grand" and "add
high-intensity complications" are instructions to inflate, and inflation here is fabrication. The
rules stay real, the numbers stay logged, and a day that went fine gets reported as a day that went
fine.

**Cap:** one running challenge at a time. Two competing arcs confuse the feed and neither pays off.

**On the cliffhanger:** "Follow to see part 2" is banned (Rule 4). The Rule 10.6 cross-video open
loop does the same work legally: state that the next installment exists, do not direct anyone to go
get it.

### 7. Win -- adapt, rare, proof or nothing

**Arc:** the win -> the proof -> what it means -> the nod to the work.
**Fit:** genuinely large, dated, verifiable milestones only. Roughly quarterly at most.
**Default hook family:** 6 (Named Stakes).

**The tension with our own standards:** Rule 5 says wear the authority lightly, and a victory-
announcement lane run at any real frequency reads as bragging and fails the Rule 10.3 recruiting
test. The source is right that wins work when the proof is undeniable and the delivery is raw and
short. It is wrong to imply you should be looking for reasons to run them.

Where it does work: the emotional beat, not the number. "I almost quit in year three, this is why I
did not" carries a win further than the number alone, and it is the beat that overlaps with the
identity lane.

## Rejected, and why

### 4. Before and After

The format's entire power is the visual gap between two states, on screen, synced to a beat drop.
D.J. films walk-and-talks on a selfie stick. There is no visual before and after to reveal, and the
source's own advice ("a bigger, more surprising gap = more watch time") is direct pressure toward
exaggerating whatever gap exists.

The only honest adaptation is a business-metric before and after from a named, consented source --
and at that point it is Format 2 with a chart, so build it as Format 2. Do not create a lane for
this. If a genuinely visual transformation ever presents itself (an office build-out, a listing), it
can be run as a one-off under Format 2's proof discipline.

### 5. Goal / Dream Journey

Open-ended personal aspiration content, built on the audience wondering whether the creator will
pull it off. It is a parasocial attention format, and it does not survive the Rule 10.3 recruiting
test: an agent evaluating where to hang a license does not benefit from watching the broker chase a
personal dream. The bounded, work-relevant version of this is Format 6, which is already adapted
above. There is nothing left over worth keeping.

## Cadence

The back catalog is close to all-tactical. Story formats are how the identity lane (Rule 10.7)
actually gets built, so the cadence below is a floor, not a quota to hit:

| Format | Target frequency |
| --- | --- |
| 2. Personal Learning | The default whenever a script has a real result to lead with. No cap. |
| 10. Lesson From Others | Weekly is achievable given the KIR archive. Currently well under that. |
| 1. Hero's Journey | Roughly every other week, as the identity-lane slot (Rule 10.7). |
| 9. Personal Update | Only on real news. |
| 8. Day In The Life | A few planned shoots per year. |
| 6. Challenge | One at a time, when a real one is running. |
| 7. Win | Quarterly at most. |
| 3. About Me | Once, pinned. Revisit annually. |

Formats 1, 3, 7, 8, and 9 mostly land in the identity lane. Formats 2, 6, and 10 can run tactical.

## Frontmatter

```yaml
story_format: 1-10 | none   # Optional. Which story shape this script runs (this doc)
```

Set it on any script that deliberately runs one of these shapes; leave it off or set `none` for a
standard tactical build. It exists so format variety can be audited the same way `hook_family` is.
Nothing reads this field automatically yet -- `scripts/news_brief.py` does not surface a story-format
banner the way it does for hook cadence and the identity lane. If the field proves useful in
practice, wiring it into the brief is the follow-up.
