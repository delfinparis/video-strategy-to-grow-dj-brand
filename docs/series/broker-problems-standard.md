# Broker Problems — Series Standard

Per-series rules for **Broker Problems**, Pillar 3, 2 videos a week (Tue/Thu). Builds on
[`../editorial-standards.md`](../editorial-standards.md), which is the universal standard.
Everything there applies here. This document only adds what is specific to this lane.

**If this document and the universal standard ever conflict, the universal standard wins.**

Why the lane exists and who it is aimed at: [`../strategy/2026-08-18-why-agents-leave.md`](../strategy/2026-08-18-why-agents-leave.md).
The candidate bank: [`../../data/brokerage-pain.md`](../../data/brokerage-pain.md).

## What a Broker Problem is

A short-form video where D.J. names one thing about the arrangement between an agent and
their brokerage that the agent has stopped noticing, and makes it impossible to un-notice.

**Core promise to the viewer:** *You have been living with this. Here is what it actually is.*

That is the whole promise. There is no fix beat in the sense the other lanes have one, and
that is deliberate -- see **The close** below.

**The objective is to make the agent explore other options.** Not to make them join Kale, and
not merely to make them feel seen. The seed that produces exploring is **"I have never once
checked whether this is normal,"** never "my brokerage is bad" -- the first is undefendable
and costs nothing to resolve, the second is arguable and makes the agent defend six years of
their own judgment out loud. **Seed ignorance of the market, never inadequacy of the firm.**
Full reasoning in the strategy doc under *The actual objective*.

**Success is not views.** A Broker Problem worked if a Chicago agent recognized themselves in
the comments, or said out loud that they do not know their own number. The second is the
better signal: "that's my brokerage" is recognition, **"now I want to know what mine is" is
doubt.** The video does reach; the comments do recruiting.

## The four beats

Shorter than the take's five, because there is no who-profits turn and no swap.

```
1. THE THING     - Named flat in the first spoken line. 0:00-0:03, spoken, no warm-up.
                   Specific about the practice, category-level about the party.
2. THE SIZE      - What it actually is. A year's arithmetic, a scene played out, or the
                   question with no good answer. This is the beat that does the work.
3. THE MIRROR    - Turn it to the viewer's own situation. Never "you should leave."
                   Always "go look." A number they can compute, a thing they can check,
                   a person they can ask.
4. LOOP-BACK     - Rule 9.1. The last line reloads the first with its new meaning.
```

**Beat 3 is the lane's whole ethic and the beat most likely to be written wrong.** The
temptation is to resolve it -- to tell the agent what to do about what they just learned. Do
not. An agent who has been somewhere six years will not be argued out of it by a 55-second
video, and trying makes the video a pitch. Hand them the *look*, not the *leave*. The
conclusion has to be theirs or it is not a conclusion, it is an ad.

**Word count 130-165.** 45-70 seconds at D.J.'s pace. Shorter than the take because there is
no argument to win.

## Flavor rotation (mandatory)

Three flavors, defined in the strategy doc and tagged on every bank entry.

| Flavor | Beat 2 is | Hook family | Heat | Usual rung |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Slow Leak** | annualized arithmetic | 6, 4 | 3.5 | 1 or 2 |
| **The Thing That Doesn't Make Sense** | the question with no good answer | 4, 7 | 3.5, occasionally 4 | 2 or 3 |
| **The Silence** | a scene | 1, 5 | 3 | 1 |
| **The Permission Slip** | D.J.'s own position, stated plainly | 5, 3 | 3 | 2 or 3 |
| **Run The Math With Me** | arithmetic performed live, in a rehearsed confrontation | 3, 6 | **4** | 2 |

- **Never the same flavor twice in one week** (Tue and Thu are always different flavors).
- **Never the same flavor on consecutive posts across the week boundary** -- if Thursday was
  a Slow Leak, next Tuesday is not.
- **The Silence runs at least twice a month.** It is the flavor that converts and the first
  one a fee-focused lane stops making. Audit it monthly.
- **Run The Math With Me runs weekly if the heat budget allows** (see below). It is the
  register D.J. asked for on 2026-08-18 and it is the highest-conviction voice in the lane.
- **The Permission Slip runs about monthly.** It is the flavor that makes acting on the doubt
  feel allowed, and it only works in D.J.'s mouth -- a brokerage president saying agents should
  be looking is a position, not a pitch (Family 3, The Defector). **It fails completely if it
  resolves to Kale**, which would retroactively turn the previous month of the lane into a
  funnel in the viewer's mind. Heat 3, Rule 10.7 register, payoff is a reframe not a tactic.

**The Slow Leak never ships without annualization.** The monthly number is exactly why nobody
has ever reacted to it. Convert to a year and to a thing the agent would have bought instead.

## The Run The Math With Me register (added 2026-08-18)

D.J. supplied a raw rant on 2026-08-18 and asked for scripts in that register. It is captured
here as its own flavor because it is not a hotter version of the others -- it is a different
structure, and six specific moves make it work. **BP-005 is the reference implementation.**
Copy the moves, not the topic.

**1. "If I were an agent, I'd walk in and say this."** The single most important move, and the
one the rest of the lane was missing. D.J. does not tell the viewer what to do; he does it
himself, out loud, in the first person, as a scene. This bypasses the defensive reflex entirely
-- there is nothing to resist, because nobody is being instructed. It is permission by
demonstration rather than by argument, and it is a stronger version of what the Permission Slip
flavor achieves through persuasion.

It also does something no other move in this lane does: **it rehearses a confrontation**, which
is one step from having it. That is rung 3 delivered without ever asking for rung 3.

**2. Perform the arithmetic. Do not present it.** "Commission's what, seventy-five hundred?"
The hesitation is the technique. A clean number reads as a recital; a number being worked out
reads as thinking, and it invites the viewer to run their own alongside. **Editors and rewrites
will try to smooth these. Refuse.** The looseness is the credibility.

**3. The money becomes a fast list of foreclosed purchases.** Not one substitution -- four,
delivered quickly, none elaborated. A coach, two client parties, a sponsorship, a charity check.
Speed is what turns a line item into a life the agent did not get to have. Slowing down to
explain any single item converts the beat into a tip and drains it.

**4. One-clause concession, no elaboration.** "Obviously the firm has to make money, I get
that." This is what separates the register from a rant against arithmetic, and it is what keeps
qualifying test 4 satisfied. **Never expand it, never move it earlier, never cut it for time.**

**5. Pre-empt the counterargument, answer it with a checkable number.** "They'll tell you it's
the brand. Really?" then a figure a viewer can confirm tonight. Naming the objection before the
viewer can raise it converts a skeptic into a participant.

**6. Close on irony, not instruction.** "They must be insane." Then the loop-back reveals the
irony. The viewer is left holding an unanswered question, which produces both the comment
(Rule 10.4) and the search (rung 2), and D.J. never told anyone to do anything.

**What this register costs, and the honest constraint.** It runs at **heat 4**, not 3.5. It
names no party, so the ship test passes easily, but it names an industry behavior pattern as
wrong, which is the Rule 9.2 definition of 4.

## The heat budget, and the case for revisiting it

Rule 9.2 caps heat 4-5 at **one post per week across the entire schedule**, and the Wednesday
take normally spends it. **Under the current cap, Run The Math With Me can run only in weeks the
take lane yields**, which is roughly never, since the take lane's Wednesday slot is what makes
that lane distinctive.

**The recommendation is to raise the cap to two heat-4 posts a week, with one reserved for this
flavor.** The argument: the cap exists so the brand does not become the controversy, but the
risk it was written against is *targets*, not temperature. A heat 4 that names a cohort and a
heat 4 built entirely on public arithmetic with no named party carry very different brand risk,
and the current rule prices them identically. **A target-based cap would be a better instrument
than a temperature-based one.**

**Resolved 2026-08-20, and the argument above won.** D.J. did not raise the cap from one heat-4
post to two. He removed the temperature cap entirely and replaced it with a band of **4 to 5**,
the body of work averaging **4.3-4.5** -- which is precisely the "target-based cap would be a
better instrument than a temperature-based one" recommendation, taken further than this section
proposed. Heat 5 went from banned to permitted-and-priced the same day, so a 5 in this lane is
allowed by Rule 9.2 and argued against by this lane's own strategy. That is a judgment call the
writer makes deliberately, per the pre-flight above.

Run The Math With Me no longer waits for a surrendered slot. What it has to clear instead is the
lane's own judgment call: the arithmetic is public and the target is a category, and the moment a
viewer could name one firm with confidence the script has become a 5. Rule 9.2 permits that now.
This lane's strategy still argues against it, because an agent whose firm is named defends it out
loud. Decide on purpose, and write down which way you went.

## Heat and the identifiable-target rule

**3.5 default. Heat 4 only in a week the take lane surrenders its slot** -- Rule 9.2 caps heat
4-5 at one post across the entire publishing schedule and the Wednesday take normally spends
it. Check the `news_brief.py` hook-cadence banner before drafting.

**Heat 5 is banned permanently.** Same floor as the take lane, same reason: D.J. runs a
769-agent brokerage and is on the record in trade press.

**The rule is "no identifiable target," not "no name typed."** Read the *Specific about the
practice, unidentifiable as a party* section of [`../../data/sacred-cows.md`](../../data/sacred-cows.md)
before drafting beat 1. Two failure modes sit either side of the good version:

- **A knowing wink is not a shield.** "We all know which brokerage does this" carries the same
  exposure as naming them, with less punch.
- **Hedging into mush is worse for this lane specifically.** "Some brokerages could do better"
  is safe, says nothing, and earns no comment -- and the comment is the entire recruiting
  mechanic. A Broker Problem that draws no replies has failed at its actual job even if it
  reaches a hundred thousand people.

**Ship test:** could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? If yes, rewrite.

### Naming someone as *right* is a different category, and it is allowed

The heat-5 ban is on naming a person, firm, coach, or product **as wrong**. It has never been a
ban on saying names. Naming a real person as an *exemplar* is allowed, is already established
in this lane (Sara DeWulf in BP-001), and is often the strongest move available -- an
abstraction is a statistic, a named person the viewer respects is proof.

The rules that make it safe:

- **Everything said about them is verifiable and flattering.** Verify their firm, role, and any
  ranking claim against a source before air, and put the citation in the pinned comment.
- **Get their situation right.** BP-005's first draft described two Chicago principals as
  self-sponsored agents; both actually own or co-own independent firms. Getting it wrong hands
  a correction to anyone who knows the market, and the true version was the better argument
  anyway. **Check what someone actually is before using them to prove something.**
- **Never comparative.** No named person or firm may be set against another, and none may be
  compared to Kale. Either turns a citation into a prop in an ad.
- **Courtesy heads-up before air**, especially for anyone who has not been on the podcast. D.J.
  runs a competing brokerage; the people he cites should hear it from him first. It costs two
  minutes and it converts a possible irritation into a likely reshare.
- **Do not tag them in the post.** Tagging is a summons; it puts a named person on the spot
  inside a competitor's comment section. If they want in, they will find it.

**Extra care in this lane:** two of the strongest bank receipts (B1, C4) contain a product or
franchise name inside a sourced quote. The quote may be logged in the bank. **It may not be
spoken.** Strip to the category and keep the meaning.

## The Kale rule

**No Broker Problem video mentions Kale. Ever.** Not in the close, not in a single line, not
as "at my brokerage we do this differently."

Two things die the moment it does. Reach -- D.J.'s own Facebook data puts overt Kale content
at -5.3x and webinar promo at -10.5x. And the comment mechanic, which is worse: nobody outs
their own brokerage in the replies underneath a competitor's advertisement.

**The video's job is to be right, not to be Kale.** The Kale answer exists and it is good; it
lives in the DM Ana sends after Jennica flags the commenter, and it draws on the eight scripts
in [`../../scripts/kale-signaling/`](../../scripts/kale-signaling/). Be right in public, be
Kale in private.

The one permitted self-reference is the credential when it is load-bearing for the claim --
Family 3, The Defector: *"I run a brokerage. I'll tell you how this actually works from the
other side of the desk."* That is authority, not a pitch, and it is allowed exactly as long as
it does not resolve to an offer.

## The close: pick a rung

Rule 4 requires a "here's what you do now" close and bans engagement asks. Both hold. What is
specific to this lane is that the action is always small, always the agent's own, and sits on
one of three rungs. **Declare the rung in frontmatter** so the mix can be audited.

| Rung | The close asks for | Example | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | check your own situation | "open what you send clients after closing and look for an export button" | none |
| **2** | **find out what normal is** | "find out what the rest of this market charges for that, then look at yours again" | none |
| **3** | have one conversation | "ask your broker what it pays for, in writing" | small, social |

**Rung 2 is the engine and it is the one that gets skipped.** Most agents do not know what
other firms charge, provide, or answer for, and pointing them at that gap costs D.J. nothing,
names nobody, and cannot be read as a pitch. It is also, literally, asking them to explore
other options. **Across any four-week block at least half the closes sit on rung 2.** A lane
sitting entirely on rung 1 is producing recognition and calling it doubt.

**Rung 3 no more than one script in four.** An ask requiring a social act fails more often, and
a lane that keeps asking for hard things trains the viewer to ignore the ask.

**Still banned, and it has not loosened:** leave, switch, "there are better options," or
anything naming a destination. This is sequencing, not softness. A 55-second video cannot beat
six years of sunk cost, and an agent who feels pushed defends their firm out loud -- which
converts a private doubt into a public commitment against the doubt. That is worse than doing
nothing. **And the moment an alternative is named, the doubt gets re-attributed to whoever
benefits from it and gets discarded as a sales technique. The doubt has to be theirs.**

The close resolves to a *decision the viewer makes on their own time*, which Rule 10.7 already
permits for the identity lane. Then the loop-back (Rule 9.1) reloads the hook.

## Comments without asks

Rule 4 bans engagement asks, and Instagram's own recommendation guidance backs it
independently -- content that "explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags,
or other actions" is not recommended, with "comment a specific word, number, or emoji" named as
the example. This lane is ungated precisely so it can earn reach, so an ask would cost the one
thing it exists for.

The two Rule 10.4 devices that fit:

- **Intentional incompleteness.** Name four things and fully unpack three. The fourth arrives
  in the replies, supplied by agents, which is the recruiting signal arriving free.
- **The defensible line that recruits defenders.** A heat-3.5 claim specific enough that the
  agents it is true for confirm it against the agents who say it is not.

**And one this lane gets that the others do not:** a question with no good answer produces a
reply from anyone who thinks they have one. B-flavor scripts should end beat 2 on the question
and never answer it on camera.

## Comment duty (the operating rule, not a suggestion)

**Someone reads every comment on every Broker Problem video and flags Chicago complainers into
Close** for Ana and Jennica. This is written into
[`../../schedule/master-calendar.md`](../../schedule/master-calendar.md) as Tuesday and
Thursday duty and it is the only reason this lane is worth two slots a week.

**Blocker as of 2026-08-18:** the `Brokerage Pain` custom field does not exist in Close. It
was a launch prerequisite in the gate-layer doc and was never created. Without it there is
nowhere to record *what* the commenter complained about -- which is the whole value of the
signal and the thing that tells Ana which of the eight kale-signaling arguments to open with.
Until the field exists, this lane produces reach and no retrievable recruiting data.
