# tapthis.co Walk-and-Talk - Series Standard

This document defines the per-series rules for the **tapthis.co Walk-and-Talk** series. It builds on [`editorial-standards.md`](../editorial-standards.md), the universal standard. Everything in the universal standard applies here. This document only adds what is specific to this series.

**If this document and the universal standard ever conflict, the universal standard wins.**

---

## What this series is

A walk-and-talk video series that drives real estate agents to tapthis.co, D.J.'s free library of copy-paste AI prompts for agents. Each episode takes one real frustration an agent has with AI or with a part of their business, and shows that the fix is a prompt that already exists, for free, at tapthis.co.

Unlike Agent Tip of the Day, these are not guest-sourced. They are product-led: the tip is "stop fighting a blank box, the prompt is already written." This is top-of-funnel. A viewer who lands on tapthis.co gets pixeled for retargeting and asked for an email.

**Core promise to the viewer:** *In about three minutes, you'll see exactly why your AI keeps disappointing you, and the free fix you can use before your next appointment.*

---

## Structure (fixed for this series)

Every episode follows: **Hook -> Problem -> Solution -> Results -> clever final line.**

- **Hook (0:00-0:20):** Drop into a specific, relatable moment mid-stride. Spoken, not text-only (captions.ai renders audio). No "hey guys."
- **Problem (0:20-0:55):** Name the real reason it's happening. Reframe so it's not the agent's fault and not the AI's fault, it's the input.
- **Solution (0:55-2:00):** tapthis.co, the specific category to tap, and the exact mechanic (copy, fill the brackets, paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini).
- **Results (2:00-3:00):** The concrete before-and-after. What the agent walks away with.
- **Clever final line (3:00-3:15):** A distinct closer per episode. Never the same template twice. This is the line that gets quoted.

## The CTA

The close is always a "here's what you do now" action aimed at the viewer's own life, ending at tapthis.co. Never an engagement ask. "Go grab one before your next listing appointment" is the shape. "Follow for more" is banned.

## Fixed facts (always accurate)

- tapthis.co is a free library of **570** copy-paste AI prompts for real estate agents. Use 570 across the series for consistency. (Note: the live data file currently holds 620 entries; 570 is the published brand figure. Reconcile site vs. content before changing this number.)
- Four ways in: **Write Something For Me**, **Help Me Follow Up**, **Coach Me Through It**, **Plan My Strategy**.
- Works with **ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini**.
- Prompts have fill-in **brackets** the agent completes before pasting.
- D.J.'s credibility anchor: *"I've never sold a house. But I've interviewed more than 700 agents who have."* Use sparingly, once per episode at most.

## Anti-patterns (in addition to the universal list)

- Do NOT invent usage stats, time-saved numbers, or adoption percentages. Product facts and D.J.'s own observations only.
- Do NOT reuse the same Problem opener or the same closer shape across episodes. Vary them. Cross-episode sameness is the tell.
- Do NOT claim a specific access model ("three free then email") unless it matches the current live gate. As of June 2026 the site asks for an email on the first copy.
- Do NOT make the prompt-category mechanic sound like a demo. Voice carries it. No screen recording required.

## Distribution copy

Each script ships with a teaser and a social caption in its Distribution Copy section. These follow the universal Rule 9 writing-discipline checks: one concrete anchor each, straight ASCII quotes, no engagement asks, no curly quotes, no repeated pattern across the set.
