# What Actually Works — Series Standard

This document defines the per-series rules for the **What Actually Works** series. It builds on
[`editorial-standards.md`](../editorial-standards.md), which is the universal standard.
Everything in the universal standard applies here. This document only adds what is specific to
What Actually Works.

**If this document and the universal standard ever conflict, the universal standard wins.**

The content engine (ranked problem bank, scoring, do-not-cite list) lives in
[`what-actually-works-problem-index.md`](what-actually-works-problem-index.md). This standard is
how a chosen entry becomes a finished script.

---

## What What Actually Works is

A short-form video series where D.J. takes a real problem an agent actually has -- the kind they
feel but rarely name -- and hands them a **non-obvious, research-backed move that fixes it.** Not
the advice everyone already knows ("follow up more," "be more disciplined"). The *second-order*
fix: why the obvious advice fails, and the specific mechanism that makes the right behavior
actually happen.

**Core promise to the viewer:** *You're stuck on something, you think it's a willpower or talent
problem, and it isn't. Here's the real reason, backed by research, and the one thing you do
tomorrow.*

If a draft only restates advice the viewer has heard before, it is not What Actually Works.

### Why this series exists alongside Inside the Industry

Inside the Industry answers **"what's happening?"** (the news layer). What Actually Works answers
**"why am I stuck, and what fixes it?"** (the how layer). The second question is the one agents
wake up with. The two pillars reinforce each other; they don't compete.

This is also the most crowded niche on social media (research-backed communication / persuasion /
self-improvement). The only thing that keeps D.J. out of the generic-creator pile is **the moat**:
700 interviews, a brokerage-operator's seat, NAR access. The guardrails below exist to keep every
script inside that moat.

## The five-beat format (mandatory)

Every script follows this arc. It maps onto the universal Problem-Agitation-Solution structure
(Rule 3); the difference is beat 2, which is the series' whole reason for existing.

**3-act spine: tactical series, compressed.** What Actually Works runs the *compressed*
[Viral 3-Act Spine](viral-3-act-spine.md). Beat 2 ("why the obvious fix fails") IS the one story
turn -- the "you think it's discipline, but here's the turn" moment. That single turn is the whole
Act 2; don't expand it into a developed narrative. The mapping: PROBLEM + WHY THE OBVIOUS FIX FAILS
= Act 1 hook and the story turn; MECHANISM + APPLICATION = Act 3 payoff (resolve the loop, hand over
the move); CLOSE = the loop-back. If the story turn ever pushes the script past the 75-second cap,
compress it. Length wins (universal Rule 7).

```
1. PROBLEM    - A specific friction the viewer feels. Ideally the second-order one:
                not "you should prospect," but "you know you should and you don't."
2. WHY THE    - The obvious fix is named and shown to fail. This is the hook that earns
   OBVIOUS      the next 50 seconds. ("Discipline was never the problem.")
   FIX FAILS
3. MECHANISM  - One named, citable research finding. The non-obvious WHY.
4. APPLICATION- One thing, do it tomorrow, framed for an agent. Concrete and physical.
5. CLOSE      - Soft recruiting CTA, action close, or watchpoint (see below). One only.
```

A required sixth element, woven into beats 3-4 (not a separate beat): the **archive/operator
tie-in** (G1 below). It usually sits right before the close.

If a draft can't fill all five beats with a verified mechanism and a verified tie-in, it is not
ready to script.

## The five guardrails (non-negotiable)

These are restated from the problem-index because they decide whether a script gets made. **If a
concept can't clear all five, it doesn't get made.**

**G1 — The archive (or operator) tie-in is REQUIRED.**
Every script must carry the proof layer: a *real, verified* top producer from the 700-episode
archive who already does this intuitively, or a firsthand brokerage-operator observation. This is
what re-bridges D.J.'s actual authority to borrowed science. **No verified tie-in → no script.**
Never fabricate or composite a guest (universal Rule 1). Until the guest is confirmed against the
actual episode, the script stays `status: draft` and the tie-in line uses the safe qualitative
form ("the top producers I've interviewed who never lose a lead...") with a flagged
`[VERIFY/INSERT]` note in the Data Source.

**G2 — The science is the explanation, never the headline.**
Position every video as *"the science behind what top producers already do"* -- not "here's a
self-help tip." The 700-episode credential stays in the driver's seat; the research explains *why*
it works. This keeps D.J. in the lane he owns.

**G3 — The strip test.**
Apply before greenlighting: *"Could this exact video run on a generic self-help account with the
real estate parts removed?"* If yes, kill it or sharpen the agent application until the answer is
no.

**G4 — Beachhead first.**
At launch, only two categories ship: **sales communication / persuasion** and **prospecting /
follow-up discipline.** Pure life/mindset/money entries are parked until the beachhead proves out.
The launch-eligible roster is in the problem-index.

**G5 — Source rigor + the do-not-cite list.**
Every claim verified to a primary source before scripting. No "studies show." Never cite a
replication-crisis casualty (power posing, ego depletion, generic priming, strong-form Mozart
effect, learning styles -- full table in the problem-index). Prefer findings with a meta-analysis
or multi-lab replication behind them.

## Research / source requirements (extends Rule 1)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Named researcher + work** | The script names the researcher and, where it fits the cadence, the year. "A psychologist named Peter Gollwitzer found..." Not "studies show." |
| **One mechanism per script** | Never stack two findings. The second-order problem is the whole payload. |
| **Verify before air** | Every citation re-confirmed to a primary source (study, book, or meta-analysis). The Data Source section records the full citation and a "VERIFY to primary source before air" note until confirmed. |
| **Do-not-cite check** | Every script's Data Source states "Do-not-cite check: clean" (or flags the caveat, e.g. choice overload is contested). |
| **No fabricated quantification** | Same rule as IS synthesis. If the effect size is real and citable, use it ("two to three times more often"). If not, use the qualitative form. Never invent a percentage. |

The mechanism is a *finding*, not a statistic about real estate. Rule 1's stat-integrity rules
still apply to any real estate number in the script (production volume, market figures, etc.).

## Length and pacing (extends Rule 7)

- **Target: 45-60 seconds** (~100-150 spoken words).
- **Hard cap: 75 seconds** (~185 spoken words).
- These run slightly longer than a pure tip because beat 2 (why the obvious fix fails) and beat 3
  (the mechanism) both need room. The extra length is earned only when the mechanism genuinely
  needs the setup. If beats 2 and 3 can be tightened, tighten them.

---

## The close (extends Rule 4)

Three close forms are allowed. Use **one** per script, and **vary across a run** so the series
doesn't become formulaic:

- **Soft recruiting CTA** — *"If your brokerage isn't teaching you things like this, I'd love to
  talk."* This series is the natural home for the soft-CTA sub-pillar (see the 2026-05-11 addendum
  to the pillar pivot decision). It earns the line because the video just made the viewer better.
  Use the per-video UTM / tracking the addendum requires.
- **Action close** — *"Three texts. Today. Watch what comes back."* A concrete, do-it-now action
  tied to the mechanism.
- **Try-this / watchpoint close** — *"Next upset client, name it before you fix it."* An invitation
  to test the move in the wild.

All three are compliant with Rule 4 (no engagement asks). None of them ask for likes, comments,
follows, or shares.

**Cadence guard:** the soft-CTA close should run on roughly 1 in 3 of these videos, not every one.
Wall-to-wall CTAs erode the authority that makes the CTA work.

---

## Hook variation (extends Rule 8)

The series' native move -- "the thing you think is right is actually wrong → the real mechanism" --
leans on the contrarian-reveal and "it's not X, it's Y" patterns flagged in
[`../ai-tells-field-guide.md`](../ai-tells-field-guide.md). It is allowed, but **rotate the hook
shape across a run** so a week of these doesn't read formulaic or AI-generated:

- **Confession / reframe** — "You don't have a discipline problem. You have a trigger problem."
- **Scenario / permission** — "There's a text you've been meaning to send for a month."
- **Scene** — "Your seller just got a lowball and they're furious. Watch what most agents do next."
- **Counterintuitive stat** — open on the surprising finding itself.

Max one "it's not X, it's Y" construction per script (universal Rule 8). Don't stack it with a
Rule-of-Three in the same script.

## Protected voice signature (extends Rule 6)

D.J.'s signature -- *"I've never practiced real estate. But I've interviewed 700 people who have."*
-- fits this series well, because the archive tie-in (G1) is already doing that work. Use it no more
than once per script, and only when it earns its place (usually folded into the archive tie-in or
the reframe). Don't bolt it on.

## AI Music Prompt (required, per project rule)

Every What Actually Works script is a walk-and-talk and **must end with an `## AI Music Prompt`
section beneath `## Data Source`**, per [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) and
[`../ai-music-prompts.md`](../ai-music-prompts.md).

- **Default preset:** the **Playbook / coaching** preset (modern coaching underscore, light forward
  energy, ~105 BPM, major-leaning). This series is tactical and forward, like The Playbook.
- **Tune to the script's register:**
  - Motivating / habit / fix-it-today → coaching, 105 BPM, light forward energy.
  - Warm / human / relationship → blend coaching + documentary, ~100 BPM, warm and encouraging.
  - Composure-under-heat / de-escalation → coaching, ~96 BPM, calm and controlled.
- **CapCut prompt: 300-character hard cap** (per CLAUDE.md). Keep style, BPM, mood, 3-4 instruments,
  the no-vocals constraint, and a runtime hint.
- **Non-negotiables in every prompt:** `[no vocals]` / `instrumental only` at the start;
  `no melodic hook, no vocal chops, no risers, no whooshes`; `unobtrusive background for voiceover`;
  `loopable`; BPM as a number.

## Social copy (extends Rule 5)

- All six caption blocks (LinkedIn primary, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, X).
- **Strictest scrub, applied automatically every time** (CLAUDE.md social rule + Rule 5):
  - **Zero em dashes.** Not `—`, not `--`. Restructure into short sentences.
  - **Zero AI-speak transitions** ("here's what this means," "worth noting," etc.).
  - **Zero throat-clearing setups.** Lead with the substance.
- The LinkedIn caption is the long form (it can carry the full mechanism + application). IG / TikTok
  are tightened to the hook + the one move.
- Hashtags stay realtor-first. Broad communication / self-improvement tags are allowed as
  secondary, never as the lead, so the recruiting-qualified audience stays the target.

## Where it sits in the schedule

- **Add, don't replace.** Inside the Industry stays primary. The April 18 cross-surface data shows
  news is the measured engagement winner; this series is unproven until it has its own data.
- **Pilot at ~1/week** with its own clean attribution baseline (a per-video UTM on any soft-CTA).
- **The July 19 pivot review is the ramp decision.** If the pilot shows engagement in line with IIR
  *and* the soft-CTA produces at least one attributable recruiting inquiry, it earns a second weekly
  slot. If it underperforms IIR or pulls the wrong (non-Chicago, non-agent) audience, it stays a
  utility slot or gets cut.
- **Slot fit:** the Wed (Playbook-eligible) and Sat (Access-eligible) slots are the natural homes,
  since both already trend tactical.

## Anti-patterns

- **Generic self-help in a real estate costume.** If the script fails the strip test (G3), it's not
  this series. The realtor application must be load-bearing, not bolted on.
- **Citing a debunked finding.** Power posing, ego depletion, generic priming, etc. One debunked
  claim in the comments torches the credibility the whole series runs on. Run the G5 do-not-cite
  check every time.
- **Inventing or compositing a guest** to satisfy G1. The tie-in waits until a real one is
  confirmed. Safe qualitative form + VERIFY placeholder in the meantime.
- **Stacking two mechanisms** in one script. One finding, one move.
- **Leading with the science instead of the access** (violates G2). "Here's a UCLA study" is a worse
  open than "Watch what most agents do when a seller gets a lowball."
- **Wall-to-wall soft CTAs.** Erodes the authority that makes the CTA convert. ~1 in 3.
- **Formulaic hooks.** Rotate the hook shape; don't open every video with the same contrarian
  reveal.
- **Em dashes or AI-speak in social captions.** Strictest scrub, every time, before commit.
