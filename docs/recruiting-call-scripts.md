# Warm Call Scripts: Content-Sourced Recruiting

This document is the call script library for D.J.'s personal recruiting outreach. It pairs with [`content-recruiting-integration.md`](content-recruiting-integration.md), which defines the Sunday bridge protocol that produces the warm leads these scripts work against.

---

## The Core Strategic Insight

**The first call is not a recruiting call. It's a research call.**

This is the difference between converting at 1-2% (cold-recruit script) and converting at 8-15% (warm-research script). Pick up the phone Tuesday morning with "I want to talk about your career at Kale" energy and conversion craters. Pick up with "I'd love to learn what you're seeing in the field" and conversion is much higher - and the relationship survives the call regardless of outcome.

Why this works for D.J. specifically: he is a podcast host first. That's his authentic identity. Asking an agent for their perspective is exactly what he does every week with podcast guests. It is not a manipulation tactic - it is literally his job. The first call is just an unrecorded version of a podcast interview.

**The recruiting conversation comes 60-90 days into the relationship, not on call 1.** D.J.'s job on the first call is to earn the right to call again.

## Three Opener Variants (Pick by Lead Type)

### Opener A: Content Research Frame - DEFAULT

**Use for:** Anyone who reacted to or shared a post. The vast majority of weekly leads.

```
"Hi [Name], this is D.J. Paris from Keeping It Real Podcast.
Do you have 60 seconds?

[wait for yes / yes-but-quick / no]

I noticed you reacted to my post about [specific post topic]
this past weekend. I write content for working agents and I'm
trying to make sure it's grounded in what you're actually
seeing in the field, not just my opinions. Would you be willing
to tell me what your read on [topic] is?"
```

**Why this works:**

- "Do you have 60 seconds?" disarms the "what is this call?" reaction
- "I write content for working agents" is honest, low-stakes, and explains the call without naming Kale
- "Make sure it's grounded in what you're seeing in the field" positions the agent as the expert
- The ask is for their expertise, not their business

**Average call length:** 8-15 minutes. Some go longer.

### Opener B: Direct Reference Frame

**Use for:** Anyone whose engagement was emotional - specifically commented "this is so true" or shared with their own commentary.

```
"Hi [Name], this is D.J. Paris. We haven't met but I host
the Keeping It Real Podcast in Chicago. You reacted to my
post about [topic] over the weekend, so I figured I'd pick
up the phone and say thank you. Are you in the middle of
something or do you have a minute?"
```

**Why this works:**

- More relational, less interview-y
- Almost no one calls to thank an engager - pattern interrupts the "this is a sales call" assumption
- Lower bar to the conversation than Opener A

**Average call length:** 5-10 minutes.

### Opener C: The Question Frame

**Use for:** People who left a substantive comment on a post, not just a like or a brief "great post."

```
"Hi [Name], this is D.J. Paris with Keeping It Real Podcast.
You commented on my post about [topic] this weekend and your
comment got me thinking. I wanted to call and ask you about
it directly. Got 60 seconds?"
```

**Why this works:**

- "Your comment got me thinking" is the highest possible compliment to a substantive commenter
- Positions them as someone whose opinion matters enough to chase down on the phone
- Earns immediate attention

**Average call length:** 10-20 minutes. Substantive commenters love being asked to elaborate.

### Opener D: The Celebration Frame (Courted-Sourced Leads)

**Use for:** Leads sourced from Courted.io's daily 30, where the warm signal is a recent accomplishment, milestone, or flight-risk indicator surfaced by Courted's analytics - NOT a prior content engagement. These leads have never engaged with D.J.'s content directly.

```
"Hi [Name], this is D.J. Paris. We haven't met, but I host
the Keeping It Real Podcast in Chicago. I noticed you just
[specific accomplishment from Courted - closed listing,
record month, contract anniversary, etc.] - that's a great
win. I called to say congratulations and ask you a question.
Got 60 seconds?

[wait for yes]

How did you [land that listing / hit that price / close that
fast]?"
```

**Why this works:**

- "I called to say congratulations" is genuinely surprising. Almost no one calls to celebrate a stranger's win. It pattern-interrupts the "this is a sales call" assumption immediately.
- The accomplishment reference proves it's not a list call - Courted gives D.J. the specific data point.
- Asking "how did you do it" puts the agent in expert mode. They love to talk about their wins, especially to someone who took the time to notice.
- Builds rapport without ever mentioning brokerage change.

**Average call length:** 10-20 minutes. Agents love being asked to recount their wins.

**Critical: do NOT use this opener if Jennica has already SMS'd them about the same accomplishment within the last 7 days.** Check Close for `jennica-sms-[date]` notes before dialing. If Jennica already touched them, skip and check back at day 14+. Double-touch via two channels for the same accomplishment burns trust fast.

**The flight-risk variant (use sparingly, never lead with it):** If the Courted lead is sourced from a flight-risk signal rather than an accomplishment (e.g., contract anniversary approaching, activity gap, negative momentum), do NOT reference the flight-risk signal in the opener. That's creepy. Instead, find the most recent positive accomplishment in the same Courted profile and lead with that. Courted gives you both signals - use the celebration as the entry, not the warning.

## The Voicemail Script

70-80% of warm calls go to voicemail on the first attempt. This is the script for those.

```
"Hi [Name], this is D.J. Paris from Keeping It Real Podcast
in Chicago. Last weekend you engaged with my post about [topic]
and I wanted to call and follow up. I'm trying to make sure
the stuff I'm writing matches what working agents are seeing
in the field. No agenda - just curious to hear your take.

My number is [your direct number]. I'll try you again
[specific day] morning. Thanks."
```

**Why this works:**

- Identification (D.J. Paris + Keeping It Real Podcast) builds recognition for the next post they see
- Specific reference to the post proves it's not a list call
- "No agenda" disarms the defensive reaction before it forms
- "I'll try you again [day] morning" creates expectation and increases callback rate

**Voicemail length:** 20-25 seconds. No longer.

### Voicemail anti-patterns

- Do not say "I'd love to chat about your career"
- Do not say "I'll try you back at your convenience"
- Do not leave a message longer than 25 seconds
- Do not mention Kale by name in the voicemail

## Objection Handling: Top Five Real-World Responses

### Response 1: "How did you get my number?"

```
"Fair question. You engaged with my LinkedIn post about
[topic] over the weekend, and I looked you up because your
background looked interesting. If you'd rather not talk,
no problem at all - I just wanted to actually hear from
someone who's living what I write about."
```

**Key move:** "If you'd rather not talk, no problem at all" removes the pressure instantly. 80% of askers will keep talking because you've shown you won't push.

### Response 2: "I'm happy where I am" / "I'm not looking to switch"

```
"Totally fair - I'm not calling about that anyway. I'm calling
because you engaged with my post on [topic], and I wanted to
learn what you're seeing in the field. Can I ask you about
[topic]?"
```

**Key move:** Confirm their stance, explicitly remove the threat, redirect to research frame. They will almost always relax and answer the question, because you just promised you weren't recruiting them.

### Response 3: "I don't have time right now"

```
"Got it - I won't keep you. Real quick: I write a weekly
post about [topic] and I'd love to send you the one that
sparked this conversation when you have 60 seconds to look
at it. What's the best way to send it - text or email?"
```

**Key move:** Converted a "no" call into permission to follow up with content. The agent gave you their preferred channel. Now you have a warm follow-up path.

### Response 4: They ask about Kale

```
"Happy to talk about that - but later. Right now I'm just
trying to learn what you're seeing in the field. If we get
to know each other and you're curious about what I do for a
living, that's a different conversation we can have anytime."
```

**Key move:** Counter-intuitive but critical. Slowing down the recruiting conversation builds more trust than racing to it. Agents who ask about Kale on call 1 are usually testing you. Refusing to immediately pitch makes them respect you more.

### Response 5: Hostile / "Take me off your list"

```
"You got it. Sorry to interrupt your day. I won't call again."
```

**Then:** Update Close immediately - set Status to "Lost - Negative Reply" or equivalent. Move on. Do not retry. Do not internalize. Hostile responses are about their day, not your call.

## The Follow-Up Cadence After a Warm Call

Most recruiters fail because they pitch on call 2. This cadence does not.

| Day | Action | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Thanks for the time. Send link to the actual content piece you discussed. | Text or email (whichever they preferred) |
| Day 7-10 | Send another piece of content with a personal note: "Saw this and thought of our conversation about [topic]" | Text or email |
| Day 21-30 | Second call - different reason (new topic, event invite, podcast guest interest) | Phone |
| Day 45-60 | First mention of "if you ever want to hear what we're building at Kale, let me know" | Whatever channel feels right |
| Day 75+ | Direct ask if they're interested in a 30-minute conversation about Kale | Phone or in-person |

This is a 60-90 day relationship cycle. The recruiting conversation happens because the relationship has earned it, not because it was forced.

## What NOT to Say on the First Call

- "I'm with Kale Realty" in the opener (fine to mention later if asked, never lead with it)
- "I want to talk about your career"
- "Are you happy at your current brokerage?"
- "I can offer you a better split"
- "Have you ever considered making a change?"
- "I'd love to grab coffee" (reads as recruiting)
- Anything about money, splits, fees, or commission structure

These all kill the research frame and turn the call into a sales pitch.

## Authenticity Check

These scripts are templates. Make them your voice. If "Got 60 seconds?" does not sound like you, change it. If "I'd love to know what you're seeing" feels too formal, soften it. The structural insight (research frame, not recruiting frame) is what matters. The exact words are yours to adapt.

Every line in these scripts is 100% true for D.J. He does write content for working agents. He is trying to make it grounded in what they are seeing. He is genuinely curious about their take. None of this is manipulation. It is an honest opener for an honest relationship that may or may not become a recruiting conversation later.

If a call ever feels like a performance, hang up. Start over. Authentic conversations beat scripted ones every time.

## Logging the Call in Close

After every call (answered or voicemail), update Close immediately:

1. **Last Contact Date** - Close auto-updates this when you log a call
2. **Latest Source** - leave as-is unless they re-engaged from a different channel
3. **Notes** - write 2-3 sentences capturing:
 - What they said about [topic]
 - Any pain points they mentioned about their current brokerage (even casually)
 - Whether they were warm/neutral/cold
 - What you committed to send them as follow-up
4. **Next Action** - set to specific date for the next touch (per the cadence table above)
5. **DJ Outreach Status** - usually stays "DJ Working" unless you're handing off to Ana

The notes field is the most important. Three sentences after every call. Future-you (or Ana) will need this context for the next touch.

## Metrics to Track Per Call Block

Log these in a simple spreadsheet or Notion page after every dial block:

- **Dials attempted** (target: 20)
- **Connections** (target: 4-7 actual conversations from 20 dials = 20-35% pickup on warm calls)
- **Voicemails left** (typical: 12-15)
- **Conversations that earned a "yes, send me that"** (target: 3-5 from the 4-7 connections)
- **Hostile or negative replies** (target: under 1 per block - if higher, the warm list is wrong, not the script)

If pickup rate is below 20%, the issue is calling time of day (try different windows). If pickup rate is above 35%, the warm list is excellent - double down on whatever sourced it. If the "yes, send me that" rate is below 50% of conversations, the script is too pitchy and needs softening.
