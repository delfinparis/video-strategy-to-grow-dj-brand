# 90-Day Pivot Review — Apr 20 to Jul 18, 2026

**Written:** 2026-07-21 (two days late; the review was due Jul 19 and had not been done).
**Reviews:** the pillar pivot decided in [`../strategy/2026-04-18-pillar-pivot-decision.md`](../strategy/2026-04-18-pillar-pivot-decision.md).
**Verdict:** keep the content, do not reverse the pivot, add the capture layer it was missing. Full forward plan: [`../strategy/2026-07-21-goal-reset-and-gate-layer.md`](../strategy/2026-07-21-goal-reset-and-gate-layer.md).

---

## The test that was run

On Apr 18 the strategy pivoted short-form content to Inside the Industry (news-first, LinkedIn-primary, no engagement asks) as the primary pillar, with a 90-day window and five success criteria. This is the scoring of those criteria.

## Scorecard against the April success criteria

| # | Criterion | Result | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LI Personal engagement rate rises toward 2%+ | Held; IIR content engages well where it reaches | ✅ soft |
| 2 | IIR cross-surface reach averages >2,000 views/script | Organic ~1,000-1,600/script; the 2K+ posts were traced to **paid** Zillow/NAR pushes, not organic | ⚠️ mixed |
| 3 | Follower count flat or positive on 6 surfaces | Flat to modestly positive | ✅ |
| 4 | 3+ industry/sponsor conversations from sharper positioning | Not demonstrably attributable | ❌ |
| 5 | 1+ recruiting conversation attributable to IIR content | **Zero.** D.J. confirmed directly | ❌ |

Two of five passed, both of them engagement/vanity metrics. The two outcome criteria (4 and 5) failed. Criterion 2 failed once the paid pushes were stripped out (see the [2026-06-22 snapshot](../../data/metrics/2026-06-22-snapshot.md): two "viral" posts were Zillow-promoted and the LinkedIn spike was NAR-pushed; there is no validated organic-viral formula).

**A goal set that lets you pass on vanity while failing on outcomes is the real finding here.** The pivot was scored on the wrong things.

## What actually happened

The content machine works. Every post reaches a few hundred to low thousands per surface organically, production quality is high, and the editorial system is sound. The pivot even aged *into* the 2026 LinkedIn algorithm (news-first, topical, no-bait all independently rewarded — see the [June stress test](2026-06-14-short-form-stress-test.md)).

And it produced zero recruiting conversations in 90 days.

## Root cause: no capture layer

The failure is not the content. It is that **no post in 90 days asked a viewer to raise a hand.** Every pillar terminated in a follow. Content produced followers because followers were the only thing it was structurally able to produce.

Supporting evidence:

- The one capture mechanism that exists (tapthis.co email capture) has produced **one** real lead ever; Close holds 4 records from it, 3 of them D.J.'s own tests. It also writes to the wrong Close field (Lead Type instead of Kale Lead Source).
- The primary goal was **unmeasurable** the entire window: roster syncs and genuine recruits share the "Kale Agent" status in Close, so recruits-from-content cannot be counted.
- Cold outbound produced 249 leads in a comparable 30-day window. Content produced ~1. The gap is not a content-quality gap; it is a capture gap.

## Decision

**Keep the content. Do not reverse the pivot. Add the gate.**

The pivot bet the right content and won on reach. It simply had no mechanism to convert reach into a lead. The forward plan keeps everything that reached people (news, podcast promos, the production rigor) and adds:

1. A gate layer (ManyChat comment-to-DM, IG/FB) on offer content, routing Chicago → Kale and national → KIRP/tools.
2. A refined 5-pillar structure with a dedicated lead engine (Giveaways) and a recruiting-wedge pillar (Broker Problems).
3. An intent qualifier in the DM so captured leads are sorted by recruitability, not just geography.
4. Instrumentation so the primary goal becomes measurable (a Brokerage Pain field; a fixed Close source scheme; separating real recruits from roster syncs).

Details and the council review that hardened the plan: the [2026-07-21 goal-reset doc](../strategy/2026-07-21-goal-reset-and-gate-layer.md).

## The lesson worth keeping

Do not score a content strategy on engagement when the goal is leads. This review exists because the April criteria were 3 parts vanity, 2 parts outcome, and the vanity parts passed loudly enough to hide that the outcome parts had flatlined. The next review (week 4 of the gate layer) scores comment-to-lead conversion, the Illinois split, and qualified Chicago leads — the things that actually predict a recruit.
