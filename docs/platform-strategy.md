# Platform Strategy

This document defines which platforms matter most for D.J. Paris's brand goals, why, and how content should be distributed across them. It is grounded in the April 2026 Metricool baseline (D.J.'s own audience data, still the source of truth for the platform ranking below) and layered with the mid-2026 industry refresh. Updated quarterly.

---

## Mid-2026 Algorithm Refresh (July 2026)

D.J.'s own April baseline still sets the platform priority. What changed since then is *what each algorithm rewards* and *how the platforms are trending industry-wide*. Sources: Metricool 2026 Social Media Study (~40M posts) and 2026 Instagram Study (24M+ posts), Buffer 2026 (52M+ posts), Hootsuite 2026, Mosseri/Instagram. All are population averages, not D.J.'s audience, so treat them as starting points and A/B test.

**The signal stack that now governs every platform** (in order of weight):

1. **Watch time / completion + getting past the 3-second mark.** The single heaviest signal on Reels, Shorts, TikTok, and Facebook video. A hook that holds past 3 seconds is what triggers the wider push. This is why the spoken hook and the 15-second re-hook are the highest-leverage things in the script. **As of the 2026 data, completion is only half of it:** every platform now weights **total watch time per viewer** alongside completion percentage, which is why Rule 7 moved the runtime to 30-35s on 2026-08-25. The number we solve for is 25-30 seconds actually watched, not a percentage. A very short cut wins the completion stat and starves the distribution signal -- Socialinsider's Jan-Jun 2026 study of 6M Reels found sub-30s Reels underperform on both engagement rate and median views.
2. **Sends / shares to DM.** Weighted ~3-5x a like on Instagram; the top distribution signal on both IG and Facebook. Design for "would one agent DM this to another agent."
3. **Saves.** The engagement layer carousels are built for (see the carousel strategy doc).
4. **Likes and follower count are weak signals.** Up to ~50% of the Facebook feed and most Reels reach is now non-followers. Chasing follower count is chasing the wrong number.

**Platform-level trend since early 2026 (industry-wide, Metricool/Buffer 2026):**

- **Facebook is the surprise winner:** reach +51%, impressions +57%, interactions +56% YoY. Reels are shown to non-followers *first* to test. This reinforces Facebook as a genuine #2, not a cross-post afterthought.
- **Instagram Reels reach fell ~35% YoY** (algorithmic overcrowding), but Reels engagement is the only format still *growing* (+24.76%) and shares are +67%. Fewer people reached, but the send is the escape hatch. Carousels get ~9x the saves of single images. Reels for reach, carousels for saves.
- **LinkedIn got harder:** impressions -23%, interactions -14% YoY (B2B overcrowding). Native vertical video is the biggest open lane, watched ~80% sound-off, so burned-in captions are mandatory. Put any link in the first comment, never the post body.
- **YouTube is the health story:** views per video +30% YoY. The big mechanical change: **Shorts flipped from swipe-rate to watch-time-per-impression** as the primary signal, so Shorts now rank on retention like main-feed YouTube. Shorts also cross-pollinate with long-form, so they can feed the 700-episode podcast play.
- **TikTok raised the bar:** completion + rewatch are the two heaviest signals and the virality threshold moved to ~70% completion. Still secondary for D.J. (national reach, least-aligned audience), and it demands native, TikTok-first creation. Never cross-post a watermarked Reel here.

The full myth-busting companion to this refresh is [`myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md`](myths-that-dont-move-the-needle.md).

1. **Kale Realty recruiting in Chicago**
2. **NAR relationship deepening**
3. **Keeping It Real Podcast subscriber growth**
4. **National thought leader reach**

Platform priority is calculated against these goals. A platform is high-priority if it serves goals 1-2 well, medium-priority if it serves goals 3-4, and low-priority if it serves none of them with concentrated value.

## Account Inventory

D.J. operates two account systems: **personal accounts** (D.J. Paris) and **brand accounts** (Keeping It Real Podcast). They have dramatically different audiences and serve different purposes.

**This is the most important strategic distinction in the entire content plan.** Personal accounts matter more for Kale recruiting because brokers vet the human, not the brand. Brand accounts matter more for podcast growth because podcast discovery happens through brand handles.

## Why Personal Instagram Is #3

**Performance signal:**

- 7,569 followers (largest of any account)
- 16,304 views in 30 days
- 6,758 accounts reached
- **72.2% of views from non-followers** (the Reels algorithm is actively distributing the account)

- Most active follower hours: 7pm-10pm (peak ~9pm)

**Why it's #3 not higher:**

Personal Instagram has the highest raw scale, but the audience demographic is broader than Chicago and the engagement is more passive than LinkedIn. The reach is real, but the conversion path to Kale recruiting is less direct than LinkedIn.

**Implication for content:**

Personal Instagram is Reels-first. Every Playbook and every Inside the Industry script gets a Reel adaptation. Post timing should target the 7-10pm window. The 72.2% non-follower reach means we should optimize for hooks that stop the scroll for cold audiences, not just for D.J.'s existing followers.

## Why YouTube Is #4

**Account:** Keeping It Real Podcast (brand)

**Performance signal:**

- 2,440 subscribers (largest brand account)
- 3,538 views on most-viewed videos
- 26 likes, 3 comments on top videos

**The Bartlett opportunity:**

D.J. has 700+ podcast episodes recorded. YouTube is where Steven Bartlett built Diary of a CEO into 4.8M TikTok followers and millions of YouTube subscribers by clipping the most explosive 45-90 seconds of each interview as a standalone Short. The 700-episode archive is sitting unleveraged.

**Implication for content:**

YouTube needs a dedicated long-form clip strategy that mines the podcast archive for Bartlett-style 5-12 minute clips, posted to the YouTube main channel, with Shorts as marketing trailers. This is a separate workstream from the four content pillars and should be prioritized in Q3 2026.

## Why Brand LinkedIn Is #5 (Not #1)

The Brand LinkedIn account (Keeping It Real Podcast) has 413 followers, perfectly qualified by industry and geography (Greater Chicago, Real Estate, senior). On paper, that's the right audience.

In practice, it's 17x smaller than D.J.'s Personal LinkedIn (7,001 followers). The same audience exists at much greater scale on Personal LinkedIn.

**Implication:** Personal LinkedIn carries the Inside the Industry strategy. Brand LinkedIn becomes a cross-post destination, not a primary publishing target.

## Distribution Cascade by Pillar

The same script runs on multiple platforms but with different primary surfaces depending on content type. This is the cascade by pillar:

### Pillar 1: AI Agent Minute

**Primary:** Personal Instagram (Reels), Personal Facebook
**Secondary:** Personal LinkedIn, brand accounts as cross-posts
**Reasoning:** AI tutorial content works well on Reels and Facebook. LinkedIn cares less about how-to videos.

### Pillar 2: Agent Tip of the Day

**Primary:** Personal Instagram (Reels), brand accounts (because the podcast association is built in)
**Secondary:** Personal LinkedIn, Personal Facebook
**Reasoning:** Guest-driven content reinforces the podcast brand and works across personal and brand accounts.

### Pillar 3: The Playbook

**Primary:** Personal Instagram (Reels), Personal Facebook
**Secondary:** Personal LinkedIn (as tactical posts)
**Reasoning:** Tactical "do this" content is the most shareable format. Personal Facebook (74.2% Chicago) is the recruiting goldmine. Instagram Reels distribute beyond followers.

### Pillar 4: Inside the Industry

**Primary:** Personal LinkedIn (canonical text post)
**Secondary:** Personal Facebook, Personal Instagram, brand accounts
**Reasoning:** Inside the Industry is the only pillar that wins specifically on LinkedIn for D.J.'s audience. The senior decision-makers in his LinkedIn audience reward sharp opinions and insider access more than any other platform.

## Posting Time Recommendations

**Posting time is a weak factor, not the secret sauce** (Buffer 2026). It's worth getting in the right window, but a strong hook and high completion move reach far more than an exact minute. D.J.'s *own* audience data (below) beats any population average, so these are starting points to test, not rules.

**Personal Instagram:** D.J.'s followers peak 7pm-10pm (9pm strongest) per his own insights. Note the conflict: Buffer 2026 finds a morning + 6pm surge industry-wide, Metricool finds a 7-9pm global peak. His own evening data wins until a test says otherwise. (Metricool: ~70% of a post's views land in the first 72 hours, so the first-day window matters more than the exact hour.)
**Personal LinkedIn:** His baseline is 7am-9am Tue-Thu. **Sources now conflict:** Buffer's 2026 (~5M-post) data says afternoons beat mornings (Wed/Fri 3-4pm), while the older Hootsuite model holds Tue-Wed early AM. Genuinely unsettled. Test a morning slot vs a 3-4pm slot on his account and let the data decide. Keep at least 12 hours between LinkedIn posts (a second same-day post cannibalizes the first).
**Personal Facebook:** Varies by content; tactical content performs at lunchtime, opinion content performs evenings. Buffer 2026 industry data leans mornings (Thu 9am, Wed-Thu 9-11am).
**YouTube:** Shorts and long-form want *different* slots (Buffer 2026). Shorts perform evenings (weekday 6-11pm, Fri ~4pm); long-form performs weekend/weekday mornings (8-11am, Sun ~10am). Schedule them separately.

These blend D.J.'s April 2026 baseline with the mid-2026 industry data. Retest quarterly, and trust his own account data over any average.

## Decisions This Strategy Forces

1. **Stop optimizing brand accounts as a primary play.** Personal accounts have larger, more qualified audiences for D.J.'s actual goals.
2. **Stop posting the same generic content on every platform.** Each pillar has a primary platform and the script files include platform-specific adaptations.
3. **Stop chasing follower counts.** Personal LinkedIn at 7,001 followers with a hyper-targeted audience is more valuable than 30,000 generic followers anywhere else.
4. **Start mining the YouTube long-form opportunity.** The 700-episode archive is sitting unused. This is a Q3 priority that should be planned in May.
5. **Start treating Personal Facebook as a recruiting asset, not a personal life journal.** 74.2% Chicago concentration means every post is a potential recruiting touch.
