# AI Tells: Field Guide for Editors and Drafters

A comprehensive reference of patterns that signal AI-generated writing. Used as the master scrub list across every script, social caption, and published surface in this repo.

**Read this before:** drafting any social caption, polishing any spoken script, or reviewing any draft for publication.

**The principle:** Each item on this list is a real rhetorical or vocabulary tool that *can work* once, in the right context. What makes it an AI tell is *stacking and repetition*. A human writer might use one of these patterns occasionally. AI uses them in every paragraph. The discipline isn't to banish every rhetorical move; it's to prevent any one of them from becoming a stacked signature across a script.

**Sourcing:** This list merges the project's own editorial standard (`docs/editorial-standards.md` Rule 5) with curated AI-detection guidance from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" reference page, the chrisgherbert ChatGPT overused words gist, multiple writer/editor opinion pieces, and academic linguistic studies. Full source list at the bottom.

## PART 1: Vocabulary — Words to Cut

### Banned adjectives (high-frequency AI markers)

compelling, insightful, noteworthy, profound, transformative, nuanced, multifaceted, intricate, enlightening, commendable, vital, essential, robust, cutting-edge, pivotal, impactful, holistic, seamless, comprehensive, unique blend, unique perspective, unique opportunity, vibrant, dynamic, bustling, whimsical, gossamer, meticulous, meticulously, daunting, indelible, virtuoso, exemplified, akin, arguably, undoubtedly, enduring

### Banned verbs (the "AI verb pile")

delve, dive into, deep dive, drill down, embark, journey, navigate, unveil, uncover, unlock, illuminate, shed light, harness, leverage (non-financial sense), spearhead, championing, foster, bolster, empower, elevate, supercharge, revolutionize, level up, align with, enhance, fostering, highlighting, showcasing, garner, transcend, reverberate, resonate, emphasizing

### Banned metaphorical / abstract nouns

journey, quest, realm, labyrinth, tapestry, landscape, symphony, crucible, treasure trove, beacon, game changer, sea change, paradigm shift, gold standard, testament (as in "a testament to..."), interplay, new era, new chapter, new frontier, hustle and bustle, camaraderie, enigma

### Banned hedging / emphasis markers

It's worth noting that, Important to note, Important to understand, Remember that, Worth pointing out, Worth knowing, Certainly, Indeed, Notably, Crucially, Significantly, It's no secret that, As you may know, As we all know, Little did

### Banned transition phrases

Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, However (when leaning on it), Nonetheless, That being said, Therefore, Ultimately, In conclusion, In summary, To sum up, All in all, At the end of the day, In today's world, In today's fast-paced world, Firstly, Subsequently, Finally, Let's face it, Let me clarify, To put it simply, Fear not

### Banned promotional / travel-guide markers

nestled, vibrant, rich, natural beauty, boasts, showcasing, exemplifying, commitment to, "stands as," "serves as a," "marks the," "represents the," "stands as a testament," "serves as a beacon," "ventured into"

### Banned conclusion / outlook clichés

"Despite challenges, [subject] continues to..."; "Looking ahead..."; "As we move forward..."; "In an ever-changing landscape..."; "[Subject] has come a long way..."; "Only time will tell..."; "Time will tell..."

### Banned "concrete" / "real" intensifiers (paradox: they signal AI)

"Concrete evidence," "concrete examples," "Real talk," "honest truth," "the bottom line is," "At its core," "fundamentally," "essentially," "to put it simply"

### Banned conversational AI fillers

really, basically, kind of, you know, like, right?, hey guys, today I want to talk about, in this video, let me tell you about, so anyway

### Banned brand-buzz vocabulary

game-changer, level up, supercharge, revolutionize, take it to the next level, best practices, circle back, bandwidth, move the needle, double down, actionable insights, unpack (except as slang in spoken context), elevate, synergy, foster

## PART 2: Sentence-Structure Tells

### Negative parallelisms (each banned beyond ONE use per script)

1. **"It's not X. It's Y."** — antithesis-correction
2. **"Not just X. Y."** / **"Not just X, but also Y"** — upgrade construction
3. **"No X, no Y, just Z"** — exclusion-then-reveal
4. **"On one hand X. On the other hand Y."** — forced bilateral
5. **"Either X or Y"** as a forced binary
6. **"Both X and Y"** as a connector

### Rule of Three (overuse)

The Rule of Three is a real rhetorical tool. AI applies it everywhere.

- Triple adjectives in a row ("authoritative, confident, conclusive")
- Three-item lists where the third is the punchline
- "Three things to do," "Three plays," "Three reasons" — when overused as the default structure
- Three-word staccato sentences as emphasis ("Three years. Three states. Done.")

**Rule:** One Rule-of-Three structure per script max. If multiple sections need a count, vary it (two questions, four steps, etc.).

### Uniform sentence length

LLMs default to 15–25-word sentences. Humans alternate dramatically — a three-word sentence next to a forty-word sentence.

**Apply:** Mix sentence lengths intentionally. Read aloud — if every sentence feels the same length, vary.

### Elegant variation / lexical diversity for its own sake

AI substitutes synonyms to avoid repeating words. Humans repeat key terms intentionally.

- *Tell:* "Real estate agents... realtors... brokers... industry professionals..." all in one paragraph
- *Fix:* If the right word is "agents," say "agents" four times. The reader doesn't notice repetition; they notice contortion.

### Stating-then-restating (polysemic redundancy)

Saying the same idea three slightly different ways for "rhythm."

- *Tell:* "Open houses don't generate buyers. The Sunday afternoon isn't producing leads. The data shows this is the wrong outcome."
- *Fix:* Cut to one sentence. If the idea matters, state it once, sharply.

### Avoidance of basic copulatives

AI replaces "is/are" with elaborate constructions: "serves as," "stands as," "marks," "represents," "features," "maintains," "offers."

- *Tell:* "The Zillow study stands as a testament to the cost of dual agency."
- *Fix:* "The Zillow study shows dual agency costs sellers money."

### Symmetrical phrasing / parallel adjective pairs

AI loves mirrored clause framing: "X and Y" pairs that should be one or the other.

- *Tell:* "Bold and decisive," "Sharp and incisive," "Clear and concise"
- *Fix:* Pick one. Or restructure entirely.

## PART 3: Rhetorical / Structural Tells

### The numbered preview

- *Tell:* "Three things to do this week" / "Five plays for listing agents"
- AI uses this in every script. Vary the action layer — sometimes count, sometimes don't. When you do count, vary the number across scripts.

### The "Here's the [adjective] part" reveal

- *Tell:* "Here's the interesting part" / "Here's the wild part" / "Here's what nobody is saying"
- Setup-payoff requires actual surprise after. If what follows is obvious, the setup is hollow.

### Data-as-character personification

- *Tell:* "The data isn't going to change its mind" / "The math is settled" / "The numbers don't lie"
- Once per topic max. Then retire it.

### Setup-payoff with telegraphed beats

- *Tell:* "Here's what I mean" (followed by literal restatement)
- *Tell:* "Here's why this matters" (followed by one-sentence rephrase of what was just said)

### Outline-like conclusions

- *Tell:* "Despite its challenges, [subject] continues to evolve."
- *Tell:* Separate "Challenges," "Future Prospects," "Future Directions" subsections.

### Contrasting parallel structure (overuse)

- *Tell:* "Most agents do X. Top producers do Y."
- One of the most powerful moves in the playbook AND one of the most clearly AI when overused. One per script max.

### "Welcome to X" ironic frame

- *Tell:* "Welcome to monetary policy" / "Welcome to the new real estate"
- Almost always reads as written. Hard to use without it sounding scripted.

### "And that's the [noun]" coda

- *Tell:* "...and that's the math" / "...and that's the problem" / "...and that's the system"
- The stuck-on closer that adds nothing.

### Aphoristic life-advice closes

- *Tell:* "The Sunday morning that builds the next four years of your business"
- Each script closing on a mentor-poster line is an AI signature.

### Tech aphorisms outside tech contexts

- *Tell:* "It's not a bug, it's a feature" — used about something non-technical
- *Tell:* "Move fast and break things" used outside startup context

### Performative collaborative language

- *Tell:* "I appreciate the feedback" / "Let me clarify" / "Happy to discuss further"
- Reads as polite AI assistant, not a writer.

### Vague attribution / weasel words

- *Tell:* "Experts say..." / "Studies show..." / "Researchers believe..." / "Industry observers..." / "Critics argue..."
- Either name the source or cut the claim.

### Self-answered rhetorical questions

- *Tell:* "Why does this matter? Because..."
- Either state the answer directly or pose the question and let it sit a beat.

### Present participle constructions appended to sentences

- *Tell:* "The market shifted, reflecting broader trends in housing affordability."
- AI tends to add "-ing" trailers to elaborate on ideas. Often empty.

### "Imagine this" / "Picture this" false-engagement setup

- Rarely earns the next sentence. Cut.

### Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers

- *Tell:* "As of my last training data..." / "I may not be aware of the most recent..."
- Never in published content.

### Triple-word repetition for emphasis

- *Tell:* "Every year. Every state. Every transaction."
- Strong rhetorical move. AI uses it in 70% of takes. Reserve.

### "X is just Y" reduction

- *Tell:* "Sphere outreach is just discipline" / "Listing presentations are just marketing"
- Often a reductive setup. Use sparingly.

## PART 4: Punctuation & Formatting Tells

### Em dashes (—) — banned anywhere in this repo

The single most-recognized AI tell. AI inserts em dashes where commas, periods, or restructured sentences would do.

- **Spoken scripts:** use `--` (two hyphens) as substitute IF needed
- **Social captions:** zero em dashes AND zero `--`. Restructure the sentence
- **Editorial scaffolding (Data Source, Production Notes):** `--` permitted

### Smart/curly quotes (" " ' ') instead of straight (" ' )

AI defaults to curly. Some platforms render them inconsistently. Stick with straight.

### Colons before short lists in social captions

- *Tell:* "Three things: focus, follow-up, framing."
- *Fix:* Use a period and a new sentence per item. Or restructure inline.

### Ellipses for stylistic effect (...)

- *Tell:* "It's almost like... they don't know what they're doing."
- *Fix:* Write the trailing thought in words or cut to a period.

### ALL CAPS for emphasis

Restructure so emphasis falls naturally when spoken or read.

### Excessive boldface

Mechanical, repeated bolding of key terms. **Bolding** every "important" phrase signals AI summarization. Reserve bold for genuine emphasis.

### Inline-header vertical lists in prose

- *Tell:* Bullets followed by bold-with-colon headers, every item
- *Fix:* Use clean prose. Lists are for genuinely list-like content (three actions, six platforms), not for elaborating on prose.

### Title Case in headings

AI tends to Title Case Every Word. Repo convention is sentence case for body headings; reserve title case for the main script title.

### Skipping heading levels

Going from H2 to H4 without H3. Maintain hierarchy.

### Excessive horizontal rules (thematic breaks)

AI places `---` between every section. Use sparingly.

### Markdown emphasis where bold/italic isn't needed

AI tends to *italicize* phrases and **bold** phrases inside sentences. Most platforms strip this anyway. Reserve for true emphasis.

## PART 5: Tone & Content Tells

### Excessive positivity / no negativity

AI is trained to be positive-leaning. Humans criticize, complain, contradict.

**Apply:** Every script should have at least one moment of genuine critique, contradiction, or "this is wrong" energy. If a script reads as relentlessly upbeat, something's off.

### Overstatement of significance

- *Tell:* "This represents a watershed moment for the industry"
- *Tell:* "Reflects broader trends in real estate"
- Specifics beat significance claims every time.

### Generic claims without specific evidence

- *Tell:* "Real estate is changing rapidly"
- *Fix:* Name what's changing, when, who reported it.

### Listing sources to prove notability

- *Tell:* "As covered by [outlet], [outlet], and [outlet]..."
- *Fix:* Cite once where it lands, not as a credibility parade.

### Cautious / uncertainty-marking language

AI hedges constantly: "may," "could," "potentially," "tends to," "in some cases."

- *Apply:* Pick a position and own it. Hedge only when the claim genuinely requires it (e.g., predictions about future regulation).

### Adjective stacks for vibe instead of specificity

- *Tell:* "Bold, decisive, transformative leadership"
- *Fix:* Show the leadership through specifics. Cut the adjective stack.

## PART 6: The "Used Once" Rule (sparingly-allowed patterns)

Several patterns above are listed as "use once per script max." This is the editorial discipline that separates one human-tier use from a stacked AI tell. The pattern itself is fine. Stacking the pattern is the problem.

Patterns governed by the Used Once rule:

- **"It's not X. It's Y."** antithesis-correction
- **"Not just X. Y."** upgrade construction
- **Rule of Three** anywhere (lists, adjective stacks, staccato sentences)
- **Contrasting parallel structure** ("Most agents do X. Top producers do Y.")
- **Data-as-character personification** ("The data isn't going to change its mind")
- **Three-word staccato emphasis** ("Three years. Three states. Done.")
- **Aphoristic life-advice closes**
- **Triple-word repetition** ("Every year. Every state. Every transaction.")
- **"Here's the [adjective] part" reveal**

When in doubt: choose one pattern per script, deploy it where it lands hardest, retire it for the rest of the script.

## PART 7: Application to This Repo's Pipelines

### Spoken scripts (talking-head)

- Vocabulary bans: full list applies
- Sentence-structure bans: full list applies
- Punctuation: em dashes banned entirely (substitute `--` permitted in editorial scaffolding only)
- "Used Once" rule: strictly enforced per script

### Social captions (LinkedIn, IG, TikTok, FB, X, Threads, YouTube descriptions)

- Vocabulary bans: full list applies AND tighter (no "Worth knowing," no "Here's why that matters," no "Here is what this means")
- Punctuation: zero em dashes AND zero `--`. Restructure
- AI transition phrases: zero tolerance
- Apply at draft time, not as follow-up scrub

### Editorial scaffolding (WOW gate, Data Source, Production Notes, Length Justification)

- Vocabulary bans apply but more leniently — this is internal documentation
- `--` em-dash substitute is permitted because these sections are not customer-facing
- Bullet lists with bolded headers are acceptable

## PART 8: Pre-Commit Self-Audit Checklist

Before committing any draft to the repo, run this audit:

- [ ] Read every social caption for em dashes (`—` or `--`). If found, restructure
- [ ] Count "It's not X, it's Y" / "Not just X, but Y" constructions. Max one per script
- [ ] Count Rule-of-Three constructions (triple lists + triple adjectives + triple-word staccato). Max one per script
- [ ] Scan against the vocabulary lists in Parts 1
- [ ] Read the close aloud. If it sounds like a motivational poster, rewrite
- [ ] Verify no script ends every section the same way (parallel close fatigue)
- [ ] Check the action layer count — if every recent script has "Three plays," vary it
- [ ] Scan for self-answered rhetorical questions
- [ ] Verify hedging is intentional, not reflexive
