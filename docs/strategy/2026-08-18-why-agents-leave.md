# Why Agents Leave: the Broker Problems lane, defined

Decision doc, 2026-08-18. This fills the hole under Pillar 3.

Broker Problems has been on the calendar since the 2026-07-21 goal reset at 2 videos a week,
Tuesday and Thursday. [`master-calendar.md`](../../schedule/master-calendar.md) names its
source as the "brokerage-pain bank." **That file did not exist.** Neither did a series
standard or a single video script -- one carousel had been built against the pillar and
nothing else. Every other mature lane in this repo (The Take, Stupid Things, What Actually
Works) runs on a bank plus a standard plus a scripts folder. This lane had a slot.

This document is the strategy. The bank is [`../../data/brokerage-pain.md`](../../data/brokerage-pain.md).
The build rules are [`../series/broker-problems-standard.md`](../series/broker-problems-standard.md).

---

## The reframe, and why the old definition was wrong

[`content-pillars.md`](../content-pillars.md) defines the pillar as "one specific broker-side
pain: bad splits, junk fees, no support, dead company leads, no training, no tech. Name the
math, name the fix."

That is a **comparison** frame, and it assumes the viewer is shopping. The evidence says they
are not:

- **Relitix Agent Movement Index closed 2025 at 96.0** on the trailing twelve months, under
  the 100 baseline for more than a year. Agent mobility is at a multi-year low.
- **NAR's 2025 Member Profile puts median tenure with the current firm at six years,** with
  28% at twelve years or more.
- **Mike DelPrete's August 2025 study interviewed ten agents who had actually left** within
  the prior six months. What drove them out was not the split. It was leadership
  inaccessibility, technology that ate their week, and the absence of belonging. One line
  from that study is the whole lane in nine words: *"I might have only communicated with my
  broker 10 times in 13 years."*
- **NAR Magazine, 2026:** "compensation splits, technology, and recruiting pitches were not
  lead factors in retention."

So the audience is not the agent comparing two fee schedules. It is the agent who has been
somewhere six years, is quietly annoyed about eleven different things, and has normalized
every one of them. A fee comparison does not reach that person. It reads as an ad, and it
walks straight into the objection map's losing argument -- "we're cheaper" -- which
[`recruiting-objections.md`](../recruiting-objections.md) already established loses to the
number-one fear that cheap means abandoned.

### The theory of the lane: departure is an accumulation, not a decision

Nobody leaves over one thing. They leave after the twentieth small thing, on the week one of
them lands badly. D.J.'s framing when he asked for this lane was **"chip away,"** and that is
the correct mechanic, so it is now the stated theory rather than a vibe.

**The job of a single Broker Problem video is not to make anyone leave.** It is to move one
normalized annoyance from background to foreground, and to get D.J. named in that agent's
head as the person who said it out loud. Thirty of those over a year is what a departure is
made of. One of them is not.

## The actual objective: seed the doubt, not the destination

Added 2026-08-18 after D.J. clarified the goal. **The lane exists to make agents explore other
options.** Not to make them join Kale, and not merely to make them feel seen. Explore.

That distinction matters because the first version of this document optimized for
**recognition** and stopped there, and recognition on its own produces a *resigned* agent
rather than an exploring one. An agent can fully agree that their reviews are not theirs and
still never open a browser tab, because the thing blocking exploration was never ignorance.
It is two beliefs already documented in [`../recruiting-objections.md`](../recruiting-objections.md):
switching means starting over (objection #2), and, underneath it, that looking around is a
small disloyalty.

### The doubt that works is not the doubt you would guess

"My brokerage is bad" is a weak seed. It is arguable, it is insulting to six years of the
agent's own judgment, and defending their choice is cheaper for them than reconsidering it.
Sunk cost and identity both push back.

**"I have never once checked whether this is normal" is the strong seed.** It is
undefendable, because it is a statement about the agent's own behavior and it is true. It
carries no accusation against their firm and no admission that they were foolish. And the
resolution to it is a single low-cost act -- looking -- which is the exact behavior the lane
is trying to produce. The agent who looks has already started exploring, and D.J. never
mentioned an alternative.

**So the seed is always ignorance of the market, never inadequacy of the firm.**

### The doubt ladder

The close is not one rule, it is three rungs, and the lane climbs them over months rather
than inside a single video. A script declares its rung in frontmatter.

| Rung | The close asks for | What it seeds | Cost to the agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Look at yours** | check your own situation | "I didn't know that about my own business" | none |
| **2. Find out what normal is** | check what the rest of the market does | **"I have no idea if my deal is good"** | none |
| **3. Have one conversation** | ask your broker, or ask anyone | "this is a thing I'm allowed to ask about" | small, social |

**Rung 2 is the one that was missing and it is the engine of the whole lane.** Most agents
genuinely do not know what other firms charge, provide, or answer for. Telling someone to go
find out what normal is costs D.J. nothing, names nobody, cannot be read as a pitch, and is
the literal definition of exploring other options. It is the closest thing to a free lunch in
this entire content system.

Rung 3 is the highest-intent rung and the rarest. Use it sparingly -- roughly one script in
four -- because an ask that requires a social act fails more often, and a lane that keeps
asking for hard things trains the viewer to ignore the ask.

### What is still banned, and why it has not loosened

**"Go leave" is still banned, and so is "there are better options."** This is not softness, it
is sequencing. A 55-second video cannot beat six years of sunk cost, and an agent who feels
pushed defends their firm out loud, which converts a doubt into a public commitment against
the doubt. That is worse than doing nothing.

**Kale is still never mentioned.** Unchanged and load-bearing. The moment an alternative is
named, the doubt is re-attributed to the person who benefits from it, and the agent discards
it as a sales technique. **The doubt has to be theirs.** Naming the destination is the single
fastest way to make it not theirs.

The seed is planted in public, ungated, with nothing attached. It gets watered in the DM.

## The per-video success test

> **Rung 1 and 2:** did a Chicago agent recognize themselves in the comments, or say out loud
> that they do not know their own number?
> **Rung 3:** did anyone report back that they asked?

The second half of that first test is new and it is the better signal. "That's my brokerage"
is recognition. **"Now I want to know what mine is" is doubt**, and it is the comment Jennica
should be flagging first.

## The four flavors

The lane rotates four sub-types so it does not collapse into "fees, fees, fees." Each has a
different emotional job, a different hook family, and a different evidence requirement.

| Flavor | The feeling | Hook family | Evidence | Heat | Usual rung |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Slow Leak** | "wait, that adds up to *what*?" | 6 (Named Stakes), 4 (System Indictment) | A real number, converted to an annual figure | 3.5 | 1 or 2 |
| **The Thing That Doesn't Make Sense** | "why *is* that, actually?" | 4 (System Indictment), 7 (The Forbidden) | An arrangement whose only explanation is that it benefits someone else | 3.5, occasionally 4 | 2 or 3 |
| **The Silence** | "that happened to me" | 1 (The Mirror), 5 (The Confession) | A scene, not a stat | 3 | 1 |
| **The Permission Slip** | "wait, am I allowed to?" | 5 (The Confession), 3 (The Defector) | D.J.'s own position, stated plainly | 3 | 2 or 3 |

**The Slow Leak.** A cost small enough that it never triggers a decision. The discipline is
that it is always sized to a year and to a thing the agent would have bought with the money.
$45 a month is nothing. $540 is a conference. Never run this flavor without the annualization
-- the monthly number is precisely why nobody has ever reacted to it.

**The Thing That Doesn't Make Sense.** D.J.'s own words when he asked for this lane, and the
highest-comment flavor of the three, because the viewer resolves the tension by answering the
question themselves in the reply box. The test is strict: it only qualifies if a reasonable
person cannot construct a defensible reason for the policy that serves the agent. If there is
a good reason and the agent just does not know it, that is a Value Giveaway explaining the
reason, not a Broker Problem.

**The Silence.** The moment you needed your broker and nobody picked up. This is the flavor
that converts, because abandonment is objection #1 in the map -- the highest blocking power
on the way *in* to Kale -- and DelPrete's interviews say it is also the leading driver on the
way *out* of wherever they are. It is a scene lane, not a stat lane. It runs at heat 3
because it does not need heat; recognition does the work.

**The Permission Slip.** Added 2026-08-18 with the doubt ladder, and the flavor most directly
responsible for the objective. The other three produce a doubt; this one makes acting on it
feel allowed. Agents do not explore because exploring feels like a small betrayal of people
who have been decent to them, and nobody has ever told them otherwise. Somebody has to say out
loud that looking is normal, professional, and not disloyalty -- and the only person whose
saying it carries weight is somebody who runs a brokerage and would lose from it.

That is why **D.J. is the only person who can make this flavor work**, and why it is the one
place his credential is load-bearing rather than promotional (Family 3, The Defector). "I run
a brokerage. My agents should be looking too, and here's why that doesn't scare me" is a
position, not a pitch, and it is the single highest-trust thing in the whole lane.

It runs at heat 3, roughly **once a month**, in Rule 10.7's emotional/identity register. The
payoff is a reframe, not a tactic. **The one failure mode that kills it:** if it resolves to
"and that's why you should look at Kale," it stops being permission and becomes the pitch it
was pretending not to be, and the viewer re-reads the previous four weeks of the lane as a
funnel. That would cost more than the video gains.

**Rotation:** never two of the same flavor in a week, and never the same flavor on
consecutive posts across weeks. Silence runs at least twice a month -- it is the one that
recruits, and it is the one a fee-obsessed lane will quietly stop making. Permission Slip runs
about monthly. Across any four-week block, at least half the closes should sit on **rung 2**;
a lane sitting entirely on rung 1 is producing recognition and calling it doubt.

## Four hard rules

**1. Never gated, on any platform.** Unchanged from the pillar definition, and D.J.'s own
Facebook data is the reason: overt Kale content runs -5.3x, webinar promo -10.5x. A keyword
ask on a broker-problem video costs reach and reads as the pitch it is trying not to be.

**2. Never pitch Kale on camera. Not at the end, not in one line, not "at my brokerage we."**
This is the new rule and it is the one most likely to get broken. The instant the video
resolves to Kale, two things die: reach (see above), and the comment mechanic -- nobody outs
their own brokerage underneath a competitor's advertisement. **The video's job is to be right,
not to be Kale.** The Kale answer exists; it lives in the DM Ana sends after Jennica flags the
commenter. Sequence matters: be right in public, be Kale in private.

**3. Heat 3.5 default, heat 5 banned permanently.** Same floor as the take lane and for the
same reason -- D.J. runs a 769-agent brokerage and is on the record in trade press. No named
brokerage, franchise, coach, or product, and the rule is "no identifiable target," not "no
name typed." A knowing wink carries the same exposure with less punch. Go maximally specific
about the *practice* while the target stays a category. The Rule 9.2 heat-4 budget is one post
per week across the entire schedule and the Wednesday take already spends it, so a Broker
Problem only runs at 4 in a week the take lane gives it up.

**4. Comments come from Rule 10.4, never from an ask.** Rule 4 bans engagement asks, and the
platform research backs it independently -- Instagram will not recommend content that
"explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags, or other actions." The two
10.4 devices that fit this lane:

- **Intentional incompleteness.** Name four fees and fully unpack three. The fourth arrives in
  the replies, supplied by agents, which is the recruiting signal arriving for free.
- **The defensible line that recruits defenders.** A heat-3.5 claim specific enough that the
  agents it is true for feel compelled to confirm it against the agents who say it is not.

Both produce the comment without directing it. If a line directs, it is dead.

## What this lane is not

**Against The Take:** the take points at a *practice, belief, or incentive* anywhere in the
business and requires the who-profits beat. Broker Problems points at the *arrangement between
an agent and their brokerage*. When a candidate is really about splits, fees, or support, it
runs here and the take slot stays free for prospecting, marketing, and money beliefs. That
boundary is already written into [`take-standard.md`](../series/take-standard.md) and this
document does not move it.

**Against the kale-signaling scripts.** Those eight scripts in
[`../../scripts/kale-signaling/`](../../scripts/kale-signaling/) answer objections to joining
Kale, and they say Kale out loud. They are a different job for a different moment -- the agent
who is already considering it. Broker Problems is upstream of that by months. Do not merge
them, and do not let a Broker Problem drift into one.

**Against a fee comparison.** joinkale.com/compare exists and is not this. Never put Kale's
numbers next to a competitor's on a Broker Problem video.

## Where the doubt lands (the hole this lane opens)

Seeding doubt creates a problem the previous version of this document did not have. **An agent
who watches BP-001, decides their situation is worth looking into, and wants to do something
about it has nowhere to go.** The lane is ungated on purpose. There is no keyword, no link, no
next step. The doubt is created and then abandoned, and a doubt with no outlet decays back
into resignation inside a week.

The wrong fixes, ruled out:

- **Gate the Broker Problems videos.** Costs the reach the lane exists for (-5.3x on overt Kale
  content, and Instagram will not recommend a video that asks for a keyword comment), and it
  converts the seed into an ad on contact. No.
- **Send them to joinkale.com/compare.** Names the destination, which re-attributes the doubt
  to the person who profits from it. No.
- **Do nothing and rely on the comment mining.** This is the current state. It catches only the
  agents willing to complain in public under their own name, which is a small and unusually
  bold slice of the people who felt the doubt.

**The fix is a cross-pillar handoff.** The artifact that catches this doubt is a genuinely
neutral evaluation tool -- a "what to ask any brokerage" scorecard that works on the agent's
*current* firm as readily as on a new one. Roughly a dozen questions covering ownership of
reviews and database, what the fees are and who receives them, what support means in writing,
how leads are routed, and what happens on a Saturday.

Why that artifact and not a Kale pitch:

1. **It serves rung 2 directly.** The whole ask across this lane is "go find out what normal
   is." This is the instrument for doing that, and handing someone the instrument is not the
   same as telling them where to point it.
2. **Requesting it is the qualification.** Nobody downloads a brokerage evaluation scorecard
   idly. An agent who asks for it has self-identified as exploring, which is a far stronger
   intent signal than anything the current gate collects. It answers the lead-quality problem
   the gate-layer doc was worried about, from the other direction.
3. **It is honest.** It genuinely helps an agent who then decides to stay, and it has to keep
   being that or it stops working. The scorecard cannot be built so that Kale wins it. If
   Kale's answers are good, the agent finds that out on their own, later, from someone else.

**Where it lives: a Value Giveaway, not a Broker Problem.** Giveaways are the gated pillar
already, six a week, with the ManyChat flow built. Run the scorecard as a tool use-case
giveaway roughly monthly, on its own day, with no reference to the Broker Problems videos.
Both lanes keep their rules intact and the giveaway quietly catches what the broker lane
created. **The two must not be posted the same day** -- adjacency is what makes a viewer read
the ungated video as setup for the gated ask.

**Status: not built.** This is the single highest-value unbuilt thing attached to the doubt
objective, and it is a documentation job, not a content job. Flagging it here rather than
building it, because it touches the gate layer and the keyword registry and is D.J.'s call.

## The blocker that has to be fixed before this lane pays for anything

**The `Brokerage Pain` custom field does not exist in Close.** It was a launch prerequisite in
the [gate-layer doc](2026-07-21-goal-reset-and-gate-layer.md) and it was never created -- I
checked the org's lead custom fields on 2026-08-18 and it is not among them.

That field is where the comment-mining mechanic writes. Right now Jennica can flag a Chicago
agent who complained in the replies, and there is nowhere to record *what they complained
about* -- which is the entire value of the signal, and the thing that tells Ana which of the
eight kale-signaling arguments to open with.

**Until that field exists, this lane produces reach and no retrievable recruiting data.**
Creating it is a five-minute job in Close. It is the highest-leverage item attached to this
document and it is not a content task.

Second, smaller: content-sourced Kale signs this month are **zero** (D.J., 2026-08-18). That
is the baseline this lane is measured against. The gate layer has been live since late July
and has produced no signed agent, so nothing here should be justified by assuming the
downstream conversion works -- it is unproven.
