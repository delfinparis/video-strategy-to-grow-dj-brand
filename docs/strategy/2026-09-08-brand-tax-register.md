## What D.J. asked for, in his words

> "I need to start coming up with regular short form videos sowing the seeds of doubt for agents
> to stay at their current firm. I need to start getting a little more direct and controversial
> with what a brand really is, and why it might not be worth it to stay at a firm with a big
> brand but a low split. Or bad support, or bad coaching, or not using the tools they offer but
> paying for them, or not having good tools, etc. Can we work on a script engine that can pump out
> 3 of these a week so I can film? Let's run it by the council."

Four asks: a register shift (more direct, about the brand), a cadence lift (2 to 3 a week), an
engine, and a council review of the whole thing. This document is the council's review and the
decisions that came out of it.

## The tension, stated plainly before the council gets it

The August strategy is built on one finding: **"my brokerage is bad" is a weak seed and "I have
never once checked whether this is normal" is the strong one.** An agent whose firm is attacked
defends six years of their own judgment out loud, and a private doubt becomes a public commitment
against it. That is why the lane bans "go leave," bans naming a destination, and bans Kale.

D.J.'s ask reads, on its face, like the weak seed: *why it might not be worth it to stay.* If the
register shift means the video's close says "it's not worth staying," the lane walks into the
exact failure the strategy was written to avoid.

**It does not have to mean that, and the council's job was to find the version that is more
direct without being that.** Here is the resolution, and then the room that produced it.

### The resolution: direct about the arrangement, never about the firm, never about the exit

Three things move. Three things do not.

**What moves:**

1. **The target of the directness is the big-brand model as a category.** "The brand was built
   to recruit you" is a direct, controversial claim about how the arrangement works. It names
   nobody. A viewer cannot identify a firm from it. It sits at heat 4.5 and it is inside the
   band Rule 9.2 opened on 2026-08-20.
2. **The seed is still ignorance, but ignorance of something specific: what the premium buys.**
   "You have never checked whether this is normal" becomes "you have never checked what the
   brand line actually costs, and what arrived for it." Same undefendable structure, sharper
   object. That is the whole Brand Tax spine: *the premium was supposed to buy something; here
   is what showed up.*
3. **"It might not be worth staying" is the conclusion the arithmetic produces, and it is never
   the sentence D.J. says.** BP-005 already does this: "so you're good giving away eighteen
   thousand a year for a logo, maybe something to think about." The number pushes. D.J. does
   not. The register goes wider than split math (support, coaching, tools, the bundle) and
   keeps that discipline in every one.

**What does not move:**

1. **No identifiable firm, ever.** Direct about the practice, category-level about the party.
   Heat 5 is permitted by Rule 9.2 and argued against by this lane's own finding, and the
   engine treats a 5 as a deliberate judgment call that gets written down, not a default.
2. **Kale is never mentioned.** The moment a destination is named, the doubt is re-attributed
   to whoever profits from it. Be right in public, be Kale in the DM.
3. **The close still hands over a look, not a leave.** Rung 2, "find out what normal is," stays
   the engine. What changes is that the look is now specific: find out what the premium costs
   and what it is supposed to include, somewhere other than where you are.

## The council, convened on the register

This is a strategy review, so the whole board sat, not the usual four to seven. Each member says
what they would change, in character. The witnesses rule on mechanism claims.

**Hormozi.** "Finally. Two a week at heat 3.5 was a lane apologizing for existing. The reward in
this register is a number the viewer computes about their own money, and that is the densest
reward this brand makes. My change: every brand-tax script has to leave the viewer holding a
figure or a checkable item. 'Notice where the brand pitch gets said' is an observation. Make sure
the next line is something they can do tonight."

**MrBeast.** "Thirty seconds is right and the arithmetic scripts hold retention because every
clause pays off a number. My worry is the Silence flavor at the new floor. A scene at heat 4 has
to name the cost in the first line or the first three seconds are a man describing an evening.
BP-007 does it. Make it the rule."

**Kane.** "You have never tested a Broker Problem hook against a second variant because nothing
has aired. Three a week means you can finally A/B. My change: the Tuesday script always ships
with its Family 6 variant ready, because 'you pay for' lands faster than 'was built to' and I
want to know which one this audience stops for."

**GaryVee.** "Three a week is volume, good. Does it smell like the platform or like a brokerage
president's blog? The brand-tax register is natively Chicago-realtor-TikTok if the delivery is
dry and fast. If it gets earnest, it reads as a pitch and dies. And ride the news: when a
franchise raises fees or a big brand gets sued, that is a brand-tax week, at category level."

**Miller.** "Who is the hero? The agent, and the brand is the thing standing between them and
their own money. That is clean. My objection is the two-step close on rung 1 plus rung 2. Two
CTAs is zero CTAs. I lost this one on BP-006 because the recognition beat produces the comment,
and the comment is the success test. I want the A/B run."

**Byron Lazine.** "This is the industry's real conversation and almost nobody with a brokerage is
willing to have it on camera because it costs them. That is the take. D.J.'s credential makes the
brand-tax argument a position instead of a grievance. My change: when the news gives you a
brand-tax story, react inside 48 hours. The engine plans on Sunday; leave room to swap a slot."

**Eric Simon.** "The recognition is real. Every agent has fed a dashboard at nine pm and every
agent has a cap they have not hit. The 'this is us' beat is in all three scripts. Keep the
sarcasm aimed at the claim, never the agent, or the recognition flips into feeling mocked."

**Welsh.** "Can D.J. run this alone, three a week, forever? The scripts, yes, the engine writes
them. The bank, no. Twenty-eight entries at an eight-week rotation is roughly nine weeks of
runway at three a week before repeats, and the two sources the August doc asked for, his
recruiting-call notes and Kale's own departures, have not landed. The refill is the constraint.
`health` exits 10 when the bank runs low; somebody has to answer it."

**Youshaei.** "'Run it with me' is a franchise. Calculator up, number computed live, question
left open. That is a format viewers learn to expect and come back for. My change: use it every
two to three weeks and vary what gets computed. Split, cap, monthly fee, the bundle."

**Chris Do.** "There is no human beat in the brand-tax register and there should not be. It is
arithmetic delivered by someone who has decided to say the quiet part. The human beat is the
Permission Slip, and it is the only script in the lane where D.J.'s vulnerability is the
payload. Protect its slot. The engine holding it back until three posts have aired is right,
because permission from a stranger is recruiting and permission from someone who has named
problems without selling is permission."

**Heath (witness), on the "seed" claim.** "The August doc says the strong seed is a gap in the
agent's own knowledge. The brand-tax version keeps that: 'you have never checked what the brand
line costs' is a gap, and gaps produce curiosity. 'Your brokerage is ripping you off' is a
conclusion, and conclusions produce defense. The register survives as long as every close hands
over a gap to fill and never a verdict to accept."

**Berger (witness), on shareability.** "Anger is the most shared high-arousal emotion and this
register runs on it. The condition is that the anger is aimed at the model and the viewer is the
wronged party, so forwarding it is Social Currency ('I already knew this') rather than
self-incrimination ('I fell for this'). The retell test passes on all three: 'the brand is a
recruiting tool,' 'the required software is a second job I pay for,' 'the cap is priced for the
top producers.' Each survives compression with D.J.'s point intact."

### The real disagreement, and the decision

**Miller and Chris Do versus Hormozi and GaryVee, on how direct.** Hormozi and GaryVee want the
close to push: "so what are you paying for?" delivered as a challenge. Miller and Chris Do hold
that a challenge in the close converts the look into a dare, and a dare gets defended.

**Decision: the arithmetic pushes, the close does not.** The point beat can be as direct as the
receipt supports. The close always resolves to a look. BP-005's "maybe something to think about"
is the register's ceiling for the last line, and the engine's pre-flight keeps it there. If
D.J. wants to test a harder close, it is an A/B on one script, judged on comment mix, not a
change to the standard.

## Decisions

1. **Three a week: Tuesday, Thursday, Friday.** Friday's Video 3 slot was empty on the grid;
   it now carries the third Broker Problem. That makes the week 16 videos, not 15. **D.J.
   decides whether it is a 16th video or displaces the Friday take or the second KIRP promo.**
   The engine and the board are built for 16 until he says otherwise.
2. **Flavor F, The Brand Tax, added to the bank** with six entries (F1-F6), and seven existing
   entries tagged `Register: brand tax` (A2, A3, B3, B6, C4, E1, E2). The engine requires at
   least two of the week's three to carry the register.
3. **Heat floor 4 applies to every flavor.** The Silence and the Permission Slip used to run at
   3. They now name the wrong default in the hook to earn 4, and the scene or the position still
   does the work. The standard's flavor table is superseded on heat by this line.
4. **The Permission Slip waits until three posts have aired.** The strategy said so in August;
   the engine now enforces it.
5. **BP-001 through BP-005 are re-cut candidates, not inventory.** The engine offers them with a
   "re-cut to 68-84 words, lift to heat 4" note when their entry comes back into rotation. Do
   not re-draft them from scratch; the arguments are good.
6. **Broker Problems is a mirror lane on the Content Board.** The board no longer researches it.
   The engine commits finished scripts to `scripts/broker-problems/` and the board mirrors them,
   the same way it mirrors KIRP promos and spotlights.
7. **Two experiments, from the dissents:** BP-006 two-step close versus single rung-2 close,
   judged on comment volume; BP-008 "your cap" versus "the best thing in your split," judged on
   3-second retention and comment mix.
