## BEFORE YOU FILM (do these first)

1. **Apply before you shoot.** Their page: *"Apply before you film so you never spend a day on a video we cannot pay for."* Email them your channel link and the concept, get the approval checklist back, then film. Publish within 30 days.
2. **Their hard requirements:** minimum 4 minutes; you on camera using the app with your own setup; original footage only (no stock, no AI voiceover, no screen-recording-only); say the full name **"Teleprompter.com"**; keep it public 12 months. One payout per creator.
3. **Check the YouTube "contains paid promotion" box** and put the disclosure line in the first line of the description. The verbal disclosure at 0:12 does not replace either.
4. **Fill the one personal slot:** `[TAKES]` at 0:44. Use your real worst-take number. If you don't have one, cut the line and keep "my brain leaves the building." Do not invent a number.
5. **Shoot the B-roll first** so the demo cuts are real, not reenacted (their rule, and it's the whole credibility of the video).

## SPOKEN SCRIPT

### 0:00 HOOK
*(Talking head, mid-sentence energy. No greeting.)*

I'm reading this sentence off a screen. Right now. Watch.

*(Flip the phone around to camera. The script is visibly scrolling in sync with your mouth. Hold three seconds.)*

You didn't notice. Neither has anybody watching my videos this year.

### 0:12 THE DISCLOSURE (and the loop)

Teleprompter.com paid me a hundred dollars to review their app. They didn't write this, they didn't approve it, and their own partnership page says a review nobody believes is worth nothing to either of us.

So a hundred bucks bought that sentence. It didn't buy the one at three minutes in, where I tell you the thing about this company that almost stopped me from making the video.

### 0:35 THE PROBLEM

Here's what it actually fixed, and it isn't memorizing.

It's this.

*(Do the eye drift. Up and to the left. Hold it, silent, three seconds.)*

That thing your eyes do when you're reaching for the next line. That's not thinking. That's a man searching his own attic. Everyone watching can see it.

You know the feeling. I do. [TAKES] takes for a forty five second video, and my brain leaves the building somewhere around the third sentence.

### 0:55 THE ONE FEATURE: VOICEGLIDE

Every teleprompter app scrolls. That's the low bar. You pick a speed, the words crawl, and now you're racing a machine that doesn't care that you sneezed.

Teleprompter.com has a mode called VoiceGlide. It listens to you.

*(Demo. Speak fast, speak slow, stop dead for two seconds, resume.)*

I talk, it moves. I slow down, it slows down. I stop because a garbage truck's going by, it waits for me.

I'm not chasing the script anymore. The script is chasing me. That's the whole difference between reading and talking, and everything else in this app is a nice to have.

### 1:35 THE REMOTE (and a tangent)

You can run it off an iPad, a bluetooth keyboard, a clicker, a foot pedal, a game controller, your Apple Watch.

And a MIDI controller. Who is teleprompting off a piano?

*(Beat.)*

Anyway. The watch is the one that changed my day. Phone's out on a selfie stick, I'm walking, I can't reach it. Tap the watch, it rolls. Tap it again, it stops. That's why I can shoot alone.

### 2:05 THE SCRIPTS

Scripts sync from Google Drive, Dropbox and iCloud. I write at my desk, it's on my phone before I'm out the door.

You can edit inside the app, and that matters more than it sounds. Two lines in you hear yourself say a clunky sentence, and you fix it right there.

They built in dyslexia friendly fonts, OpenDyslexic and Lexend. If you've been avoiding video because reading on camera is a nightmare, that's not a small feature.

### 2:35 IT ALSO RECORDS

It records, too. Four K with no watermark on the paid tier, noise removal, auto subtitles, live streaming to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.

I use almost none of it. I cut in CapCut and I'm too far into my workflow to change. But starting from zero, you could shoot, caption and post without leaving the app.

Their customer list has Compass on it, next to Netflix and the BBC. I spend my life trying to get agents to make videos, so that's the logo I noticed.

### 3:05 THE PART I PROMISED

Go read the one star reviews. I did. Almost none of them are about the app. They're about the bill.

People start the free trial, forget it exists, and get charged. Then they can't work out how to cancel. That's a design choice, and it's the one thing I don't love here.

So don't become one of those reviews. The trial runs seven days. Put a reminder on day six before you download anything. Love it, do nothing. Don't love it, cancel where you subscribed. On an iPhone that's your Apple settings, not inside the app.

It's twenty a month monthly, seven fifty a month for the year. The free tier is real, and it watermarks everything you record.

### 3:55 WHO IT'S FOR

If you make one video a month, don't pay for this. Use the free tier, live with the watermark, or read off a sticky note like a normal person.

If you talk for a living, it pays for itself the first time you get it in one take.

### 4:12 CLOSE

Here's what you do now. Get the free version of Teleprompter.com. Don't write anything new. Open your notes app, find the worst script in there, the one you've been avoiding, and paste it in. Turn on VoiceGlide. Record thirty seconds.

Then watch it back and see if you can tell you were reading.

Because I've been reading this whole time.

*(Beat. Straight into the lens.)*

You still didn't notice.

*(Hard cut. No outro card, no wave.)*

## COUNCIL REVIEW

**Convened:** Youshaei, MrBeast, Chris Do, Hormozi, Kane, Miller, Byron Lazine, Eric Simon. Witnesses: Heath, Berger.

### What changed from v1

**Youshaei (the biggest fix).** "What's on screen at 0:00? A guy claiming he's reading. Claiming. Go physical -- flip the phone at the lens and let the scroll prove it in the same frame. You had the best visual anchor of your career sitting in your hand and you described it instead." **Adopted.** The prop flip is now the pattern interrupt.

**MrBeast vs. Chris Do (the real fight).** MrBeast: "the disclosure at 0:12 is a brand-deal crater in the most expensive twelve seconds you own. old-style scripted brand reads make people click off. move it to 0:45 after they're hooked." Chris Do: "Moving the disclosure behind the sell is precisely the move that makes audiences stop believing creators. The disclosure is the most valuable asset in this video. Do not launder it." **Resolution:** it stays at 0:12 but stops being a speed bump. It now *opens the loop* ("a hundred bucks bought that sentence, it didn't buy the one at three minutes in"). Both doctrines satisfied: the honesty leads, and the honesty is the retention device.

**Hormozi.** "The star rating and the million-creators line is their marketing read aloud. Zero value. Cut it, buy yourself twelve seconds for the demo." **Adopted** -- pulled, logged in Data Source. Compass survived because it's the one logo his audience reacts to.

**Miller.** "For the first minute of v1 you were the hero. Podcast, episode count, your problem. Flip it -- the viewer's problem is the eye drift, and it's the only thing in the video they've felt personally." **Adopted.** The credential dump is gone; the eye drift moved up to 0:35.

**Heath (witness, on the gap).** "The hook opens a strong gap and v1 took sixty-five seconds to start closing it. In a 4:30 video that's the whole first act spent on a promise. Also, 'VoiceGlide' is a name, not an idea -- you're the tapper. Define it by what it *does* before you name it." **Both adopted:** the demo lands at 0:55 and the function precedes the name.

**Berger (witness, on shares).** "This gets watched, not shared. Reviews rarely spread. Your one shareable unit is Practical Value: the day-six reminder. In v1 it sat at 3:40 where most viewers never reach it, and the brand isn't load-bearing in the retelling. Keep the tip late -- it's the payoff -- but cut it as its own Short so it can travel with the app name attached." **Adopted** as Short #3.

**Eric Simon.** "The eye drift is your 'this is literally me.' Don't say it, *do* it and hold the silence." **Adopted** -- it's a silent held shot now.

**Byron Lazine vs. Kane (the second fight).** Byron: "Don't make a general creator review. Your people are agents, and the number one thing an agent says about video is 'I hate how I sound.' That's this video." Kane: "The search demand is 'teleprompter app review.' Package it broad or it never gets found." **Resolution tied to the goal** (12-month evergreen asset + credibility with his own list): Byron wins the *story beats* (selfie stick, walking, agents, Compass), Kane wins the *title and structure* (feature walkthrough, timestamps, review-native title). It's an agent's story inside a searchable review.

### Why it should work

- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** a Gap-Theory open where the proof arrives inside the same frame as the claim. "Huh?" and "Aha" land three seconds apart, then a bigger gap replaces it.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** Practical Value, not emotion. The day-six reminder is the forwardable unit, and Teleprompter.com is inseparable from it.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the disclosure is not a crater, it's a re-hook. It plants the 3:05 payoff at second 20, which is exactly where the first drop-off happens.

### The dissent

**GaryVee still objects:** "One video? Make ten. You're doing a review when you could be documenting -- every video you post this year is already made with this app. Post the process, not the verdict." Fair, and unusable under a one-payout program. **A/B test:** run Short #1 (the phone flip) against Short #4 (the eye drift) on the same day. If the eye drift wins, D.J.'s audience wants the mirror, not the reveal, and the next tool video leads with the pain instead of the proof.

**Hormozi's smaller dissent:** he wants a hard time-saved number at 0:35 instead of `[TAKES]`. There isn't a real one, and Rule 1 outranks him. If D.J. starts logging takes, that number belongs in the next version.
