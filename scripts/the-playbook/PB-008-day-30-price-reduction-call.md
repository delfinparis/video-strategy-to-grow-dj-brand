# The Day 30 Price Reduction Call

> **WOW: Tactical specificity plus a surprising statistic. It hands the viewer the exact sentence for the price reduction call they are avoiding this week, and a June 2026 Realtor.com number that moves the deadline off the seller's ego and onto the calendar.**

## Shareable Moment
> "We're at week four. I'd rather move while we still pick the number."

## Full Script (Spoken)

**SCENARIO**
Your listing hits day thirty this week. Most agents open that call with the exact wrong sentence.

**DON'T**
Don't say "we need to talk about the price." Your seller hears you quitting, and starts defending a number.

**DO**
Say this instead. "Realtor.com ran the numbers. Homes that close at four weeks do 1.8 percent better against asking than average. At eighteen weeks, worse. We're at week four. I'd rather move while we still pick the number."

**WHY**
Nobody had to be wrong. You put a deadline in the room instead of an opinion.

**TRY THIS**
Here's what you do now. Find your listing closest to day thirty. Make that call today.

**LOOP-BACK**
Day thirty isn't when you lose the listing. It's the last week you get to choose.

## Data Source

- Homes that close at four weeks do 1.8 percent better against asking than average
  - Source: Realtor.com, "By Week Four, Your Listing Is Either Getting Offers or Price Cuts," Joel Berner, Senior Economist, published June 11, 2026
  - Exact wording in source: "homes that close at the four week mark sell for 1.8% more relative to asking price than the average home sold in the same period"
  - Who was measured: closed home sales, deed and MLS records matched to Realtor.com listing histories, 2021 through 2026
  - Status: confirmed

- At eighteen weeks, worse
  - Source: Realtor.com, Joel Berner, June 11, 2026 (same report)
  - Exact wording in source: "those sitting at 18 weeks close 1.3% below expectations"
  - Who was measured: same dataset
  - Status: confirmed. The script states this qualitatively rather than citing the second figure, to stay inside the two-numbers-per-spoken-line rule and to avoid implying the two findings are a single causal result.

### Corrections made to the morning brief

The Aug 1 brief attributed this material to "Zillow Research, The Price of Overpricing" and "NAR Senior Economist Nadia Evangelou, 2026." That attribution is wrong. The 1.8 percent and 1.3 percent figures come from Realtor.com (Berner, June 11, 2026), not Zillow and not NAR.

The brief also framed the stat as "homes that go under contract in weeks 1-2 close 1.8 percentage points above the monthly average." That conflates two separate findings in the source: the 1.8 percent figure applies to homes that **close** at the four-week mark, while "the top performers went under contract within the first two weeks" is a separate observation about that cohort. Merging them would violate Rule 1's ban on combining two findings in one sentence.

The brief's other claim, "homes priced 3-5% over market face longer DOM and deeper eventual reductions (NAR 2026)," could not be verified against a primary source and was cut rather than shipped.
