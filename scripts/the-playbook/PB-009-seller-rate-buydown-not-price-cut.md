# Ask for the Buydown, Not the Price Cut

> **WOW: The same seller concession is worth roughly eight times more to the buyer's payment when it buys the rate down instead of the price. The comparison is arithmetic anyone can check, and almost no offer on the street is written this way.**

## Shareable Moment
> "Sixty versus five hundred. Same dollar out of the seller's pocket."

## Full Script (Spoken)

**HOOK**
Stop asking sellers for a price cut. It is the weakest thing your buyer can ask for, and I can prove that in thirty seconds.

**STORY**
Realtor dot com says sellers cut prices on twenty percent of active listings in July. Sellers are already moving. Your offer decides what they move on. On a four hundred thousand dollar loan, a two one buydown runs the seller about nine thousand dollars and drops your buyer's payment five hundred a month in year one. The same nine thousand as a price cut moves the payment sixty dollars.

**TURN**
Sixty versus five hundred. Same dollar out of the seller's pocket. One gets your offer accepted. The other gets a polite pass. And a price cut becomes a public comp that drags the whole block. A buydown does not.

**PAYOFF**
One play. Next offer, put seller paid two one rate buydown on the concession line instead of a price reduction. Call the listing agent first and say it: same dollar for your seller, better comps.

- Rate assumption behind the payment math (not spoken in either cut)
  - Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, week ended August 20, 2026. 30-year fixed averaged 6.65 percent, down from 6.67 percent, the second consecutive weekly decline.
  - URL: https://freddiemac.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/mortgage-rates-decline-second-consecutive-week
  - Status: confirmed

- The payment comparison
  - $400,000 loan, 30-year fixed at 6.65 percent. Base principal and interest is $2,567.86.
  - A 2-1 buydown (year one at 4.65 percent, year two at 5.65 percent) costs the seller $9,170.74 in escrowed funds. Year-one payment is $2,062.55, a saving of $505.31 a month.
  - The same $9,170.74 applied as a price cut, with the full amount coming off the loan, reduces the payment to $2,509.00, a saving of $58.87 a month.
  - $505 against $59 is a factor of 8.6. The script says nine thousand, five hundred, and sixty, all rounded toward the price cut so the comparison understates rather than overstates the buydown.
  - The price-cut side assumes the entire cut reduces the loan. With 20 percent down it would only reduce the loan by 80 percent of the cut, so the real gap is wider. Stating the generous version of the opposing case is deliberate.
  - Status: computed, reproducible from the rate above

### Corrections to the original draft

The draft ran three numbers that do not hold up:

- "An eighty-six-hundred-dollar price cut saves the buyer about fifty dollars a month." At 6.65 percent an $8,600 cut saves $55.21, not about $50.
- "That same eighty-six hundred, spent on a two-one buydown, saves the buyer around four hundred fifty a month in year one." A full 2-1 buydown on a $400,000 loan costs $9,170.74, not $8,600, and the year-one saving is $505.31, not around $450. The draft priced the buydown below cost and then understated what it delivers.
- "Ten times the impact." The true multiple on a like-for-like concession is 8.6. The claim is now carried by the two numbers instead of a multiplier, so nothing has to be rounded up.

The buydown cost now leads the comparison rather than a chosen budget, which is both accurate and the stronger version of the argument.
