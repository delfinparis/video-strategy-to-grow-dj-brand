# Most Agents Quit at Touch Two. The Sale Closes at Touch Five.

> **WOW: Big-number contrarian + tactical. A scrolling agent stops because the 80% stat names exactly why their pipeline feels dead (they quit too early), and the play hands them one concrete action they can run tonight (pull the 20 oldest dead leads, send touch five tomorrow). Mid-year timing makes it urgent instead of evergreen.**

## Full Script (Spoken) (~55 seconds)

### SCENARIO (0:00-0:08)
Eighty percent of sales take five or more follow-ups to close. Most agents quit after one or two. That gap is where your deals are hiding.

### DON'T (0:08-0:18)
So don't write off your spring leads. The ones who went quiet in March didn't say no. They said not yet, and then nobody followed up.

### DO (0:18-0:32)
Here's the play. Pull your cold leads from the first quarter. Take the twenty oldest. Run five real touches over three weeks. A call, a text, a market update they'd actually use.

### WHY (0:32-0:46)
Because the deal was never gone. The follow-up was. Four in ten sellers pick their agent off a referral, and a referral is just follow-up that kept going. And right now every other agent has mentally quit on their cold pipeline. That's your opening.

### TRY THIS (0:46-0:54)
Here's what you do now. Tonight, pull your twenty oldest dead leads. Tomorrow, send touch number five to the first five. That's where your deals are hiding.

**Estimated Duration:** ~55 seconds (~140 words)

**Length justification:** Five seconds over the 50-second Playbook cap. The extra room is earned by the DON'T beat, which reframes "dead leads" as "un-followed-up leads" before the play lands -- without that reframe the agent files this under generic "follow up more" advice and scrolls. The WHY also carries two jobs here: the referral-is-compounded-follow-up mechanic plus the mid-year why-now (competitors have quit on their cold pipeline), and cutting either one drops the reason an agent acts tonight instead of "someday."

  - Source: Compiled in AgentZap, "Real Estate Lead Statistics 2026" (agentzap.ai/blog/real-estate-lead-statistics) and REDX, "Best Real Estate Leads 2026 Ranking Guide" (redx.com/blog/best-real-estate-leads-2026-ranking-guide).

"Eighty percent" is exact; "one or two" matches the cited "most quit after the first or second contact."
  - Status: confirmed as cited by both 2026 sources; original-study provenance flagged above per Rule 1.

  - Status: confirmed as cited by AgentZap; held to the long-form social captions, kept off the spoken track to avoid stacking two unverified-origin percentages on camera. Expressed as a ratio-friendly "seventy percent higher."

  - Source: NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (referral-from-friend/family/neighbor share of sellers consistently lands in the high-30s to low-40s percent range), as echoed in the AgentZap/REDX 2026 roundups (cited there as 42%).
  - Status: confirmed directional. Expressed as "four in ten" per Rule 5 ratio convention rather than a single contested decimal. The reframe ("a referral is just follow-up that kept going") is D.J.'s qualitative interpretation, not a measured claim.

- **Strategic framing:** "Right now every other agent has mentally quit on their cold pipeline. Mid-year is the window."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's operational read on the mid-year follow-up gap -- Q1 leads have gone quiet and most competitors have moved on from cold pipeline by summer.
  - Status: confirmed as D.J.'s observation. Qualitative, not a measured stat.
