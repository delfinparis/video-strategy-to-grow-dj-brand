# You Have 7 Days to Sell Your Listing. After That, You're Begging.

> **WOW: Contrarian listing-appointment ammo + two-number proof. A scrolling agent stops because it names the conversation they dread (telling a seller no on price) and hands them the data to win it: a right-priced home goes pending in ~17 days, the median listing sits at 66, and more than one in three listings has already cut price. The contrast between 17 and 66 is the whole argument in two numbers.**

## Full Script (Spoken) (~58 seconds)

### SCENARIO (0:00-0:08)
Your seller wants to list high and try it a couple weeks. Tell them this. A listing gets about one week of real attention. Waste it and you chase for two months.

### DON'T (0:08-0:18)
So don't let them overprice to leave room. Even three to five percent high burns week one, the only week buyers see it fresh. Stale sells for less.

### DO (0:18-0:30)
Here's the play. Bring the data, not your opinion. A right-priced home still goes pending in about seventeen days. The median listing now sits sixty-six. Price for week one.

### WHY (0:30-0:43)
Because week one is the only peak attention you get for free. More than one in three listings on the market right now has already cut price. They all started too high and chased the market down.

### TRY THIS (0:43-0:53)
Here's what you do now. Before your next listing appointment, pull every price cut in that zip code and how long each one sat. Show your seller what overpricing really costs. Price it for week one, or beg for the rest of summer.

**Estimated Duration:** ~58 seconds (~150 words)

**Length justification:** Eight seconds over the 50-second Playbook floor. The room is earned by the two-number proof in the DO beat -- the contrast between "right-priced goes pending in 17 days" and "the median listing sits at 66" is the entire persuasive engine, and dropping either number collapses the argument into a generic "don't overprice" cliche the seller has already ignored. The WHY then needs the "one in three has cut price" stat to convert the contrast from D.J.'s opinion into market fact, which is what actually arms the agent for the listing appointment.

## Data Source

- **Claim (the window):** "A listing gets about one week of real / peak attention."
  - Source: AddressUSA, "How to Price Your Home So It Actually Sells in 60 Days or Less in 2026" (addressusa.com), as cited in the 2026-06-24 Walk & Talk brief.
  - Provenance note: The first-week-peak-interest principle is standard listing strategy -- portal traffic (Zillow/Realtor.com saved-search alerts, new-listing notifications) spikes when a listing hits the market and decays after. Framed in-script as observable listing behavior and a pricing principle, not a single measured metric. Per Rule 8.5, no specific "X% of views happen in week one" number is claimed on camera.
  - Status: confirmed as the source's framing; represented as a principle.

- **Claim (the fast number):** "A right-priced home still goes pending in about seventeen days."
  - Source: AddressUSA 2026 pricing guide (cited in the brief). Refers to the well-priced segment's days-to-pending, not the all-listings median.
  - Status: confirmed as cited. Important distinction held in the script: this is how fast a *correctly priced* home goes under contract, which is deliberately contrasted with the all-active median below.

- **Claim (the slow number):** "The median listing now sits at sixty-six days."
  - Source: FRED median days-on-market, ~66 days nationally, early 2026 (cited in the brief). "Up seven days from last year" per the brief's framing.
  - Status: confirmed directional. Note: 66 days is the median across ALL active listings (dragged up by overpriced/stale inventory); it is NOT in conflict with the 17-day figure, which is the right-priced subset. The script uses the 17-vs-66 gap as the argument, not as a contradiction.

- **Claim (the proof):** "More than one in three active listings has already cut price."
  - Source: AddressUSA 2026 guide / widely-reported 2025-2026 price-reduction share (Realtor.com and Redfin have both reported price cuts on roughly a third or more of active listings through this period), cited in the brief as "more than 1 in 3 active listings is taking a price cut nationally."
  - Status: confirmed directional. Expressed as "more than one in three" per Rule 5 ratio convention rather than a single contested percentage.

- **Strategic framing:** "Overprice by three to five percent and you burn week one; stale listings sell for less; price for week one on purpose."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's listing-appointment framing built on the source's pricing logic (overpricing forfeits peak interest and invites the stale-listing stigma, which historically transacts below a right-priced comp).
  - Status: confirmed as D.J.'s tactical framing. The "three to five percent" band is the source's illustrative overpricing example, not a measured threshold.

### Personal LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
Your seller wants to list high and "try it for a couple weeks."

Here is the conversation that wins you the listing and the sale.

A new listing gets about one week of real attention. The saved-search alerts fire, the new-listing buyers all look at once, and then the traffic drops off. Waste that week on a price nobody bites at and you spend the next two months chasing the market down.

So do not let them overprice to "leave room to negotiate." Even three to five percent too high burns week one, the only week buyers see the home fresh. And stale listings sell for less than they would have at the right number on day one.

Bring the data, not your opinion. A right-priced home still goes pending in about seventeen days. The median listing nationally now sits at sixty-six (FRED, early 2026). That gap between seventeen and sixty-six is not a market problem. It is a pricing problem.

And the proof is already on the board. More than one in three active listings has taken a price cut. Every one of those started a few points too high and chased the market down anyway, just slower and for less.

Here is what you do before your next listing appointment. Pull every price cut in that zip code and how long each one sat before the seller blinked. Walk in and show them what overpricing actually costs in days and dollars.

Price it for week one, or beg for the rest of the summer.
