# You're Prospecting on Facebook. Your Referrals Are on LinkedIn.

> **WOW: Tactical specificity + contrarian + surprising stat. A scrolling agent stops because the NAR number names exactly where they're wasting their effort (Facebook), and the play hands them one concrete daily action (comment, don't just post) they can run tomorrow.**

## Full Script (Spoken) (~50 seconds)

### SCENARIO (0:00-0:06)
You're prospecting on Facebook. The agent about to take your next relocation referral is working LinkedIn.

### DON'T (0:06-0:18)
Almost nine in ten agents are on Facebook. Fewer than half are even on LinkedIn. And the few who are there just post and disappear.

### DO (0:18-0:30)
Here's the play. Every morning, leave a real comment on ten to fifteen professional posts. Not "great post." A real one.

### WHY (0:30-0:44)
Because a post only reaches your followers. A comment puts you in front of theirs. That's how a relocating executive finds you first. The commenting is the move, not the posting.

### TRY THIS (0:44-0:52)
Here's what you do now. Tomorrow, before Facebook, open LinkedIn and leave ten real comments. Start now, you'll feel it by the fourth quarter. That's where your referrals are hiding.

The extra room over a 40-second Playbook is earned by the stat-proof beat (the NAR contrast is the scroll-stopper and needs both figures) plus the mechanism in the WHY beat (post reaches your followers, comment reaches theirs) -- which is what makes an agent actually change behavior instead of nodding at generic "be on LinkedIn" advice.

**EP-polish note (v4):** The WHY beat was rebuilt to prove the thesis rather than assert it. v3 said "the commenting is the move" without explaining why; v4 delivers the mechanism (a comment surfaces you to the post-author's audience, a post only reaches your own). The redundant "Posting isn't the strategy" line was cut, "someone relocating for a corporate job" was tightened to "a relocating executive," and the why-now was compressed so the loop-back lands clean.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Almost nine in ten agents live on Facebook. Fewer than half are even on LinkedIn."
  - Source: NAR 2024 Technology Survey (published August 2024) -- Facebook 87%, Instagram 62%, LinkedIn 48%, YouTube 25%.
  - Who was measured: NAR members (REALTORS), self-reported business use of each platform.
  - Status: confirmed. "Almost nine in ten" = 87%. "Fewer than half" = 48%. Both expressed as ratios per Rule 5; neither number rounded in a way that changes the claim.
  - Note: an earlier framing of this script used "92% Facebook / 12% LinkedIn." The 92% traces to the NAR 2023 survey; the "12% on LinkedIn" figure is unsupported and contradicts NAR's own data (48-52% of agents report LinkedIn use). It was pulled per Rule 1 and replaced with the current NAR 2024 figures.

- **Claim (the play):** "Comment on ten to fifteen professional posts every morning; post your own a few times a week; the commenting matters more than the posting."
  - Source: JamilAcademy -- LinkedIn for Realtors (2026). Presented in the script as "the play" (a recommended routine), not as a measured statistic.
  - Status: confirmed as the source's recommended cadence; the systematic-commenting emphasis is the source's central tactical claim. Not represented in-script as field-tested by a named guest.

- **Claim (the mechanic):** "A post only reaches your followers. A comment puts you in front of theirs."
  - Source: description of observable LinkedIn behavior -- a post distributes primarily to the author's own network, while a comment appears publicly beneath the post it's on, visible to that post-author's audience.
  - Status: framed as platform mechanics, not as a metric. No reach percentage or multiplier is claimed on camera (Rule 8.5: observable behavior, not a narrated algorithm secret).

- **Illustrative detail:** "An executive relocating to your city" finds you / "someone relocating for a corporate job."
  - Source: illustrative example of the high-end / corporate-relocation buyer the strategy targets. Not a measured stat. No dollar figure is stated on camera to avoid presenting an invented specific as data.
  - Status: framed as illustrative.

- **Strategic framing:** "The pipeline you start this summer closes in the fourth quarter."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's operational framing of the mid-year prospecting-to-close lag. Qualitative, not a measured claim.
  - Status: confirmed as D.J.'s observation.
