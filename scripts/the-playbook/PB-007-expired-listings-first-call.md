# Expired Listings Relist at 44%. Most Agents Won't Make the Call.

> **WOW: Big-number contrarian + a do-it-tomorrow script line. A scrolling agent stops because 44% of expireds relist and almost nobody is working them in summer, the peak expiration window. The edge isn't a lead source, it's the one call your competition is too uncomfortable to make. The DO beat hands over the exact disarming first-call frame (diagnostician, not vulture), which is what turns the awkward call into a listing appointment.**

## Full Script (Spoken) (~52 seconds)

### SCENARIO (0:00-0:08)
Forty-four percent of expired listings come back on the market. And in peak summer expiration season, almost nobody calls those sellers.

### DON'T (0:08-0:18)
Don't skip them because it feels awkward. Calling a seller whose home just failed feels like walking into their bad day. That's exactly why your competition won't dial.

### DO (0:18-0:36)
Call within the hour, and don't pitch. Say this. "I'm not calling to relist your home today. I'm calling to find out what went wrong, because if nobody names it, the next agent just repeats it." Now you're the diagnostician, not the vulture.

### WHY (0:36-0:46)
It works because every other call this seller gets is a pitch. You're the only one who called to help instead of to sell.

### TRY THIS (0:46-0:54)
Pull this week's expireds. Call the first five within the hour, and lead with that line. That's your edge.

## Data Source

- **Claim (the anchor):** "Forty-four percent of expired listings come right back on the market / relist."
  - Source: RealEstateAgentLeads.com, "Real Estate Lead Generation Statistics 2026" (realestateagentleads.com/real-estate-lead-generation-statistics), as cited in the 2026 Walk & Talk brief (option 6).
  - Provenance note: This is an aggregator-compiled lead-gen statistic. The 44% relist figure is repeated across prospecting/lead-gen content; the original measurement methodology (relist within what window, what market) is not fully specified by the aggregator. Represented in-script as a relist rate, which is accurate to the source. "Forty-four percent" is stated exactly, not rounded.
  - Status: confirmed as cited; original-study provenance flagged per Rule 1.

- **Claim (the speed/first-call advantage):** "The agent who calls first, calm and useful, usually gets the relist."
  - Source: speed-to-lead / first-responder principle (the same lineage behind "most sellers go with the first agent who contacts them"). Framed in-script as a principle, not a hard percentage.
  - Status: intentionally softened from the brief's original hook ("the agent who calls within sixty minutes wins nearly all of them"). "Wins nearly all" is not separately sourced and overstates the magnitude, so the script claims "usually gets the relist" and keeps the 60-minute window as the recommended tactic, not as a measured win-rate.

- **Claim (seasonality):** "Summer is peak expiration season."
  - Source: seasonal listing logic cited in the brief. Spring is peak listing season; 60-to-90-day listing agreements signed in spring age out across summer, and listings that took price cuts without selling expire in the same window.
  - Status: directional / seasonal-pattern claim, not a precise measured monthly peak. Framed as the reason the tactic is timely now, not as a hard statistic.

- **Strategic framing:** "Open with the diagnosis, not the pitch. You're the diagnostician, not the vulture." + the verbatim first-call line.
  - Source: D.J. Paris's tactical framing of the expired-listing first call. The disarm-first approach (acknowledge the failure, position as the person who names what went wrong) is a coaching frame, not a measured claim.
  - Status: confirmed as D.J.'s framing.
