# Real Estate Agents in 2027: Do These 5 Things on Social Media, Not These

> **WOW: Criterion 3 (pattern reveal) backed by criterion 4 (surprising statistic). D.J. admits he got zero recruiting conversations from 90 days of video because no post asked for anything, then hands over what 3,234 brand posts show about asking, teaching and follower counts.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You can't get a call you never asked for. I tested that for 90 days."

---

## Teleprompter

You posted all year, and your phone didn't ring.

Everybody tells you to post consistently. So I did. I run sales and marketing at a brokerage in Chicago, so getting agents to talk to me is my job. From April to July this year, I put out short videos for 90 days.

I got zero conversations from those videos.

Not one post in those 90 days asked anybody to raise a hand.

Then I read a report from Metricool, a company that sells social media software. They studied 3,234 posts from 72 U.S. consumer brands in August of 2026. Almost two out of three of the captions they read asked for nothing.

None of these brands sell real estate, and it's one month from a small sample. But they sell to regular people, and so do you.

So here are five moves to make before 2027. Move three got one brand about five times its usual views. Move five is going to make you stop checking your follower count.

They're all in a one-page plan, and the link is below. You get every move right here whether you click or not.

Move one. Ask for one thing. Don't hope they message you.

Most agents finish a post and just stop. They figure anybody who wants them will call. That was my plan too. It got me zero.

Metricool compared posts from the same brand. Captions with an ask did the same as captions without one.

That sounds boring. It's the best news in the report, because the ask didn't cost those brands anything.

Now here's the limit. They counted likes and comments, not sales. So this report can't promise you an ask gets you a client. It only says the ask didn't hurt the post.

So end one post a week with one ask, tied to one thing. Say this. "If you own a home around here, send me your address. I'll send you what the three closest homes sold for."

Move two. Teach. Don't pitch.

Metricool sorted every post by type, then compared each type to that brand's usual results. Promotion posts did the worst of any type.

Those are the "buy now" posts. For you, that's the "call me today, I'm your trusted expert" post.

Tutorials did slightly better than the brand's usual. Slightly. I won't oversell it.

But 33 of the 72 brands didn't publish a single tutorial that month. They're leaving this wide open.

So write down every question a client asked you this week. Pick one. Say the question out loud, then answer it in 30 seconds.

Move three. This is the one I told you about. Run a countdown. Don't announce it once.

Most agents post "open house Sunday" one time, usually on Saturday night.

A bag company called BÉIS had a Disney collection coming out. In the five days before the launch, they posted nine teasers on TikTok. Every teaser gave the launch date and time.

A typical teaser got 91,300 views. That's about five times their other posts.

That's one brand, and Disney helped. You don't have Disney. You do have a Sunday open house.

So copy the shape. Post once a day for five days, and put the same date and time in every post. Tuesday, show the kitchen. Wednesday, show the yard. Thursday, walk the street. Friday, show your favorite room. Saturday, say "see you tomorrow at one."

I wrote that countdown out for you, day by day, with the words. It's in Your 2027 Social Media Plan, and the link is below. Yes, it's on my brokerage's website, and yes, it asks for your email. That's the trade. You can use all of it at any brokerage.

Move four, you can check in about a minute, and brands you've heard of got it wrong.

Move four. Change the first line. Don't paste the same post everywhere.

Two out of three Facebook captions in this report repeated the same brand's Instagram caption. Reposting is fine. I do it too.

The trouble starts when nobody reads it first. 32 Facebook posts still told people to check the link in bio. That line only makes sense on Instagram.

You don't need four calendars. Pick one main platform and make your post there. Then, for every other platform, change the first line and change the ask.

Now open your Facebook page. Look at your last ten posts. Find every "link in bio" and fix it.

Move five. Here's the follower count one. Beat your typical post. Stop chasing followers.

The food brands in this report had the biggest Instagram audience, 9.5 million followers. Their typical post got 162 likes and comments.

The beauty brands had 5.4 million followers. Their typical post got 1,074.

So beauty had far fewer followers and almost seven times the response. And it wasn't one viral brand. Eight of the ten beauty brands beat the typical post across the whole report.

The report doesn't prove why, so I won't pretend it does. But a bigger audience didn't buy those food brands more attention.

So take your last 11 posts and rank them by likes and comments. Look at the sixth one. That's your typical post. Next month, your only job is to beat that number.

So, back to your phone.

Here's what you do now. Write one ask, and put it on your next post today.

The rest of the week is in the plan, and the link is below.

You can't get a call you never asked for. I tested that for 90 days.

**What the board changed:**

- **MrBeast:** the loops were planted but never paid off out loud, and the offer was a place to leave. Added two callbacks, plus a pull into move 4 right after the offer.

- **Byron:** there was no reason to act now. "Before 2027" went in, with no prediction attached.
- **Berger:** the share moment is the link in bio check, because retelling it in one sentence keeps the point. High-arousal emotion is mild embarrassment, so this is a send-to-one-agent video, not a viral one.

**The real disagreement, and the call:** Hormozi and MrBeast wanted the source limits cut for pace. Chris Do and the truth filter wanted them kept. The goal is trust that earns an email, so they stay, but each is one or two sentences. MrBeast also wanted the offer moved to the end. It stays after move 3, because the goal is tagged leads and the countdown is the best reason to click.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the viewer's problem opens the loop, and the 90 days makes them need the reason.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): exact words and a one-minute check they can act on today.
- Retention move (MrBeast): numbered moves, two paid-off loops, and an abrupt last line that returns to the phone.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Kane says the title and first line carry too little heat for a floor of 4, since the Mirror family usually sits at 2 to 3. Run title 1 for thirty days, then test title 3. Judge on tagged signups, not views.

**Recruiting test:** a good agent watches a sales and marketing president admit a public miss, state the limits of the source, disclose the firm, and give the plan away. Respect goes up. It fails if the page turns into a pitch.
