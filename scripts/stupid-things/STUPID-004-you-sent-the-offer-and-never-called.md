# You Sent The Offer And Never Called

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD

## Shareable Moment
> "Your offer is a document. Your call is the proof a real person is attached to it."

## Script (~30 seconds, 78 words)

### HOOK (0:00-0:01.5) - 8 words
You sent the offer and never called her.

### VERDICT (0:01.5-0:03.5) - 5 words
That's really stupid. Here's why.

### TENSION (0:03.5-0:08) - 12 words
She got a notification. No voice, no name, nothing human attached.

### THE POINT (0:08-0:22) - 35 words
She has ten offers on that house. She is not annoyed by a phone call, she is reading the silence. And she is asking her seller a question you never hear: how committed is this guy?

### PAYOFF (0:22-0:30) - 19 words
Send it, then call inside five minutes. Name yourself, name the house, ask when the seller reviews.

**Word count:** 78 spoken words (68-84; counted, not estimated)

**What got cut:** The page version carries four separate producers saying this independently. On camera that is a credential, not a beat, so it lives in the LinkedIn caption. Also cut: the "ten days before closing when something breaks" line, which asks the viewer to imagine a second scene.

## Production Notes
- One Prop: the phone, held up on the hook and put in the pocket on the payoff.
- "She" throughout for the listing agent. One consistent person beats a role noun repeated.
- The friction points at the silence, never at the agent. Rule 9.4.
- NEEDS RECEIPT posture. No number is spoken. "Ten offers" is scene detail, not a statistic.
- Sideways heat: the wronged party is the listing agent and the buyer who lost the house.

## Social Media

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
You sent the offer and never called the listing agent.

I have asked four different top producers about this on my podcast, years apart, and they all said the same thing without being prompted. Most agents do not call. One of them had three listings in multiple offers, around ten offers each, and not one agent picked up the phone to ask whether the offer arrived.

Here is the part agents miss. The listing agent is not annoyed by the call. She is reading the silence. And what she reads into it is how committed you are, and whether you will be reachable ten days before closing when something breaks.

Your offer is a document. Your call is the proof that a competent person is attached to it.

Send it, then call inside five minutes. Say who you are, say which property, and ask when the seller plans to review.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "You sent the offer and never called her." - Family 9 Swap/List, Berger emotion: anxiety.
2. "Listing agents can tell who is going to be a problem." - Family 8 Cohort Callout, Berger emotion: curiosity.
3. "Ten offers came in. Nobody called." - Family 4 System Indictment, Berger emotion: anger.

**Pattern interrupt:** One Prop. The phone is visible for the whole hook and gone by the payoff, which is the physical version of the swap.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the gap opens on a thing the viewer already did today. It is not a claim to evaluate, it is a fact about their afternoon.
- Share/save driver (Berger): listing agents forward this to buyer agents. The sideways target means the person sharing it is the wronged party, which is the cheapest share there is.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "a question you never hear" at roughly eighteen seconds re-hooks by naming something happening off screen, about them.

**The dissent:** Kane argues the hook is too close to an accusation to survive a cold audience, and that a viewer who did not send an offer today scrolls at word three. The A/B is variant 2, which opens on the listing agent's judgment instead of the viewer's behavior and lets the viewer decide whether it is about them.
