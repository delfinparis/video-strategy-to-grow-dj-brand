# Telling Buyers To Wait Until Spring

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Buyer agents
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Whip (camera on a for-sale sign or a street with fall leaves for one beat, whip to face on "Hey Realtors").

## Shareable Moment
> "Shop when sellers are ready to bargain."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, telling your buyers to wait until spring is really stupid.

Realtor.com found listing prices usually drop three and a half percent from the summer high by early fall. If they wait for spring, they miss it.

Here's the solution - get your serious buyers touring homes this month.

Say this. "Redfin says October is when Chicago buyers usually get the best deals. Sellers who listed this summer may be open to a lower price now. Let's tour three homes this week. If none fit, we wait."

Shop when sellers are ready to bargain.

## Production Notes
- The Whip: one beat on a for-sale sign or a leafy fall street, then whip to face on "Hey Realtors." The season is the point, so let the leaves show.
- Contrarian on purpose: "wait for spring, there'll be more houses" sounds like good advice to most agents. That is why the hook works.
- "If none fit, we wait" keeps the pitch honest. The ask is three showings, not a purchase.
- Agents outside Chicago: Redfin's list also names Boston, Minneapolis, New Brunswick and Orlando for early October, and several Ohio metros, Philadelphia and Phoenix for mid to late October. Swap the city only if it is on Redfin's list.
- Post in October. After October the SAY THIS line goes stale; do not post it in November.
- Rule 6: "Say this" frames the words as the viewer's script to a client, not D.J.'s own deal.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "Realtor.com found listing prices usually drop three and a half percent from the summer high by early fall."
  - Source: Realtor.com, "Realtor.com: September 27th - October 3rd Marks the Best Time to Buy a Home in 2026," press release, 2026-09-14: https://mediaroom.realtor.com/2026-09-14-Realtor-com-R-September-27th-October-3rd-Marks-the-Best-Time-to-Buy-a-Home-in-2026
  - Exact wording: "Listing prices typically fall 3.5% from their seasonal high by the Best Week. This year, that seasonal shift could mean roughly $14,000 in savings compared with the summer peak on a median-priced home of about $416,000."
  - Who was measured: Realtor.com listing data (listing prices, not sale prices), national.
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Typically" said as "usually"; "by the Best Week" (Sep 27-Oct 3) said as "by early fall." "Seasonal high" said as "summer high," matching the release's own "summer peak." Spoken as a listing-price figure, not a sale price.
- **Claim:** "If they wait for spring, they miss it."
  - Source: editorial reading of the same seasonal pattern (prices peak in summer and ease into fall), supported by Redfin's methodology note in the release below: "Starting from the spring trough, the week when homes sell closest to (or above) their list price..." (original uses a dash).
  - Status: commentary on a sourced seasonal pattern. No number spoken.
- **Claim:** "Redfin says October is when Chicago buyers usually get the best deals."
  - Source: Redfin, "October Is the Best Time For Homebuyers to Score a Deal in These Major U.S. Metros," Celia Fernandez, 2026-09-22: https://www.redfin.com/news/october-best-time-to-buy-home-us-metros/
  - Exact wording: "Early October is typically the best time for homebuyers to score a deal in Boston, Chicago, and a few other U.S. metros, according to a Home Economics and Redfin analysis." Metro table, Chicago, IL: "Best Deals: Early October."
  - Who was measured: Redfin/Home Economics analysis of the seasonal discount off original list price ("the analysis was originally completed in April").
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Early October" said as "October" because the script posts mid to late October; the release's headline itself frames the whole month ("October Is the Best Time...").
- **Claim:** "Sellers who listed this summer may be open to a lower price now."
  - Source: same Redfin release, Asad Khan, Redfin senior economist: "Sellers who put their home on the market over the summer might be open to dropping their price or offering concessions now that we're past Labor Day."
  - Status: confirmed, paraphrased for grade 7 with the hedge kept ("may be open").
- **Claim:** "Let's tour three homes this week. If none fit, we wait."
  - Source: editorial recommendation (the action), not a statistic.
- **Not used (on purpose):** "31.9% more active listings" and "competition historically 30.1% below its annual peak" (both tied to the Sep 27-Oct 3 week, now past; one stat per script). Chicago's Best Week row (listing prices 5.8% below peak) for the same reason.
- **Fabrication audit:** one statistic spoken, once, with its owner.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:
1. "Hey Realtors, telling your buyers to wait until spring is really stupid." - Berger emotion: anxiety (the advice they gave last week).
2. "Hey Realtors, telling buyers to take the fall off is really stupid." - Berger emotion: surprise.
3. "Hey Realtors, putting your buyers on hold until the spring market is really stupid." - Berger emotion: anxiety, sharper picture of the habit.

**Pattern interrupt:** The Whip confirmed. A fall street is the whole argument in one frame. Different from STUPID-030 (Walk-Toward).

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): it calls advice most agents think is careful "stupid," which opens the loop. The price drop closes it at 0:10.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the SAY THIS is a ready text for every buyer who said "maybe next year." Agents send it to their team in October.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "If none fit, we wait" at 0:34 is the surprise turn. The script pushes, then takes the pressure off.

**The dissent:** Byron says the Chicago line limits reach outside Chicago and wants "Redfin says October is a top month for deals in sixteen big metros." Miller says one city keeps it concrete and D.J.'s audience is Chicago. Decision: keep Chicago. Experiment: if non-Chicago views run above 60%, cut a national version with the sixteen-metro line.
