# Telling Sellers To Call You When They're Ready

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Listing agents
**Post Date:** TBD

## Shareable Moment
> "Start the plan now, not when they call."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, telling a homeowner to call you when they're ready to sell is really stupid.

Realtor.com found nearly eight in ten recent sellers wish they'd listed sooner. If you wait for them to call, they'll most likely wait too long.

Here's the solution - book a walk-through now and hand them a plan with dates on it.

Say this. Let's walk through the house this week. If you want to sell in the spring, we'll pick an April list date today. We'll finish repairs by February and take photos in March.

Start the plan now, not when they call.

## Production Notes
- The Stop: walk fast for one beat, then plant and lean in on "call you when they're ready." Differs from STUPID-030's Walk-Toward and STUPID-033's One Prop.
- The April list date, February repairs and March photos are an example plan the agent says to the homeowner. They are not a claim about the best week to sell. D.J.: if you want a different month, change all three together.
- "They'll most likely wait too long" is the plain reading of the stat (most recent sellers wish they had listed sooner), said as a consequence, not a second number.
- Target is `self`; heat about 4.2. Friction at the habit (a polite brush-off), never at the agent.
- Words in SAY THIS are what the agent says to the homeowner. D.J. does not practice (Rule 6).

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** The opener "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:
1. "Hey Realtors, telling a homeowner to call you when they're ready to sell is really stupid." - Berger emotion: surprise (it sounds polite).
2. "Hey Realtors, saying 'just call me when you're ready' is really stupid." - Berger emotion: recognition, amusement.
3. "Hey Realtors, waiting for a seller to call you back in the spring is really stupid." - Berger emotion: anxiety.

**Pattern interrupt:** The Stop. Confirmed: the plant-and-lean lands on the polite line the viewer has said a hundred times.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): contrarian. It calls a courteous habit stupid, and the viewer stays for the reason.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): SAY THIS is a four-line script with months in it. Agents can say it at the next "we're thinking about it" conversation.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the months in SAY THIS (April, February, March) are the concrete payoff at about 0:25.

**The dissent:** Eric Simon says variant 2 is the line every agent has actually said, so it wins on recognition and sends. Heath says the quote inside a quote is harder to follow by ear on one listen. Decision: keep variant 1 for the first post. **Experiment:** run variant 2 as the A/B; if the quote version wins on sends, the quoted-phrase format becomes a test for other tips.
