## Script

### HOOK (0:00-0:03)
The silence after acceptance is why your client never refers you. One sentence at the signing table ends it.

*Production note: Walk-Toward. Camera at arm's length, walking straight at it, background sliding past. The motion carries the first line so the delivery does not have to push. Say "never refers you" plainly. This is a cost, not an accusation, and the difference is entirely in the tone. No on-screen text; captions.ai builds captions from the audio.*

### STORY (0:03-0:11)
Under contract everyone relaxes. Then the inspection response goes out, nobody tells the buyer, they assume it died.

*Production note: The whole of Act 2, compressed. The turn is "nobody tells the buyer," and the payoff of the turn is the client's private conclusion in the last three words. Deliver "they assume it died" as the buyer's experience, not as a punchline. No named market, no dollar figure, no day count, because a specific here reads as a real transaction.*

### PAYOFF (0:11-0:29)
So say the window out loud before you sign. "You'll hear from me every Friday, inside four hours on anything urgent." Then put Friday in your phone. Silence isn't neutral. It's an answer.

*Production note: The quoted sentence is the payload and it needs air on both sides. "Then put Friday in your phone" is the executable half, and it has to sound like an instruction rather than a flourish, because a standard nobody scheduled is a standard nobody keeps. Land on "answer" and stop. No sign-off.*

**Word count:** 70 spoken words | **Estimated duration:** ~29 seconds

**Length note:** Inside the 22-30 second band. The quoted standard runs eleven words on its own and it is the reason the viewer stayed, so the words came out of Act 2 instead.

## Data Source
- **No statistic is spoken in this script, and that is a deliberate correction.** The bank entry for ST-0002 carries a receipt attributed to NAR with the year 2026 and the claim that Gen X and Boomer buyers rank responsiveness above process knowledge. Re-verification on 2026-08-23 does not support that claim as written. The NAR article at the banked URL is dated **April 4, 2024** and draws on the **NAR 2024 Home Buyers and Sellers Generational Trends Report**. It reports buyer communication preferences, not a ranking against process knowledge. Rather than soften the banked figure, the claim was cut from the spoken script.
- **What the source does support, used in the long captions only:**
  - Source: NAR, *What Buyers and Sellers Want Most From Real Estate Agents*, published April 4, 2024, drawing on the NAR 2024 Home Buyers and Sellers Generational Trends Report. Buyers reported wanting agents who personally call to inform them of activity (73%), who send property information and communicate by text (71%), and who send postings as soon as a property is listed or the price changes (70%). <https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/sales-marketing/what-buyers-sellers-want-most-from-real-estate-agents>
  - Who was measured: home buyers, self-reported survey responses. Not agents, not transaction data.
  - Status: confirmed, re-verified 2026-08-23. Cited with its real 2024 publication year everywhere it appears.
  - **Why it stays out of the spoken line.** A 2024 buyer-preference figure spoken on camera in 2026 reads as current data, and at 70 words the citation would have to be compressed to the point where the year disappears. In a caption it can carry its year and its survey population, so that is where it lives.
- **The claim that silence costs referrals is framed as D.J.'s position, not as a measured effect.** No sourced figure ties post-contract silence to referral rate, so the script argues it rather than reporting it, and the captions do the same.
- **The quoted standard is a script the viewer writes for themselves.** "Every Friday, inside four hours" is a commitment the agent chooses and can change. It is not presented as an industry benchmark or a rule.
- **Nothing here names a party.** No brokerage, franchise, team, coach, coaching program, or software product appears in the script or in any caption.

**Pattern interrupt:** Walk-Toward, confirmed. Eric Simon pushed for move 1, The Stop, arguing that a hard plant on "silence" is the more literal read. The board declined on register: a plant plus a heat-4 first line is two pieces of aggression aimed at a viewer who has not been accused of anything yet, and this script's entire safety mechanism is that it hands over a sentence rather than a verdict. Continuous forward motion keeps the tone collegial while the words do the work.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the curiosity gap is located in the promise, not the accusation. "One sentence at the signing table ends it" names a cheap, specific reward. Heath's ruling on an earlier draft, which opened on "acceptance isn't the finish line," was that it stated a position rather than opening a loop, and it also spent the script's one permitted negation before the close needed it.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): this is a broker-to-new-agent forward, which is exactly the Facebook signal in the Rule 9.5 map. The quoted standard is short enough to be retold verbatim, and Berger's valuable-virality check passes because D.J.'s point survives the retelling even when the framing does not.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the escalation runs cost, then cause, then fix, and the client's private conclusion at 0:11 is the escalation beat. Hormozi's cut was "then set the recurring task before you leave the table," reduced to "then put Friday in your phone" for four words that buy the loop-back its pause.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Chris Do is the holdout and his objection is that the script is entirely mechanical. He wants one honest beat about why agents go quiet after acceptance, which is not laziness but the feeling that there is nothing worth reporting, and he argues the permission slip in naming that would earn more follows than the sentence earns saves. Alex Hormozi and Justin Welsh held the line on length: at 70 words a vulnerable beat costs the quoted standard, and the standard is the deliverable. Test variant 2, which opens on the mirror instead of the cost, and film a second cut that adds one line naming the "I have nothing to report" instinct before the fix. If the identity-lane version holds completion and pulls longer comments, it belongs in the roughly biweekly Rule 10.7 slot rather than here.
