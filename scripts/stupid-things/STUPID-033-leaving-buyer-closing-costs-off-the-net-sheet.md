# Leaving Buyer Closing Costs Off The Net Sheet

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Listing agents
**Post Date:** TBD

## Shareable Moment
> "Show them the smaller check first."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, leaving buyer closing costs off your seller's take-home estimate is really stupid.

Zillow found about two in three sellers covered some or all of the buyer's closing costs. Skip that, and their check is smaller than you promised.

Here's the solution - put a line for buyer closing costs on every estimate, at the listing meeting.

Tell the seller this. Here's what you walk away with if the buyer asks for nothing. Here's what you walk away with if you cover part of their closing costs. Plan on the second number.

Show them the smaller check first.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** The opener "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:
1. "Hey Realtors, leaving buyer closing costs off your seller's take-home estimate is really stupid." - Berger emotion: anxiety.
2. "Hey Realtors, handing a seller one take-home number is really stupid." - Berger emotion: curiosity (which number is missing?).
3. "Hey Realtors, promising a seller a check you can't deliver is really stupid." - Berger emotion: anxiety, sharper.

**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat (palm-to-camera "stop" on the hook; one finger, then two, on the two take-home numbers). Youshaei first backed One Prop (a sheet of paper); switched because STUPID-032, the post before it, uses One Prop.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the hook names a document every listing agent hands out, then WHY opens the gap with a number they did not plan for.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): SAY THIS is two sentences an agent can paste into a listing presentation tonight. That is the save.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the second "Here's what you walk away with" at about 0:27 is the turn; the viewer hears the missing line appear.

**The dissent:** Hormozi wants a dollar example on screen ("on a $400,000 sale, 1% is $4,000"). Miller and Heath say an invented example reads as a typical concession and the pack has no typical number (Rule 1). Decision: no example in the spoken script. **Experiment:** if comments ask "how much do sellers usually give," answer with a sourced figure in a follow-up, not in this one.
