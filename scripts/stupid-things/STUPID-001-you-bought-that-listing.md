# You Bought That Listing

> **WOW: Tactical specificity plus a contrarian take. The industry frames overpricing as a seller problem the agent then has to manage. This names it as an agent decision made at the appointment, before the seller ever did anything wrong, and hands over the exact sentence that makes the honest number survivable. Realtor.com's June 2026 timing analysis puts a number on the spread between the house that sells and the house that sits.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You can win a listing with somebody else's number. You just can't sell it."

**Series:** Stupid Things Realtors Do (STUPID-001) | **Hook Family:** Cohort Callout | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Gate:** none
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Best on a residential block with a for-sale sign that has obviously been up a while.
**Pattern interrupt:** Location Cold-Open. Start planted in front of a house with a sign in the yard, not mid-stride. Say "Listing agents" from a stop, then start walking on "You didn't win that listing." The sign is the only B-roll this needs and it is free on almost any Chicago walk.

## Spoken Script (~71 seconds, walk and talk)

### THE STUPID THING (0:00-0:07)
Listing agents. You didn't win that listing. You bought it with a number you knew was wrong.

### THE MIRROR (0:07-0:19)
You're in the kitchen, the seller names a price the comps don't support, and you know the agent who came Tuesday already said yes. So you do too.

### THE TURN (0:19-0:28)
You tell yourself you'll walk it down in a few weeks. You won't. And Realtor.com put a number on what that costs.

### THE RECEIPT (0:28-0:47)
A house that closes around week four beats the market average. One still sitting at week eighteen comes in under it. More than three points of spread, and it's your seller's money. The average house closes under asking now.

### THE FIX (0:47-1:02)
Here's what you do. Before your next appointment, write this down. I'll tell you what your house is worth, and I won't tell you what you want to hear. Say it before you give a number. Then hold your number.

### LOOP-BACK (1:02-1:07)
You can win a listing with somebody else's number. You just can't sell it.

STUPID-001 is about *the number you agreed to before there was anything to cut*, and the close is a sentence you say at the appointment, not a clause you sign after. Different source, different metric, different action. **Space them at least a week apart**, and post NF-067 first if both go out, because its close only makes sense to someone who already believes the price was wrong on day one.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "A house under contract around week four beats the market average on price. One still sitting at week eighteen comes in under it. More than three points of spread."
  - Source: Realtor.com, "By Week Four, Your Listing Is Either Getting Offers or Price Cuts," published 2026-06-11 ([link](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/by-week-four-your-listing-is-either-getting-offers-or-price-cuts-302797293.html)). Report language: "homes that close at the four week mark sell for 1.8% more relative to asking price than the average home sold in the same period" and "those sitting at 18 weeks close 1.3% below expectations." Realtor.com's own summary characterizes the gap as more than 3 percentage points between best and worst timing outcomes.
  - Who was measured: U.S. home sales sourced from deed or MLS records combined with Realtor.com listing histories, comparing 2021-2022 against 2025-2026.
  - Economist quoted in the release: Joel Berner, senior economist, Realtor.com.
  - Status: confirmed. Verified 2026-08-12.
  - Note on the spoken form: the script says "more than three points of spread" rather than reciting 1.8 and 1.3 separately, because three numbers in one beat breaks the two-numbers-per-line rule and because the spread is Realtor.com's own framing, not our arithmetic.

- **Claim:** "The average house closes under asking now. That wasn't true in the boom."
  - Source: Same Realtor.com release, 2026-06-11. The report states the average home now closes below its asking price, reversing pandemic-era patterns when homes sold above asking. The same analysis notes price reductions peaked at week three in 2021 and have pushed out to week six in 2026.
  - Who was measured: same dataset as above.
  - Status: confirmed. The week three versus week six figures are deliberately left out of the spoken script to keep the beat to one receipt.

- **Claim (editorial framing, not a finding):** "You're in the kitchen, the seller names a price the comps don't support, and you know the agent who came Tuesday already said yes."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's editorial framing of the mechanism. This is the sanctioned mirror-moment scene (the viewer is the character), not a claim about a specific appointment. No named agent, no named seller, no invented number.
  - Status: editorial framing, labeled as such.

- **Causation caveat:** The data shows houses that go under contract early sell closer to asking and houses that sit sell below it. It does not prove that a lower list price causes an early contract, and homes still sitting at week eighteen are plausibly the most mispriced at list to begin with. The script's claim stays inside what the source supports: the spread is real and the seller pays it. The close is a conversation change, not a promise of a specific dollar saving.

- **Overlap check:** Does not reuse NF-067's REcolorado Denver figures ($2,321 / $28,278), its Realtor.com inventory count, or its 20 percent July price-cut share. Different release, different metric, different close.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the gap opens on "bought it," which is the wrong verb for what happened and the viewer has to stay to find out why it is the right one. Curse of Knowledge check passes. No MLS literacy, no local market knowledge, and no prior script required.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the emotion is high-arousal and it is the useful kind, recognition with a way out. Social Currency runs both directions, an agent who holds their number sends this to feel vindicated and an agent who didn't sends it to a teammate. Valuable-virality check passes: retold in one sentence ("you agreed to the price, that's on you") D.J.'s point survives. Hormozi signs off because the payload is one sentence and takes ten seconds to write down.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the turn is two words, "You won't," landing at roughly 0:20. It flips the register from a scene the viewer is enjoying to an accusation they can't dodge, so the second half is a different experience than the first.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Eric Simon and Chris Do split on the target.
