# You Sent The Contract With Blanks In It

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD

## Shareable Moment
> "You never find out that's why you lost."

## Script (~30 seconds, 78 words)

### HOOK (0:00-0:01.5) - 8 words
You sent the contract with blanks in it.

### VERDICT (0:01.5-0:03.5) - 5 words
That's really stupid. Here's why.

### TENSION (0:03.5-0:08) - 10 words
Misspelled name, an old form, a price that doesn't match.

### THE POINT (0:08-0:22) - 38 words
Nobody calls you out. The other agent reads a careless document, decides the rest of this deal will be careless too, and tells her seller to take the cleaner offer. You never find out that's why you lost.

### PAYOFF (0:22-0:30) - 17 words
Read the whole thing out loud before you send it. Out loud catches what your eyes skip.

**Word count:** 78 spoken words (68-84; counted, not estimated)

**What got cut:** The six-item check from the page, including which four fields are wrong most often. That is caption material. On camera the single instruction, read it out loud, is the one that survives.

## Production Notes
- Walk-Toward. D.J. walks into the lens on "You never find out that's why you lost," which is the line that has to land flat.
- Self target, capped at 4.3. The cost is to the viewer, and the script gets its heat from the specificity of the loss, never from a harder verdict about the person.
- NEEDS RECEIPT posture. No number is spoken.
- No form name, no state form, no brokerage. The friction is on the blanks.

## Social Media

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
You sent the contract with blanks in it.

A misspelled name. An old version of the form. A blank line where an initial goes. A purchase price that does not match the number written out in words.

Here is what that actually costs, and it is not what agents think. It is almost never legal. It is inference. The agent on the other side reads a careless document and concludes that the rest of this transaction will be careless too, and then she prices that into how she advises her seller.

In a multiple offer, nobody calls to tell you that is why you lost.

Read the whole contract out loud before you send it. Out loud catches what your eyes skip. Then check the four that are wrong most often: legal names exactly as they appear on the pre-approval, the address including the unit number, the price in both numerals and words, and the date.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "You sent the contract with blanks in it." - Family 1 Mirror, Berger emotion: anxiety.
2. "Nobody tells you why you lost that offer." - Family 7 Forbidden, Berger emotion: curiosity.
3. "Your paperwork is a forecast, and they're reading it." - Family 4 System Indictment, Berger emotion: anxiety.

**Pattern interrupt:** Walk-Toward on the loss line.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the gap is the unknown loss. The viewer cannot check whether this has happened to them, which is exactly why it nags.
- Share/save driver (Berger): listing agents share it sideways even though the script is written at the viewer, because it says out loud what they have been thinking.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "Nobody calls you out" resets the stakes at eight seconds by telling the viewer the feedback loop is broken.

**The dissent:** Heath objects that "read it out loud" is a weak physical swap compared to the lane's usual do-it-Monday instruction, and that the four-field check is the real fix. The A/B: run the four fields as the payoff and move "out loud" to the caption.
