# Letting Your Newest Review Get A Year Old

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Push-In (start at arm's length, pull the phone in on "a year old").

## Shareable Moment
> "Keep your newest review under three months old."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, letting your newest online review get a year old is really stupid.

BrightLocal found 74 percent of consumers only care about reviews from the last three months. So to most people, a year-old review doesn't count.

Here's the solution. Ask at closing, and again on the one-year home anniversary.

This week, text your last 20 closed clients your review link. Then set a reminder one year after every closing. That day, text this. Happy one year in the house! Would you write a quick, honest review? Here's the link.

Keep your newest review under three months old.

## Production Notes
- Push-In: start at arm's length, pull the phone toward your face on "a year old."
- Say "seventy-four percent" slowly. "Twenty" closed clients is an instruction, not a stat.

- "Honest" stays in the ask. It keeps this script in line with STUPID-035 (no rewards tied to a positive review).
- Friction at the habit (asking once and forgetting), never at the agent. Rule 9.4. No review site named.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "BrightLocal found 74 percent of consumers only care about reviews from the last three months."
  - Source: BrightLocal, "Local Consumer Review Survey 2026: Star Ratings Keep Rising, Old Reviews Don't Cut It," Feb 11, 2026. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/
  - Exact wording: key-findings box, "74% only care about reviews written in the last three months." Body text: "74% seek reviews written in the last three months."
  - Who was measured: "a representative panel of 1,002 US adult consumers via SurveyMonkey." Consumers on local businesses in general, not real estate agents.
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Written in the last three months" said as "from the last three months."
- **Claim:** "So to most people, a year-old review doesn't count."
  - Source: editorial inference from the stat above (for the 74 percent who only care about the last three months, a 12-month-old review falls outside the window). Not a separate finding.
  - Status: commentary.
