# You Forwarded The Signature Email And Nothing Else

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD

## Shareable Moment
> "You made the person presenting your offer do your work first."

## Script (~30 seconds, 77 words)

### HOOK (0:00-0:01.5) - 8 words
You forwarded the signature email and nothing else.

### VERDICT (0:01.5-0:03.5) - 5 words
That's really stupid. Here's why.

### TENSION (0:03.5-0:08) - 11 words
She can't tell it's an offer, or even who sent it.

### THE POINT (0:08-0:22) - 36 words
She has to open the file and dig out the price, the closing date and every contingency, just to tell her seller what you want. You made the person presenting your offer do your work first.

### PAYOFF (0:22-0:30) - 17 words
Put the price, the money down, the closing date and the contingencies in the email body. Bullets.

**Word count:** 77 spoken words (68-84; counted, not estimated)

**What got cut:** The full nine-line template from the page. On camera that is a list nobody can hold; it lives in the LinkedIn caption where a reader can take it at their own speed.

## Social Media

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
You forwarded the signature request and put nothing in the body of the email.

From the other side it looks like this. A notification arrives that looks like it came from the signing service. She cannot tell it is an offer. She cannot tell which agent sent it without opening the file. And now the person who has to walk your offer into a seller has to reconstruct it first.

Here is the fix, and it is a five minute template you write once.

Above the attachment, put the property and your buyer's name. Purchase price. Earnest money and when it goes up. Loan type, down payment, and the lender's name and number. Closing date, and whether it can move. Every contingency with its days. Any concession, in dollars. Anything that makes this buyer easy. Then one line offering a call.

Save it as a snippet. Fill in the numbers.

**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat. The four flat-hand beats on the payoff are the whole fix, and they read even with the sound off.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): concrete and small. The viewer can picture the exact email, because they sent one this week.
- Share/save driver (Berger): this is a save. The four items are a checklist, and checklists get saved more than opinions do.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "You made the person presenting your offer do your work first" lands at around twenty seconds and reframes the whole scene from sloppy to costly.

**The dissent:** Hormozi argues the payoff is four items where the lane says one physical move, and that a viewer remembers none of them. The A/B: cut to the single highest-value item, the price and closing date in the body, and let the caption carry the rest.
