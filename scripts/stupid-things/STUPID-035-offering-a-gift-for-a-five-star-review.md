# Offering A Gift For A Five-Star Review

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Stop (walk fast, then plant and lean into the lens on "five-star review").

## Shareable Moment
> "Never tie a gift to a review."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, offering clients a gift card for a five-star review is really stupid.

If the gift depends on five stars, you're breaking a federal rule. In 2024 the FTC banned rewards tied to positive reviews.

Here's the solution. Give the closing gift with no strings, and ask for an honest review separately.

Never put the gift and the review in the same message. A week after closing, text this. Would you write an honest review of working with me? Good or bad, I want to hear it.

Never tie a gift to a review.

## Production Notes
- The Stop: walk fast through "Hey Realtors, offering clients a gift card," plant on "five-star review."
- "2024" is said "twenty twenty-four." There is no other number. No fine amount is spoken because the FTC does not publish one on either page; do not ad-lib "thousands of dollars."
- Contrarian on purpose: most agents think a gift card for a review is a nice thank-you. The script names the one version that breaks the rule (the gift depends on a positive review) and leaves the closing gift itself alone.
- "A week after closing" is an editorial choice, not a stat. It keeps the review ask away from the gift.
- Friction at the habit, never the agent. Rule 9.4. No review site or vendor named.
- Not legal advice. The script repeats the rule's plain words and the FTC's own Q&A. If a commenter asks about Google's or Zillow's own review policies, point them to those policies; this build did not open them.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "In 2024 the FTC banned rewards tied to positive reviews."
  - Source: Federal Trade Commission press release, "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials," Aug 14, 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials
  - Exact wording (bullet "Buying Positive or Negative Reviews"): "The final rule prohibits businesses from providing compensation or other incentives conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, either positive or negative. It clarifies that the conditional nature of the offer of compensation or incentive may be expressly or implicitly conveyed."
  - Who/what: a federal trade regulation rule (16 CFR Part 465), not a survey.
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Compensation or other incentives conditioned on ... a particular sentiment, either positive" said as "rewards tied to positive reviews."
- **Claim:** "If the gift depends on five stars, you're breaking a federal rule."
  - Source: FTC, "The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers" (page dated November 2024). https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
  - Exact wording: Q: "Can my business pay incentives for 5-star reviews on third-party review platforms, so long as we ask the reviewers to add a disclosure about the incentive?" A: "No. That conduct would violate Section 465.4."
  - Effective date, same page: "which went into effect on October 21, 2024."
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14).
- **Claim:** "Give the closing gift with no strings, and ask for an honest review separately."
  - Source: editorial recommendation, consistent with the same Q&A: "The rule does not prohibit giving incentives for reviews, as long as there isn't an express or implied requirement that the reviews have to express a particular sentiment." The Q&A also says "failing to disclose incentives could be a violation of the FTC Act," which is why the script separates the gift from the ask entirely instead of offering a gift for any review.
  - Status: commentary.
- **Penalty language (captions only, no amount):** press release: the rule will "allow the agency to seek civil penalties against knowing violators." Q&A: the rule "authorizes courts to impose civil penalties for knowing violations." Neither page states a dollar figure. Confirmed 2026-10-14.
- **Claim (captions only):** "11% of consumers were offered an incentive to write a positive review." BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, Feb 11, 2026 (1,002 US adult consumers). https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/ Exact wording on page. Confirmed 2026-10-14.
  - Note: the same BrightLocal page also says "Offering incentives for reviews isn't illegal, but it violates the guidelines of platforms like Google and Yelp." That matches the FTC Q&A (incentives are not banned unless tied to sentiment). The platform-guideline half is BrightLocal's claim and was not checked against Google's or Yelp's own pages, so it is not used.
- **Fabrication audit:** no statistic spoken. One rule cited by agency and year. No fine amount anywhere.
