# Not Telling Buyers What To Avoid Before Closing

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Buyer agents
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Gesture-On-Beat (palm to the lens, a "stop" hand, on "Hey Realtors").

## Shareable Moment
> "Tell them the rules before they break one."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, not telling buyers what to avoid before closing is really stupid.

A new car loan before closing adds debt. NAR's 2025 survey found too much debt for their income is the top reason lenders say no.

Here's the solution - the day the seller accepts their offer, text them a do-not list.

Text this. The lender checks your credit again before closing. Until we close, don't buy a car. Don't open a new credit card. Don't finance furniture. Don't change jobs. Call me before you move any big money.

Tell them the rules before they break one.

**What got cut:** "Or lease" after "buy a car," and "right" before "before closing" in the text (both to stay at or under 100). The other denial reasons in NAR's table (low credit score, not enough money in reserves) are not spoken; one number, said once.

## Production Notes
- Gesture-On-Beat: palm to the lens on "Hey Realtors," then drop the hand and walk.
- In SAY THIS, count the four "don'ts" on your fingers. It is a list the viewer is meant to copy into a text, so the repeated "Don't" is on purpose.
- Say "twenty twenty-five" for the year.
- Do not say or imply that loans often fall apart. NAR's number is about why lenders turned buyers down, among the 4% who were ever turned down.
- Friction points at the habit (no warning list), never at the agent or the buyer. No lender named.
- "Call me before you move any big money" is the viewer's line to their buyer. D.J. does not practice; the frame "Text this." makes that plain.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "NAR's 2025 survey found too much debt for their income is the top reason lenders say no."
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS, 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (released Nov 2025), Exhibit 5-9, "Buyer Mortgage Application Had Been Rejected From Mortgage Lender," p. 71. PDF: https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf
  - Exact wording: "Four percent of successful home buyers had their mortgage applications rejected by a lender before securing a mortgage. They typically had a median of two rejections. The most common reasons buyers reported being rejected were because of their debt-to-income ratio (45 percent), low credit score (24 percent), not enough money in reserves (13 percent), and because their income could not be verified (10 percent)."
  - Who was measured: recent home buyers (consumer survey), reporting on their own rejections. Base for the reasons is the 4% who had an application denied.
  - Status: confirmed (PDF re-opened 2026-10-14). "Debt-to-income ratio" said as "too much debt for their income"; "most common reason" said as "top reason." The 45% figure is not spoken; the ranking is.
  - Scale caveat: the brief framed this as "when a loan dies." The exhibit is about applications rejected before the buyer secured a mortgage, by buyers who did buy. The script does not say loans die at closing.
- **Claim:** "The lender checks your credit again before closing."
  - Source: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, "When will my lender run or obtain a copy of my credit report?" (last reviewed 2024-12-31): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/when-will-a-lender-run-a-credit-check-or-obtain-a-copy-of-my-credit-report-en-322/
  - Exact wording: "A lender will run a credit check or get a copy of your credit report when you apply for credit, just before you close on a loan, or as part of managing existing credit accounts."
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). Not a statistic.
- **Claim:** "A new car loan before closing adds debt." / the do-not list.
  - Source: editorial. Plain arithmetic (a new loan is new debt) and standard buyer guidance. No statistic implied.
  - Status: commentary.
- **Fabrication audit:** One survey finding spoken, once, with its source and year.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:
1. "Hey Realtors, not telling buyers what to avoid before closing is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 1 Mirror | emotion: anxiety]
2. "Hey Realtors, letting your buyer finance a car before closing is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 6 Named Stakes | emotion: anxiety, recognition]
3. "Hey Realtors, waiting until closing week to warn buyers about new debt is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 1 Mirror | emotion: anxiety]

**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat, confirmed. The "stop" palm matches a script that is a list of things to stop.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the hook opens a loop ("what are they supposed to avoid?") that SAY THIS closes item by item.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the text is copyable word for word. Agents save it for the next accepted offer; brokers forward it to new agents.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the list starts at about 0:24 and each "Don't" is a small new payoff, which carries the viewer to the close.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Eric Simon says variant 2 is the "this is us" line, because every buyer agent has a car-dealership story, and a concrete thing beats "what to avoid." Heath agrees it is more concrete. Miller says variant 2 narrows the tip to cars when the fix is the whole list. Decision: ship variant 1 so the hook matches the fix. **Experiment:** cut variant 2 as the re-hook test and compare 3-second hold.
