# Never Replying To Your Online Reviews

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Walk-Toward (walk straight at the lens on "Hey Realtors").

## Shareable Moment
> "Every review gets a real reply."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, never replying to your online reviews is really stupid.

If you never reply, the people reading your reviews notice. BrightLocal found 42 percent of consumers are unlikely to use a business that never replies.

Here's the solution. Reply to every review within a day, and name one thing from their deal.

Say this. Thanks, Sarah. Getting that house after two lost offers was a big day. Enjoy the new kitchen, and text me when you need a plumber. Then write a fresh reply for the next one.

Every review gets a real reply.

## Production Notes
- Walk-Toward: walk straight at the lens on the hook, slow to a stop on "Here's the solution."
- Say "forty-two percent" slowly. It is the only number in the script.
- "Sarah," "two lost offers," "the new kitchen" and "a plumber" are a sample reply for the viewer to copy the shape of, not a deal of D.J.'s. D.J. does not practice (Rule 6). Keep it as the agent's words to the client.
- "Then write a fresh reply for the next one" is the anti-template line. BrightLocal also found templated replies put off half of consumers; that is a second stat, kept to captions and Data Source so the script says one number once.
- Friction at the habit (letting reviews sit), never at the agent. Rule 9.4. No review site named as wrong.

  - Who was measured: "a representative panel of 1,002 US adult consumers via SurveyMonkey" (BrightLocal's methodology note). Consumers talking about local businesses in general, not real estate agents specifically.
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). Said word for word, with "consumers" in place of "say they're."

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:

**Pattern interrupt:** Walk-Toward confirmed. It pairs with the Cohort Callout and keeps the open simple; STUPID-035 takes The Stop and STUPID-036 the Push-In.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): a plain habit with a cost in the next sentence. The 42 percent is concrete enough to sting.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the sample reply is a template for not using templates; agents save it to copy the shape.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the sample reply at 0:21 is the payoff, read in D.J.'s voice like a real text.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Kane wants variant 2, the contrarian version: most agents reply to the angry review and skip the happy ones. Heath rules against using it as the main hook, because the spoken 42 percent is about businesses that "never reply," not ones that skip good reviews, so variant 2 would need a different receipt. **Experiment:** if this one does well, build variant 2 as its own tip on BrightLocal's "responds only to positive (45%) or negative (47%)" finding after re-opening it.
