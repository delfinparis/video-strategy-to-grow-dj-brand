# Writing Open Floor Plan In Your Listing

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Listing agents
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Walk-Toward (walk straight at the lens on "open floor plan").

## Shareable Moment
> "Write down what the house is made of."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, writing "open floor plan" in your listing description is really stupid.

It sounds like a selling point. But Zillow found homes with an open floor plan sold for 0.2 percent less than expected.

Here's the solution. Cut that phrase and name the materials in the house.

Walk the house and write down every natural material by name. White oak floors. Walnut cabinets. Soapstone counters. Zillow says homes with materials like these can sell for up to 3.5 percent more.

Write down what the house is made of.

## Production Notes
- Walk-Toward on the hook; stop walking on "Here's the solution."
- Say the numbers as "point two percent" and "three and a half percent." Two numbers, two sentences, never in the same line.
- Contrarian on purpose: "open floor plan" is the phrase most agents and sellers think helps. Nearly half of homeowners in Zillow's survey picked it as the feature most likely to raise the price.
- Only name materials the house actually has. The three examples are Zillow's own; they are not a shopping list.
- SAY THIS is orders, not "I do." D.J. does not write listings (Rule 6).
- Friction at the phrase, never the agent or the seller. Rule 9.4.

## Data Source

  - Source: same release. Exact wording: "By contrast, homes with natural materials like white oak flooring, walnut cabinets and soapstone countertops can sell for as much as 3.5% more. Only 9% of homeowners recognize this advantage."

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the middle varies:

**Pattern interrupt:** Walk-Toward confirmed. STUPID-032 runs One Prop, so no repeat inside the batch.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the phrase is one agents type out of habit, so the hook calls out their own copy. The loop is "why would that hurt?" and the 0.2% closes it at 0:12.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): surprise plus a word list. Agents save it to rewrite their next description.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the three material names at 0:24 are concrete and fast, a second reward after the stat.

**The dissent:** Byron wants the 45% in the spoken WHY ("almost half of sellers think this adds value") because the gap between belief and result is the story. Hormozi says three numbers in 40 seconds is a spreadsheet. Decision: keep two spoken numbers, carry the 45% in the captions, and test a version that swaps "It sounds like a selling point" for "Almost half of homeowners think it raises the price" if saves come in low.
