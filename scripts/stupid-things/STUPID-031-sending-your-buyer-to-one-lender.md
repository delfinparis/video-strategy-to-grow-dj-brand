# Sending Your Buyer To Just One Lender

**Pillar:** Practice | **Avatars:** Buyer agents
**Post Date:** TBD
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Stop (walk fast one beat, plant and lean in on "one lender").

## Shareable Moment
> "Make them compare lenders before they pick one."

## Teleprompter

Hey Realtors, sending your buyer to just one lender is really stupid.

With one quote, there's nothing to compare. Realtor.com found a very competitive lender is worth about fifty-eight hundred dollars more house than a typical one.

Here's the solution - have every buyer get three written Loan Estimates in the same week.

Say this. Before you pick a lender, get a Loan Estimate from three of them this week. That's the lender's official cost sheet. Lay them side by side. Compare the rate and the total cost, not just the rate.

Make them compare lenders before they pick one.

## Production Notes
- The Stop on "one lender." Plant, lean in, then walk again on WHY.
- Say the number slowly: "fifty-eight hundred dollars more house." It is the only number in the script.
- "Loan Estimate" is the federal form name, so it stays; the next line explains it in plain words ("the lender's official cost sheet").
- Contrarian on purpose: sending a buyer to one trusted lender feels like help. The script never says the trusted lender is bad. It says one quote can't be checked.
- Friction points at the habit (one quote), never at the agent or any lender. No lender, bank or company named. No talk of referral fees or who pays whom.
- Not a contradiction of NF-084 ("get them in front of a lender this week"). That one is about timing; this one is about how many lenders.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "Realtor.com found a very competitive lender is worth about fifty-eight hundred dollars more house than a typical one."
  - Source: Realtor.com, "Homebuyers Can Beat the Headline Mortgage Rate by Shopping, Saving and Strengthening Credit," press release, 2026-10-01: https://mediaroom.realtor.com/2026-10-01-Homebuyers-Can-Beat-the-Headline-Mortgage-Rate-by-Shopping,-Saving-and-Strengthening-Credit
  - Exact wording: "The difference between a typical retail lender and a very competitive one was approximately 19 basis points, worth about $5,800 in buying power on a $2,000 monthly principal-and-interest budget."
  - Who was measured: mortgages, not people's opinions. Freddie Mac's Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset, 1,351,302 purchase loans originated January 2023 to December 2025 (per the release's methodology).
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). Spoken changes, meaning unchanged: "retail" dropped; "buying power" said as "more house"; the $2,000 monthly principal-and-interest condition is not spoken (it is in the captions and here). The figure is for that budget and scales with it.
- **Claim (support for the fix, not spoken as a quote):** comparing several written lender offers and the full terms.
  - Source: same release. Jake Krimmel, senior economist at Realtor.com: "Buyers should seek multiple loan estimates, compare the full terms [em dash in original] not just the advertised rate." Also: "Compare total costs: Rate is only one component of affordability. Buyers should assess mortgage insurance, closing costs and any discount points alongside the interest rate."
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Three" lenders is the tip's recommendation, not a number from the source.
- **Not spoken (LinkedIn caption only):** Freddie Mac, "When Rates Are Higher, Borrowers Who Shop Around Save More," research brief, 2023-02-16: https://www.freddiemac.com/research/insight/20230216-when-rates-are-higher-borrowers-who-shop-around-save . Exact wording: "Two rate quotes could have saved as much as $600 annually." / "At least four rate quotes could have saved more than $1,200 annually." Based on October-November 2022 data. Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14).
- **Not used (on purpose):** "A strong lender offer can be worth roughly twice as much as moving from a 690 to a 720 credit score" (Krimmel, same release). True and confirmed, but a second comparison in a 40-second script.
- **Fabrication audit:** One statistic spoken, once, with its source. "Three" and "this week" are the tip's instructions, not data.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B).** "Hey Realtors," and "is really stupid." are fixed; only the thing in the middle varies:
1. "Hey Realtors, sending your buyer to just one lender is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 2 Sacred Cow | emotion: surprise]
2. "Hey Realtors, letting your buyer take the first rate they hear is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 1 Mirror | emotion: anxiety]
3. "Hey Realtors, skipping a second lender quote is really stupid." [8 Cohort Callout, thing framed as 9 Swap | emotion: curiosity]

**Pattern interrupt:** The Stop, confirmed. The plant lands on "one lender," the exact habit being called out.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): it calls a habit agents think is service (the trusted-lender handoff) stupid. The loop is "why would that be wrong?", and WHY answers it with a dollar figure.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the SAY THIS is a text an agent can send a buyer tonight. Practical value plus a number a buyer understands ("more house").
- Retention move (MrBeast): the $5,800 lands at about 0:12, the re-hook before the fix.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Byron says variant 2 is more honest about the cost and less likely to read as an attack on preferred-lender relationships, which every agent has. Hormozi says variant 1 is the bigger stop because it sounds wrong at first. Decision: ship variant 1, because the contrarian line is what the format is built on. **Experiment:** if comments argue "my lender is the best," film variant 2 as the re-cut.
