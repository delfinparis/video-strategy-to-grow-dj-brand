## Production Notes
- **This is the plain-language re-cut.** If a future edit tightens it back toward the original ("Seven listings open in ten"), that is the failure Rule 5's comprehension rule exists to stop. Cut an idea, never the grammar.
- The verdict is in the hook and it is said once. Do not add a second "this is dumb" in the payoff; the payoff belongs to the fix.
- "A buyer's agent" is a she throughout, on purpose. One consistent person is easier to follow in thirty seconds than a role noun repeated four times.
- NEEDS RECEIPT posture. No statistic is spoken. "Eight houses," "seven," "ten minutes," "thirty seconds" are scene detail and the test itself, not cited research. Do not add a stat in the edit.
- Sideways heat: the friction points at leaving a listing inaccessible. It never names a brokerage, an MLS, a lockbox brand, or an agent. Supra and ShowingTime stay out.
- Evergreen. A slipped filming day costs nothing, and "tonight" can become "today" without a rewrite.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the verdict opens the loop by withholding the thing, not the judgment. "A stupid way to lose a sale" makes the viewer ask which way, and TENSION answers in one sentence instead of making them decode it. Heath's objection to the first cut was that the tapper was hearing the whole scene in his head while the listener got three fragments.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): Eric Simon's test passes now in a way it did not before. A buyer's agent forwards this to a listing agent with "this is you," and the reason survives the retelling, because the reason is one sentence long.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "and before you called back, her buyer had flown home" is the re-hook at roughly fifteen seconds, and it resolves the afternoon the viewer has been following rather than introducing a new fact.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Brendan Kane objects that "Here's a stupid way to..." is a template, and a template is a hook family, not a hook. He argues it will fatigue fast if every tip in the lane opens the same way and that the verdict should move to TENSION on every second script. The A/B: run variant 2, which folds the verdict and the behavior into one line, and watch whether naming the thing inside the verdict holds the first three seconds better than promising it does.
