# You're missing sellers already in your database

**Filming:** Walk and talk, 30 seconds. Visual open: The Whip (camera on a row of houses for one beat, whip to face on "sellers").

## Teleprompter

You're missing sellers already in your database.

Most of your contacts don't even have an address saved.

At Zillow Unlock, Tiffany Gelzinis and Gabe Cordova showed the problem. In a typical database, about one in five contacts has an address. Without one, you can't tell if your buyer owns a home.

Pull every contact missing an address. Ask where they live. Some of those buyers are your next listings.

## Production Notes

- **Speaker on screen:** Tiffany Gelzinis and Gabe Cordova headshots + Follow Up Boss / Gelzinis Group logo lower-third on "At Zillow Unlock, Tiffany Gelzinis and Gabe Cordova showed the problem" (about 0:06.5-0:10). Two faces side by side; no other manual text.
- **The Whip** sells the idea: houses on screen, then "you're missing sellers." The houses are the sellers the viewer can't see.
- **Hook changed in round 2** from "Your database is hiding sellers from you" (a database can't hide anything; Rule 5 says name a real actor) to a plain cost line. Same claim, no metaphor.
- **Friction points at the data gap, not the agent.** Hook family 4: the system (an empty address field) is the thing failing the agent.
- **Receipt problem, two numbers dropped on purpose.** The slate listed "59% sell before they buy, 73% of those use the same agent." Both appear in the session summary, but I could not trace either to a public report. Zillow's own past Consumer Housing Trends data shows different figures for this question (58% sold first in the 2023 edition, 54% in 2024; about 65% used the same agent for both). And Zillow's Dec 30, 2025 release uses 59% for a different fact: sellers who hired the first agent they spoke with. That looks like a possible mix-up in the AI summary. The reel runs on the address stat only, and THE POINT makes the seller logic plainly ("you can't tell if your buyer owns a home") without a number. If the slides confirm 59/73 with a source, they can go in the LinkedIn first comment, not the script.
- **No product named.** The session named CRM and data-enrichment partners. The payoff is a manual action any agent can do with any CRM.
- **"Ask where they live"** is the session's own fix (they even suggested emailing contacts to collect missing addresses). Keep it plain; don't say "enrichment."

## Council Review

Round 2 (fresh eyes): new hook. "Your database is hiding sellers" gave a feeling to a database (Rule 5); "You're missing sellers already in your database" says the same cost flat. 59%/73% stay out.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "You're missing sellers already in your database."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: anxiety]
2. "Most of your contacts have no address saved."  [hook_family: 1 Mirror | emotion: recognition, mild alarm]

**Pattern interrupt:** The Whip, confirmed. Houses first, face second, on "sellers."

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): "missing sellers" opens the loop; "no address" closes it with something concrete and checkable in two minutes.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): a team leader sends this to the team with "fix your address field." Ten-minute task, listing at the end.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the one-in-five number lands right at the re-hook and makes the viewer picture their own list.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Hormozi still wants "most people sell before they buy" back for the bigger reward. Heath holds the line: two unverified numbers in 30 seconds lose trust faster than they win it, and the 59% looks like a mix-up in the AI summary. Decision: no 59/73 on camera. Experiment: test variant 2 (the Mirror) against variant 1 on 3-second hold.
