## Production Notes

- **Speaker lower-third:** Amanda Pendleton headshot + Zillow logo lower-third on "At Zillow Unlock, Amanda Pendleton shared Zillow's 2026 consumer survey" (about 0:06-0:09). No other manual text.
- **Receipt change from the slate, on purpose.** The slate angle used "72% of sellers use AI for pricing strategy before talking to an agent." The same session summary also says only 64% of sellers use AI at all during their search. Seventy-two percent of all sellers cannot use AI for pricing if only sixty-four percent use AI, so the 72% is almost certainly a share of AI-using sellers, not of all sellers. Said on camera as "72% of sellers," it would be wrong. The reel runs on the 64% (stated plainly in the summary) plus the summary's qualitative line that consumers use AI to develop pricing strategies before contacting an agent, said as "many," not as a number.
- **"Now you have to prove it right or wrong"** replaced round 1's "Now you're checking their number," which took a second listen to parse. Same turn, plainer words.
- **"That number beats yours"** is D.J.'s read: without local proof, the seller trusts the number they already have. It is a cost statement, not a statistic.
- **Plain words.** "What nearby homes actually sold for" replaces "comps" per the Rule 5 translate table.
- **Friction points at walking in unprepared**, not at the seller and not at any AI product. No tool named. Heat 4.
- **Loop-back:** the hook's AI price meets the close's real sales. On rewatch, "already got a price" reads as the thing you now check, not the thing you fight.
