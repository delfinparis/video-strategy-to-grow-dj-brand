# Renting a house isn't much cheaper anymore

**Filming:** Walk and talk, 30 seconds. Visual open: The Push-In (start at arm's length, pull the phone in on "cheaper").

## Teleprompter

Renting a house isn't much cheaper anymore.

Your renters still think it is. Nobody showed them the numbers.

At Zillow Unlock, chief economist Mischa Fisher showed the real gap. Renting a house used to save about five hundred a month. Now it saves about two hundred. That's for single-family homes, not apartments.

Today, call every renter in a house. Show their rent next to a real payment.

## Production Notes

- **Speaker on screen:** Mischa Fisher headshot + Zillow logo lower-third on "At Zillow Unlock, chief economist Mischa Fisher showed the real gap" (about 0:06.5-0:10). No other manual text; captions build from audio.
- **Push-In** lands on "cheaper." The hook is the belief being broken, so the move sits on the word that names it.
- **Say "about" both times.** The session gives both figures as approximate. Never say "only two hundred" or round further.
- **"Single-family homes, not apartments" stays.** The finding is for renting a house. Apartment renters face a different gap, and dropping that line would overstate the claim.
- **National number, local math.** If a comment says "not in my market," the honest reply is that this is Zillow's national figure and the payoff tells agents to run their own renter's numbers. That is why the close says "a real payment," not "two hundred dollars."
- **Overlap check (round 2):** UNLOCK-20 (same speaker, same session) is renters waiting on rates, and UNLOCK-25 is the live rental call. This one is only the rent-vs-payment math for people renting a *house*, and the payoff says "every renter in a house" so the three never ask for the same action. Post at least a week apart from UNLOCK-20; if only one Fisher reel can run, run UNLOCK-20.
- **Not a Zillow ad:** no product named, no tool pushed. The insight and the number only.
- **Truth filter:** the speaker is presented as a source D.J. agrees with. Friction points at an outdated belief, not at renters or agents.
