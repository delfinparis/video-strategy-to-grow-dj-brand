# Your inbox is burying your best clients

**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: One Prop, phone held to the lens showing a full inbox, on "burying."

## Teleprompter

Your inbox is burying your best clients.

You treat it like a to-do list. People get missed.

At Zillow Unlock, Tim Ferriss gave the fix. Archive every email older than three to six months. If a reply takes two minutes, send it now. Put anything longer on your calendar.

Tonight, set a ten-minute timer and archive. Then text your top clients. Ask what they're waiting on from you.

## Production Notes

- **Speaker lower-third:** Tim Ferriss headshot + name only on "At Zillow Unlock, Tim Ferriss gave the fix" (about 0:06-0:09). The credit opens THE POINT here, which the brief allows. No other manual text.
- **One Prop:** the phone with a crowded inbox; lower it after the hook. UNLOCK-10 used Walk-Toward.
- **"Archive," not "delete."** Ferriss said archive. Archived mail is still searchable, which is the answer to the first comment that says "I can't delete my clients' emails."
- **"Three to six months"** is the session's range, said as given. Do not pick one end.
- **The text step is back in (round 2).** Round 1 cut Ferriss's next step (text or call your top recurring contacts and ask what they're waiting on) to keep one idea. Round 2 puts it in the close because it pays off the hook: archiving finds the buried clients, the text reaches them. It replaces "Your calendar is the to-do list now," which was a poster line. "Top clients" is his "top recurring contacts" said plainly.
- **Pinned first comment:** "Tim Ferriss at Zillow Unlock 2026: archive anything older than three to six months, answer anything that takes two minutes, and put the rest on your calendar, not in your inbox."
- **Cold read:** Mistake: using the inbox as a to-do list, which buries the clients waiting on you. Fix: archive old mail, answer two-minute replies now, calendar the rest, then text your top clients and ask what they're waiting on.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): "burying your best clients" opens a who-and-how gap the viewer feels at once; the archive steps close the how, the text closes the who.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): anxiety (someone is waiting on me) plus a ten-minute fix with exact steps, which is practical value people save.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the close adds a new action instead of a summary line, so there is no wind-down before the cut.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Hormozi says two actions in the close (archive, then text) is one too many. Byron and Eric say the text is the only real-estate part of the tip and the reason an agent sends it. Decision: keep both, they run as one sequence tonight. **Experiment:** cut an alt ending on "archive" alone and compare completion rate.
