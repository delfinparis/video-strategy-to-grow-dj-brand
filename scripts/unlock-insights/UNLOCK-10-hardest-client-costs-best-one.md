# Your hardest client costs you your best one

**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Walk-Toward, walking straight at the lens, background sliding past, on the first word.

## Teleprompter

Your hardest client costs you your best one.

At Zillow Unlock, Tim Ferriss told agents to run an eighty-twenty test.

List last year's clients. Circle the few who made you the most money. Now circle the ones who took your nights and weekends. Give those hours to the first group.

Make both lists tonight. Call your best client this week. Stop answering the hard one's texts at midnight.

## Production Notes

- **Speaker lower-third:** Tim Ferriss headshot + name only (no company logo; he spoke as an author) on "At Zillow Unlock, Tim Ferriss" (about 0:02-0:05). No other manual text.
- **Walk-Toward:** keep walking at the lens through the hook; slow on "your best one."
- **"Eighty-twenty"** is explained by THE POINT itself (the few who bring most of the money), so it passes the uncle test without a definition.
- **Friction points at the habit** (over-serving demanding, low-paying clients), never at clients by name or at the agent. No client is called bad; the cost is time.
- **No number on camera.** The 80/20 is a rule of thumb, not a measured stat, and the script never claims a share of anyone's clients.
- **D.J. does not practice.** "Your clients" and "call your best one" are the viewer's, not D.J.'s.
- **Pinned first comment:** "From Tim Ferriss at Zillow Unlock 2026, applying the DEAL framework from The 4-Hour Workweek: define, eliminate, then automate. Run the 80/20 on your clients before you automate anything."
- **Round 2 close.** "Here's what you do now" was filler and the close had no edge. The new last line ("Stop answering the hard one's texts at midnight") is the session's "stop over-servicing high-maintenance, low-yield customers" said as an order. It points at the habit of answering, not at the client. "Give those hours back" became "Give those hours," since the best clients never had them.
- **Cold read:** Mistake: giving your nights and weekends to clients who bring in the least. Fix: list the few who bring in the most money and give them those hours.

## Council Review

Round 2 (fresh eyes): cut the "Here's what you do now" filler and ended on a flat order ("Stop answering the hard one's texts at midnight") so the close has the edge the hook promised.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "Your hardest client costs you your best one."  [hook_family: 1 Mirror | emotion: recognition, mild anger]
2. "Stop giving your weekends to your smallest deals."  [hook_family: 9 Swap | emotion: relief]
3. "Treating every client the same costs you money."  [hook_family: 2 Sacred Cow | emotion: anger]

**Pattern interrupt:** Walk-Toward, confirmed. Slow on "your best one."

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): every agent pictures one specific client in under a second, then wonders who "your best one" is.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Eric Simon): "this is us" recognition; the midnight line is the one an agent sends to the friend who is drowning.
- Retention move (MrBeast): two lists, one after the other, give the viewer a task to follow to the end, and the last line lands as a verdict.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Chris Do says the hook is the best human beat in the batch. Hormozi says variant 2 is plainer and names the action up front. Ship variant 1 for reach; re-cut with variant 2 if 3-second hold comes in under the series average.
