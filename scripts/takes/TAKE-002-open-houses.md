# You quit open houses for the right reason and the wrong conclusion

> **WOW: Contrarian take (2), aimed at the contrarian consensus rather than the mainstream one. The sophisticated agents in the audience already believe open houses don't work, so telling them they're right and still wrong is the only version of this that can still surprise them.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You were right that they don't sell the house. You were wrong to stop."

## Spoken Script (22-30 seconds, 72 words)

**THE COW**
> If you stopped holding open houses because they don't sell the house, you're right. You quit the wrong thing.

**WHO PROFITS**
> I'll find your buyer here was never for the buyer. It won the listing.

**THE RECEIPT**
> Forty-eight percent of buyers walked through an open house last year. That's NAR.

**THE SWAP**
> Run it for the room instead. Take every name that walks in.

**LOOP-BACK**
> You were right that they don't sell the house. You were wrong to stop.

## Production Note

- **Shape 3, cohort reversal, and the reframe half is mandatory.** "You're right" with no "it's actually for this" is a pose. The reframe is beat 4 and it is the reason this entry survives at all: the practice is fine, the stated purpose is wrong.
- **The right/wrong echo between beat 1 and beat 5 is deliberate**, not a stacked contrast tell. Rule 9.1 wants the close to lock the hook's meaning, and here the same two words come back inverted. Do not let the AI-tells scrub flatten it.
- **Pattern interrupt: Walk-Toward**, which the cheat sheet pairs with Cohort Callout. If a real open house sign is available on the walk, whip past it, but do not stop.
- **Who profits, honestly.** The beneficiary here is the listing agent making the pitch, and the viewer is often that agent. The friction still points at the pitch, not the person. The sentence names the line, not the caller.
- **Repeat check.** `EVERGREEN-003` (2026-07-08) ran this receipt as a straight myth-bust data card, six weeks back. The bank allows reuse inside eight weeks only with a genuinely different angle. This is the reversal shape plus a who-profits beat that the carousel never made, and it uses a different exhibit from the same report. Logged as a distinct angle.

## Data Source

| 48% of buyers used an open house as an information source in their home search | NAR, *2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers*, Exhibit 3-2, "Information Sources Used in Home Search" | All buyers; identical 48% for first-time and repeat buyers | **Verified 2026-08-18** from the report PDF |

**Supporting context, unspoken:** Exhibit 3-6 of the same report, "Where Buyer Found the Home They Purchased," gives 52 percent internet, 27 percent real estate agent, 9 percent friend, relative or neighbor for 2025. The gap between "half of buyers walked through one" and "almost none found their house that way" is the entire take.

**Re-verification note, and a correction to the brief.** The Aug 17 brief cited *"4% of buyers find their home via yard sign or open house, 52% online, NAR 2025."* The 52 percent is correct for 2025 and confirmed in the report's prose. The 4 percent is not: that figure traces to the 2024 edition, and in the 2025 Exhibit 3-6 the yard sign / open house segment reads closer to 5 percent. It also appears only inside a stacked chart, not in prose, so the exact 2025 value cannot be spoken with confidence.

Rather than speak a number that a commenter with the PDF open could dispute, beat 3 was swapped to the 48 percent information-source figure, which is printed as a clean table value, is from the current edition, and carries the reversal better than the original stat did. The indictment half now runs qualitatively inside the hook, which Rule 1's fallback explicitly allows.

## Council Review

**Convened:** Byron Lazine, Eric Simon, Miller, Kane, Chris Do. **Witnesses:** Heath, Berger.

### Spoken scroll-stop variants

- **A. Default, Family 8 Cohort Callout.** "If you stopped holding open houses because they don't sell the house, you're right. You quit the wrong thing."
- **B. Family 7, Forbidden.** "Nobody is going to tell you to go back to holding open houses, so I will."
- **C. Family 6, diagnosed loss.** "You gave up the only two hours a week where strangers walk up and talk to you for free."

### The room

**Byron.** This is the right call on a tired topic. Everyone in this industry has done the open-houses-are-dead video, including this account. The reversal is the only unclaimed position left on it. My objection is beat 2: the person who profits from the pitch *is* the listing agent, and half your audience is the listing agent. You're pointing friction at the viewer sideways.

**Eric Simon.** Disagree, that's exactly why it works. Every agent has said "I'll find your buyer here" on a listing appointment and known it was a stretch while saying it. Getting caught is the recognition. They'll send this to the person who trained them to say it.

**Miller.** The hero is the agent who quit for a smart reason. The guide hands them back the practice with a new purpose. Clean. My only note is that the swap has to be physical, and "take every name that walks in" is physical. Good.

**Kane.** Variant C is the strongest cold hook because it's a loss, stated concretely, with no industry vocabulary in it. A runs on the reversal, which is more interesting but takes an extra second to register. On a cold feed you don't get the extra second.

**Chris Do.** This one has more humanity than most takes in this lane, because it lets people be right. Being told you were half correct is a much easier thing to receive than being told you were wrong, and they'll actually change behaviour on it.

**Heath.** The reversal aims at the contrarian consensus rather than the mainstream one, which is why it can still surprise the sophisticated half of the audience who had already discounted the standard version. The 48 percent is Unexpected in the precise sense: it violates the model the viewer built when they quit.

**Berger.** High-arousal and sendable, and the who-profits beat is what makes it sendable rather than merely true. Watch the valuable-virality trap though. "Open houses are for the neighbors" survives a retell without D.J. attached. That's fine here because reach is the goal, but do not expect attribution.

### Why it works

**Hook (Heath):** attacks the contrarian consensus, so it surprises the viewers who had stopped being surprised. **Share driver (Berger):** high-arousal recognition plus a beat that names a line the viewer has personally said. **Retention (MrBeast):** the turn lands around second six and the receipt arrives before any doubt can form.

### The dissent to A/B

**Byron holds the objection on beat 2.** Run the current version, where the profiting party is the listing agent making the pitch, against one that points at the training instead ("that line is in every listing presentation template you were handed"), judged on comment sentiment rather than volume. If the current version draws defensive comments rather than confessional ones, the friction is landing on the viewer and beat 2 moves.

**Kane's separate test:** variant C against variant A on equal cold spend, 3-second retention only.
