# Every Listing Description Sounds the Same

> **WOW: Pattern reveal + mindset reframe -- the listing description is the real first showing, and if it reads like every other listing you lost the buyer on the couch, not at the house. Then the free fix: tapthis.co Write category with prompts that ask you the right questions first.**

**Filming:** Walk and talk, ideally past a For Sale sign or two. No screen recording.
**Destination:** tapthis.co

## Shareable Moment
> "The listing description is the first showing. If it reads like every other listing, you lost them on the couch."

## Script (~3:30, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:20)
I pulled up ten listings in one neighborhood this morning. Different agents, different houses, different prices. Nine of them used the word "charming." Seven said "must-see." Six said "won't last." Same three words. Same house, basically. No wonder buyers skim.

*Production: Walking, holding up phone like you just scrolled to it. Quick cut. Overlay the repeated adjectives as a stack: 'charming. must-see. won't last.'*

### PROBLEM (0:20-0:55)
Your listing description is the first showing. It's the one a buyer does at 11pm in bed, deciding whether to book the real one. And if it reads like every other listing, you didn't lose the buyer at the house. You lost them on the couch. Before they ever walked in. The problem isn't that you're a bad writer. It's that it's 9pm, you've got three of these to do, and "charming" is right there.

*Production: Slow down, look at camera. Text overlay: 'The description is the first showing.' Boldest text moment.*

### SOLUTION (0:55-2:00)
So go to tapthis.co. Tap "Write Something For Me." There's a whole stack of listing description prompts in there. And here's the part that matters. They don't just say "write a description." They ask you questions first. What's the neighborhood. Who's the buyer, a first-time couple or a downsizer. What's the one feature you'd actually brag about. You fill in those blanks, the brackets, in about thirty seconds. Then you paste the whole thing into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. The prompt did the hard part. It told the AI how to think like you.

*Production: Walking again, energy up. Text overlays: 'tapthis.co -> Write' / 'You fill in the blanks.'*

### RESULTS (2:00-3:00)
And what comes back isn't "charming." It's the morning light in the kitchen. It's the fact that the train's a six-minute walk. It's a line that makes a tired buyer at 11pm actually sit up. Same thirty seconds you'd have spent typing "charming, cozy, must-see." But now your listing is the one that doesn't blend in. And you didn't stay up writing it. Do it once and you'll never start a description from a blank box again.

*Production: Stop walking, look at camera (visual beat first). Text overlay: 'Not charming. Specific.'*

### CLOSE (3:00-3:15)
Your seller's paying you to make their house the one that stands out. Tough to pull off when your description reads like the three houses around it. The better words already exist, over at tapthis.co. Take them.

*Production: Turn to camera, small grin. Keep it dry, it's a wink not a pitch.*
