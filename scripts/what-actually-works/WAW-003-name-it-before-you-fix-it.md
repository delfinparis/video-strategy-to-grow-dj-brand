# Name It Before You Fix It

> **WOW: Takes a moment every agent dreads (the furious seller on a lowball) and hands over a brain-research move that defuses it in one sentence. Beachhead category: sales communication.**

**Pillar:** What Actually Works | **Type:** Research-backed | **Beachhead:** yes
**Primary Platform:** LinkedIn | **Post Date:** TBD

> **Strongest Line:** "You can't reason someone out of a feeling they're still having."

## Script (45-60 seconds)

### HOOK / SCENARIO (0:00-0:14)
Your seller just got a lowball offer and they are furious. Watch what most agents do next. They defend the offer. "It's actually not that bad, the market's been shifting." And the seller gets angrier. Because you can't reason someone out of a feeling they're still having.

### MECHANISM (0:14-0:38)
There's a study out of UCLA, Matthew Lieberman, 2007. They put people in brain scanners and found something almost strange. The moment you name an emotion out loud, the part of the brain generating that emotion gets quieter. They call it affect labeling. Putting the feeling into words drains some of its charge. Naming it does what arguing never could.

### APPLICATION (0:38-0:54)
So before you say one word about the offer, name what they're feeling. "You're frustrated this came in this low, after everything you put into the place." That's the whole move. You'll watch their shoulders drop. Then you negotiate, with a person who can finally hear you.

### ARCHIVE TIE-IN (0:54-1:00)
Every great negotiator I've had on the show does some version of this. Most of them have no idea there's a brain study behind it.

### CLOSE / TRY THIS (1:00-1:05)
Next upset client, name it before you fix it. See what happens.
