## Script (~63 seconds)

### HOOK / PROBLEM (0:00-0:11)
Stop posting your just-listeds and your just-solds. I know your broker told you to. Posting's fine. What you're posting is the problem. There's a study on why, and here's the fix.

### WHY THE OBVIOUS FIX FAILS (0:11-0:22)
When one flops, you blame your photos, or you decide to post more often. That's not it. Your just-sold dies because it's about you, and nobody scrolling was ever watching you.

### MECHANISM (0:22-0:40)
Two psychologists, Gilovich and Savitsky, called it the spotlight effect. We're sure everyone's watching us. They're not. Everyone's running their own spotlight, scanning for one thing: does this help me. Your sale answers a question nobody asked.

### APPLICATION + ARCHIVE TIE-IN (0:40-0:57)
So give them their own spotlight. Take the last thing a client asked you at nine at night, and answer that, like you're talking to one person. It's not about you, it's about them. I've never sold a house, but the agents I've interviewed whose social actually feeds their business don't post their wins. They post answers.

### CLOSE / ACTION (0:57-1:03)
Before your next just-sold, write down the last question a client asked you. Post that instead. Watch who shows up in your inbox.

## Data Source
- **Mechanism:** The spotlight effect. Primary: Gilovich, T., Medvec, V. H., & Savitsky, K. (2000). "The spotlight effect in social judgment: An egocentric bias in estimates of the salience of one's own actions and appearance." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 78(2), 211-222. **VERIFY to primary source before air.** The finding: people systematically overestimate how much others notice and remember their appearance and behavior.
- **Application bridge (DJ's, not the study's claim):** The study is about over-attention to the self. The move "therefore make the post about the viewer's problem" is D.J.'s operator extension, not a finding of the paper. Stated as a reframe, not as proven by the research.

**Caption:**
Stop posting your just-listeds and your just-solds.

I know your broker told you to. Posting isn't the problem. What you're posting is.

When one of those posts flops, you assume you need better photos, or you need to post more often. That's not it. A just-sold post dies because it's about you, and your audience was never really watching you in the first place.

Two psychologists, Thomas Gilovich and Kenneth Savitsky, named this the spotlight effect. We walk around convinced everyone is watching us and tracking what we do. They ran the studies. They're not. Everyone is running their own spotlight, scanning every feed for one thing: does this help me.

Your sale answers a question nobody scrolling was asking.

So give them their own spotlight. Take the last thing a client asked you at nine at night, and answer it in one post, like you're talking to one person. It's not about you. It's about them.

I've never sold a house. But the agents I've interviewed whose social actually feeds their business almost never post their wins. They post answers.
