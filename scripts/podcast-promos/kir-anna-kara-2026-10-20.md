## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (three spoken first-line options mapped to hook families):**

1. "Waiting for lower rates is costing your buyers $150,000." (Family 2 -- Contrarian Claim -- uses Anna's verbatim dollar figure; challenges the universal 'wait for lower rates' belief) [USED]
2. "Your buyers are making a $150,000 mistake and you're letting them." (Family 1 -- Mirror -- implicates the agent directly; higher heat, more personal loss framing) [A/B test]
3. "One lender gave your buyer an answer. It's not the answer." (Family 7 -- Forbidden -- the bank's single-answer limitation as a hidden disservice) [alternative framing test]

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: Contrarian Claim + specific loss figure. "$150,000 more" is Anna's verbatim quote -- the specificity makes the hook credible on its own, not a number D.J. is asserting. Challenges the deeply held buyer belief that waiting for lower rates is the smart play.
- Share driver: "Their bank's rate is one answer. It's not the answer." is quotable and sendable -- an agent pastes this into a buyer text the same day. The three-lender fix is physical, specific, and costs nothing to act on.
- Retention move: The payoff closes with a question the agent will use in their next buyer call ("how many lenders have you called?") -- they remember the video because they're going to say those exact words.

**Single dissent to A/B test next:** Try the Mirror variant ("Your buyers are making a $150,000 mistake and you're letting them") on a second filming take. Direct agent indictment tends to outperform pointing at the market, because the cost is personal and the fix is in the agent's hands.
