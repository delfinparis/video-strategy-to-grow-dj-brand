## Council Review

**Tested scroll-stop variants (spoken):**

1. **"Your listing should be selling a lifestyle."** (Contrarian Claim, Family 2) -- the script's choice. Reframes what agents are actually selling. Forces the viewer to ask: is mine?
2. **"This agent walked away from Ralph Lauren and built $144 million in real estate."** (Data Reveal, Family 4) -- leads with the credential and story. Opens a narrative gap ("how does that connect?") that the TENSION beat closes. May hold curious scrollers longer before the 3-second drop-off.
3. **"Walk every room before you book the photographer."** (Swap / Trigger, Family 9) -- action-first. Gets higher saves from agents in the middle of a listing. Lower shares because it skips the reframe.

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: Contrarian Claim -- "lifestyle not square footage" reframes what the listing is actually selling. Agents who've been pitching beds and baths self-identify and stop scrolling.
- Share driver: "You're not selling square footage. You're selling the life someone is about to step into." -- a sendable one-sentence repositioning that holds up outside the video.
- Retention move: The Ralph Lauren credential in TENSION creates a story gap ("what does fashion have to do with real estate?") that THE POINT resolves with a concrete tactic. The loop opens in the hook and closes in the payoff.

**Single dissent to A/B test:**
Test variant 2 ("This agent walked away from Ralph Lauren and built $144 million in real estate") against the current hook for 15-second retention. The story hook may carry viewers further into THE POINT before the drop-off. If the current hook's abstraction ("selling a lifestyle") loses scrollers at second 3, the story hook trades some initial friction for a stronger narrative pull.
