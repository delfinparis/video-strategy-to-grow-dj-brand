## Council Review

**Tested scroll-stop variants (spoken):**

1. **"Some agents become gig workers in 18 months."** (Named Stakes, Family 6) -- the script's choice. Opens on a concrete, time-stamped fate. Forces the viewer to self-classify: am I one of them?
2. **"AI already knows which agents it's replacing."** (System Indictment, Family 3) -- implies AI has made a list, which creates urgency without an explicit timeline. May hold curious scrollers longer before the drop-off at second 3.
3. **"Your following is the one thing AI can't take."** (Contrarian Claim, Family 2) -- leads with the solution, not the threat. Lower initial alarm; trades stop-rate for comfort-seekers.

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: Named Stakes -- the "18 months" deadline turns a vague fear into a specific window. Viewers who feel transactional stop and self-assess.
- Share driver: "AI can write your captions but it can't build your following." -- that line is sendable on its own. One-sentence thesis agents will text their teams.
- Retention move: The script opens on the threat, holds the resolution until THE POINT, then closes with one specific action. The loop the hook opens ("which agents?") closes in THE POINT ("the ones with no following").

**Single dissent to A/B test:**
Test variant 2 -- "AI already knows which agents it's replacing." -- against the script's hook for 15-second retention. If watch-through drops mid-TENSION on the current version, the mystery framing in variant 2 may hold longer before the tip lands.
