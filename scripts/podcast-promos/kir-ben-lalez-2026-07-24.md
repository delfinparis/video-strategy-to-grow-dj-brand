## Council Review

**Tested spoken scroll-stop variants (Hook Family: Mirror):**

1. **(V1 -- lead)** "You stopped calling your client the day they went under contract. That's when you lose them." -- Mirror: names the private behavior directly, second-person accusatory. Viewer recognizes themselves and freezes.
2. **(V2 -- A/B candidate)** "Going under contract isn't the finish line. It's when most agents disappear." -- Named Stakes (Family 6): softer angle, names the pattern rather than accusing. May drive more comments from agents who want to push back.
3. **(V3 -- backup)** "Your client is under contract right now and wondering if you forgot them." -- Mirror: present-tense urgency, more specific emotional state. Strong if V1 feels too accusatory for the account's current audience.

**Why it works:** The Mirror hook (heat 2-3) arrests viewers by naming something they've done. The Act 2 turn ("Ben calls that backwards") reframes silence from courtesy to liability -- that's the retention move because the viewer needs to hear why. The tip is specific enough to act on before listening: 30 seconds, exact words supplied. Shareability: agents forward this to their team because the daily-text habit is a gift, not a lecture. Heath's curiosity gap: the hook opens a loop ("when you lose them" -- when? how?) that only the tip resolves.

**Single dissent to A/B test next:** V2 ("Going under contract isn't the finish line. It's when most agents disappear.") -- less direct accusation, likely drives higher comment count. Test comment-to-save ratio against V1 over the first 2 hours.
