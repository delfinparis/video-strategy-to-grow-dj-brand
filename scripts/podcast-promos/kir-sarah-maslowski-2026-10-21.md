## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (three spoken first-line options mapped to hook families):**

1. "Your camera fear is losing you leads." (Family 1 -- Mirror -- agent's own inaction named as a personal cost; loss framing) [USED]
2. "Sarah Maslowski hit $140 million on YouTube and she still gets nervous filming." (Family 4 -- Data Reveal -- the credibility-through-contradiction hook; proof IS the scroll-stop) [A/B test]
3. "You don't need to be camera-ready to get leads from YouTube." (Family 7 -- Forbidden -- mirrors the episode title; challenges the 'get good first' default belief) [alternative]

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: Mirror + loss framing. "Your camera fear is losing you leads" implicates the viewer directly and names a specific, ongoing cost. "Camera fear" is plain and concrete -- no metaphor required.
- Share driver: "The agents who never start are waiting to feel ready" is sendable. An agent pastes it into a group chat because it describes their peers exactly. The teleprompter tip is physical, free, and actionable in under 10 minutes.
- Retention move: The loop-back ("camera fear or not, film today") closes the exact loop opened in the hook -- the viewer hears the episode's promise fulfilled in the last line.

**Single dissent to A/B test next:** Try variant 3 ("You don't need to be camera-ready") on a second take -- it mirrors the episode title verbatim and may earn more organic shares from agents who are currently in the "I'm waiting to be ready" position. Direct title alignment sometimes outperforms indirect loss framing for a recurring series guest (this is Sarah's fourth episode on the show).
