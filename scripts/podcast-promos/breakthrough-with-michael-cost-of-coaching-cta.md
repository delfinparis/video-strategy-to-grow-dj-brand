## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "You looked up a real estate coach, saw the price, and closed the tab." [hook_family: 1 Mirror | emotion: recognition, mild guilt]
2. "Coaching didn't price you out. It gave you a reason, and you took it." [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: sting]
3. "An agent closed six million dollars in eighteen months and still couldn't justify a coach. She's the reason we made one seat free." [hook_family: 6 Named Stakes | emotion: curiosity]

**Pattern interrupt:** One Prop (move 5) -- the phone comes up on line one and drops out of frame on "closed the tab." The prop physically acts out the private behavior the hook names. Rotates off Walk-Toward in the series-explainer script.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the Mirror names a behavior the viewer has done and never said out loud. The gap is "how does he know that," and the twist (money was never the blocker) is the Aha that closes it.
- Share driver (Berger): practical value plus the sting of recognition. Simon's test passes clean, one agent sends this to another with "ok this is called out."
- Retention move (MrBeast): "Here's the part that got me" at 0:20 is the re-hook, and the number lands right after it. The offer arrives at 0:38, before the drift point.
- The human (Chris Do): "Michael can't do much with the version of you that's fine" is the honest beat that costs something. It disqualifies the casual applicant on purpose, which raises application quality instead of raw volume.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Vaynerchuk says leading with a price objection smells like an ad even when the thing is free, and the platform will read it that way. His experiment: cut a version that never mentions cost until the last eight seconds, opening instead on Emma's line about needing something more personal than a podcast. Hypothesis: fewer clicks, higher watch time, and better-qualified applications because the free seat reads as a consequence of the story rather than the pitch.
