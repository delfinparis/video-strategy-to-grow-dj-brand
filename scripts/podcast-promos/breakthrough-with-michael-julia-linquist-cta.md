## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "A top producer told our coach her family kept losing to her calendar." [hook_family: 6 Named Stakes | emotion: recognition + dread]
2. "You're chasing a production number nobody asked you for. Not your spouse, not your kids, not your clients." [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: sting]
3. "If you don't fix this today, what's it going to look like six months down the line?" [hook_family: 7 Forbidden | emotion: anxiety -- opens cold on Michael's question, highest risk, highest ceiling]

**Pattern interrupt:** Push-In (move 2) -- start at arm's length, pull the phone in on "scared to answer." The physical approach mirrors the emotional one. Rotates off One Prop and Walk-Toward in the other two scripts.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): "a question I'd be scared to answer" is a pure curiosity gap with a named, deliverable payoff. The video is obligated to produce the question, and it does, at the exact drift point.
- Share driver (Berger): the six-month question is the sendable line. It survives the retell test with zero context, which is the actual test for a private DM between two agents.
- Retention move (MrBeast): the swap at 0:30 (volume out, income in) is a mini payoff before the real one, so there are two reasons to stay rather than one. The question lands at ~0:42.
- Relatability (Simon): "her family kept losing to her calendar" is the inside beat. Every producing agent has had that exact Sunday.
- Clarity (Miller): the hero is the viewer, Julia is proof, Michael is the guide, and there's one action plus one door.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Kane argues variant 3 is the real hook and the current open buries it behind setup. His experiment: cut a version that opens cold on Michael's question in D.J.'s voice, then reveals whose session it came from. Hypothesis: sharper three-second retention and more shares, with a completion dip because the viewer gets the payoff first. Worth knowing which one this audience actually rewards.
