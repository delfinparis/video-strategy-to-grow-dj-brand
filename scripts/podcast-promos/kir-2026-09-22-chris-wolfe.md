## Council Review

**Hook mechanism:** Sacred Cow + Loss Framing. "Your sphere won't save your first year" attacks the most common first-year belief directly, opens a loop (what WILL save it?), and the video closes it. That's Heath's curiosity gap operating cleanly -- the answer is withheld just long enough that skipping the next 28 seconds is uncomfortable.

**Share driver:** The tip is a physical sequence (pull list, call by 8:30, exact opener) plus a scoreboard number (33 deals, 95%). Berger's practical value test: will someone screenshot the payoff beat and text it to a friend? Yes -- "Hey, good morning, how are you? -- no script" is sendable on its own.

**Retention move:** The loop-back ("He made two hours of these calls before we recorded") lands after the tip, which means viewers who got the tactic and nearly swiped away get pulled back by the story they didn't know they wanted. The loop closes at the end, not mid-scroll.

**2-3 scroll-stop variants to A/B test:**

1. *(Sacred Cow, current)* "Your sphere won't save your first year." -- 7 words, loss frame, tested above.
2. *(Data Reveal, alt)* "Thirty-three deals year one. No sphere. Cold calls." -- hits the same proof but leads with the number; risks feeling like a boast before D.J. earns it. Use if the current hook underperforms with established agents who already know the sphere myth.
3. *(Contrarian Claim, alt)* "Cold calling isn't dead -- you're just doing it wrong." -- Family 2, heat 4; shifts friction to the practice rather than the belief. Stronger for an audience that already rejected cold calling; weaker for total newbies.

**Single dissent:** The TENSION beat ("New city, no sphere, 33 deals" paratactic three-beat) is a deliberate Rule-of-Three. It's the only one in the script and it earns its place because the three pieces of information don't fit any other efficient structure. Flag to review if the next two scripts also use paratactic staccato -- it becomes a tell if it repeats.
