## Council Review

### Scroll-Stop Variants (tested spoken alternatives)

1. **"A thousand-dollar rental can become a twenty-million-dollar buyer."** *(Named Stakes, Family 6)* -- The episode's own quote as the hook. Highest specificity, maximum credibility token. Gives away the payload but earns the click on the proof alone. Test when the prior post used a Forbidden frame.

2. **"You're treating your rental clients like small deals. They're not."** *(Sacred Cow, Family 2)* -- Attacks the practice, defends the agent. Heat 4. Works when D.J. wants more friction in the week's lineup.

3. **"Agents doing rentals. You're ignoring your biggest future buyers."** *(Cohort Callout, Family 8)* -- Direct address to the cohort that needs this most. Tightest reach, highest engagement from that segment.

### Why It Works

The Forbidden frame ("nobody told you") opens a genuine curiosity gap -- the viewer has no idea what the revelation is -- which forces the next 28 seconds. The tip is physical and time-bound (CRM tag + 6-month call), so an agent can act before the episode even loads. The loop-back ("rental clients aren't small deals, they're your pipeline") closes the curiosity loop opened in the hook and makes the payoff feel earned.

### Dissent to A/B Test

Chris Wands mentions Close CRM by name in the episode (`resources_mentioned[0]`). Naming it makes the tip more concrete but risks feeling like an ad. Test: "tag that client as a future buyer in Close CRM" vs. "in your CRM." Hypothesis: the generic "your CRM" outperforms because it is inclusive; Close CRM may outperform with audiences already using it.
