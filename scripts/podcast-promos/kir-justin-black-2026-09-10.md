## Council Review

**Hook tested:** System Indictment (Family 3). "ChatGPT is already recommending agents. Not you." pins the loss on the viewer immediately -- the AI system has already chosen, and the viewer is not the winner. Loss framing in the first three words.

**Scroll-stop variants to A/B test (spoken, 5-8 words each):**
1. "AI just recommended a different agent to your buyer." -- Mirror (Family 2), highest personal-stake framing, most viscerally specific
2. "A Breckenridge agent said AI sent him five million." -- Data Reveal (Family 4), opens curiosity on the number rather than the loss; different valence
3. "Your website is losing you buyers right now." -- System Indictment, loss-first without naming ChatGPT, broader scroll appeal outside AI-curious audiences

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: "Not you" creates immediate personal relevance through exclusion -- the viewer is primed to stay for the fix before they know what they're watching.
- Share driver (Berger): The tactic is verifiable in real-time. An agent opens ChatGPT while watching and types in their market -- they get instant results. That verifiability makes this a forward to broker group chats and masterminds.
- Retention move: The $5M stat in THE POINT reanchors attention after the hook; the "do this now" command closes the loop and drives action before the episode.

**Single dissent to test next:** Open with the dollar figure -- "AI sent a Breckenridge agent almost five million in listings." Data Reveal hook, aspiration vs. loss framing. May outperform on saves among agents who are already thinking about AI but aren't yet scared they're losing ground.
