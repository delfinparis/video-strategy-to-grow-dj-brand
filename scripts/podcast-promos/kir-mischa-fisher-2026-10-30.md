## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (three spoken first-line options mapped to hook families):**

1. "Your buyers' mortgage payment doubled in five years." (Family 4 -- Data Reveal -- the number stops the scroll before the context arrives; loss framing implied by "doubled") [USED]
2. "Mortgage costs doubled. Your buyers are still waiting." (Family 4 -- Data Reveal -- blunter, names the inaction cost directly; useful A/B if the possessive opening feels too soft)
3. "Your buyers aren't scared of the market. They're scared of the payment." (Family 2 -- Contrarian Claim -- leads with the reframe instead of the data; softer hook but stronger at mid-scroll) [A/B test]

**Why it works:**
- Hook mechanism: Data Reveal + loss framing. The doubled-payment stat opens a loop the viewer can only close by listening. It implicates the agent's buyers without accusing the agent.
- Share driver: "They're scared of the payment" is sendable. An agent pastes it to a team chat because it names the exact stall they hear every week. The Zillow Research tip is physical, free, and takes under five minutes.
- Retention move: The loop-back ("pull that chart today") returns to the hook's promise -- here is the specific action that addresses the doubled payment. The loop closes on a tool, not a feeling.

**Single dissent to A/B test next:** Try variant 2 ("Mortgage costs doubled. Your buyers are still waiting.") -- the two short declarative sentences create staccato urgency that plays well on TikTok specifically, where D.J.'s cadence is compressed further by the algorithm. Also tests whether the agent-inaction implication ("still waiting") outperforms the buyer-ownership framing ("your buyers' payment") from an engagement standpoint.
