## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
The basement story is not rare. The lawsuit is not either.

**Body:**
Illinois' Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77) requires the seller to complete
a 24-item disclosure form and keep it current under Section 30 until closing, not just at
listing. Section 55 sets the cost of skipping it: a seller who knowingly discloses false
information, or knowingly violates the Act, "shall be liable in the amount of actual damages and
court costs," and the court can add the buyer's attorney's fees on top. The agent who tells a
seller to paper over a known defect is not saving them an awkward line on a form. They're
handing them a bigger bill with a judge's signature on it.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What to do instead, before the disclosure form goes out:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask the seller one question first.** "What would you want to know if you were buying this
   house?" Write down whatever they pause on before answering.
2. **Put it on the form, not around it.** The 24-item Illinois form has a line for material
   defects. Use it instead of steering the seller toward a repaint and a maybe.
3. **Treat the form as a living document, not a closing-day formality.** The duty to supplement
   runs until closing. Something that changes after listing still has to go back on the form.
