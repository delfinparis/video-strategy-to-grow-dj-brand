## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
Every renter is a future buyer, if you don't treat them like a discount client.

**Body:**
On Keeping It Real, Chris Wands, whose team ranks fourth nationally for rental transactions, put it
this way: "a $500,000 transaction, a $1,500 rental, or a $55 million transaction, I personally
treat everyone the same." He is not describing being polite. He is describing where his buyers
come from. "If you do good enough on your job, money will become a byproduct of it," he said. The
rental you write off as too small to matter is the buyer conversation you are handing to whoever
answers the phone next.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The system that keeps the small deal from disappearing:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Set a notification the day you close a rental.** Chris logs one for sixty days before that lease renews, so the follow-up never depends on memory.
2. **Call thirty days after move-in, just to check in.** "How's the apartment going? Are you still happy there?" No pitch. Just contact.
3. **Call again sixty days before the lease ends.** That's when a renter who is ready to buy starts looking, whether you reach out or not.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
The $1,500 rental was never the deal.

**Subhead:**
The relationship you build doing it right is.
