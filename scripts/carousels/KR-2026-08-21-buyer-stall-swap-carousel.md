## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
Most agents answer the stall with silence.

**Body:**
"Take your time, here's my card" hands the buyer a clean exit and ends the conversation before
you find out what's actually wrong. The stall isn't indecision, it's an unnamed objection
wearing a polite sentence. Name the three likely reasons out loud instead and the buyer will
correct you. People will tell you which guess is wrong faster than they'll ever volunteer the
real one on their own.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What to do the next time you hear it:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask the three-option question.** "Is it the price, the timing, or something else you're weighing?" Say those words. It's specific enough that they'll correct you if you're wrong.
2. **Stop talking after you ask it.** Let the silence sit. Whoever talks next loses the frame, and it isn't you.
3. **Answer only what they name.** Don't rebut all three reasons. Answering objections they didn't raise sounds like you're selling something, not solving something.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
The next stall you hear, ask instead of nod.

**Subhead:**
It was never a no. It was a question you hadn't asked yet.
