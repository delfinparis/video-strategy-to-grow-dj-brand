## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
The pandemic gave sellers a free pass on pricing. That pass expired.

**Body:**
Realtor.com tracked home sales by how long they sat before going under contract. Homes that
closed around the four-week mark sold about 1.8 percent above the typical asking price. Homes
still on the market at eighteen weeks closed about 1.3 percent below it, a gap of more than
three percentage points between pricing it right and pricing it to win the appointment. Senior
economist Joel Berner put it plainly: the pandemic gave sellers a free pass on pricing, and that
pass expired. The extra month of showings, price chatter, and a stale listing photo doesn't win
the seller more money. It costs them.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Before you quote a price to win the appointment:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Bring the comps, not a number.** Show the seller the three to five closed sales your price
   is built on, so the figure is defensible before they ever hear it.
2. **Name the ceiling out loud.** Tell the seller what the market will actually pay before they
   hear a bigger number from someone else. It's a harder conversation now and a cheaper one than
   the fortieth day of silence.
3. **Put the reduction schedule in the listing agreement.** If a seller insists on testing high,
   write the adjustment date into the agreement on day one, so the cut is a plan instead of a
   monthly argument.
4. **Ask what the other agent's number is based on.** If the answer is "what you wanted to
   hear," that agent priced the appointment, not the house.
