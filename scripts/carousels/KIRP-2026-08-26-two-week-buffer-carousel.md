## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
One week to plan. One week to film. Never the same week.

**Body:**
Sarah Maslowski has closed over $150 million in real estate from YouTube alone. Her rhythm: week
one is content planning only, one hour per video, pulling from a four-bucket topic rotation so she
never starts from a blank page. Week two is filming only, two videos batched in one sitting. "My
personal target is always be two weeks ahead," she said on Keeping It Real, "and that means the
video posting this week was filmed and edited at least two weeks ago." The cycle repeats every two
weeks, and the buffer it builds is the whole point.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Her two-week cycle, start to finish:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Give planning its own week.** One hour per video, pulling the topic from a four-bucket rotation so you're never guessing what to make.
2. **Give filming its own week.** Batch two videos in one sitting instead of one video a day. It protects your energy on video two.
3. **Hand your editor one self-contained folder.** Raw footage, full script, and B-roll shortcuts together, so there's no back-and-forth and no hunting.
4. **Post from two weeks out.** If this week's post was filmed and edited two weeks ago, one bad week can't take your channel dark.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
The buffer isn't a nice-to-have. It's the whole system.

**Subhead:**
Two weeks ahead means one emergency is a Tuesday, not a missed post.
