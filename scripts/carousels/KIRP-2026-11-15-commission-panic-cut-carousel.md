**The one idea:** A seller who gets an instant discount the moment they push back just learned
exactly how little resistance it takes to move the agent's number, and they will use the same
move again at every other point in the deal. The fix is to anchor the fee to what it buys before
the objection lands, and to trade rather than give if an adjustment happens at all.

## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
It isn't generosity. It's a tell.

**Body:**
An instant discount on pushback has no defense in it. No explanation of what the fee funds, no
cost of under-marketing named, just an immediate concession to escape the discomfort. The seller
didn't learn you're flexible. They learned you fold under the first bit of friction, which is the
exact information they need to push harder on repair credits, closing costs, and every number
after this one.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD

**Headline:**
What to do instead of folding:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Anchor the fee to the plan before the objection ever comes.** Walk every seller through what
   the fee funds at the listing appointment, not after they balk.
2. **On pushback, name the cost of under-marketing, not just defend the percentage.** "Here's what
   a thin marketing budget actually costs you in days on market."
3. **Trade, never just give.** If you adjust at all, trade it for something: price, terms,
   exclusivity. A concession with nothing attached is a discount, not a negotiation.
4. **Pause before you answer.** A fee cut inside the same breath as the objection reads as
   weakness. A fee held steady for even one beat reads as conviction.
