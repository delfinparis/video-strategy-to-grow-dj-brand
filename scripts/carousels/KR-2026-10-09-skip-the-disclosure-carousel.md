## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
Disclosed and priced in closes. Hidden becomes a judgment.

**Body:**
Illinois' Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77) makes a seller who knowingly
leaves a material defect off the form liable for the buyer's actual damages plus court costs. A
basement that floods every spring, painted over and marked "unaware," isn't a problem that goes
away. It's a problem that waits for the first storm, then becomes the seller's bill and the
agent's reputation. A defect disclosed and priced into the deal, repaired or credited at closing,
still closes. The same defect hidden turns into a rescission fight or a judgment.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What you do before the next listing goes live:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask the direct question yourself.** "Has anything ever flooded, leaked, or been repaired for
   water damage?" Don't wait for the seller to volunteer it.
2. **Put the answer on the form, in writing.** If a seller asks you to leave something off,
   that's the moment to say no, not the moment to go along with it.
3. **Price it in instead of hiding it.** Get a repair bid or credit the buyer at closing. A
   known, priced defect is a normal negotiation, not a lawsuit.
4. **Know the statute number.** 765 ILCS 77 gives you something concrete to point to when a
   seller pushes back on disclosing.
