## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The swap, for your next listing appointment:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Don't repeat the seller's number back as agreement.** Say this first: "I'll tell you what it's worth, and I won't tell you what you want to hear."
2. **Don't blame the market for a number you agreed to.** The comps set the number. The appointment doesn't.
3. **Don't wait for thin showings to prove you wrong.** A listing still sitting past week four is already telling the seller something you didn't want to say at the table.
4. **Don't confuse losing the appointment with losing the listing.** You can win a listing with somebody else's number. You just can't sell it.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Homes that go under contract around week four sell 1.8 percent closer to asking than the average home sold in the same period. Homes still sitting at week eighteen close 1.3 percent under it, a spread of more than three percentage points."
  - Source: Realtor.com, "By Week Four, Your Listing Is Either Getting Offers or Price Cuts," published 2026-06-11. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/by-week-four-your-listing-is-either-getting-offers-or-price-cuts-302797293.html
  - Who was measured: U.S. home sales sourced from deed and MLS records combined with Realtor.com listing histories, comparing 2021-2022 against 2025-2026. Economist quoted: Joel Berner, senior economist, Realtor.com.
  - Status: confirmed. Carried forward from `scripts/stupid-things/STUPID-001-you-bought-that-listing.md`, verified there 2026-08-12.

- **Claim:** "The average home now closes under asking, reversing the pandemic-era pattern."
  - Source: Same Realtor.com release, 2026-06-11.
  - Who was measured: same dataset as above.
  - Status: confirmed. Carried forward from STUPID-001.

- **Causation caveat:** The data shows homes under contract early sell closer to asking and homes that sit sell below it. It does not prove that a lower list price causes an early contract, and homes still sitting at week eighteen are plausibly the most mispriced at list to begin with. This deck's claim stays inside what the source supports: the spread is real, and the close is a conversation change at the appointment, not a promise of a specific dollar saving.
