**The one idea:** Google's Business Profile algorithm runs on a recency clock, not a lifetime
review count -- a profile with a hundred five-star reviews from before last December gets read
as a dead business the same way a profile with none does, while a profile posting every few days
and collecting a steady trickle of new reviews gets treated as active and gets recommended,
including inside AI search overviews that now pull straight from the Business Profile. Rule 0
criterion 3 (pattern reveal: most agents think review count is the whole game) and criterion 1
(tactical specificity: the posting cadence, the closing-table QR block, the exact review-request
script). Hook family `mirror` (Family 1: the private assumption "my hundred reviews mean my

## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
Bobby Kerr calls this the **dark house REALTOR.**

**Body:**
Bobby Kerr, founder of the Google Business Profile agency Locate, has optimized more than a
thousand REALTOR profiles. His read on the algorithm: "There's a massive recency bias." Google
puts every business profile on roughly a thirty-to-forty-five-day clock. Stop posting and stop
collecting new reviews, and it reads the business as dormant, no matter how many five-star
reviews already sit on the page from years back. Once that happens, Google stops recommending
the profile in search and in the AI overviews that now pull directly from it. Kerr's own name
for the pattern, from his coaching: the dark house REALTOR, an agent with the best listing in
the world where every showing happens with the lights off. Nobody stops in, because nobody can
see it's occupied.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Turn the lights back on this week:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Post to your profile every two to three days, minimum twice a week.** That cadence is the recency signal that keeps you out of the "dormant business" bucket.
2. **Put a QR code review block at the closing table.** A printed card or coaster with your direct Google review link, handed over when they're holding the keys, not buried in a post-closing email.
3. **Coach the review before they write it.** Ask them to mention you by name, the neighborhood, and the problem you solved. Those are the details Google's AI overviews actually read.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
A hundred old reviews is a dark house. Turn the lights back on.

**Subhead:**
The reviews you already earned don't expire. The silence since the last one does.
