## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
The goal isn't to be remembered. It's to make them feel remembered.

**Body:**
Most agents walk into a networking event with a sharp pitch and a stack of business cards ready to go. The agents who actually get the referral six months later did something smaller in the room: they asked a question that had nothing to do with the transaction, found the one thing the other person actually cares about, and left with one specific thing to follow up on. A prospect forgets your pitch by the parking lot. They don't forget being asked a real question.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Do this at your next networking event, not that:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Don't ask "where are you from." Ask what's gotten harder for them lately.** A real answer tells you something a pitch never will.
2. **Don't stop at their job title. Find the thing they'd rather talk about.** A hobby, a passion project, whatever actually lights them up.
3. **Don't leave with just a card. Leave with one specific thing to do.** A recommendation, an introduction, an article, something small and real.
4. **Don't file the contact and forget it. Write down what you actually discussed.** The follow-up that proves you remembered is the one that gets returned.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
Make them feel memorable. **Not the other way around.**

**Subhead:**
The follow-up that proves you remembered them is worth more than the pitch ever was.
