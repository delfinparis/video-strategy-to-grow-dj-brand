## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
Brightening the sky is not the same move as erasing a power line.

**Body:**
NAR's Code of Ethics requires members to show a "true picture" in marketing and to refrain from
concealing pertinent facts, and its own August 2026 guidance says AI tools should be used to
"show possibilities" in a home, never to "rewrite reality." California's AB 723, in effect since
January 2026, goes further and requires licensees to disclose any digitally altered image with a
public link to the original. Wisconsin's Act 69 follows in 2027. Adjusting light or color is
enhancement. Removing a permanent fixture, concealing a defect, or changing a structural feature
like a garage or a pool crosses into the kind of edit both the ethics rule and the new laws are
built to catch.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Run every listing photo through this before it goes live:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask what actually changed.** Color, brightness, and sky replacement are enhancement. Removing or adding a permanent feature is not.
2. **Keep the original file.** If your state ever asks for it, "I don't have the unedited version" is not an answer you want to give.
3. **Check your state's disclosure rule.** California and Wisconsin already require a public link to the original photo when an image is digitally altered. More states are watching those two.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
If the edit changes what a buyer will see in person, it's not enhancement anymore.

**Subhead:**
Before your next listing goes live, open the photo folder and ask what was actually changed.
