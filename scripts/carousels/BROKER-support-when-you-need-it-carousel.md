# Broker Problem: what support actually costs you when you need it

Broker-problem comparison carousel, weekly slot (Tuesday). Angle chosen this week: **what
support costs when you actually need it.** No prior `BROKER-*-carousel.md` exists in this
repo, so there is no 8-week rotation conflict to check. Rotation table starts with this
entry.

**Why this angle.** It maps to the number-one, highest-blocking-power objection in
`docs/recruiting-objections.md`: "Cheap means I'll be on my own. No support, no leads,"
often true at low-fee firms. It also matches the named broker pain in
`docs/content-pillars.md` Pillar 3 ("no support" is listed alongside bad splits, junk
fees, dead company leads, no training, no tech). The turn is economic, not just anecdotal:
new agents earn almost nothing in their first two years, which is exactly the window they
need real support most, and it's exactly the window a lot of brokerages have the weakest
incentive to spend real time on an agent who isn't producing yet.

**Compliance with the hard rules:**
1. No competitor brokerage, coaching program, or person named anywhere. Friction points at
   "most brokerages" and "the arrangement," never at a company.
2. No line implies agents at other firms are foolish or getting what they deserve. The
   framing stands with the agent against an incentive structure that leaves new agents
   least supported exactly when they need it most.
3. The one number on the deck (Slide 3) is sourced, dated, and re-verified live at build
   time (see Data Source). The comparison rows on Slide 4 are written qualitatively,
   without invented percentages, because no clean industry-wide figure exists for
   mentor-response times, contract-review rates, or escalation paths by brokerage.
4. No Kale pitch inside the slides. The footer already carries "learn more at
   joinkale.com."

## SLIDE 4 -- SCREENSHOT PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What "support" should mean vs. what most agents actually get:

**Numbered list:**
1. **A mentor who answers.** Most brokerages assign one on the roster and leave you to track them down yourself.
2. **A second set of eyes before you sign.** Most leave your first contract review to you, alone.
3. **Real coaching, not a login.** Most hand you a library of old webinars and call it training.
4. **A name for who owns it when a deal falls apart.** Most tell you to "escalate" and never say to whom.
