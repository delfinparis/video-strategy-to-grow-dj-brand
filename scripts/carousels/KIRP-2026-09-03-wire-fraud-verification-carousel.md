## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
The mistake is trusting the verification, not skipping it.

**Body:**
Most agents already tell clients "call before you wire." The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center
ties last year's fraud surge to AI-generated impersonation: convincing fake emails, cloned voices,
and personalized messages that make the follow-up call part of the con instead of the safeguard.
In one case NAR reported, a buyer at closing wired more than $449,000 after a scammer posed as the
closing attorney by email, and the buyer believed they had already confirmed it.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The swap, before your next closing:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Never call a number from the email.** Use the number already saved from your first real conversation with the title company or attorney, not the one printed at the bottom of "updated wiring instructions."
2. **Treat any last-minute change to wiring instructions as fraud until proven otherwise.** A real title company does not change account numbers three days before closing over email.
3. **Say this to every client before they wire a cent.** "Nobody, including me, will ever email you new wiring instructions. If you get one, call me first, on the number you already have."

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
The warning email was never the safeguard.

**Subhead:**
The independently-verified phone call is. Make sure your client knows the difference.
