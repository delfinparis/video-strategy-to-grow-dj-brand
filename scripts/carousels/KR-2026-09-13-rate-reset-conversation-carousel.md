**Body:**
HousingWire's weekly mortgage rate tracker put the average at 7.12 percent for the week ending
September 11, 2026, the first time this year the number moved back above 7 percent after
favorable mortgage spreads had held it under that line since January. The move followed an
escalation in the Iran conflict, which pushed the 10-year Treasury yield higher. HousingWire's
Logan Mohtashami notes that housing demand tends to fade once rates cross roughly 6.64 percent
and keeps fading the further above 7 percent they go. A buyer who has spent the year hearing
"rates will come down eventually" just watched the number move the wrong direction, and the
agent still repeating that line is coaching a plan the data no longer backs.

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Reset the buyer conversation this week:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Say today's number, not the memory of an old one.** 7.12 percent as of last week, not the
   5.99 percent low from earlier this year they're still anchored to.
2. **Ask what they can actually afford at today's rate.** Not the payment they calculated in the
   spring.
3. **Put a rate buydown or lender credit on the table now**, instead of an open-ended "wait and
   see."
4. **Set an actual re-check date on the calendar.** Not "whenever rates come down." A specific
   week beats an indefinite wait every time.
