construction could mean lower insurance costs" (2026-08-27). That article itself did not carry the
insurance figure, so it was traced to its underlying source and verified directly: fetched

published May 21, 2026), which reports Realtor.com found the average annual home insurance
premium on a newly built home ran 35 percent lower than on a 20-year-old home in 2025, a savings

article's own separate median-price figures were not pulled in here, to avoid combining two
different studies' numbers into one implied claim (Rule 1).

premium, that runs meaningfully lower than an older comparable home, and most buyer conversations
never surface it. Rule 0 criterion 4, a surprising, sourced statistic (35 percent, $1,002/year),

## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
The sticker price is not the whole cost comparison.

**Body:**
Realtor.com's 2026 analysis found the average annual home insurance premium on a newly built home
ran 35 percent lower than on a 20-year-old home, a savings of about $1,002 a year and roughly
$7,300 over the first decade. Newer wiring, newer roofs, and current building codes mean fewer
claims, and insurers price that in before a buyer ever asks. A buyer weighing a new build against
an older home is not just comparing purchase price. They're comparing two different insurance
bills for as long as they own the place.

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What to add to every new-construction conversation this month:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask for an insurance quote before the offer, not after.** A same-day quote on both properties turns an abstract number into a real one.
2. **Put the annual premium gap on the comparison sheet.** Buyers weigh a $1,002-a-year difference the same way they weigh a monthly payment.
3. **Say the ten-year number out loud.** $7,300 over a decade reframes a sticker-price hesitation into a total-cost decision.
