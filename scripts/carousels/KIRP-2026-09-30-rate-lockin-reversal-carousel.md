## Data Source

Every figure matches `scripts/inside-the-industry/NF-033-the-thaw-is-here.md`, re-checked
against that script's own Data Source section at carousel-build time.

- **"The share of homeowners with a rate at 6 percent or higher just tripled, from 7.3 percent
  to 21.2 percent... bigger than the under-3-percent group for the first time"**
  - Source: FHFA National Mortgage Database, via Redfin and WolfStreet. The 6%+ share rose from
    a 7.3% low (Q2 2022) to 21.2% (Q3 2025), the highest since roughly 2015, and now exceeds the
    sub-3% share for the first time
    ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/mortgage-rate-lock-in-effect-eases/),
    [WolfStreet](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/01/03/locked-in-homeowners-nevertheless-pay-off-below-4-mortgages-their-share-of-all-mortgages-drops-to-lowest-since-q4-2020/)).
  - Who was measured: All outstanding US first-lien mortgages.
  - Status: confirmed, exact figures, not rounded.

- **"First-time buyers... were more than half of every purchase loan in March. Highest since
  2020"**
  - Source: ICE May 2026 Mortgage Monitor: "First-time homebuyers accounted for more than half
    of all purchase loans closed in March, the highest share since June 2020"
    ([link](https://mortgagetech.ice.com/resources/data-reports/may-2026-mortgage-monitor)).
  - Who was measured: Share of mortgaged purchase loans closed in March 2026 (not share of all
    buyers; NAR's all-buyer first-timer figure is different and lower because it includes cash
    buyers).
  - Status: confirmed, "more than half" is ICE's exact framing.

- **"The share of owners stuck under 6 percent has dropped every single quarter, from 92.7 down
  to 80.3"**
  - Source: FHFA NMDB via Redfin: below-6% share 92.7% (Q2 2022), 87.7% (Q3 2023), 82.8%
    (Q3 2024), 80.3% (Q2 2025)
    ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/rate-lock-q2-2025/)).
  - Who was measured: US mortgaged homeowners.
  - Status: confirmed, exact endpoints; the "every quarter" decline is supported by the full
    series.

- **Not spoken on the slides, kept for integrity:** the lock-in effect persists even as it
  eases. ICE's May 2026 data still describes lock-in actively constraining existing owners, and
  a Coldwell Banker April 2026 agent survey found 61% still call it a major or moderate factor.
  The deck's claim is "thawing," never "thawed."
