# Carousel: 80 percent of sellers contact only one agent

Evergreen tip deck, built from a single sourced data point with an actionable payload.
Theme is `light`, alternating off the dark `BROKER-support-when-you-need-it-carousel`
built earlier the same day. Hook family is `data-card`, which differs from the previous
deck's `system-audit`.

**Why this data point.** Exhibit 7-3 of NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers is
the rare industry number that changes what an agent does on Monday morning rather than
what they think about the market. If four out of five sellers never contact a second
agent, then listing-presentation polish is being optimized for a contest that usually is
not happening, and the actual competition is over whose name a seller already has. Exhibit
7-2 in the same report says where that name comes from, so the payload can be built out of
the same source rather than out of opinion.

**Rule 10.4 mechanic: the defensible line (slide 2).** "Your listing presentation is
rarely a competition" is a contestable read of the data, not a settled fact. Listing
agents who have lost head-to-head appointments will want to argue the point, and agents
who have won on relationship will back it up. Nobody is asked to comment.

**Numbers audit.** Four figures appear on the slides (80, 12, 5, 37, 29, 1 percent), all
of them from the same NAR report, all re-fetched from the primary PDF at build time. No
number is stated anywhere else in the deck, and no percentage is attached to slide 4's
fourth item, which is a skill-development action with no data claim behind it.

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Four ways to become the one agent they call:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Write the list of people who would say your name.** 37 percent of sellers were referred by a friend, neighbor, or relative. Build that list this week and count it. The number you get is your real pipeline.
2. **Put every past client's closing date in your calendar.** 29 percent of sellers used the agent they had already bought or sold with. One call a year on that date is what keeps you the default.
3. **Post for the people who already know you.** 1 percent of sellers found their agent through a social page they had no connection to. Your feed is a reminder to your circle, not a net for strangers.
4. **Rehearse your pricing explanation out loud, alone.** With one agent in the room, nobody is comparing you to anyone. The reasoning has to hold up on its own, so practice it until it does.
