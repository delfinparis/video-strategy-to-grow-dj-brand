repeat/referral split, so this is a new angle on an already-vetted source, not a repeat of either.

**The one idea:** a brand-new agent's repeat and referral business isn't low because they are
worse at the job. It is zero because they have not been in business long enough to have past
clients yet, which means a split or support structure built around a 16-year veteran's referral
pipeline does nothing for someone starting from zero. Rule 0 criterion 3 (pattern reveal: what
separates production isn't skill, it's tenure) and criterion 4 (a sourced number that changes
who a recruiting conversation should be aimed at). Hook family `named-stakes` (the two tenure
groups, named directly against each other), differing from today's two KIRP decks
(`one-tactic-breakdown` and `data-card`) and from the most recently committed carousel pair
(`mirror` and `myth-bust`, both `*-2026-10-11-*-carousel.md`).

**Headline:**
Repeat business isn't earned by being good. It's earned by having been in business long enough.

**Body:**
NAR's 2026 Member Profile found repeat clients make up a median 49 percent of business for
agents with 16 or more years in the field, and referrals make up another 32 percent. For agents

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
If you're in your first two years, or weighing where a new agent should start:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Don't budget for repeat business you don't have yet.** A new agent's plan has to run on
   fresh leads, not referrals, because the referral pipeline takes years to build.
2. **Ask a brokerage exactly how a brand-new agent gets leads.** A split built around a
   15-year veteran's referral book does nothing for someone starting at zero.
3. **Track your own repeat and referral percentage every year.** Watching it rise off zero is
   the real sign the business is compounding, not just surviving.
4. **Weight training and lead support higher than commission split in year one.** The split
   matters more once you have the repeat business to actually keep.

  - Source: same NAR 2026 Member Profile release, same HousingWire article.

  reading that a new agent has not yet had years to accumulate repeat or referral clients.
