## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
Most agents agree to list high. **Top agents show the math** instead.

**Body:**
A listing priced above the comps doesn't get a chance to prove itself; it just sits. Then the price cut reads as a red flag instead of a correction, because buyers who watched it sit already assume the seller is difficult or the house has a problem. The agents who avoid that outcome don't argue with the seller about the number. They just show the math before anyone agrees to one.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The swap, three conversations:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Don't say "let's start high and see."** Say "the first two weeks get more eyes than this listing will ever see again. Let's price it to win that window."
2. **Don't agree to a number with no comps behind it.** Pull the three closest sold comparables and show the seller the range before you agree to a list price.
3. **Don't wait for the seller to ask for a price cut.** Set a 14-day check-in on the calendar the day you sign the listing, so the pricing conversation happens on your terms.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
Price it to **win** the first two weeks, not to please the seller today.

**Subhead:**
The showings you get in week one are the offers you'll be comparing in week four.
