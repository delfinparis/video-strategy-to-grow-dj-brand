## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
Nearly half of listings now come with something extra.

**Body:**
Realtor.com's August 2026 Monthly Rent Report puts the national concession rate at 43.5 percent
of studio, one-, and two-bedroom listings across the 50 largest metros: free weeks of rent,
waived fees, or a reduced deposit. In Denver, 71.9 percent of listings carried one. In Austin,
70.7 percent. Senior Economist Jiayi Xu's report frames it plainly: renters are entering fall
with more choices and more negotiating room than they've had at any point since the market
peaked in 2022. The math that used to favor the landlord in the lease conversation has been
tilting the other way for three straight years.

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
What to do differently with renters right now:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Tell renter clients they have room to ask.** In markets running near 70 percent
   concessions, most listings already include one. Coach them to ask before they sign, not after.
2. **Bring the concession number into the buy-versus-rent conversation.** A renter weighing a
   purchase should know their current lease has more give in it than it did two summers ago,
   which changes the urgency math on both sides.
3. **Use the national trend as a Chicago-agnostic opener.** "Rent has fallen nationally for over
   three years straight" is a real conversation starter with an investor or a first-time buyer,
   and it's honestly a national figure, not a local one, so frame it that way.
