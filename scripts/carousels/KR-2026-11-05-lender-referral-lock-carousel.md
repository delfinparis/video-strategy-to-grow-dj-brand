**The one idea:** when a mega-brokerage folds its mortgage arm into one in-house lender, the loan
officer across the table from a buyer increasingly answers to the same parent company as a
competing brokerage's agents. An independent agent who never formalized a lender relationship of
their own is the one who quietly loses that referral loop, not the one who loses a single sale.

## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
The loan officer isn't neutral anymore.

**Body:**
When a brokerage owns the mortgage company, the loan officer sitting across from a buyer reports
up through the same parent as a competing agent's whole referral network. Compass's CEO called
the new venture a "powerhouse lending platform," and Rate's CEO says onboarding hundreds of
originators is "just the beginning." None of that loop is open to an agent outside Compass. The
buyer still gets a loan either way. The referral relationship that used to belong to the agent
just became somebody else's asset.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Do this before your next buyer:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Pick one lender and put the referral relationship in writing.** A verbal "I usually send
   people to Dave" isn't a partnership, it's a habit someone else can outbid.
2. **Ask your go-to lender what they can match on speed.** A 10-day close promise is now the bar
   a buyer's other agent might casually mention.
3. **Tell your buyer upfront who you work with and why.** The agents who lose this referral loop
   are the ones who never named a lender at all.
