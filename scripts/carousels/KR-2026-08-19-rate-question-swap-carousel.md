## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
They didn't ask for **optimism.** They asked for a plan.

**Body:**
When a buyer asks if rates will drop, they're really asking whether to act now or keep waiting.
Answering with a guess, in either direction, hands them a reason to stall. Answering with their
actual number at today's rate hands them something they can decide with.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The swap for the rate-drop question:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Don't say "rates might come down."** Nobody can promise that, and when they don't, the buyer stops trusting your read on everything else.
2. **Don't say "just buy now regardless."** It sounds like a pitch, not advice, and the buyer hears the difference.
3. **Say this instead.** "Today's rate is 6.54%. Let's run your actual payment at that number, so you're deciding on something real, not guessing about the future."
4. **Offer to re-run the numbers if the rate moves.** It makes the buyer feel tracked instead of pressured.
