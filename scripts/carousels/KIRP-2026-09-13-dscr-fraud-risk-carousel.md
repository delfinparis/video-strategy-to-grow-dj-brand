## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN

**Headline:**
The risk lives in the property type, not the borrower's story.

**Body:**
Cotality, which tracks mortgage fraud risk signals, found that by the end of the second quarter of
2026, 1 in 119 mortgage applications overall showed signs of elevated fraud risk. For investment
properties, that jumped to 1 in 44, and for two-to-four-unit properties it rose to 1 in 27. Matt
Seguin, Cotality's senior principal of mortgage fraud solutions, said those two segments have been
the riskiest in the market for fifteen-plus years, and their share of total loan volume climbed
from about 7 percent in 2024 to about 12 percent in 2026, a 58 percent jump in two years. The data
tracks where the red flags fire, not confirmed fraud, and it includes both DSCR and
government-backed investor loans. Either way, the segment agents wave through as a routine close
is the one the fraud data is watching hardest.

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
Before you send a client to a DSCR lender:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Ask what fraud checks the lender actually runs.** LLC cross-checks, document verification, and
   appraisal review aren't standard at every DSCR lender.
2. **Get the loan-to-value in writing.** A borrower putting 30 percent down is a different risk
   than one putting 10.
3. **Confirm a personal guarantee is required on the LLC.** It's the lender's main leverage if the
   deal goes bad, and it's worth knowing whether yours has one.
4. **Ask about the property type before the loan type.** Two-to-four-unit investment properties
   carry the highest flag rate in the data. Price that into the conversation with your client.
