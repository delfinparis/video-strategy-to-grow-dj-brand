## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
Buyers have a name for it. Regulators are catching up.

**Body:**
California's AB 723, effective this year, requires any digitally altered real estate photo to
carry a disclosure and a link to the unedited original. New York City's mayor has proposed the
same rule for rental listings, after "housefishing" spread as the word for repainted walls,
invented lawns, and a listed "loft" that turned out to have no ladder, let alone a room. Even
Zillow told Fortune it supports the disclosure and wants buyers able to see the original image next
to the altered one. The direction is the same everywhere: label it, or don't run it.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Headline:**
The swap, before your next listing goes live:

**Numbered list:**
1. **Never post a virtually staged or AI-edited photo without labeling it.** One line, "virtually staged," costs nothing and stops the walkthrough from feeling like a bait and switch.
2. **Keep the real, unedited photo on file, disclosed or not.** California's law now requires a link to it. Where it isn't required yet, a buyer who asks should get it the same day.
3. **Don't let AI remove anything a buyer will notice is gone.** The utility pole, the busy street, the cracked driveway. Say so before the showing, not after.
