## SLIDE 3 -- THE TURN
**Headline:**
It's not venting. It's a rule with your name on it.

**Body:**
NAR's Code of Ethics Article 15 says REALTORS shall not knowingly or recklessly make false or
misleading statements about other real estate professionals, their businesses, or their business
practices. Standard of Practice 15-2 extends that to repeating someone else's false statement,
in person, in writing, or online. SOP 15-3 adds a duty to correct or take down a false statement
on any electronic media you control once you know it's false. "That agent's impossible to work
with" or "the seller's lying about the repairs" said to your own client, with nothing behind it,
is the exact conduct the article exists to stop.

---

## SLIDE 4 -- PAYLOAD
**Say this instead:**
1. **Describe the behavior, not the person.** "They haven't responded to our last two emails" beats
   "that agent's useless."
2. **Name what you don't know as what you don't know.** "I'm not sure why they've gone quiet, but
   here's what we're doing about it" beats guessing at a motive out loud.
3. **Keep a guess about the seller's state of mind out of your client's head.** A fact you can
   prove is fine to share. A story about why you think they're acting that way is not.
4. **Put the frustrated version in a text to your broker, not your client.** Vent somewhere it
   doesn't become a record of what you told your buyer.

---

## SLIDE 5 -- TAKEAWAY
**Headline:**
Before your next frustrated text about the other agent, read it like NAR would.

**Subhead:**
If you couldn't prove it in front of an ethics panel, don't send it to your client.
