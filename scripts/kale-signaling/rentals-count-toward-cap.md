# Walk & Talk — "Rentals Now Count Toward Your Cap"

**Format:** Talking-head / walk-and-talk, ~75s
**Pillar:** Kale-signaling (recruiting + agent hype)
**Created:** 2026-06-16
**Scroll-stopper:** Spoken in line one (captions.ai pulls from audio, so the hook lives in what you say)

Plays double duty: hypes current Kale agents and shows recruits what a brokerage on their side looks like (D.J.'s personal feed is a recruit audience per `docs/content-recruiting-integration.md`).

## Spoken Script

> Rentals used to be the thing nobody at Kale wanted to do. We just turned them into the fastest way to a 100% commission year.
>
> Quick refresher on the cap. Every agent pays the company up to 6,000 dollars a year. Hit that number and you're capped. After that you keep 100% of every commission until your anniversary date resets it.
>
> The old math was rough. The only thing that counted toward your cap was the 400 dollar fee on a sale. Fifteen closings just to get there. (I did the multiplication twice. It's fifteen.)
>
> Here's what changed. Your rental commissions count toward the cap now too.
>
> We're 80/20 on rentals. You keep 80%, the company takes 20%, and that 20% lands right on your cap.
>
> Say you rent a place at 2,500 a month. The commission's one month's rent. You pocket 2,000, and 500 of it knocks your cap down. Every rental does that now. It pays you and shrinks your cap at the same time.
>
> Mix in a couple sales and you'll cap long before fifteen closings. Then every deal the rest of your year, rentals included, is 100% yours.
>
> Rentals just went from busywork to your head start. So go rent something.

## Data Source

Internal Kale Realty policy change (provided by D.J., 6/16/26): $6,000 annual cap; previously reached only via the $400 per-sale transaction fee; rental commissions now apply toward the cap; rentals split 80/20 (agent/company); the company's 20% counts toward the cap; rental commission typically equals one month's rent; cap resets annually on the agent's join-date anniversary. The $2,500/month rent in the example is illustrative, not a fixed figure.
