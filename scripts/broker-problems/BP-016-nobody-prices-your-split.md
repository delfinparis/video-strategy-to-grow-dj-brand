# Nobody prices your split

> **WOW: This is the one script in the lane that agrees with the objection. The split does buy real things. Nobody has ever put a price next to any of them, which means the defense of the split has never been tested by the people making it.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You know one of those numbers exactly. You've never checked the other."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 81 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
I run a brokerage. Nobody prices your split.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
Everybody defending it names the same five things, and they're all real.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
Insurance. Compliance. A managing broker on the hook for your deal. Somebody chasing paperwork. The office. Good list, so write those down and find out what each one costs. Obviously the firm has to make money, I get that.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then total it against what you handed over last year. You know one of those numbers exactly. You've never checked the other.

## Production Note

- **This is the concession made into a whole script, and that is the entire design.** BP-013 ran the split arithmetic on 2026-09-24 and the objection it draws is predictable: the split buys errors and omissions coverage, compliance, a managing broker carrying the liability, transaction coordination, and a place to work. The bank's instruction for E2 is explicit and it is the right one. Do not rebut that list. Concede it, in full, out loud, and then ask what each line on it costs. The reply to the objection is not an argument, it is a price.
- **No figure is spoken, and none may be added.** The register's second move is "perform the arithmetic, do not present it," and the instinct on a script like this is to hesitate toward a number: insurance is what, a few hundred a year. **There is no sourced market price for any of the five items and the bank marks E2 receipt-not-needed precisely because the agent supplies the arithmetic.** The performed beat here is the listing, not the pricing. If a number appears in this script, the receipt was invented and the script dies, not the entry.
- **The concession is intact and it is verbatim from the register.** "Obviously the firm has to make money, I get that." Never expand it, never move it earlier, never cut it for time. It is what separates this from a rant against arithmetic, and in this script it is doing double duty, because the whole premise is a concession.
- **Family 3, and the credential is load-bearing here more than anywhere in the lane.** "I run a brokerage" is the permitted self-reference and it is the reason the concession reads as knowledge rather than as fairness. A brokerage president listing what a brokerage actually provides, and then declining to price it, is a different act from an agent complaining. BP-014 ran Family 5 and BP-015 runs Family 4, so the Defector is clean on the two-post rule. BP-013 also ran Family 3 seven days earlier; that is the risk in this build and it is accepted, because E2 without the credential is just BP-009 again.
- **Pattern interrupt: Location Cold-Open.** Planted outside an office building, a storefront, a lobby, anything that reads as overhead rather than as a listing. The walk starts on "five." The office is one of the five items and it is the only one with a visual, so the frame is doing an item of the list before the words get to it. BP-015 runs The Whip and BP-014 ran The Stop, so the move is clean. Do not shoot this outside anything with a readable brokerage name on the glass.
- **Rung 2, unambiguously.** "Find out what each one costs" is the market look, and it is the rare version an agent can do without talking to a competitor: insurance has a quotable price, coordinators quote per file, desk space has a rate. That is why this entry is the best rung-2 script in the bank. The payoff then refuses to do the comparison for them.
- **The comparison is never resolved on camera and the last line is not a verdict.** "You know one of those numbers exactly. You've never checked the other" is a gap, not a conclusion, and the register's sixth move is to close on the open question. If D.J. adds "and I think you know which is bigger," the script becomes a dare and the viewer defends six years of their own judgment out loud.
- **Ship test:** the five items are what every brokerage in the country provides, named in the plainest available words. No firm, franchise, product, or coach is identifiable. Kale is not mentioned, and "I run a brokerage" is the one permitted self-reference, with nothing offered.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president concede their broker's entire defense, name the five things his own firm provides, and then tell them to go price each one. Respect goes up, and this is the script in the lane most likely to be forwarded to a managing broker, which is fine. It says nothing a managing broker can dispute.
- **First-hour ops:** no link to pin. The pinned comment carries the two items with the easiest prices to find, because the most common reply on this script will be that the agent does not know where to start. **Comment duty applies.** Flag every Chicago agent who reports their own total into Close.

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

The most common reply to this is that people do not know where to start pricing it, so the order I would go in, easiest first.

Errors and omissions coverage: independent brokers quote it, and so do the carriers directly. It is a phone call. Transaction coordination: coordinators publish per file rates and you probably know two. Office space: a desk or a small private office has a market rate in any neighborhood you would actually work in.

Compliance review and the managing broker are the two that resist pricing, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. What they cost a firm is real and it is not on a rate card. My honest suggestion is to leave both of them out of your total, run the arithmetic on the three that do price, and see where you land before you have counted the hard two at all.

If the three alone clear what you handed over last year, that is worth knowing. If they do not, that is worth knowing too.
