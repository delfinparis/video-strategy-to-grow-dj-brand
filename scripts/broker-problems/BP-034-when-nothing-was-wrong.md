# When nothing was wrong

> **WOW: the agent who never hears from her broker reads it as her own fault, or as being needy for noticing. One stranger's number turns a private feeling into arithmetic, and the second question turns the arithmetic around: not how often you called them, how often anybody called you when there was nothing to fix.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Nothing was ever wrong enough to make the call."

## Teleprompter

You stopped expecting your broker to call.

A study last year interviewed ten agents who just quit.

Ten people, that's not proof of anything. One of them said this. Maybe ten conversations with their broker in thirteen years. Nobody was cruel. Nothing was ever wrong enough to make the call.

So count yours. Then the better one. When did anyone there last call you when nothing was wrong?

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** this is a gap hook aimed at the viewer's own adjustment rather than at a fact. **"You stopped expecting" presupposes that she once did expect it, which she has never thought about, and the gap is between the agent she was at licensing and the one watching.** Heath rates the mechanism above BP-002's counting instruction on one ground and only one: an instruction can be refused in the first second, while a presupposition has to be examined before it can be refused. **His objection, logged:** the point beat asks the viewer to hold a number, a hedge and a turn in fifteen seconds, which is a lot. He accepts it because the number is small and odd enough to stick on its own, and because "nobody was cruel" is the kind of concession a viewer does not have to work to accept.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** this one shares privately, agent to agent, in a DM, with "this is us," and private sends are the signal that moves non-follower reach. **The retell is "one agent talked to her broker ten times in thirteen years and nobody was even mean to her," which survives compression with the point intact.** Berger's warning, and it is specific to this script: the compressed version loses the sample size every single time, which is why the hedge is spoken rather than only pinned. **He also notes the emotion is the weakest of the week.** Recognition is lower arousal than anger, which is why this flavor needs the wrong default in the hook to earn its slot, and why it still gets fewer forwards than a fee script and more conversations.
- **Retention move (MrBeast, doctrine applied):** the second question arriving at twenty-two seconds is a fresh hook at the point where a one-idea video normally ends, and it is a different question from the one the video opened. **The viewer who was counting her own conversations has to start a second count.** The doctrine's objection: at thirty seconds there is no room for the stall BP-002 used, the repetition of "Ten. In thirteen years," so the number gets one pass and one pass only. **Accepted, and Pass 3 answered it:** the number is now the oddest thing in the sentence rather than a repeated one, and oddness holds without a stall.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Eric Simon says the script buries its best line and the lane keeps doing this.** His position: "When did anyone there last call you when nothing was wrong" is the sentence agents will actually repeat to each other, it is a question with no good answer, and it is sitting at twenty-four seconds where a third of the audience has already gone. He wants it at 0:00 and the "you stopped expecting" framing moved into the tension beat. **Chris Do and Miller oppose, and they are the two who care most about the close.** Chris Do: the video's whole value is that the question arrives after the viewer has been given a stranger's number to measure herself against, and asked cold it is just a complaint about her broker. Miller: promoting it leaves the script ending on a count, which is an instruction, and this is the one script in the lane whose payload is a feeling rather than an action. **Where Eric Simon is right is that the lane has now twice declined to test its own strongest line in the hook position, once on BP-002 and once here.** **Decision: ship the default, because the question only works as a reversal and a reversal needs something to reverse.** **The A/B:** run the question-first variant, same receipt, same turn, with "you stopped expecting your broker to call" demoted to the tension beat, judged on 3-second retention and on whether the comments carry dates or only agreement. **A date in a comment is the success condition for this entry and it is worth finding out which order produces more of them.**

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a good agent who might join Kale watches a brokerage president quote one stranger, say out loud that one stranger is not evidence, remove the villain from the story, and then ask her a question about her own life that he does not answer. **Respect goes up, and the sample-size concession is the reason.** The delivery that flips it is pity. Puzzled, it is a man noticing something odd about his own industry. Sorry, it is a man selling a feeling, and the next thing a viewer looks for is what he is selling.
