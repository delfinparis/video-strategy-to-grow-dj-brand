# Your reviews aren't yours

> **WOW: Criterion 2 (contrarian take) into criterion 4 (a specific number that lands wrong). Every listing agent believes their reputation is an asset they own. The eleven is what makes it visceral -- thirteen years of goodwill, and the part she actually owned fit on one screen.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Thirteen years of people saying nice things about her. She owned eleven of them."

## Spoken Script (~58 seconds)

**THE THING**

Your reviews aren't yours. Not if they're sitting on a platform your brokerage pays for.

**THE SIZE**

A top producer told me this on my podcast a couple years ago. Thirteen years in the business. Hundreds of testimonials, all of them collected through the review system her brokerage licensed. She changed firms. Every single one stayed behind. So she went and looked at her Google page, the one place that was actually hers, and she had eleven reviews. Thirteen years. Eleven reviews.

**THE MIRROR**

So go find out where yours live. Open the thing you send clients after closing and look for an export button. If there isn't one, you already know. And then go find out something harder, which is whether that's just how this works everywhere, or whether that's how it works where you are. Because I don't think most agents have ever checked. It's not just reviews either. It's your database, your listing photos, the email address on ten thousand business cards.

**LOOP-BACK**

Thirteen years of people saying nice things about her. She owned eleven of them.

## Production Note

**Pattern interrupt (move 5, One Prop).** Phone up before word one, a Google reviews page on screen. Do not narrate the phone. Hold it through "not if they're sitting on a platform your brokerage pays for," then drop it. Bring it back up on "go check that one tonight."

**Re-hook placement.** The re-hook is "eleven reviews," at roughly 0:22. Deliver it flat and then repeat the pair, "thirteen years, eleven reviews," with a real beat of silence between them. That silence is the whole video. Do not fill it.

**The name stays off camera.** The review platform is named in the source quote and it is a real product. **Do not say it.** "The review system her brokerage licensed" carries the identical meaning with none of the exposure. Same for the brokerage. This is the heat-5 floor and it is not negotiable (see the standard's identifiable-target rule).

**Naming the guest is fine and it helps.** Sara is the protagonist here, not the target, and a named guest from a real episode is exactly the first-party receipt Rule 9.3 asks for on a stat-light day. Confirm the spelling and the episode number against the show archive before the caption goes out (see Data Source).

**Beat 3 discipline.** The close is "go look," never "go leave." An agent six years into a firm is not switching because of a 58-second video, and trying to make them turns this into a pitch, which kills both the reach and the comments. Hand over the look. The conclusion is theirs.

**Intentional incompleteness (Rule 10.4).** Four things get named in beat 3 -- reviews, database, listing photos, the email address -- and only reviews gets unpacked. The other three are the comment engine. Agents will supply the fifth one themselves, and that fifth one is the recruiting signal. Do not unpack them on camera to be thorough. Thoroughness is what kills this device.

**Rung 2, and this is the line that does the actual work:** *"whether that's just how this works everywhere, or whether that's how it works where you are. Because I don't think most agents have ever checked."* Rung 1 alone would have this video end at an export button, which is recognition. That sentence is what turns it into exploring, and it does it without naming a single alternative -- the agent has to go find out, and finding out is the objective. **Deliver it slower than the rest of beat 3.** It is doing more than it sounds like it is doing.

**First-hour ops.** Pin a first comment naming the episode and the date so the story is checkable. **Comment duty applies:** read every reply and flag Chicago agents who out their own firm into Close for Ana and Jennica.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "Your reviews aren't yours. Not if they're sitting on a platform your brokerage pays for."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: alarm]
2. "A top producer with thirteen years in this business owned eleven of her own reviews. She just didn't know it yet."  [hook_family: 6 Named Stakes | emotion: curiosity]
3. "Nobody at your brokerage is going to bring this up, so I will. Go find out who owns your reviews."  [hook_family: 7 The Forbidden | emotion: suspicion] **Use this one if BP-001 lands in a week where variant 1's family already ran.**

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, move 5 (One Prop). Phone up on the hook with a Google reviews page visible, dropped on "pays for," raised again on "tonight." The prop is the argument made physical -- the small number on the screen is the whole point.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** violates a possession schema, which is a stronger break than violating a belief. Agents will argue about whether open houses work. Nobody has ever considered whether their reviews are theirs, so there is no counter-position loaded and the gap opens instantly. "Not if" in the second clause is what converts the alarm into a check rather than a dismissal.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** practical value plus a small, free, immediately-doable action. The forward is agent-to-agent inside the same firm, which is the highest-value share this lane can get -- it puts the question inside a brokerage rather than in front of one agent.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the number arrives at 0:22, exactly where a story-shaped video starts to sag, and the repetition of "thirteen years, eleven reviews" is a deliberate stall on the strongest beat. The close is abrupt on "she owned eleven of them," no wind-down.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** the sharpest reply will be "that's a platform problem, not a brokerage problem, and she should have been collecting on Google the whole time." That reply is correct and it is *good for the video* -- it is the defensible-line device from Rule 10.4 working, and the agents it happened to will show up to back the story. Do not pre-empt it in the script. The A/B worth running is Variant A as written against Variant B, which adds one line after the mirror: "and yes, she should have been collecting on Google the whole time. Nobody told her that either." B concedes the objection and redirects it at the silence rather than at her. Judge on reply volume and on how many repliers name their own firm's setup, not on views.

**Recruiting test (guardrail 3):** a good agent who might join Kale watches this and sees a brokerage president warning them about a thing that would cost him nothing to stay quiet about, with no pitch attached. That is the entire recruiting mechanism in this lane, and it only works because the video does not ask for anything.
