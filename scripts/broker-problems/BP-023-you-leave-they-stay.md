# You leave, they stay

> **WOW: BP-001 found the one brokerage cost that cannot be refunded, which is a decade of other people's words living on a system somebody else licenses, and then spent 195 words getting there. The re-cut gives the whole budget to the number. Thirteen years in, and the part she owned fit on one screen.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Nobody there is going to bring it up."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 76 words)

Nobody will tell you your reviews aren't yours.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**

So go find out whether other firms let you keep yours. Nobody there is going to bring it up.

## Production Note

- **This is a re-cut of BP-001, not a new build.** BP-001's argument is intact: the testimonials an agent spends a decade collecting live on a system the brokerage licenses, and leaving does not bring them along. What changed is the runtime, the hook family, the heat, and the name handling. BP-001 ran 195 words to the retired 45-70 second spec and sat at heat 3.5. - **The guest's name is not spoken and is not in any caption, and that is a change from BP-001.** BP-001 named her and flagged, in its own Data Source, that the spelling needed confirming before air because the auto-transcript renders it one way and the archive filename another. This build could not resolve that: the transcript archive is a separate repository that was not reachable from this container, and a public search returned a real estate agent with the same name in a different market, which is exactly the kind of near-miss that produces a wrong tag. **So the story runs as "a top producer told me this on my show," which costs the script nothing at 30 seconds and removes the one line in it that could be wrong about a real person.** If D.J. confirms the spelling and the episode number against the archive, the name can go in the pinned comment. It still should not go in the spoken script, because the name is not what makes the story land and 76 words have no room for it.
- **The receipt is first-party and the limit is spoken.** She said "I think I had 11." The script says "Eleven reviews. 3. "Thirteen years of reviews. She kept eleven."  [hook_family: 6 Named Stakes | emotion: anxiety] MrBeast's pick and the strongest on retention, because the number is the whole video and it arrives at second one. **Rejected on a Rule 1 risk, not on craft:** "she kept eleven" states as fact what she said she thought, and the hedge cannot fit in a seven-word hook. **Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, The Stop. Walk fast for a beat, plant, lean in on "aren't yours." BP-022 ran Walk-Toward, so the move is clean. Eric Simon wanted BP-001's One Prop back, phone up with a reviews page visible, on the grounds that a small number on a screen is the argument made physical. **Overruled, two ways.** Hormozi: at 30 seconds a prop has no time to pay off and the hook is already carrying the load. The number is said out loud and that is enough.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** this violates a possession schema, which Heath rules is a harder break than violating a belief, because there is no counter-position loaded. Agents argue about open houses. No agent has ever formed an opinion on whether their reviews are theirs, so the gap opens instantly and completely. His note on this cut specifically: moving the hook from the possession claim to the silence around it ("Nobody will tell you") adds a second gap on top of the first, which is why the heat goes up without the hook getting louder. His Curse-of-Knowledge check: "testimonial platform," "license," and "data portability" are all trade terms and none are spoken. The script says "a platform your brokerage pays for."
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** high-arousal suspicion, with the forward running agent to agent inside the same firm, which is the highest-value share this lane produces because it puts the question inside a brokerage rather than in front of one agent. Berger's retell test: "your reviews live on your brokerage's platform and you lose them when you leave" survives compression with D.J.'s point intact, and it survives better than the eleven does, because the number needs its hedge and a retelling will drop it. **That is an argument for the current hook over variant 3**, independent of the Rule 1 risk.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the number lands at second eighteen, which is exactly where a story-shaped 30-second video sags, and the pause after it is the one deliberate stall in the script. His objection: BP-001 repeated "thirteen years, eleven reviews" and the repetition was the best moment in it, and this cut cannot afford it. Accepted as a real loss. His condition, adopted: the pause gets protected in the edit, and the close is abrupt with no wind-down.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Miller says the hook buried the lede.** His position: "Nobody will tell you" is a sentence about information, and the actual news is that the reviews are not yours. The viewer has to get through five words of framing before the claim arrives, which at 1.5 seconds is most of the hook. He wants variant 2 as the default and says the Forbidden framing is a flourish the lane added for heat rather than for clarity. **Kane and Heath oppose.** Kane: variant 2 is BP-001's hook, BP-001 never aired, and running the same frame with no test is how a lane learns nothing from three scripts a week. Heath: the two gaps stack rather than compete, and the silence gap is what makes the possession gap feel deliberate rather than accidental. **Where Miller wins is that Family 4 would be the correct default if rotation allowed it this week.** Decision: ship variant 1, and run Miller's variant next time B1's angle 2 comes up, which the bank already has queued as entry B2. **The A/B:** variant 1 against variant 2, the silence against the possession claim, judged on three-second retention and on whether repliers name where their own reviews live. If variant 2 wins, the Forbidden family should stop being used to lift heat on scripts whose claim is already strong enough to lead.
