# Add up your split

> **WOW: Every agent knows their split as a percentage and almost none have converted it into a dollar figure with a year attached. The moment it becomes eighteen thousand dollars it stops being a rate and becomes a purchase, and a purchase has to have bought something. The brand is the answer they are handed, and it is the one thing on the list nobody has ever priced.**

> **Strongest Line:** "So what did eighteen thousand buy?"

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 81 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
I run a brokerage. Add up your split.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
Nobody does. It stays a percentage, and percentages never feel like money.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
Twelve deals, commission's what, seventy-five hundred? Twenty percent is fifteen hundred a closing. Times twelve. Eighteen thousand dollars. For eighteen grand you hire a coach, throw client parties, sponsor events. Obviously the firm has to make money, I get that.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So what did eighteen thousand buy? They'll say the brand. Find out what this market charges. Then add yours up again.

*Word count: 81 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 8, tension 12, point 40, payoff 21. If the take runs long, cut "sponsor events" and let the first two items carry the list. Do not cut the concession, the hesitation on "commission's what," or the closing question.*

## Production Note

- **This is a re-cut of BP-005, not a new argument.** BP-005 was written to the 45-70 second spec Rule 7 retired on 2026-08-25 and ran 224 words. The arithmetic, the concession, the foreclosed-purchase list, the brand objection and the soft close are all preserved. What came out was the two named Chicago principals and the spoken 1,480, which could not survive the word budget. BP-005 stays in the repo as the long-form original; do not delete it and do not post both.
- **Pattern interrupt: The Push-In.** Start at arm's length, pull the phone in as "eighteen thousand dollars" lands, calculator visible in frame if the light allows. BP-012 ran One Prop and BP-011 ran The Stop, so the move is clean on the two-post rule.
- **Perform the arithmetic. Do not present it.** "Commission's what, seventy-five hundred?" with the upward inflection is the single most important delivery note in the script. A clean number reads as a recital; a number being worked out reads as thinking, and it is what makes the viewer run their own alongside. An editor will want to smooth it into "commission is seventy-five hundred." Refuse it.
- **The concession is load-bearing and it is one clause.** "Obviously the firm has to make money, I get that." Do not expand it, do not move it earlier, do not cut it for time. Without it this is a man angry at arithmetic. With it, he is reasonable and the number does the work.
- **The list goes fast.** Coach, client parties, events. Three items, none elaborated. Speed is what turns a line item into a life the agent did not get to have. Slowing down on any one of them converts the beat into a tip and drains it.
- **The numbers are openly hypothetical and the script says so.** "Twelve deals" and "commission's what, seventy-five hundred" are a worked example, not a market claim, and the hesitation is what signals it. Do not upgrade them to averages, do not cite a median, and do not put a Chicago sale price on camera without a named source and date.
- **The closing question is left unanswered on camera.** "So what did eighteen thousand buy?" and "They'll say the brand" are the pre-empt, and D.J. does not rebut it. The rebuttal is the viewer's homework and the comment section is where it arrives.
- **Rung 2, and the close is the softest ask in the lane.** "Find out what this market charges" is the whole CTA. Every instinct in an edit will want to end harder. Ending soft after that arithmetic is the move, because the number already did the pushing.
- **No firm is named and none may be added.** "This market" is a category. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No.
- **Kale is not mentioned anywhere**, including the captions and the pinned comment. "I run a brokerage" is the permitted credential and it is load-bearing here: the hook only works because it is a man arguing against his own economics.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president do arithmetic unflattering to brokerages, concede the brokerage's side unprompted, name nobody, and ask for nothing. Respect goes up on every one of those.
- **Comment duty applies**, and this script produces the highest-value comments in the lane: agents posting their own split math, and agents itemizing what their split buys. Both are the audit the lane wants. Flag every Chicago one into Close.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, The Push-In, with the calculator visible on the pull. Youshaei's upgrade, adopted: the number has to appear because D.J. just computed it. A pre-made graphic that was already on screen kills the whole device, and that is true of the CapCut text layer too.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the credential-against-interest is the loop. A brokerage president telling agents to audit what brokerages take is a gap in the viewer's model of who says what, and gaps hold attention better than claims. Curse of Knowledge risk is zero: "split" needs no definition for this audience. Passes.
- **Share/save driver (Berger and Hormozi):** two drivers stacked, which is unusual. Practical value, because the arithmetic ports to any split and any volume. And Social Currency, because forwarding it makes the sender look like somebody who has run their own numbers. Expect sends and saves both. One-sentence retell: "your split is eighteen grand a year, what did it buy?"
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the arithmetic is a continuously resolving loop, every clause paying off a number and opening the next, so there is no drift point for the first twenty seconds. The deliberate deceleration is the concession, and the brand objection immediately after is what re-accelerates it.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Miller objects to the close, and this time he is objecting to the opposite of BP-006.** The payoff carries a question ("what did eighteen thousand buy?") and an action ("find out what this market charges"), and he argues the question is so much more interesting than the action that nobody will do the action. He wants the question cut and the close run on the market look alone. **Hormozi and Byron hold the question**, because it is the only thing in the script that produces a reply, and a reply is this lane's success test. **Decision: ship the question.** The lane is measured on comments, not on completed homework, and the comment is where rung 2 actually gets handed over by other agents. **The A/B:** a cut that ends on "Find out what this market charges. Then add yours up again," with the question deleted, judged on comment volume against this version. If the silent version draws the same "here is what my split buys" replies, Miller wins and the lane's default close loses a clause.

**The second dissent, and it is the one that will arrive in the replies:** a commenter will say the split buys errors-and-omissions coverage, compliance review, a managing broker on the hook for their license, transaction coordination and the office. **That is a real list and the script does not pre-empt it, deliberately.** An agent itemizing what they get is doing the lane's work: they are now auditing. The reply is not a rebuttal, it is "good, write that list down and see if it comes to eighteen thousand." That is rung 2 arriving in the comment section, and it is also entry E2, which is its own video.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a brokerage president does arithmetic unflattering to brokerages, concedes the brokerage's side without being asked, names no competitor as wrong, and leaves without asking for anything. Respect goes up. The two things that would flip it are naming a firm, which turns arithmetic into an attack and makes the concession read as a setup, and mentioning Kale, which converts the whole thing into an ad. Neither happens and neither may be added.
