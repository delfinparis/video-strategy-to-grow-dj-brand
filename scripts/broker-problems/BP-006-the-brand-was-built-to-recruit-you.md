# The brand was built to recruit you

> **WOW: The one line every recruiting pitch rests on, turned over. The pitch says the brand wins you business. The consumer research says buyers do not rate it and two of three sellers hire a referral. So the only room where "the brand wins business" is actually said is the recruiting room, which means the agent paying for the brand is the person it was built to sell to.**

> **Strongest Line:** "It recruited somebody. Not a client."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 83 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Your brokerage's brand was built to recruit you.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
You pay for it every closing. Supposedly it brings clients.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
The research is old, and it says buyers picking an agent don't rate the national brand as important. And two of three sellers hire a referral or someone they already used. Notice where "the brand wins business" gets said. In recruiting.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Pull your last five closings. Who hired you for the name? Find out what it costs anywhere else. It recruited somebody. Not a client.

*Word count: 83 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 8, tension 10, point 41, payoff 24. If the take runs long, cut "Who hired you for the name?" and let "pull your last five closings" carry it. Do not cut the last two sentences or the receipt hedge "the research is old."*

## Production Note

- **Pattern interrupt: The Stop.** Walk fast for one beat, plant and lean in on "recruit." The cheat sheet pairs motion-to-freeze with System Indictment. This is the week's Tuesday post; Thursday runs Walk-Toward and Friday runs One Prop, so nothing repeats.
- **Family 4, not Family 7.** The Forbidden version ("nobody at your brokerage will tell you who the brand is for") was drafted and cut. It spends four words on the tease before the claim. The flat declarative gets the accusation into the first eight words, which is what heat 4.5 needs.
- **The friction points at the model, and the agent is the wronged party.** The line is "the brand was built to recruit you," never "you fell for it." Qualifying test 4: the agent accepted the pitch because it was the pitch everyone got. Delivery is dry. If it reads as pity or contempt it fails Rule 9.4.
- **"The research is old" is the receipt's limit and it is spoken on purpose.** The Baylor finding is 2008 data. Saying so first is what lets the claim survive a commenter who looks it up. Do not upgrade it to "a new study" and do not drop the hedge for time.
- **Buyers and sellers are two different claims and the script keeps them apart.** Baylor: buyers rate national brand as not very important, and somewhat more important for sellers. NAR: sellers, 66% referral or repeat. Saying "clients don't care about the brand" would collapse two sources into one claim neither of them makes. The script says buyers for the research and sellers for the behavior. Keep it that way in the replies.
- **"Notice where the brand wins business gets said. In recruiting."** This is the turn, and it is an observation, not a statistic. It is the line most likely to draw "the brand is on every yard sign" in the comments. The reply is: right, the sign is for the seller, and the seller hired a referral. The claim is about where the *argument* lives, not where the logo appears.
- **Rung 2, two-step close.** "Pull your last five closings" is rung 1, the look at your own. "Find out what it costs anywhere else" is rung 2, the look at the market, and it is the whole point of the lane. The council flagged this as two asks; see the dissent. It ships as one motion: audit, then price.
- **No firm is named and none may be added.** "Your brokerage's brand" is a category. Ship test: can a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No. Keep it that way in the captions and the pinned comment.
- **Kale is not mentioned anywhere**, including the captions and the pinned comment, and it never is in this lane. The video's job is to be right in public.
- **Recruiting test:** a good agent watches a brokerage president say the brand pitch is aimed at agents, not consumers, with a sourced number and no destination named. Respect goes up. The two things that would flip it are naming a firm and mentioning Kale, and neither happens.
- **Comment duty applies.** The highest-value replies will be "my last five were all referrals" and "I've never checked what the brand line costs." Flag every Chicago one into Close.

**The limit, and it is spoken.** The Baylor data is 2008. ### LinkedIn

Your brokerage's brand was built to recruit you.

Every real estate brokerage recruiting pitch rests on the same line: the brand wins you business. So you pay for it, on every closing, as a share of a check your own name earned.

The consumer research on this is old, and it has never been reversed. Baylor's Keller Center found that buyers choosing an agent do not rate the national brand as important. They go on social influences first, then reputation.

Sellers behave the same way. NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers puts it at 66%: two of three sellers found their agent through a referral or had already worked with them.

So notice the one place "the brand wins business" gets said out loud. It is not the listing appointment. It is the recruiting meeting.

Pull your last five closings and write down why each one hired you. If the name on the building is not on that list, find out what that name costs at any other firm. The brand recruited somebody. It just was not a client.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the hook reassigns the purpose of a thing the viewer has paid for for years. That is a gap opened, not a fact stated, and the viewer stays to find out how the brand could possibly be *for* them. The Curse of Knowledge risk is "national brand," which agents understand instantly. Passes.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** anger, high arousal, pointed at the model. Social Currency is strong because forwarding it says "I already knew this." One-sentence retell: "the brand is a recruiting tool, not a client tool." The point survives compression.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the receipt lands by second nine, the turn ("in recruiting") lands at second twenty, and the close re-hooks with a task the viewer can do tonight. The one soft joint is "the research is old," which is a deceleration. It is also the reason the script survives a fact-check, so it stays.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Miller objects to the close.** "Two CTAs is zero CTAs," and the payoff carries two: pull your closings (rung 1) and find out what the name costs elsewhere (rung 2). The lane's own strategy says rung 2 is the engine, so the risk is that the viewer does the easy one and skips the one that matters. **Byron and Eric hold the two-step**, because "who hired you for the name" is the recognition beat that produces the comment, and without it the video has no moment where the viewer sees their own business. **Decision: ship the two-step, because the lane's success test is the comment, and the recognition beat is what produces it.** The A/B: a cut that drops "Pull your last five closings. Who hired you for the name?" and runs only "Find out what that name costs anywhere else," judged on comment volume, not views. If the single-ask version draws the same "my last five were referrals" replies, Miller wins and the lane's default close gets simpler.
