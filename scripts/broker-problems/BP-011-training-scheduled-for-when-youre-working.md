# Training scheduled for when you're working

> **WOW: Training is one of the four things the premium is supposed to buy, and it is the only one scheduled at an hour that rules out the people who paid for it. Nobody lied. The class exists, it is on the calendar, it is free, and a working agent could not attend it if they tried. Available and attendable are two different words, and only one of them was ever promised.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Whatever you find, that was a choice."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 75 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Your training is scheduled for when you're working.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
Tuesday, ten a.m. You're with a client, or you're on the phone.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So it goes in the pitch as training included. Available, sure. Attendable, no. Count how many of this year's sessions you could physically have attended. Then ask who that schedule was actually built for.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So find out when other firms here run theirs. Evenings, weekends, recorded and searchable. Whatever you find, that was a choice.

*Word count: 75 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 8, tension 12, point 34, payoff 21. If the take runs long, cut "or you're on the phone." Do not answer "who that schedule was built for" on camera, and do not cut "that was a choice."*

## Production Note

- **Pattern interrupt: The Stop.** Walk fast through the hook and plant on "working," lean into the lens for "Tuesday, ten a.m.," then start moving again on "So it goes in the pitch." Motion to freeze on the one word that makes the sentence a problem. Tuesday's script used Gesture-On-Beat and Thursday's used The Push-In, so the move rotates.
- **The question at the end of THE POINT is never answered on camera.** The standard is explicit: B-flavor scripts end beat 2 on the question and leave it. "Who that schedule was actually built for" has no good answer, which is exactly why it produces a reply from everybody who thinks they have one. An editor will want D.J. to land the answer. Refuse.
- **"Available, sure. Attendable, no."** This is bank entry B6's angle 2 and it is the whole argument in five words. Both words are plain English, no industry shorthand, nothing to decode (D.J. 2026-09-11). Read it flat, as a correction, not as a punchline.
- **Nobody lied and the script never says anybody did.** The class exists. It is on the calendar. It is included. The friction points at the schedule, which is a system, not at any person or firm (Rule 9.4). That distinction is what holds this at heat 4 instead of tipping it into an accusation.
- **No number, anywhere.** B6 is marked receipt-not-needed and it runs on recognition. "Count how many of this year's sessions you could physically have attended" is the viewer's own arithmetic on their own calendar, which is stronger than any statistic and needs no source. Do not let a rewrite bolt on a figure for how many agents attend brokerage training. No clean source exists.
- **"Tuesday, ten a.m." is a shape, not a firm.** Mid-morning midweek is the standard slot across the industry. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence from it? No. Do not add a second identifying detail, because two specifics start to describe somebody.
- **Rung 2, and it is the lift the bank allows.** B6 sits at rung 1 in the bank, liftable to 2 with "find out when anybody else in this market runs theirs." The lift is taken here because the week needs its rung-2 share and because the comparison is the whole point: the hour was a choice, and choices can be compared.
- **"Evenings, weekends, recorded and searchable" is a list of what to look for, not a claim about what exists.** It tells the viewer what a different answer would look like so they can recognize one. It does not say any firm does it, and it must not be rewritten into a claim that some do.
- **Kale is not mentioned** anywhere, ever, in this lane. Kale's own training schedule is precisely the thing this script may never raise. Be right in public.
- **Recruiting test:** a brokerage president says training scheduled for working hours is a scheduling choice and tells agents to go compare schedules across the market, his competitors included. Respect goes up, because he runs a firm whose own calendar is now fair game. Naming a firm would flip it. It does not happen.
- **Comment duty applies.** The replies to flag: agents posting their own count of attendable sessions, and anyone answering the "built for" question. The second group is the highest-value signal this lane produces. Flag every Chicago agent into Close.

## Council Review

**Convened:** Byron Lazine, Eric Simon, Hormozi, Miller, Welsh. **Witnesses:** Heath, Berger.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "Your training is scheduled for when you're working."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: anger] The default. Eight words, states the contradiction flat, and the friction points at a calendar rather than at a person.
2. "Nobody at your brokerage will say this about the ten a.m. class."  [hook_family: 7 Forbidden | emotion: anger] Byron's pick. Rejected as the default because BP-009 ran Family 7 on Tuesday and two Forbidden opens in one week flattens the feed.
3. "You have never once been free at ten on a Tuesday."  [hook_family: 1 Mirror | emotion: recognition] Eric Simon's pick, and the most relatable line in the set. Heath's objection: it opens on the viewer's schedule rather than on what it costs them, so the first three seconds are a description instead of a problem, and this flavor has to name the wrong default in the hook to clear heat 4.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, The Stop. Plant on "working." The word is where the sentence becomes a problem and the freeze should land on it, not on "training."

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the hook is a contradiction the viewer has lived inside for years without naming, so the gap it opens is not "is this true" but "why did I never notice." That is a cheap gap to open and an expensive one to close, which is the shape that holds a 30-second video. The Curse of Knowledge risk is zero. There is no jargon and no arithmetic.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** anger aimed at a schedule, which is about as safe a target as this lane has, and the viewer is unambiguously the wronged party, so forwarding it costs nothing socially. Retell test: "the training is included and it is scheduled for when you're working." Survives compression whole, which is the best result of the three scripts this week. Hormozi's condition is met, since the viewer leaves with a count to run and a comparison to make.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the flat contradiction at 0:00 and the correction at 0:09 ("available, sure, attendable, no") are two small payoffs before the ask, and the unanswered question at 0:20 is a fresh loop opening where a one-idea video normally closes. No dull stretch, because the longest sentence in the script is fourteen words.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Welsh holds that the payoff asks for more than it looks like.** Comparing training calendars across firms is not a five-minute job the way checking a cap or a fee is, and a rung-2 ask that quietly costs an hour gets abandoned, which trains the viewer to ignore the next one. He wants the close held at rung 1, the count alone. **Miller and Hormozi hold rung 2**, on the grounds that the count by itself resolves to a feeling and the whole lane exists to send people looking, and that one phone call to one friend at another firm answers it in four minutes. **Decision: ship at rung 2, because the week needs its rung-2 share and because the comparison is what makes the last line true. A schedule is only visibly a choice once you have seen a different one.** The A/B: variant 1 as written against a version closing on "Count it. Then ask anybody you know at another firm when theirs runs," judged on comment mix and on whether replies contain actual other-firm schedules. If the softer, one-person version produces more real comparisons, Welsh is right and the lane should stop asking for market-wide looks when a single friend will do.

**Byron's note, not a test:** this is a brand-tax script with no money in it, and that is rare and useful. Three of the last four in the register ran arithmetic. Keep at least one non-arithmetic brand-tax script in every three, or the lane becomes a calculator and the non-numerical items in the bundle never get their turn.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a brokerage president points at the hour on a training calendar, says out loud that it was a choice, and then sends agents to compare that choice against every firm in the market including his own. More respect, and the exposure is real, which is what makes it credible. The move that would flip it is any claim about when his own training runs. It appears nowhere.
