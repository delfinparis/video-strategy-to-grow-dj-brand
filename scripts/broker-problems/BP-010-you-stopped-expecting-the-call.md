# You stopped expecting the call

> **WOW: The silence at a brokerage is never a decision anybody made. Nothing is ever wrong enough to trigger a phone call, so the phone call never happens, and after a few years the agent quietly stops expecting one. Naming that adaptation is what turns a private feeling into something the viewer can count, and the count is the whole video.**

> **Strongest Line:** "The call just never had a reason."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 79 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
You stopped expecting your broker to call.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
And you decided that's normal, because nothing's ever wrong enough to trigger one.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
A study last year interviewed ten agents who'd just left. Ten people, not proof. One of them said they'd talked to their broker ten times in thirteen years. Nobody was cruel to them. The call just never had a reason.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So count yours. Then count the other direction. When did anybody there last call you when nothing was wrong?

*Word count: 79 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 7, tension 13, point 40, payoff 19. If the take runs long, cut "And you decided that's normal" to "And that's normal now." Do not cut "Ten people, not proof" and do not cut the last question.*

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the hook does not tell the viewer something is wrong, it tells them something about themselves they have never put into words, which is the Aha arriving before the Huh. The gap it opens is "when did I stop, and why did I not notice." Curse of Knowledge risk is low, because there is no jargon and no arithmetic to follow.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** this is the lane's emotional register rather than its tactical one, so the share is private, agent to agent, with "this is us" attached. Private sends are the signal that moves non-follower reach, and this script is built for that rather than for saves. Retell test: "one agent talked to their broker ten times in thirteen years, and nobody was ever the bad guy." Survives, and the second half is the part that survives best.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the receipt at 0:07 is the payoff to the hook's promise, arriving early, and then a fresh question opens at 0:21 where a one-idea video normally ends. The viewer stays through the close because a new count just got handed to them.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Hormozi holds that rung 1 wastes the script.** His position: the viewer finishes holding a feeling and a count, and a count is not a checkable item they can act on. He wants the close lifted to rung 2, "find out what other firms do about this," the way BP-007 closed. **Chris Do and Eric Simon hold rung 1**, on the grounds that this is the one script in the lane where the payload is the recognition, and a market-comparison ask at the end converts a private moment into an errand. The bank agrees with them and says so in the entry. **Decision: ship at rung 1, because the entry is held there on purpose and the reverse question is already doing the exploring work without naming it as work.** The A/B: variant 1 as written against the same script with "So find out how often that happens at other firms here" replacing "Then count the other direction," judged on comment mix.
