# Nobody asked why

> **WOW: Every agent has had a quarter that fell apart and fixed it alone. The thing the bank entry gets right, and the thing this script is built on, is that the recovery was invisible too. The number went down and nobody said anything. The number came back and nobody said anything about that either. That is not a support failure at a moment of need. It is a company that has never once had a reason to look.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Noticing was never anybody's job there."

Every other support script in this lane ends at the moment of need. - **Heat 4, and it is earned in the hook, not in the body.** "Nobody asked why" is the wrong default stated in three words before the scene starts. C3 used to be the flavor that ran at 3 on recognition alone; since 2026-08-20 nothing ships under 4. The scene still does the work. The hook just names what the viewer has quietly accepted as normal.
- **"Noticing was never anybody's job there" is the outward clause and it is doing two jobs.** It points the friction at the arrangement rather than at a person, which is Rule 9.4, and it keeps the script off the weak seed. **The alternative phrasing to avoid at all costs is "nobody there cared."** That is a claim about people, it is arguable, and an agent whose managing broker is a decent human being will defend them out loud and the video is finished. - **This entry gets close to sad and the bank says so. ### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

What a check-in has to be to count, since the video only had room to say it did not happen.

A message asking if you are hitting your goals is not a check-in. Neither is a group email about momentum, and neither is being tagged in a thread about the pipeline. Those go to everyone, which is the same as going to nobody.

Three things make it real.

One. Somebody noticed without being told. The whole value of a check-in is that you did not have to raise your hand first. If you had to ask for help to get help, the system did nothing.

Two. It came from somebody who could actually change something. Sympathy from a person with no authority over your splits, your leads, your coverage, or your coaching is a nice conversation. It is not support.

Three. It happened again. One conversation during a bad stretch is a moment. Two is a process. Only the second one tells you anything about the firm.

And the one I left out of the video on purpose, because it is the part that stings. Somebody noticing when it came back. Anyone can call during a bad quarter. Almost nobody calls to say they saw you fix it.

The sharpest reply to this is going to be that it is not a broker's job to babysit your production. That is a fair position and worth saying. It is also a different argument than the one in the video, which is that nobody there is assigned to look either way.
