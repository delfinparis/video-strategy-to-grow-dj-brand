# The coaching is a replay

> **WOW: The brand premium was sold partly as coaching. What arrived was a Tuesday 10am class held during the hours an agent is with clients, and a recording nobody has updated since it was made. The agent then learns the thing they actually use off a free video somebody else made for nothing. The premium bought the word, not the coaching.**

> **Strongest Line:** "The thing you actually use, you learned off a free video."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 79 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Nobody tells you the coaching is a recording.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
You paid for it in the pitch. You pay for it every closing.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
The live class is Tuesday at ten. You're with clients at ten. So it's a replay from two years ago that nobody has updated. And the thing you actually use, you learned off a free video.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So what is that coaching line buying? Find out what real coaching costs, what other firms include. Nobody's going to tell you.

## Production Note

- **Pattern interrupt: One Prop.** Hold the phone up on the first word, a paused video on screen, then drop it and walk. The prop is the claim: a recording in his hand while he says the word "recording." Do not hold it past the hook. BP-011 ran The Stop and BP-010 ran the Push-In, so the two-post no-repeat rule is clean on the move and on the family.
- **Family 7, The Forbidden, and the friction points at the silence.** "Nobody tells you" is aimed at what does not get said in the recruiting meeting, never at the agent who believed it and never at a firm. Qualifying test 4: the agent bought the pitch everyone gets. Delivery is dry. Any edge of "you fell for it" and the script fails Rule 9.4.
- **No coaching price on camera, and this is the entry's hard constraint.** The bank marks F3 Receipt NEEDED for any claim about what a private coach costs. There is no sourced range, so no range is spoken. The close sends the viewer to find the number themselves, which is the rung-2 line the bank asks for.
- **Everything spoken is a scene, not a statistic.** Tuesday at ten, a two-year-old replay, a free video. None of it is presented as research and none of it carries a number, so there is nothing here to source and nothing to hedge. If an edit tries to add "most brokerages" or a percentage, refuse it.
- **The closing question stays unanswered on camera.** "So what is that coaching line buying?" is the comment driver. Answering it turns the video into a tip and hands the viewer a conclusion instead of a gap.
- **Rung 2, single motion.** "Find out what real coaching costs, what other firms include" is one look at the market, not two asks. Miller's two-CTA objection from BP-006 is the reason this one is written as a single clause with a comma.
- **No firm is named and none may be added.** "The coaching line" and "other firms" are categories. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No. Keep it that way in the captions and the pinned comment.
- **Kale is not mentioned anywhere**, including the captions and the pinned comment.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president say the coaching in a recruiting pitch is usually a recording, name nobody, quote no figure, and ask for nothing. Respect goes up. Naming a firm or mentioning Kale would flip it, and neither happens.
- **Comment duty applies.** The high-value replies are "ours is a Zoom from 2023" and "I've never checked what a real coach charges." Flag every Chicago one into Close.

So the honest question is what that coaching line is buying.

Find out what real coaching costs from someone who does it for a living, and find out what other firms in this market actually include. Nobody is going to volunteer either number.
