# Same deal you signed

> **WOW: The sourced number in this script is not a fee. It is a length of time. Six years is the median tenure an agent has with their current firm, and the agreement signed at the start of those six years is almost never opened again. The entry is an absence, so the arithmetic runs on the viewer's own split rather than on a dollar figure nobody has published.**

> **Strongest Line:** "A deal that renews itself with no conversation is the best one they own."

## Production Note

- **One number on camera and it is a length of time, not money.** Six years is the median tenure a member has with their current firm, from NAR's 2026 Member Profile. It is said with its source and its month attached. **No dollar figure appears anywhere in this script and none may be added.** There is no published figure for what an agent pays a brokerage over six years, and the bank's A3 precedent is explicit: when no source exists for a market range, the arithmetic runs on the number the viewer supplies.
- **"Multiply your split and your fees by the years you've been there" is the Slow Leak's annualization, performed on the viewer's figure.** The flavor never ships without it. What it ships without here is the substitution beat, the four foreclosed purchases the register usually lists, **and that is deliberate.** The substitution beat needs a dollar total to foreclose, this script has no dollar total, and inventing one to get the beat would break Rule 1 to decorate a flavor convention. The absence is the entry. Do not add a number to fix it.
- **The hook's "six years" is the viewer's own tenure and the tension's "six years" is the median.** They are the same number on purpose and that is the whole hook mechanism. Say the hook flat and second person, then hand over the source in the next breath. **If the delivery blurs the two, the script reads as a stat claim about the viewer, which it is not.** Pause after "You never asked."
- **Heat 4, and it lives in one clause: "A deal that renews itself with no conversation is the best one they own."** That is the arrangement named as working exactly as designed, which is the Rule 9.2 definition of 4. Everything before it is the agent's own arithmetic. Ship test: no firm, no franchise, no product, and "other firms here" stays a category. NAR is named as the source of a survey and is not the target of anything.
- **The friction points outward, and the line that keeps it there is "it was never going to come up on its own," carried in the renewal clause.** Without that clause this is a video telling an agent they were passive for six years, which is friction at the agent, a Rule 9.4 failure, and the exact delivery that makes a good agent close the app. **The absence is the arrangement's, not the viewer's.** Deliver the renewal line with no edge in the voice. It is an observation about incentives, not an accusation.
- **Family 6, Named Stakes.** BP-023 ran Family 7 and BP-022 ran Family 1, so 6 is clean on the two-post rule and the brief offered 6 or 4. Six wins over System Indictment because the number is the only hard thing in the script and burying it in the second beat wastes the one piece of sourced material the slot has.

BP-023 ran The Stop, so the move rotates. **The paper is blank and stays blank.** No real agreement, no letterhead, nothing legible in frame. A document with any firm's name on it is the one way this script fails the ship test, and it would fail it visually, where captions.ai cannot scrub it.
- **Rung 2, and the close is a comparison the viewer can run for free.** "What six years gets you at other firms here" is a thing an agent can learn at a closing on Monday without telling anyone they are looking. It names no destination, recommends nothing, and does not say leave. The second sentence is the loop-back, not a second ask.

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

The source, and then the three questions.

The number is NAR's 2026 Member Profile, released June 25: the median tenure with a current firm is six years. A median, across a national membership survey. It is not broken out by market and I am not going to pretend it is a Chicago figure.

I deliberately did not put a dollar amount on this. I looked. Every published range for what a brokerage charges an agent per month traces back to a brokerage recruiting page or a comparison site that makes money on the click. There is no primary survey worth quoting, so the arithmetic in the video runs on your numbers and not on mine.

Three questions a renegotiation actually turns on, if you ever have that conversation.

One. What changed on your side since you signed. Volume, average price, referral share, how much of your business arrives without the firm touching it. Write the numbers down before you say a word.

Two. What the firm's cost to serve you looks like now versus then. An agent closing twenty deals uses less of a brokerage's time per deal than an agent closing four, and the price almost never reflects it.

Three. What the comparison is. Not where you would go. What the terms are. Those are different questions and only one of them is anyone else's business.

There is a fourth question that matters more than all three and it depends entirely on your own situation, so somebody else can say it.
