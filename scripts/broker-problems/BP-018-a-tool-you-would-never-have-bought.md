# A tool you would never have bought

> **WOW: This is the Slow Leak's opposite twin and the distinction is the whole script. BP-017 was the software you never open. This is the software you open every week, that is worse than the thing you would have chosen, and that you cannot switch off because it arrived inside the brand's price. The waste is not the fee. The waste is the choice that got made for you and then billed to you.**

> **Strongest Line:** "That gap is what the brand cost you."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 78 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
You're using a tool you'd never have bought.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
You paid for it inside the brand. You use it because it's there.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So price the one you'd actually pick. Retail, tonight, ten minutes. Then ask what the bundle charges you for the one you got. Nobody can break that number out. That gap is what the brand cost you.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then find out what firms here hand their agents. And who gets to pick it. You'd never have bought this.

## Production Note

- **Say the distinction or this is BP-017 again, four days later.** A3 and F4 are adjacent entries and they shipped in the same week on purpose, but they are different problems and the second line is what separates them. BP-017 was a charge for software the agent does not open. This one is a tool the agent uses constantly and would not have chosen. "You use it because it's there" is the whole hinge. Deliver it flat, as a fact about the week, not as an accusation.
- **No figure is spoken, and the bank is explicit about why.** F4 carries the same constraint as A3: no primary survey exists for mandatory brokerage technology fees, and every findable range traces back to recruiting pages or lead-generating comparison content. So the script speaks no dollar amount in either direction. The only number in it is "ten minutes," which is how long the retail lookup takes.
- **Two prices, and the viewer supplies both.** The retail price of the tool they would pick, which they can look up tonight, and the line-item price of the one they got, which does not exist as a line item. That asymmetry is the argument. The script does not pretend to resolve it, and the resolution is not available to anyone on camera.
- **"Nobody can break that number out" is a description of a bundle, not a claim about any firm.** A bundled price has no line items by construction. Delivered as an indictment of somebody's bookkeeping it becomes an unsourced claim and fails the ship test on tone. Delivered as the plain mechanical fact it is, it is the most direct brand-tax line in the script.
- **Family 4, not the Mirror.** BP-017 ran Family 1 two days ago and the no-repeat rule forecloses it anyway, but the better reason is that the subject here is not a private behavior. It is a decision that was taken away from the agent. That is System Indictment, and the indicted system is the bundle. The brief offered 4, 7 or 6; 6 is unavailable because it is the stat-anchored family and this entry cannot speak a stat.
- **Pattern interrupt: The Stop.** Walk fast for one beat, plant, lean into the lens on "never," and let the walk resume on "So price." BP-017 ran One Prop and BP-016 ran the Location Cold-Open, so the move is clean on the two-post rule. No prop here on purpose: any screen held into frame on a script about a specific mandatory tool is a ship-test failure waiting on a logo.
- **Heat 4.5, and where it lives.** In the hook and in "That gap is what the brand cost you." Both are verdicts on the arrangement. Neither names a firm, a franchise, or a product, and no viewer can name one company with confidence from any line in this script. The flavor's register is the premium was supposed to buy something, and here is what arrived.
- **Rung 2, and the close is a two-part look.** What firms in this market hand their agents, and who chooses it. The second half is the one that matters, because the entry is about a removed choice rather than a price. Neither half names a destination and neither asks for a conversation.
- **Kale is not mentioned.** "Firms here" is the permitted universe. There is no "at my brokerage the agent picks" version of the close, even though that is exactly the line a tools script invites.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president decline to name a tool, decline to quote a fee, tell them to go price the thing they would have bought instead, and then point them at what other firms do. Respect goes up. The delivery that would flip it is any hint that using the bundled tool was a failure of nerve. They did not get a vote. That is the point of line two.

Two notes, because this one gets argued with and both arguments are fair.

First, no range in here on purpose. I went looking for a real number on what brokerage technology bundles cost and could not find one worth saying out loud. Everything available traces back to a recruiting page or a comparison site built to collect leads. Three things worth checking while you are in there. Whether the tool is actually included or separately billed, since those are different conversations. Whether the agent can expense the one they would rather use. And whether anybody at your firm can tell you what that one line is worth inside the bundle. How long the third answer takes is itself the answer.
