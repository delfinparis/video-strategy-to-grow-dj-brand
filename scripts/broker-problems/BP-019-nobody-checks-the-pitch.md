# Nobody checks the pitch

> **WOW: Every agent was recruited off a promise and not one of them has ever put that promise next to their own calendar. The pitch is the only contract in this business that nobody audits, because the moment you signed it stopped being a document and became a memory. The script does not argue the promise was broken. It asks who was supposed to deliver it, which is a question with no good answer.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Who was supposed to hand it over?"

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 78 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Nobody checks the pitch against what arrived.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
They promised mentorship, leads, training. Week three, your calendar was empty.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So go pull up the page they recruited you from. Read it out loud against your last ninety days. Line by line. Then answer the question nobody asks. Which of those arrived? And who was supposed to hand it over?

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then go read another firm's recruiting page. Even one you'd never join. See what they promise you. Nobody ever checks.

*Word count: 78 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 7, tension 11, point 40, payoff 20. If the take runs long, cut "Line by line." Do not cut "And who was supposed to hand it over," which is the question the whole script exists to leave open, and do not cut the last line, which is the loop-back.*

## Production Note

- **The question does not get answered on camera, and that is a flavor rule, not a preference.** B-flavor scripts end the middle on the question and leave it there. "Who was supposed to hand it over?" has no good answer, which is why it produces replies from everybody who thinks they have one. If the delivery supplies even an implied answer, the comment section goes quiet and the script has failed at its actual job.
- **No figure is spoken.** The bank marks B3 receipt NEEDED for any number and says plainly that it runs fine as a scene. The only numbers in the script are "week three" and "ninety days," both timeframes rather than measurements, and neither is a claim about how often this happens.
- **"Week three, your calendar was empty" is a scene, not a statistic.** It is not a claim that most onboarding fails, and it must not be delivered as one. Delivered as recognition it is the second-strongest line in the script. Delivered as an indictment of an industry practice with no source, it is an invented receipt in the shape of a sentence.
- **Family 7, The Forbidden, and the forbidden thing is the audit rather than a secret.** Nobody tells an agent to re-read the recruiting page because nobody benefits from them doing it. That is the heat-4 claim and it names no party. The brief offered 4 or 7; Family 4 ran Tuesday on BP-018 and the no-repeat rule forecloses it, but 7 is also simply the better fit, because the subject is a thing left unsaid rather than a system doing something.
- **Pattern interrupt: Gesture-On-Beat.** Palm to camera on "Nobody," then one finger up per promise on "mentorship, leads, training," then the hand drops flat on "empty." The three fingers are the only visual the script needs and they do real work: the list becomes countable and then the count becomes zero. BP-018 ran The Stop and BP-017 ran One Prop, so the move is clean on the two-post rule.
- **The three promises are the ones the bank names and they stay in that order.** Mentorship, leads, training. Fast, none elaborated, no adjectives. The temptation is to expand "leads" because it is the one that stings, and expanding it converts the beat into a tip about lead generation and drains the whole script.
- **Rung 2, and it is the strongest rung-2 close in the bank.** "Go read another firm's recruiting page, even one you'd never join" is literally exploring other options, dressed as an audit, and it costs the agent nothing socially. The "one you'd never join" clause is what makes it safe to do: it gives the agent permission to look without that look meaning anything yet. Do not cut it.
- **Heat 4, and where it lives.** In the hook and in the unanswered question. The indicted practice is recruiting on promises nobody later checks, at category level. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No. Every brokerage in this market has a recruiting page and all of them promise roughly these three things.
- **Kale is not mentioned, and this is the script in the lane where it would be most tempting.** A video about recruiting pitches has an obvious "here is what we actually do" ending and that ending would retroactively turn the audit into a sales funnel. "Another firm's recruiting page" is the permitted universe and it deliberately includes D.J.'s own, unnamed, alongside everyone else's.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president tell them to go read competitors' recruiting pages, including ones they would never join, without naming his own. Respect goes up, and the reason is that he is pointing at the whole category rather than steering. The delivery that would flip it is any suggestion the agent was naive to believe the pitch. They believed it because it was the only information available and the person giving it had no incentive to flag the gap.
- **First-hour ops:** no link to pin. **Comment duty applies** and the reply to watch for is an agent naming which of the three promises did not arrive. That is the single most useful recruiting signal this lane produces, because it tells Ana which of the eight kale-signaling arguments to open with. Flag every Chicago one into Close.

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

I am not going to answer the second question, so here is why.

"Who was supposed to hand it over" has no good answer at most firms, and the reason is structural rather than dishonest. The pitch gets made by somebody whose job ends when you sign. Onboarding is run by somebody whose job starts when you sign. Nothing in the arrangement puts those two people back in a room with your file between them, so the promise does not get broken so much as dropped.

Which means the fix is not outrage. It is a name. If one person is accountable for what you were promised, you can go find them. If nobody is, you have learned the thing worth knowing.

Three things to look for while you have that recruiting page open. Anything specific enough to check, as opposed to a word like support. Anything with a number or a frequency attached, because that is the part somebody once decided to commit to. And whether the page has changed since you signed, which the Wayback Machine will tell you in about a minute.

Then read somebody else's page the same way. Pick a firm you would never join, so you are reading rather than shopping.

## Council Review

**Convened:** Byron Lazine, Eric Simon, Miller, Hormozi, Chris Do, Kane. **Witnesses:** Heath, Berger.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "Nobody checks the pitch against what arrived."  [hook_family: 7 The Forbidden | emotion: unease, then curiosity] The default. Seven words, and the wrong default is the whole sentence. It accuses nobody of anything, which is what lets a viewer stay in it past second three.
2. "Week three, your calendar was empty. Remember that?"  [hook_family: 1 The Mirror | emotion: recognition into anger] Eric Simon's pick, and the most relatable version. It opens on the scene rather than on the absent audit, which is warmer and travels further outside Chicago. Rejected as the default because it spends the recognition beat in second one and leaves the middle with an instruction and nothing to feel.
3. "Go read the page they recruited you off."  [hook_family: 9 Swap and List | emotion: curiosity] Hormozi's pick, and the most saveable, because it is a task in eight words. Its cost is that it opens on homework, and a task with no stated reason reads as a chore rather than as a reveal.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, Gesture-On-Beat, and the board upgraded it. Palm to camera on "Nobody," one finger per promise, hand flat on "empty." Byron's addition, adopted: the fingers must go up fast and come down all at once, because the drop is the only visual argument in the script. Kane wanted the Location Cold-Open outside an office building instead and withdrew it himself on the ship test: a recognizable building in frame on a script about recruiting pitches is a named target in footage.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** this is the cleanest knowledge gap in the lane, because it is a gap about a document the viewer still has access to. "Nobody checks" opens the loop and "go read the page" closes it inside the same thirty seconds, which is the Huh-then-Aha sequence working as designed. Heath's Curse of Knowledge ruling goes the other way for once: there is no trade vocabulary anywhere in this script. Every word in it would be understood by somebody with no license, which is the reading-level floor met without effort because the subject is a job interview.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** high-arousal anger with an unusual safety property. The agent forwarding this is not admitting their firm failed them, because the script never says it did; they are forwarding a question. Social Currency is high for that reason and the retell survives compression into one line: "go read the recruiting page they hired you off against your last ninety days." Berger's ruling is that the unanswered question is the share driver rather than a weakness, because an unanswered question is what people forward to somebody who might answer it.
- **Retention move (MrBeast, in absentia, applied by Youshaei's rule and Hormozi's): the question stack at second seventeen is the retention device.** Two short questions back to back, after a long instruction, resets the rhythm exactly where a thirty-second script drifts. Hormozi's condition, adopted: "Line by line" has to be its own beat with a pause, or the instruction runs into the questions and the stack loses its shape.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Chris Do says the script has no human beat and should.** His argument is specific and not sentimental: D.J. runs a brokerage, which means he has personally made recruiting promises, and one line conceding that would make this the most credible script in the lane. "I have made that pitch" is the single most disarming sentence available here and the script does not use it. **Miller and Byron oppose on different grounds and both grounds hold.** Miller: a confession in a thirty-second script with four beats displaces the instruction, and the instruction is the entire payload. Byron: the confession is a Permission Slip move, that flavor ran twelve days ago on BP-014, and the engine holds it to about monthly for a reason. **Decision: no confession in the spoken script, and it goes in the pinned comment instead,** where it already is in substance ("the promise gets dropped, not broken"). Chris Do's objection is recorded as a real loss rather than a resolved one, because the pinned version reaches a fraction of the audience the spoken line would. **The A/B:** variant 1 against variant 2, the absent audit against the empty calendar, judged on three-second retention and on comment mix. If variant 2 wins, the B flavor should open on the scene and keep the audit for the middle, which would reverse how the last four of these were built.

**Eric Simon's note, not a test:** "leads" is the promise that will carry the comments, because it is the one with a number attached in most pitches and therefore the only one anybody can prove did not arrive. He would not expand it. Three fast words, and the one that stings is supposed to sting on the way past.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president hand them a twenty-minute audit of their own recruiting pitch, tell them to go read competitors' recruiting pages including ones they would never join, refuse to name his own, and decline to answer the question he just raised. Respect goes up, and the refusal is what buys it. The delivery that would flip it is any hint that believing the pitch was naive. They believed it because it was the only information in the room and nobody there had a reason to flag the gap.
