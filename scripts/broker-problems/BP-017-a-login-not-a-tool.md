# A login, not a tool

> **WOW: The only Slow Leak in the bank that cannot speak its own number, because every published figure for brokerage technology fees traces back to recruiting content. So the script hands the arithmetic to the viewer and keeps the one thing a source could never supply anyway, which is how many times they have opened it.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You're paying for a login, not a tool."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 81 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
You pay monthly for software you don't open.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
It comes out every month. You didn't pick it, and you can't drop it.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So pull up your own number and times it by twelve. That's the yearly price of a login you don't use. Then ask what you'd have bought instead. A coach. Real leads. A photographer on every listing.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then find out what firms here charge for it, if they charge at all. You're paying for a login, not a tool.

If the take runs long, cut "A photographer on every listing" and let the first two items carry the list. Do not cut "times it by twelve," and do not cut the last line, which is the loop-back.*

## Production Note

- **No figure is spoken, and the bank is unusually loud about why.** Every dollar range available for monthly brokerage technology fees traces to recruiting blogs and comparison-site content, not to a primary survey. The bank's instruction is exact: do not put a dollar range on camera until a real source exists, and run the annualization on a number the viewer supplies. This script obeys it literally. The only number spoken is twelve, which is the number of months in a year.
- **The Slow Leak never ships without annualization, and here the annualization is an instruction rather than a result.** "Pull up your own number and times it by twelve" is the whole arithmetic move, and it survives the missing receipt because the monthly figure is the one number the viewer can find in thirty seconds. The monthly number is exactly why nobody has ever reacted to this fee. Twelve times it is a different object.
- **Family 1, not the brief's suggestion, and the reason is the receipt.** The planner offered Family 6 or Family 4 for a Slow Leak. Family 6 is unavailable on principle: it is the stat-anchored family and every number in it has to be sourced, which is the one thing this entry cannot do. Family 4 is running on Tuesday in BP-015. So this is The Mirror with the wrong default named in the hook, which is the same construction BP-010 used to clear heat 4 on 2026-09-17. The private behavior is not opening it. The cost is in the first three words.
- **Pattern interrupt: One Prop.** Hold the phone up on line one with a login screen on it, any login screen, and lower it on "times twelve." The prop is the entire subject of the video and it is already in his hand on a walk-and-talk, so this is the cheapest interrupt in the lane. BP-015 runs The Whip and BP-016 runs the Location Cold-Open, so the move is clean on the two-post rule. **The screen must not be identifiable.** A generic email or password field, or the phone angled so the screen is bright and unreadable. A recognizable product logo in frame fails the ship test on footage rather than on words, and this entry is the one where that is most likely to happen by accident.
- **"If they charge at all" is a question and must be delivered as one.** It is not a claim that other firms do not charge for software. No source exists in either direction, which is the reason the whole close is research rather than comparison. Delivered as a question it is honest and it is the strongest line in the payoff. Delivered as an insinuation it is an unsourced claim about competitors' fee schedules, and it would be the first invented receipt this lane has shipped.
- **The foreclosed-purchase list is three items, delivered fast, none elaborated.** A coach, real leads, a photographer on every listing. Speed is what turns a line item into a life the agent did not get to have. Slowing down to explain any one of them converts the beat into a tip and drains it. "Real leads" is the one that will land, because it is the item the fee was supposed to produce.
- **Heat 4, and where it lives.** Not in the target. The mandatory-fee arrangement is indicted as a category, and the sharpest line, "a login, not a tool," is a verdict on the thing rather than on any company. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No, as long as the prop stays unreadable.
- **Rung 2, and it is the rung the missing receipt created.** Because no market range exists, the close cannot be a comparison, so it becomes the research. That is the bank's own observation about this entry and it is the reason A3 is a better rung-2 script than most of the bank.
- **Kale is not mentioned.** "Firms here" is the permitted universe, and there is no "at my brokerage we" version of this line, even though the temptation on a technology-fee script is higher than anywhere else in the lane.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president decline to tell them what software costs, tell them to look at their own statement instead, and then point them at other firms' fee schedules. Respect goes up. The delivery that would flip it is any suggestion that the agent was foolish to pay it. They could not opt out. That is the point of the second line.
- **First-hour ops:** no link to pin. The pinned comment carries the thing the script cannot say, which is why there is no number in it. **Comment duty applies** and the reply to watch for is an agent posting their own annual figure. Flag every Chicago one into Close.
