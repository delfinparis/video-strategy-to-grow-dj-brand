# Ten times in thirteen years

> **WOW: Criterion 8 (a line that reframes a private feeling as a shared pattern). The agent who feels unseen at their brokerage assumes it's a personal failure, or that they're being needy. Hearing someone else's number turns it into arithmetic, and arithmetic is arguable in a way a feeling isn't.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Nothing was ever wrong enough to trigger a phone call, so the phone call never happened."

## Spoken Script (~55 seconds)

**THE THING**

Count how many real conversations you've had with your managing broker this year. Not a hallway hello. An actual conversation.

**THE SIZE**

There's a study from last summer where a researcher sat down with ten agents who'd just left their firms. Ten people. That's it, so nobody should be calling it proof of anything. But one of them said a thing I haven't been able to put down. They said they might have communicated with their broker ten times in thirteen years. Ten. In thirteen years. And here's what gets me. That's not a villain. Nobody was cruel to that person. Nothing was ever wrong enough to trigger a phone call, so the phone call never happened. Thirteen years of that.

**THE MIRROR**

So count yours. Then count the other direction, which is the one that actually tells you something. When did anybody at your brokerage last call you when nothing was wrong?

**LOOP-BACK**

They didn't leave over money. They left because ten was the number, and nobody there ever counted it.

## Production Note

**Pattern interrupt (move 2, Stop and turn).** Walk through the hook and the setup. Stop walking on the word "Ten," turn to the lens, and deliver "In thirteen years" standing still. Start walking again on "So count yours." The stop is the whole production value and it costs nothing.

**Re-hook placement.** "Ten. In thirteen years." at roughly 0:20. It is a repetition, not new information, and that is the point -- the viewer's own arithmetic starts running underneath it. Leave a full beat after "thirteen years" before "And here's what gets me."

**Heat 3, and keep it there.** This one does not need heat. Recognition does the work, and pushing it toward indignation turns a video about being unseen into a video about a bad broker, which is a different and much worse video. Nobody is the villain in this script. That is what makes it land.

**Register warning.** The delivery risk here is pity. If "thirteen years of that" reads as sad on D.J.'s face, the video becomes something the viewer scrolls away from to avoid feeling it. Play it as puzzled, not sorry. The emotion is "how did that happen," not "poor guy."

**Beat 3 discipline.** "Count yours" and the reverse question. Not "you deserve better," not "there are firms where that doesn't happen." The second question is the one doing recruiting work, and it does it silently -- an agent who cannot remember a single no-reason call has just learned something about their situation without being told.

**No Kale.** Not in the script, not in the captions. This is the entry in the whole bank most likely to tempt a closing line about how it works at Kale. It would cost the video everything (see the standard's Kale rule). The answer lives in the DM.

**First-hour ops.** Pin the study link as the first comment, with the sample size in the pinned text so the hedge survives contact with people who did not watch to the end. **Comment duty applies.**

## Data Source

**The quote (spoken).**
Mike DelPrete, *"Research Study: What Agents Really Want in a Brokerage,"* published **2025-08-21**. https://www.mikedp.com/articles/2025/8/21/research-study-what-agents-really-want-in-a-brokerage

> "I might have only communicated with my broker 10 times in 13 years."

- **Methodology, stated in the study:** ten in-depth interviews, roughly forty minutes each, conducted **June 2025**, with agents who had 5+ years in the industry, had left a brokerage within the prior six months, and had annual sales of $3.5M to $35M, distributed across the South, West, Northeast, and Midwest.
- **Status:** verified against the source 2026-08-18.

**The hedge is not optional and it is not a weakness.**
This is one quote from one person in a ten-person qualitative study. It is **never** a percentage, never "research shows," never "agents talk to their broker ten times in thirteen years." The script says "ten people, that's it, so nobody should be calling it proof of anything" **out loud, in the spoken line**, and that sentence does not get cut for time.

Two reasons. It is honest, which is Rule 1 and the end of the argument. And it is *better content* -- a hedged story about a real person outperforms a confident fake statistic, because the viewer is not being asked to believe a number, they are being handed a human being and invited to check their own.

**Supporting context (not spoken).** The same study found leadership accessibility, personal connection, and belonging to be the departure drivers, above splits and technology. NAR Magazine 2026 reports independently that "compensation splits, technology, and recruiting pitches were not lead factors in retention." Both are already logged in `docs/recruiting-objections.md`. Either can go in the pinned comment; neither belongs in the spoken script, which is carrying one idea.

**Pronouns.** The study does not identify the agent's gender. The script says "they." Do not guess.

**Do-not-cite check:** clean. No behavioral-science finding is cited, and the study is described as what it is.

**Bank status:** entry C1 in `data/brokerage-pain.md`, angles 1 and 2 (this script uses both -- the quote and the inverse count). Log it and update the rotation table.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, move 2 (Stop and turn). Stop on "Ten," deliver "In thirteen years" stationary, resume on "So count yours."

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the hook is an instruction the viewer starts obeying before deciding to watch. Counting is involuntary, and by the time they have a number they are twelve seconds in and invested in what the number means. That is a stronger opening than any claim, because it makes the viewer a participant rather than an audience.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** this is social currency in the emotional register rather than the tactical one, which the back catalog is thin on (Rule 10.7). The share is private -- agent to agent, in a DM, with "this is us." Private shares are the sends-per-reach signal Instagram weights most heavily for non-follower reach, and this script is built for exactly that rather than for saves.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the repetition at 0:20 is a deliberate stall on the strongest beat, and the second question in beat 3 is a fresh hook arriving at 0:40, where a one-idea video normally ends. The viewer stays because a new question just opened.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** two objections are coming, and they are different in kind. The first is "that's on the agent, go talk to your broker," which is a real position and the video should not pre-empt it -- the defenders will handle it, and the argument itself is the engagement. The second is riskier: "ten people is not a study." That one is correct, and the script concedes it in advance out loud, which is why the hedge cannot be cut for runtime. Variant B for the A/B: drop the sample-size disclosure from the spoken line and put it only in the pinned comment. **Do not run Variant B.** It is logged here as the thing that will be suggested when the script runs four seconds long, and the answer is no.

**Recruiting test (guardrail 3):** a good agent watches a brokerage president say, with no pitch attached, that the quiet failure at most firms is not malice but the absence of any reason to call. They respect him more. And the agent who cannot remember a single no-reason call now has a question they did not have this morning, which is the only thing this lane is trying to do.
