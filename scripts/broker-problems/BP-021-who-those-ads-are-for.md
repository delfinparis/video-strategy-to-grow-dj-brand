# Who those ads are for

> **WOW: Every agent has scrolled past their own brokerage's brand advertising and never once asked the only question that matters about it, which is who the ad was talking to. The script does not answer it. It hands the viewer three of their firm's own posts and lets them answer it themselves, which is the one version of this argument that needs no number and cannot be argued with.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You already know who those ads were for. They do too."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 78 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Your brokerage runs ads. Who are they for?

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
You're paying a premium for that name. The ads are part of it.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So pull up the last three your brokerage posted. Not listing photos. The brand ones. Then answer one question. Was that made for a buyer in your market? Or for an agent picking a firm?

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then go find out what other firms charge for a name. You already know who those ads were for. They do too.

If the take runs long, cut "Not listing photos" and let "The brand ones" carry the distinction. Do not cut "Then answer one question," which is what keeps the beat interrogative instead of accusatory, and do not cut the last three words.*

## Production Note

- **This entry may only run as a question, and that is the whole build.** The bank marks F6 `Receipt: NEEDED` with a standing note: there is no sourced figure anywhere for the share of brokerage marketing spend aimed at recruiting versus consumers, and the entry "runs here only as a question, never as a claim." So the script makes no assertion about where the money goes. It states that a brand premium covers brand advertising, which is what a brand premium is, and then it hands the viewer the evidence and leaves. **Any rewrite that converts the hook into "your split pays to recruit your replacement" has invented the receipt. Kill the rewrite.**
- **No number on camera, anywhere, in any version.** Not a percentage, not a dollar figure, not "most." The viewer supplies the only evidence in the video, which is their own firm's last three brand posts.
- **The question is the retention device.** The B-flavor rule in the standard applies here even though this is Flavor F: end the point beat on the question and never answer it on camera. An agent who thinks they have a good answer replies with it, and an agent who does not goes and looks. Both outcomes are the objective.
- **Heat 4.5, and where it lives.** In the implication, not in a verdict. The hook asks who the advertising is talking to; the close says the firm already knows. That is the brand-tax register at its ceiling: direct about the arrangement, silent about the party. Ship test: no firm, franchise, or campaign is named, and "other firms" stays a category. A viewer cannot name one company from this script, because the script is about the one their own firm runs.
- **Family 4, System Indictment, delivered as a question.** BP-020 ran Family 6 and the brief offered 4, 7, or 6, so 6 is out on the two-post rule. Four wins over seven because the friction here points at how the arrangement works rather than at anybody's silence, and this script has no secret to reveal. The register's own signature family.
- **Pattern interrupt: The Stop.** Walk fast for one beat, plant, lean into the lens on "Who are they for." BP-020 ran One Prop and BP-019 ran Gesture-On-Beat, so the move is clean. The cheat sheet pairs The Stop with System Indictment. **Do not hold up a phone showing any real brokerage's advertising.** No logo, no handle, no screen in frame. The prop version of this script is a heat-5 script.
- **Rung 2, and it is the register's rung.** "Find out what other firms charge for a name" is a price comparison on the brand line itself, which is the one comparison nobody in this market runs. It names no destination, recommends nothing, and cannot be read as a pitch. The two sentences after it are the loop-back, not a second ask.
- **"They do too" is the last line and it does the most work.** It is the only moment D.J. says anything about the firm's intent, it is four syllables long, and it is aimed at the arrangement rather than at any company. Land it flat and stop. **No wind-down, no "something to think about" on this one.** The register's ceiling for a closing line is BP-005's "maybe something to think about," and this script spends that ceiling on three words instead.

- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president point at brokerage brand advertising, including the kind his own firm could run, and ask who it is talking to. ### LinkedIn

Your brokerage runs ads. Who are they for?

I am not being cute. It is a real question with a checkable answer, and almost no agent has ever sat down and answered it about the firm whose name is on their business card.

You pay a premium for that name. The advertising is part of what the premium covers. That is not an accusation, it is just what a brand premium is.

So go pull up the last three things your brokerage posted that were not listing photos. The brand pieces. The recruiting-season video, the anniversary post, the market-leader graphic, the billboard photo. Then answer one question about each.

Was that made for a buyer in your market? Or was it made for an agent deciding where to go?

I am not going to tell you the answer, partly because I do not know what your firm posts, and partly because there is no honest number for how brokerage marketing splits between consumers and recruiting. Anybody who quotes you one is guessing. The three posts in front of you are better evidence than a statistic would be.

What I would do with the answer is small. Find out what other firms in this market charge for a name, so the premium has something to sit next to. Right now most agents are paying it without a single comparison.

You already know who those ads were for. So does the firm running them.
