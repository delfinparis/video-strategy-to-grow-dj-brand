# Looking is not disloyalty

> **WOW: Criterion 3 (a credential used against its own interest). Every other video telling an agent to look at other brokerages is made by someone who wants them to land there. This one is made by someone who loses if they do, which is the only reason the permission is believable.**

> **Strongest Line:** "I'd rather an agent know and stay, than stay because they never checked."

## Spoken Script (~62 seconds)

**THE THING**

I run a brokerage, so I'm about to argue against myself here. You should know what the other firms in this market actually offer. And most of you have never once looked.

**THE SIZE**

I don't think that's laziness. I think looking feels like cheating. Like if your broker found out you'd spent twenty minutes on somebody else's website, you'd owe them an explanation. Nobody ever told you that. It's just in the air. Meanwhile every other professional you know has a rough idea what they'd be worth somewhere else. Attorneys know. Salespeople know. In this business you can go ten years and never find out, and we call that loyalty. It isn't loyalty. It's just not knowing.

**THE MIRROR**

So go look. Not to leave. Pick three firms you would never actually join, because you'll learn more from those than from the one you're curious about. What do they charge. What do they say support means. What happens to your database when you go. Twenty minutes.

**LOOP-BACK**

And if where you are is good, you'll find that out too, and you'll stop wondering. That's the part I'm actually arguing for. I'd rather an agent know and stay, than stay because they never checked.

## Production Note

**This one needs D.J.'s sign-off before it films.** Not for legal exposure -- there is none, no
firm is named and no claim is made. For commitment. This script publicly stakes out the
position that agents should know their market, and Kale agents will see it too. Somebody will
eventually say "you told everyone to shop, so I did." The correct answer to that is "good, and
I meant it," and D.J. should be certain he wants to be holding that position before it exists.
**If he is, this is the most valuable video in the lane.** If he hesitates, do not soften it --
shelve it. A hedged permission slip is worse than none, because the hedge is the tell.

**Pattern interrupt (move 1, Direct address).** No walking on the hook. Stand still, straight
to lens, deliver "I run a brokerage, so I'm about to argue against myself here," and only then
start walking. The stillness signals that this is a position rather than a tip, and the whole
lane has trained the viewer to expect walking.

**Re-hook placement.** "It isn't loyalty. It's just not knowing." at roughly 0:32. That is the
sentence the video exists to deliver. Full stop before it, and do not soften the delivery --
it should land as a correction, not a provocation.

**Heat 3, and the register is the entire risk.** This can be delivered two ways and only one of
them works. **Wrong:** as a dare, or with any edge of "your brokerage doesn't want you to know
this." That turns permission into an accusation, the viewer gets defensive on their firm's
behalf, and the doubt converts into a public defense of the status quo. **Right:** matter of
fact, slightly amused at the whole industry, including himself. He is describing a norm he also
lives inside, not exposing a conspiracy.

**The Kale rule at its strictest.** No mention, not one word, not "obviously I think we compare
well." This flavor carries the tightest version of the rule because it is the one that would
retroactively poison the rest of the lane -- a viewer who watches four Broker Problems and then
sees the permission slip resolve to a pitch re-reads all five as a funnel. **The entire power
of this script is that it asks for nothing and names nobody.**

**Rung 2, in its purest form.** "Pick three firms you would never actually join" is the line
doing the work, and the "would never join" qualifier is not a throwaway. It removes the last
piece of guilt from the act -- an agent researching a firm they have already ruled out is not
being disloyal by any definition, which is exactly why they will do it. And having done it once,
the second search is free.

**First-hour ops.** No pinned link. Nothing to source and nothing to offer, and a link in the
first comment would undercut the ask-for-nothing structure. **Comment duty applies, and this
one is different:** the useful comment here is not a complaint, it is an agent saying they had
never thought about it that way, or saying they are going to look. Flag those. An agent who
announces they are going to look is further along than one who complains.

## Data Source

**No statistic is claimed.** `stat_anchored: false`. There is deliberately no number in this
script, and none should be added. The claim being made is a position, not a finding, and
attaching a statistic to a position is how a permission slip turns into an argument -- at which
point the viewer starts evaluating the evidence instead of the permission.

**"Every other professional you know has a rough idea what they'd be worth somewhere else"** is
a rhetorical appeal to the viewer's own experience, not a research claim. It is safe as spoken
because it invites the viewer to check it against people they know rather than asserting a
measured fact. **Do not** upgrade it to "studies show professionals in other industries" -- no
such study is in hand and Rule 8.4 forbids laundering the point through vague authority.

**Supporting context (not spoken, available for the thread if the position gets challenged).**

and belonging drove the departures, above splits and technology. NAR Magazine 2026 reported
that "compensation splits, technology, and recruiting pitches were not lead factors in
retention." Both support the underlying claim that agents leave over things they could have
found out earlier. Both are in `docs/recruiting-objections.md` and `data/brokerage-pain.md`
with their limits recorded. **Neither goes in the script.**

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "I run a brokerage, so I'm about to argue against myself here. You should know what the other firms in this market actually offer."  [hook_family: 3 The Defector | emotion: surprise at the source]
2. "Looking at other brokerages is not cheating on yours. Somebody had to say that out loud, so it may as well be me."  [hook_family: 5 The Confession | emotion: relief] **Faster to the point; loses the credential, which is the credibility.**
3. "You can spend ten years at a firm and never once find out what anyone else offers. We call that loyalty."  [hook_family: 2 Sacred Cow | emotion: recognition] **Heat 3.5-4. Only run in a week the take lane surrendered its slot.**

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, move 1 (Direct address). Stand still through the hook, walk on "I don't think that's laziness." The whole lane walks; this one starts still, and the difference registers before a word is understood.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the credential and the claim point opposite directions, which is a schema break the viewer cannot resolve without watching. A brokerage owner telling agents to shop is either a trick or a genuine position, and finding out which one is the reason to stay. It also inoculates against the most likely dismissal, since the dismissal ("he's recruiting") is pre-named in the first sentence.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** this is the emotional/identity register (Rule 10.7), where belonging gets followed and tactics get saved. The share is private and it is the most valuable kind in this lane -- one unhappy agent sending it to another unhappy agent at the same firm, which puts the permission inside a brokerage rather than in front of one person.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the reversal is structural rather than placed. The viewer spends fifty seconds waiting for the catch, and the catch never comes, and the close ("I'd rather an agent know and stay") is where they realize there wasn't one. That is a stronger completion driver than any mid-video hook, but it only works if nothing in the back half smells like a pitch. One Kale mention and the whole mechanism collapses.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** the sharpest objection is that this is recruiting with extra steps -- that telling agents to shop is what someone with a competitive offer does, and the disclaimer is the technique. **That objection is partly right and the script should not pretend otherwise.** It survives on two things: nothing is named or offered, and the close genuinely argues for staying informed rather than for leaving. If either weakens, the objection wins.

The A/B worth running: Variant A as written. Variant B adds one sentence before the loop-back -- "and yes, I know how this sounds coming from me" -- naming the objection out loud a second time. B is more disarming and risks being too clever, since the hook already did that work once and doing it twice starts to look like technique. Judge on comment sentiment rather than volume; this is the one script in the lane where a quiet, positive comment section is a better outcome than an argumentative one.

**Recruiting test (guardrail 3):** this is the clearest pass in the lane. A good agent watches a brokerage president tell them to go look at his competitors, ask for nothing, and name nobody. There is no version of that where they respect him less. **And it is the only script here that gives an agent explicit permission to do the thing the entire lane is trying to get them to do**, which is why it is worth the sign-off conversation it requires.
