# Nobody shows you the rule

> **WOW: the bank forbids this entry from asserting how leads are actually routed, which looked like a constraint and is the whole script. A claim about lead distribution would be a mechanism narrated as fact and a viewer could argue with it. A request to see the rule in writing cannot be argued with, and the answer the viewer gets back is the video.**

> **Strongest Line:** "If there's a good answer, you've never heard it."

## Teleprompter

You've never been told how leads get assigned.

Somebody decides. You've never seen the rule, and you've never once asked.

So ask for it in writing this week. Then notice who won't put it there.

The video is one question, so here is the email, and then the part I deliberately did not say.

The email. "Hi, quick question. How are company leads assigned, and in what order do agents see them? If there's a written policy I'd like a copy." That is it. No preamble, no explanation of why you are asking, and no apology for asking.

Why in writing matters. A verbal answer to this question is almost always the recruiting version of the answer, and the recruiting version is not a rule. A written policy either exists or it does not, and both of those are useful things to learn.
