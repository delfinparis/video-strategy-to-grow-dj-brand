# You were the client

> **WOW: the receipt has two halves and only one of them has ever been used. BP-006 ran the brand half, that consumers do not rate a national brand as important. This script runs the referral half, that two of three sellers hire a referral or someone they used before, and turns it into an invoice: those relationships are the agent's, and the brand premium is charging her for them.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You were the client."

## Teleprompter

You're paying a premium for your own referrals.

A cut of every closing buys that name. That's the pitch.

The research on this is old and nobody's overturned it. Buyers picking an agent don't rate a national brand as important. Two of three sellers hire a referral or someone they used before.

Those clients were yours. Find out what that name costs at three other firms. You were the client.

## Production Note

- **The whole build exists to avoid repeating BP-006, which ran the same entry ten weeks ago.** BP-006's point beat was "notice where the brand wins business gets said. In recruiting," and its close was the last-five-closings test. **Both are off the table here.** This script never says who the brand is for and never asks the viewer to audit her closings. It states the referral figure and lets the arithmetic do the accusing: if the clients came through people who already knew her, the premium is a charge against her own network.
- **Two receipts, two different limits, and both limits are load-bearing.** The Baylor Keller Center finding is 2008 data reported by Inman in 2017, so the spoken line is "the research on this is old and nobody's overturned it." **Never "a new study," never a year on camera.** The source says buyers did not rate national brand as very important and that sellers rated it somewhat higher, so the spoken line says buyers and stops. The NAR figure is sellers only and is spoken as sellers only. **Do not swap the two populations in the read.** If the delivery blurs buyers and sellers into "consumers," the script has overstated both sources.
- **Heat 4.5, which is the register's ceiling, and it is carried entirely by the hook.** "Your own referrals" indicts the arrangement: the premium is priced as if the name produced the business, and the research says the business came from the agent. **No firm is identifiable.** Every big-brand model in the country prices a name into the split, and the ship test holds: a viewer cannot name one company with confidence from any line in this file.
- **Family 4, System Indictment, which is the only family that reaches 4.5.** The brief offered 4, 7 or 6. Family 6 is out because Named Stakes requires a sourced number in the first line and the strongest number here needs its "the research is old" hedge spoken alongside it, which will not fit in eight words. Family 7 tops out at 4 in the swipe file. **BP-029 ran Family 3, so 4 is clean on the two-post rule**, and the week runs three distinct families.
- **Pattern interrupt: The Stop.** Walk fast for a beat, then plant and lean in on "your own referrals." The cheat sheet pairs the Stop with System Indictment, BP-029 ran the Location Cold-Open, and the plant is the visual argument: the motion stops exactly where the claim turns from a fee into the agent's own work. **No repeat of the previous post's move.**
- **Rung 2, and the close is one clause long on purpose.** "Find out what that name costs at three other firms" is the look. It is not "leave," it is not "there are better options," and it names no direction. **The last three words are irony, not instruction**, which the register doc requires: the arithmetic pushes and D.J. does not. "You were the client" is the hardest this close is ever allowed to get and it is still only a statement about what already happened.
- **The pause is before "Those clients were yours."** Everything before it is sourced research. Everything after it is the viewer's own business. **That seam is the turn** and the Story Pass fails the script without it: read straight through, the figures sound like a briefing.
- **Do not let the read get wry on "That's the pitch."** Flat and fast. A knowing delivery there turns the line into a wink at a specific firm, which is the knowing-wink failure the standard names explicitly, and it costs the same exposure as naming one.
- **First-hour ops:** pin the comment, reply to every comment in the first sixty minutes. Comment duty applies. The reply to watch for is the agent who says her brand genuinely does send her business, which is real for some franchise lead programs. **Do not argue with her.** Ask what share of last year came that way. The question is the lane's whole job.

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

Thirty seconds, so here are the two sources and what they do and do not say.

One. Baylor University's Keller Center ran a consumer study on how people choose an agent. Inman reported it in 2017 and the underlying data is 2008. On buyers, nobody polled rated national brand as very important. On sellers it mattered somewhat more. That is the honest version, and it is why I said buyers in the video and not consumers. The data is old. Nobody has bothered to overturn it, which is its own kind of answer.

Two. NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers: sixty six percent of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with before. That is sellers. I am not stretching it to buyers.

What those two facts add up to is not that brands are worthless. It is that the thing you are paying the premium for is not clearly the thing producing your business, and nobody has ever handed you a number for it.

So the look is one number. What does the brand line cost at three other firms at your production level. Not what a recruiter tells you it is worth. What it costs. Then put that next to where your last ten clients actually came from.

I am not telling you what to do with the answer. I am saying you have probably never had the answer, and it is findable in an evening without talking to anybody at your own firm.

## Council Review

**Convened:** Hormozi, Kane, GaryVee, Miller, Byron Lazine, Eric Simon. **Witnesses:** Heath, Berger.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "You're paying a premium for your own referrals."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: anger, aimed at the model] The default and Hormozi's pick. Seven words, the cost is named in the first three, and the possessive is the whole hook: "your own" converts a fee into a theft of credit without a single accusatory verb.
2. "The name on your sign didn't send you a client."  [hook_family: 2 Sacred Cow | emotion: belief violation] Byron's pick, and it is the bluntest line available. **Rejected on the receipt, not on the heat:** the sources say consumers do not weight the brand highly and that most sellers come through referral. Neither says the brand sent nobody, and a flat negative claim about every brokerage is a figure the script cannot carry. Kane noted it also strands the hedge, because "the research is old" has to follow a finding, not a verdict.
3. "Your brokerage is charging you for your own network."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: anger] Eric Simon's pick, on the grounds that "network" is the word agents actually use about themselves. **Rejected on one word:** "your brokerage" points the sentence at a party, and this register is category-level about the party and direct about the practice. "A premium" has no owner and survives the ship test cleanly.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, The Stop. Walk fast for one beat, plant, lean in on "your own referrals." Cheat-sheet pairing for System Indictment, and the motion-to-freeze lands on the possessive, which is where the hook turns. GaryVee wanted the Whip, pointing the camera at a yard sign for a second and whipping to face. **Overruled on the ship test:** a real sign in frame is a real brand in frame, and no amount of blur makes that category-level. Kane's addition, adopted: the walk before the plant should be genuinely fast, because the stop reads as a decision rather than a mark.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the gap is an accounting gap, which Heath rates the most durable kind in this lane. **The viewer knows where her clients came from and has never once put that next to what the name costs.** The two facts live in separate drawers and the hook slams them together, which is the Aha: not new information, a new adjacency. His Curse-of-Knowledge check: "premium" is the only trade word and the next sentence translates it into "a cut of every closing," in the same breath, which Rule 5 requires. **His objection, logged:** three numbers arrive in fifteen seconds and one of them carries a hedge. He accepts it because the hedge is four words and comes first, but he flags that a rushed read will bury "the research on this is old," and if that happens the script is overstating a source.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** Social Currency, and the sender motive is the good one. Forwarding this says "I already knew my business was mine," which is a flex rather than a confession, so the agent who sends it is not incriminating herself. Berger's retell test: "two of three sellers come from referrals and you pay the brand premium anyway" survives compression with the point intact. **His warning:** the compressed version drops the hedge first, every time, and the hedge is what keeps the claim honest. The pinned comment leads with the limit for exactly that reason and the first paragraph should not be cut.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** every clause pays off a number, which is the pattern that holds this register at thirty seconds. The re-hook is the turn at about second twenty, and the condition is that the pause before "Those clients were yours" is a full beat. **The doctrine's objection:** the point beat is three straight declaratives and that is the shape a viewer scrolls off. **Accepted, and Pass 3 kept one thing for it:** "That's the pitch" ends the tension beat on a hit, so the viewer enters the receipt on a down-beat rather than mid-stride.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Miller says the close has two endings and that two endings are no ending.** His position: "Find out what that name costs at three other firms" is the ask, and "You were the client" is a second payload arriving after the ask has already been placed, so the viewer has to hold an instruction and an insult at once and will remember only the insult. He wants the irony cut and the close to end on the look. **Hormozi and Byron oppose, and they want the opposite trade.** Hormozi: the loop-back is the only line in the script that gets retold, cutting it to protect a CTA trades the share for a task, and this lane is measured on comments and not on completed homework. Byron: ending on an errand is how a Broker Problem becomes a blog post. **Where Miller is right is that the lane has never tested it.** Every script in the register ends on a loop-back after the look, and nobody knows whether the look survives the loop-back or gets erased by it. **Decision: ship the default with the loop-back, because Rule 9.1 requires the last line to reload the first and "You were the client" is the only line that does that here.** **The A/B:** this close against a look-only close ("Find out what that name costs at three other firms. Then look at where your last ten clients came from."), judged on comment mix. If the look-only version produces more agents reporting a number and fewer agents reporting a feeling, Miller has won something the register doc should record.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a good agent who might join Kale watches a brokerage president quote two consumer studies, narrow one of them live because the source does not support the wider version, and then point her at the market rather than at himself. **Respect goes up, and the narrowing is why.** The delivery that flips it is a smirk on "That's the pitch," which makes the whole thing sound like it is aimed at one competitor. Flat, it is a man reading an invoice out loud.
