# You called nobody

> **WOW: Every agent has had the Saturday where a deal came apart and they never considered calling the person whose name is on their license. The script does not treat that as a support failure. It treats it as a habit the agent built themselves, years ago, and never told anyone about, which is why nobody at the firm has ever had to answer for it.**

> **Strongest Line:** "You quit asking years ago, and nobody there noticed."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 78 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Saturday. The deal blew up. You called nobody.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
Inspection came back bad. The other agent went quiet. It was on you.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
You handled it alone. You always do. And you never thought about calling your broker. Not because they'd say no. Because you quit asking years ago, and nobody there noticed you quit. That's what you're paying for.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So go find out what a weekend call gets you at other firms here. Then think about that Saturday again.

*Word count: 78 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 8, tension 13, point 37, payoff 20. If the take runs long, cut "The other agent went quiet" and let the inspection carry the scene. Do not cut "Not because they'd say no," which is the line that keeps the script off the firm and on the habit, and do not cut the last sentence, which is the loop-back.*

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

On the asking, because that is the part people will push back on.

The common reply to a video like this is that the agent should have called. True, and it misses when the decision got made. Nobody decides to stop asking their broker for help. It happens once, slowly, usually in year two or three, and then it is just how you work. By the Saturday in this video there was no decision left to make.

The second reply is that a broker cannot be on call every weekend. Also true. Which is why the thing worth finding out is not whether somebody picks up at nine on a Saturday night, it is what the arrangement actually is. Who is reachable, when, for what, and whether anyone has ever written it down. Most agents have never asked that question about their own firm, and almost none have asked it about anywhere else.

That is the whole look. Ask three agents at your next closing what happens at their firm when a deal comes apart on a weekend. It costs you one conversation and it is the only way to know whether yours is normal.

And if you call your broker all the time and they pick up, say so. That is the better version of this and it is worth other agents hearing.
