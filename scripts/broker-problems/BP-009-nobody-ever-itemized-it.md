# Nobody ever itemized it

> **WOW: The premium is sold as one word, "included," and priced as one number off every closing. Nobody has ever handed an agent the line items, which is why nobody has ever noticed that some of them are empty. The script does the itemizing on camera, asks a checkable question of four of the five items, and leaves the fifth one, the brand itself, unanswered so the replies fill it in.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Somebody should be able to itemize it."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 79 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Nobody ever itemized what you're paying for.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
You hand over a cut every closing for a bundle nobody's ever opened.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
So break it down yourself. Support. Coaching. Tools. Leads. And the brand. When did support last pick up? When was the coaching live? Which tool would you have bought on your own? How many leads came from them, not you?

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So find out what another firm here includes, and what they charge. Somebody should be able to itemize it.

*Word count: 79 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 7, tension 13, point 40, payoff 19. If the take runs long, cut "and what they charge" from the payoff. Do not cut "And the brand," and do not answer the brand item on camera.*

## Production Note

- **Pattern interrupt: Gesture-On-Beat, upgraded from the brief's suggestion.** The brief offered The Stop. Counting the five items off one hand while walking is the better fit here, because the itemization is the whole script and the hand makes the list visible without a text overlay. One finger per item, on the beat, then the hand drops on "And the brand." Tuesday of last week used The Stop, Thursday used Walk-Toward, Friday used One Prop, so the move rotates cleanly.
- **The fifth item is the device and it must stay unanswered.** Four items get a question. The brand gets named and then nothing. That is Rule 10.4 intentional incompleteness, and in this lane it is also the B-flavor move: a question with no good answer produces a reply from anyone who thinks they have one. An editor will want a fifth question to make the list symmetrical. Refuse. The asymmetry is the point.
- **Every item is a question, not a claim.** "Support is a voicemail" would be a claim about a firm D.J. has never seen. "When did support last pick up?" is the viewer's own ninety days, which is what bank entry F2 asks for. This is also why the script needs no receipt: nothing is asserted about anybody's brokerage.
- **No number, anywhere, on purpose.** F2 is marked receipt-not-needed and the bank explicitly says not to attach a statistic to how many agents get nothing, because there is no clean source. "A cut every closing" is true under every compensation model and it is not a figure.
- **"Break it down yourself" versus "itemized."** The word "itemize" appears twice, in the hook and the last line, and nowhere in between. That is the loop-back (Rule 9.1) and it only works if the middle uses different words. Do not let a rewrite put "itemize" into THE POINT.
- **Delivery is flat and fast.** Direct, on the nose, no wordplay (D.J. 2026-09-11). The four questions are read as a list, not as a build. The only slowdown in the script is the beat of silence after "And the brand."
- **Rung 2.** "Find out what another firm here includes, and what they charge" is the look at the market. It does not say leave, it names no destination, and it does not claim another firm is better. The close resolves to a comparison the viewer runs on their own time.
- **No firm is named and none may be added.** "The pitch," "another firm here," "them." Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No.
- **Kale is not mentioned** anywhere, ever, in this lane. Kale itemizes its own bundle, and that is exactly the thing this script may never say. Be right in public.
- **Recruiting test:** a brokerage president says nobody itemizes what agents pay for and sends them to price the same bundle somewhere else, including at firms that compete with him. Respect goes up. Naming a firm, or hinting at his own, would flip it. Neither happens.
- **Comment duty applies.** The replies to flag: agents answering the brand item, and anyone who posts what their own support desk actually does. Both are the doubt arriving free. Flag every Chicago agent into Close.

## Council Review

**Convened:** Hormozi, Miller, Eric Simon, Youshaei, Welsh. **Witnesses:** Heath, Berger.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**
1. "Nobody ever itemized what you're paying for."  [hook_family: 7 Forbidden | emotion: anger] The default. Forbidden shape, the thing nobody inside a firm says out loud, and it names the wrong default (accepting one word for five things) in seven words.
2. "You pay for five things and can only name one."  [hook_family: 4 System Indictment | emotion: anxiety] Youshaei's pick. Sets the list up as a headline in the first frame and makes the video's structure the promise. Rejected as the default because BP-008 ran Family 4 two posts ago.
3. "Included. That's the whole description you got."  [hook_family: 7 Forbidden | emotion: anger] Hormozi's pick, and the fastest. Heath's objection: "included" with no antecedent is a Curse of Knowledge open, and a viewer who does not supply "in my split" in the first half second is gone.

**Pattern interrupt:** Upgraded from The Stop to Gesture-On-Beat. One finger per item, hand drops on "And the brand." The list is the script and the hand is the only way to show it without a text overlay, which this house does not do.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** the hook opens a gap about the viewer's own money, which is the strongest kind, and then refuses to close one fifth of it. The Aha arrives on four items and the Huh stays open on the brand, so the loop is still running when the video ends. That is what sends a viewer to the replies rather than to the next post.
- **Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi):** anger aimed at the model, not at the agent, so forwarding it is Social Currency and not a confession. Retell test: "nobody ever itemizes what your split buys." Survives compression with the point intact. Hormozi's condition is met, since the viewer leaves holding four checkable items and a market comparison.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the list is a countdown, and a countdown with a named endpoint ("and the brand") holds attention to the end because the viewer is waiting for the last one. The risk is that the four questions read as a flat stretch, which is why the bed does not lift and the only silence in the script sits right before the payoff.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **Miller holds that the unanswered fifth item is a second CTA, and two CTAs is zero CTAs.** His argument: the script asks the viewer to check four things, then dangles a fifth, then asks them to go price another firm. Three asks in nine seconds and the rung-2 ask is the one that matters. **Eric Simon and Youshaei hold the opposite**, that the open item is not an ask at all, it is the recognition beat, and it is what produces the comment this lane lives on. **Decision: ship as written, because this lane's success test is the comment, not the click, and the open item is the cheapest comment generator in the script.** The A/B: variant 1 as written against a version that cuts "And the brand" from the list entirely and moves it to the pinned comment, judged on comment volume and on how many replies name the brand item. If the cut version draws the same comments with a cleaner close, Miller was right and the lane learns that the incompleteness device does not need to sit inside the spoken list.

**Welsh's note, not a test:** the itemization is the most repeatable template this lane has produced. Five items, four questions, one silence. It re-skins onto any bundle, which means the split, the cap, the fee schedule, and the tech stack are four more scripts off one structure. Do not run it more than once every three weeks or the shape stops being a device and starts being a format.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a brokerage president tells agents that nobody itemizes what they pay for, hands them four questions about their own ninety days, and sends them to price the same bundle at firms that compete with him. More respect, and it costs him something to say, which is what makes it land. The one move that would flip it is any hint of where the itemized version exists. It does not appear.
