# You pay monthly for a second job

> **WOW: A scene every agent on a required platform has lived and never named: nine pm, logging the day into a system the brokerage chose, so a dashboard somebody else reads stays green. Then the number that makes it a Broker Problem instead of a gripe: every hour in there is an hour not talking to a person who might hire you, and the agent is paying for the privilege.**

> **Strongest Line:** "A job you pay for should at least be optional."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 84 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
You pay monthly for a second job.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
The platform your brokerage requires, fed at nine pm so somebody's dashboard stays green.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
A study last year interviewed ten agents who'd just left. One said the required system felt like a full-time job. Ten people, not proof. Count your hours in there anyway. Every one is an hour not talking to someone who might hire you.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Then find out what other firms require and charge. Because a job you pay for should at least be optional.

*Word count: 84 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 7, tension 14, point 43, payoff 20. If the take runs long, cut "so somebody's dashboard stays green" to "so the dashboard stays green." Do not cut "Ten people, not proof" and do not cut the last sentence.*

## Production Note

- **Pattern interrupt: Walk-Toward.** Camera held out, D.J. walking straight at it with the background sliding past. The cheat sheet pairs it with Mirror, and Tuesday used The Stop, so the move rotates. Film it at dusk if the schedule allows; the scene is nine pm and the light should agree.
- **This is a Silence script, and it is the week's quota post.** The Silence used to run at heat 3 on recognition alone. Since 2026-08-20 nothing ships under 4, so the hook names the wrong default ("you pay for it") before the scene starts. The scene still does the work. The hook just tells the viewer what the scene costs.
- **The hedge is spoken and it stays.** "Ten people, not proof" is the receipt's limit in four words, and it is what lets the quote survive. DelPrete's study is ten in-depth interviews. It is never a percentage and never "research shows." The council wanted it moved; see the dissent.
- **The product name is stripped and may never be spoken.** The bank's source quote names a franchise's platform. Quoting it is heat 5 and the bank marks it a hard block. "The required system" carries the meaning. Do not say the product, the franchise, or anything a viewer could use to identify either, including in the captions and the pinned comment.
- **"Every one is an hour not talking to someone who might hire you"** is the turn. It converts admin time from annoyance into opportunity cost, and it is the line an agent forwards. Deliver it slower than the rest.
- **Rung 2.** "Find out what other firms require and charge" is the look at the market. It does not say leave, it does not say any firm is better, and it does not name a destination. The last line loops the hook: a job you pay for should be optional, and the viewer is left to check whether theirs is.
- **No dollar figure anywhere.** The bank's A3 and C4 both carry the same constraint: no primary survey exists for mandatory tech fees, so no range goes on camera. "You pay monthly" is true for the tech-fee model and true through the split for everyone else, and it is not a number.
- **Kale is not mentioned** anywhere, ever, in this lane.
- **Recruiting test:** a brokerage president says required software costs agents prospecting hours and they should find out what other firms require. Respect goes up, because he runs a firm and said it anyway. Naming a platform would flip it into an attack on a competitor. It does not happen.

**Pattern interrupt:** Confirmed, Walk-Toward. Chris Do's addition, adopted: shoot it at dusk. The scene is nine pm and the frame should say so before the words do.

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath):** "second job" is concrete in the strict sense, an object the viewer already has a feeling about, and "you pay for" attaches a cost to it before the viewer can decide it is normal. The Curse of Knowledge risk is "the platform your brokerage requires," which an agent at a firm with no required system will not recognize. That agent is not the target. Passes.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** anger, high arousal, pointed at the arrangement. Practical value is the opportunity-cost reframe: "every hour in there is an hour not talking to someone who might hire you" is the sentence that gets sent. Retell test: "the software my brokerage makes me use is a second job I pay for." Survives.
- **Retention move (MrBeast):** the scene lands at second two, the quote at second ten, the turn at second seventeen, the ask at second twenty-two. The deceleration is "ten people, not proof," and it is four words.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** **MrBeast wants the hedge moved or cut.** "Ten people, not proof" sits right after the quote and kills its momentum. His fix: put the hedge before the quote ("a study interviewed ten agents, so it's not proof, but one said...") so the quote is the last thing heard before the turn. **Hormozi agrees.** **Heath rules the hedge must stay somewhere in the spoken line** (Rule 1 and the bank both require it), and the question is only placement. **Decision: hedge after the quote, as written.** A hedge before the quote tells the viewer to discount what is coming and they do. A hedge after lets the quote land and then makes D.J. the person who volunteered the limit, which is the credibility the whole lane runs on. **The A/B:** hedge-before versus hedge-after, judged on retention at second twelve. If hedge-before holds more viewers without costing the comment, MrBeast wins and the lane's receipt-limit rule gets a placement note.

**Welsh's note, not a test:** this is a repeatable template. "You pay [cadence] for [thing that is not what it was sold as]" fits half the bank. Use it once a month, not once a week, or the lane flattens.

**Recruiting test (10.3), read twice:** a brokerage president says required software costs agents prospecting time, cites a study honestly, names no platform, and sends the viewer to compare. More respect. The one thing that would flip it is naming the platform in the quote, and the bank marks that a hard block.
