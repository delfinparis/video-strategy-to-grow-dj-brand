# You don't know either

> **WOW: BP-003 found the angle nobody runs, which is that this fee is a credibility cost rather than a wallet cost, and then buried it at 189 words behind a paragraph of source caveat. The re-cut keeps the reframe and spends its whole budget getting to the closing table. The number is not the point. The number is what makes the viewer stay long enough to reach the table.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Right now you don't know either."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 79 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
Four to six hundred dollars. Off your client.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
That's what a report last month estimates. You see none of it.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
The authors say it's mostly anecdotal. So run it on your own year. Twelve closings at five hundred is six thousand dollars your clients paid. You're the one at the table when they point at that line.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
Find out what other firms here charge for it. You'll get asked about that line again. Right now you don't know either.

## Production Note

- **This is a re-cut of BP-003, not a new build.** BP-003's argument is intact: the fee is a reputation cost, not a wallet cost, and the agent is the one holding it at the table. What changed is the runtime, the heat, and the rung. BP-003 ran 189 words to the retired 45-70 second spec, sat at heat 3.5, and closed on rung 3 ("ask in writing"). This runs 79 words to the four-beat clock, sits at heat 4, and closes on rung 2. **Do not film BP-003.** It stays in the repo as the lineage record.
- **The limit is spoken and it does not get cut.** The Consumer Policy Center report states it is "based largely on anecdotal evidence." The script says "the authors say it's mostly anecdotal" in the first line of THE POINT, before the arithmetic, and that placement is deliberate: a caveat after a worked example reads as a hedge, and a caveat before it reads as rigor. It is also what converts the inevitable "that report is thin" reply into a corroborator.
- **"Estimates" is in TENSION and it is load-bearing.** Never "studies show," never "the data says." The bank is explicit on this and the number in the hook is only defensible because the next spoken sentence attributes and limits it.
- **Three numbers from the report are deliberately not spoken.** The roughly two billion dollar aggregate estimate, and the agent-sourced estimate that thirty percent of listing agents and sixty to seventy-five percent of buyer agents charge a fee. All three are the most quotable figures available and the least defensible, because they rest on the anecdotal base the authors disclose. A big round number in a hook is a Rule 1 failure. They can live in the pinned comment with the caveat attached, and they do.
- **The arithmetic is a worked example and has to sound like one.** Twelve closings at five hundred dollars is the midpoint of the reported typical range against a round transaction count. Never "the average agent pays." The spoken frame is "run it on your own year," which is what makes the viewer substitute their own figures, and it also keeps the script clear of a per-agent statistic nobody has.
- **The reversal is the retention device and it moved earlier.** In BP-003 it landed around second thirty-three. Here "You see none of it" lands at second six and the table arrives at second seventeen, which is the only way the reframe fits inside thirty seconds. Land "You see none of it" flat, then take a beat. That beat is the whole script.
- **Family 6, Named Stakes, and the number comes first on purpose.** BP-019 ran Family 7 and BP-018 ran Family 4, so 6 is clean on the two-post rule. The brief offered 6 or 4. Six wins because this is the only one of the week's three entries with a verified figure, and spending it in the first second is the correct use of the one receipt the week has.
- **Pattern interrupt: One Prop.** A redacted or dummy closing statement in hand, fee line visible, up on line one, dropped on "none of it," back up on "that line." **Never a real client's document, and no firm's letterhead in frame.** BP-019 ran Gesture-On-Beat and BP-018 ran The Stop, so the move is clean. The prop is the subject of the video and it is the one script in the lane where a prop carries more than motion would.
- **Heat 4, and where it lives.** In the hook, which leads with a sum taken off a consumer, and in "Right now you don't know either," which names the wrong default. BP-003 sat at 3.5 because its close was a polite request. This one indicts the arrangement: the fee has the brokerage's name on it and the agent's face. Ship test: no firm, franchise, or closing platform is named, and the report does not name firms either. "Other firms here" stays a category.
- **Rung 2, moved down from BP-003's rung 3.** The planner declares rung 2 for this slot and the bank says a rung-2 variant is available: find out what the fee runs at other firms in this market first. That is a better close than "ask in writing" for this re-cut, because an agent who asks without knowing the market gets an answer they cannot evaluate. The rung-3 version stays available for a later script on angle 2.
- **Kale is not mentioned.** "Other firms here" is the permitted universe. There is no version of this close that mentions what any particular firm charges, including D.J.'s own.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president raise a fee that brokerages collect, say out loud that the report behind it is thin, hand them a comparison to go run, and tell them the honest answer to their client's question is currently "I don't know." Respect goes up. The delivery that would flip it is any suggestion the agent should have asked years ago. The script never says that, and "you don't know either" is aimed at the information, not at the agent.
- **First-hour ops:** pin the report link with the caveat in the pinned text. **Comment duty applies.** This is the script in the lane most likely to pull agents posting their own fee amounts in the replies, which is the single best comment outcome this lane produces. Flag every Chicago one into Close.
