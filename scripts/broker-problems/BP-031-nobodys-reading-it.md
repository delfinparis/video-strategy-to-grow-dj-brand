# Nobody's reading it

> **WOW: C4's receipt is a real quote that can never be spoken, because it names a franchise and a product. BP-007 solved that by citing the study around the quote. This build solves it by needing no study at all: the scene is nine pm, the field, and the call that never came, and the only number in the video is one the viewer counts herself.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Nobody's reading it."

## Teleprompter

You do their data entry for free.

Nine at night, updating a system your brokerage requires you to use.

Nobody's ever called you about what's in there. Nobody's asked. Not once this year. It's a field on somebody's report. You paid for it in your split. So what did it give back?

Count the hours you spent in there this month. Find out what another firm requires. Nobody's reading it.

## Production Note

- **This script carries no statistic, and that is the build, not a shortcut.** C4's receipt is verified as a quote and unusable as spoken: the agent quote in the source names a franchise product, which is heat 5 and banned outright. BP-007 handled it by citing the study and hedging the sample out loud. **Running the same move a second time would be the third appearance of a ten-person study in this lane, so this build drops it.** The Silence is the flavor whose beat 2 is a scene, and a scene needs no source. The file is `stat_anchored: false` and there is no figure in the spoken lines, the captions or the pinned comment.
- **Heat 4 is earned in the hook, which is the only way this flavor clears the floor.** The register doc is explicit that the Silence used to run at 3 and now has to name the wrong default in the hook. **The wrong default here is "for free," and it is doing all the work:** it reframes a chore the agent thinks of as her job into labor she is paying to perform. Everything after it is a scene, which is what the flavor is.
- **"Data entry" instead of CRM, platform, system or suite, in the first line.** Rule 5's reading-level floor: the short common words every time, industry shorthand translated in the same breath. An agent with no license understands data entry instantly. **The gloss arrives in the next line** as "a system your brokerage requires you to use," which is the category-level description of the party and the practice at once.
- **Nothing in this script is a claim about every brokerage.** Every line in the point beat is second person about the viewer's own history: nobody has called *you*, nobody has asked *you*. **That is the Mirror register and it is also the receipt posture**, because none of it asserts a population fact. The one line that could drift is "It's a field on somebody's report," which is a description of what a required system does with inputs, not a claim about a particular firm's intentions. Read flat, it is an observation. Read bitter, it becomes an accusation against a party.
- **The point beat ends on a question and the question is never answered.** The standard says a question with no good answer produces a reply from anyone who thinks they have one, and that it must not be resolved on camera. **"So what did it give back?" is the comment engine** and the whole reason this script needs no engagement ask. Do not add a beat answering it and do not answer it in the pinned comment either, which names the hours and the look instead.
- **Family 1, Mirror, which the brief offered alongside Family 5.** Confession was the alternative and it cannot reach the floor on its own. **BP-030 runs Family 4 on Tuesday, so 1 is clean on the two-post rule**, and the week runs 4, 1 and 7 for three distinct families.
- **Pattern interrupt: Walk-Toward.** Camera held out, D.J. walking straight at the lens, background sliding past, not the side-profile stroll. The cheat sheet pairs it with the Mirror, and it is the right move for this script because the approach is faintly confrontational while the words are a scene. **BP-030 runs the Stop and BP-029 ran the Location Cold-Open, so nothing repeats.** Do not film this one at a desk or near a screen: the temptation is to show the system, and the system is a logo.
- **Rung 2, and the close has two halves in the right order.** The count is rung 1 and it is the setup. **"Find out what another firm requires" is the rung 2 that the entry demands**, and the bank says so: the missing receipt turns into the ask. Required-system policy is one of the easiest things in this business to find out from somebody at another firm, and it commits the viewer to nothing.
- **The pause is before "So what did it give back?"** Everything before it is the scene. The question is the turn. Said without the pause the beat sounds like a complaint, and with it the viewer answers in her head before the payoff arrives.
- **First-hour ops:** pin the comment, reply inside sixty minutes. Comment duty applies. **The reply to watch for is the agent who says her system does give back**, which is true for some firms, and she should be asked what it does for her rather than contradicted. The other reply, the one worth flagging, is the agent who names her own hours. Every Chicago agent goes into Close.
