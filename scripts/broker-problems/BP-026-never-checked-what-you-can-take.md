# Never checked what you can take

> **WOW: BP-023 told the story of one asset an agent lost on the way out and the pinned comment listed the rest. This is the list as its own video, and the move that makes it work is that the script does not claim the agent cannot take any of it. It claims the agent does not know, which is both undefendable and true, and then names the reason nobody ever found out.**

> **Strongest Line:** "Nobody meant for you to."

## Production Note

- **Sequenced after BP-023 on the bank's own instruction, and that sequencing is the reason this script works.** B2's entry says plainly: run B1 first, because B1 is the story that makes anyone care about the audit. - **The script claims the agent does not know. It never claims the agent cannot take any of it.** This is the single most important line in this note. Whether a given asset is exportable depends on the firm, the platform, the listing agreement and the state, and there is no version of those answers that is true everywhere. **What is true everywhere is that the agent has not checked.** That is why the point beat ends on a question rather than a verdict, and why the bank marks this entry `Receipt: not needed` with the explicit warning not to invent a statistic about how many agents can export a CRM. **Do not add one, and do not let a caption drift into "you will lose all of it."**
- **Three items named, three unpacked, the fourth withheld. That is Rule 10.4 intentional incompleteness and it is the comment engine.** The fourth is the email address printed on ten thousand business cards and sitting in ten thousand client phones, which is in the bank entry and in BP-023's pinned comment. **It is not spoken and it is not in any caption.** "There's a fourth one, and you already know it" is not an engagement ask. It makes no request, names no action, and asks for no comment. It states that the list is longer than the video. Agents supply the rest, which is the recruiting signal arriving free.
- **Heat 4, and it is one clause: "Nobody meant for you to."** Four words, and it is the indictment the whole script is built to earn. Everything before it is a checklist. That clause says the arrangement was not designed to leave the agent knowing, which is a system claim at category level and the Rule 9.2 definition of 4. **Say it flat and stop.** Any edge in the read turns a structural observation into an accusation about people, and the whole lane's finding is that an agent whose firm is accused defends it out loud.
- **The question is left unanswered on camera, which is the B-flavor rule.** "So which of those can you export tomorrow?" gets no answer in the script, no answer in the captions, and no answer in the pinned comment. The standard is explicit that B-flavor scripts end beat 2 on the question and never resolve it, because a question with no good answer produces a reply from anyone who thinks they have one.

### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

The full audit, since the video only had room for three, plus the one line to look for.

Six things worth knowing the answer to before you ever need it.

One. Your database. Not just the names, the contact history, the notes, the tags, the source of every lead. Names export more often than history does.

Two. Your listing photography. You commissioned it, which is not the same as holding the rights to it, and the licence usually runs to whoever paid the photographer under whatever the listing agreement said.

Three. Your closing files and transaction archive. The one people ignore until a complaint lands years later and the documents they need are behind a login that stopped working the day they left.

Four. Client reviews and testimonials, if they were collected on a system the firm licenses. There is a video from last week about exactly this and it is the asset that cannot be rebuilt on a timeline.

Five. Your work phone number and the email address on ten thousand business cards. This is the fourth one from the video and I left it out on purpose, because every agent who has changed firms names it within about four seconds.

Six. Your own production history in a format anybody else would accept. Easy to get while you are there. Oddly hard afterward.

And the line to look for, since somebody is going to ask. In most independent contractor agreements the relevant language is not under a heading about ownership. It is under termination, and it covers what happens to records, files, listings and client information on separation. Read that section, not the one you expect.

To be clear about what I am not saying. I am not saying you lose all of this. Plenty of it travels and plenty of firms handle it well. I am saying almost nobody knows which, and the answer is much easier to get before you want it than after.
