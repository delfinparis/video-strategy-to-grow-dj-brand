# I make money when you never look

> **WOW: Every other video telling an agent to look at other brokerages is made by somebody who wants them to land there. This one opens by naming who profits from the agent never looking, and the person naming it is one of the people who profits. That is the only reason the permission is believable.**

> **Strongest Line:** "It isn't loyalty. It's just not knowing."

## Spoken Script (30-35 seconds, 78 words)

**HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)**
I make money when you never look around.

**TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)**
Every brokerage does. Looking somewhere else feels like a small betrayal.

**THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)**
Every other professional knows what they'd be worth somewhere else. Attorneys know. Salespeople know. In this business you can go ten years and never find out, and we call that loyalty. It isn't loyalty. It's just not knowing.

**PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)**
So pick three firms you'd never join. Find out what they charge. Twenty minutes. Then you know, and that costs me.

*Word count: 78 spoken words (target 68-84, hard cap 88). Beat check: hook 8, tension 11, point 38, payoff 21. If the take runs long, cut "Salespeople know." Do not cut "It isn't loyalty. It's just not knowing," the "you'd never join" qualifier, or the last clause.*

BP-013 ran the Push-In and BP-012 ran One Prop, so the move and the family are both clean on the two-post rule.
- **Family 5, not Family 3, and the reason is the week.** Thursday runs the Defector. Running it twice in one week flattens the credential into a tic. The confession does the same job through a different door: instead of "I run a brokerage and here is the claim," it is "here is what I get out of your inaction."
- **Heat 4, and the register is the entire risk.** This can be delivered two ways and only one works. **Wrong:** as a dare, or with any edge of "they don't want you to know this." That turns permission into an accusation, the viewer defends their firm, and a private doubt becomes a public commitment against the doubt. **Right:** matter of fact, slightly amused at the whole industry, himself included. He is describing a norm he lives inside, not exposing a conspiracy.
- **"That costs me" is the loop-back and it is delivered flat.** No wink, no shrug, no smile on it. It reloads the hook with its real meaning: he said the thing that was against his interest and then did it anyway. Landing it as a joke kills the only proof the script has.
- **Rung 2, in its purest form.** "Pick three firms you'd never join" is the line doing the work and "you'd never join" is not a throwaway. It removes the last piece of guilt from the act, because an agent researching a firm they have already ruled out is not being disloyal by any definition. Having done it once, the second search is free.
- **The Kale rule at its strictest.** No mention, not one word, not "obviously I think we compare well." This flavor carries the tightest version of the rule, because a viewer who watches four Broker Problems and then sees the permission slip resolve to a pitch re-reads all of them as a funnel. The entire power of this script is that it asks for nothing and names nobody.
- **No firm is named and none may be added.** "Three firms" and "every brokerage" are categories. Ship test: could a viewer name ONE company with confidence? No.
- **Recruiting test, read twice:** a good agent watches a brokerage president say out loud that he benefits from their ignorance, and then tell them how to end it. Respect goes up, and this is the script in the lane that earns the follow rather than the save.
- **First-hour ops: no pinned link.** There is nothing to source and nothing to offer, and a link in the first comment would undercut the ask-for-nothing structure. **Comment duty applies** and the replies here are the most valuable in the lane: agents saying they have never looked, or that they looked once and were made to feel bad about it.
