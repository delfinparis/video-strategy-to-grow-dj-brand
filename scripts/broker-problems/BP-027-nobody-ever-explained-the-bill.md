So the annualization is the viewer's own: the script makes them add up their own year on camera, then asks them to say what it bought. ### Pinned first comment (all platforms)

Since the video only had room for the question, here is the part that is actually checkable.

What is usually in that bill. Local association dues. State association dues. National association dues. Your MLS fee, which is a separate organization from the association in most markets. Then whatever your brokerage adds on top, which is the line that varies the most and gets explained the least.

I am not going to quote you a number, because there isn't an honest one to quote. It depends on your market, your MLS and what your brokerage bundles. Yours is on your own statements, and that is the only figure in this that is real.

The thing worth noticing is that almost nobody can say what the whole thing came to last year, and almost nobody was ever walked through it. There is no onboarding session on it, no annual summary, and nobody in the arrangement whose job it is to raise it.

Here is an agent saying it better than I can, from an episode of my podcast in January of last year. Lou Eytalis, who went on to build and run her own brokerage, talking about being a new agent:

"I didn't really know that that was a thing, you know, like my broker never told me that. He just told me you have to be a part of the association. And he never really explained all the background behind it. I mean, that's a lot of problem with our industries. We don't really educate everybody on how it works."

That was about mandatory membership, and she has a lot more to say about it than fits here. The episode is "Meet The Agent Who Is Taking On N.A.R." if you want the whole thing.

To be clear about what I am not saying. I am not saying dues are a waste. Plenty of what they pay for is real and some of it you would not want to lose. I am saying you are allowed to know what you paid and what it was for, and most agents have never been told either one.
