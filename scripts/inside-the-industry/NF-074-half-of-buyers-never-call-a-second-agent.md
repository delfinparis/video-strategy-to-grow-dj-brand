# Half of Buyers Never Call a Second Agent.

> **WOW: Zillow's 2025 survey of about 10,200 buyers: 50% hired the first agent they contacted, and 41% hired after a single contact. The first conversation is not a warm-up. It is the whole interview.**

**Caption:**
Half of buyers never call a second agent.

That is from Zillow's 2025 Consumer Housing Trends Report, a survey of about 10,200 buyers. Among buyers who contacted an agent, 50% hired the first one they talked to. And 41% hired after only one contact.

Most of us were trained to treat that first call as a getting-to-know-you step. Send a few listings, follow up in a couple of days, earn the meeting later.

The numbers say there is no later. That first conversation is the interview.

Two things to change this week. Answer new leads fast, because being second means half the time you never get a turn. And on that first call, ask for the meeting. Do not wait for round two.

Source: Zillow Consumer Housing Trends Report for Agents, October 2025.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): The surprise is that the hiring decision is already made on contact one. Most agents think the first call is round one of three. 'Small talk' and 'interview' are plain words with no jargon.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): Practical value plus anxiety, which is high arousal. An agent sends this to the teammate who sits on leads. It passes the retell test: 'Zillow says half of buyers hire the first agent they talk to.'
- Retention move (MrBeast): The turn lands at about 0:12, 'four in ten hire after one conversation.' The close echoes 'second' from the hook.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Hormozi wants the payoff to hand over the actual words for asking for the meeting. Miller says that is a second idea and a second video. **Experiment:** film a follow-up Swap script ('Don't say let me send you some listings. Say this.') and see whether the pair beats this one on saves.
