## Data Source

- **Claim:** "The Fed just signaled a rate hike for year-end... median year-end rate projection moved from three point four to three point eight. That implies at least one hike."
  - Source: Federal Reserve June 2026 FOMC Summary of Economic Projections (SEP). Median year-end 2026 fed funds rate projection rose from 3.4% (March 2026 SEP) to 3.8% (June 2026 SEP). Coverage corroborating the read: CNBC stock market coverage 2026-06-17 noting "a number of Fed officials see rates increasing in 2026... the committee sees at least one rate hike as necessary in 2026."
  - Status: confirmed
  - Note: The 0.4 percentage-point upward shift in the year-end median, against a current fed funds target of approximately 3.5-3.75%, mathematically implies the median committee member now sees no cuts and at least one quarter-point hike before year-end.

- **Claim:** "Pending home sales jumped three point eight percent... Four point eight percent year-over-year"
  - Source: NAR Pending Home Sales Index for May 2026, released 2026-06-17. May pending home sales rose 3.8% month-over-month (vs. Dow Jones consensus of 1%) and 4.8% year-over-year. Particularly strong gains in the Northeast and Midwest.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Lawrence Yun's framing. Quote. 'Consumers' acceptance of above-six-percent mortgage rates as the new normal.'"
  - Source: NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun, comments accompanying the 2026-06-17 Pending Home Sales release. Yun characterized the surge as "an indication of pent-up housing demand and consumers' acceptance of above-6% mortgage rates as the new normal."
  - Status: confirmed (direct quote, paraphrased structure)

- **Claim:** "Six percent is the baseline now. Not the obstacle."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's editorial reading of the Yun quote and the data. Not a stat.
  - Status: framing
