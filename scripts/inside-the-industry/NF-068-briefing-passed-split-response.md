# One Company Answered Congress. The Other Said No Comment.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation -- both companies behind the private-listings deal got the same House Judiciary letter and the same Aug 5 deadline, and they answered in opposite ways. MRED (the Chicago MLS) put a cooperative statement on record: happy to help the committee. Compass declined to comment. Meanwhile Judge Tharp's preliminary injunction ruling in the Zillow case, pending since July 13, still hasn't landed. The script reads the split honestly (press no-comment is not proof of stonewalling), then hands the listing agent a proactive one-line update to send active sellers before either case resolves.**

## Script (~72 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
Congress gave the Chicago MLS and Compass until August fifth to explain the private-listings deal. MRED answered. Compass said no comment. Here's what that split tells you about where this is headed.

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:36)
The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee wanted both companies to explain the deal that lets Compass keep listings off the open market. The deadline was two days ago. MRED, the Chicago MLS, said it's happy to help the committee. Compass declined to comment. Same letter, same deadline, two different answers. And that's not the only thing open. Judge Tharp's ruling has been pending since July thirteenth. Still nothing.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:36-0:54)
Don't read too much into the silence. Declining to comment to the press isn't the same as stonewalling Congress. But here's what we do know. Two arms of the federal government are still looking at whether this deal locks buyers out. Neither has closed the book. Every listing you take right now happens while the rules are unsettled.

### CLOSE (0:54-1:12)
Here's what you do now. Don't wait for a seller to ask. Send your active listing clients one line this week. The rules on where listings can and can't appear are being reviewed in court and in Congress, and I'm tracking both so your home is never caught off guard. When the ruling lands, you're the agent who saw it coming, not the one explaining it after.

**Why it should work:**

- Share/save driver (Berger): "Same letter, same deadline, two different answers" is a clean retell line with Social Currency (the sender looks like they read the actual coverage). The fairness beat raises credibility, which makes agents more willing to reshare than a hot take would.

**The dissent (A/B test next):** Gary Vaynerchuk warns that opening on "Compass said no comment" primes a Compass-bashing read, which risks both a poaching tone and a platform flag on a heat-3 piece meant to be a calm explainer. Hypothesis: the neutral open draws broader, less-polarized reach; the contrast open wins saves among Chicago agents who already follow the arc. Same script body, different first 12 seconds.
