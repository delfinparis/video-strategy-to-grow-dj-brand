# $2,165. That's What Your Seller Loses When You Double-End the Deal.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic plus contrarian / accusatory take. Zillow released a study on May 14, 2026 analyzing more than 15 million transactions from 2023 to 2025. Sellers in dual-agency transactions lost an average of $2,165 per home compared with deals where the buyer had separate representation. The take, delivered direct to the agent: that money came out of your client's pocket and went into your second commission. Three concrete plays for the agent who wants to keep the listing without taking the discount: refer the buyer out, disclose the cost data in writing if you stay dual, or renegotiate your commission down on the buyer side as the price of going dual.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All

### CONTEXT (0:08-0:18)
Zillow analyzed fifteen million transactions from twenty-twenty-three to twenty-twenty-five. Sellers in dual-agency deals lost an average of two thousand one hundred sixty-five dollars per home. The pattern showed up every year.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:18-0:28)
That money came from your seller's check. It went into your second commission. The "I'll just bring my own buyer" pitch sounds like service. The data says it's a discount on your client's price.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:28-0:51)
Three plays the next time your buyer wants to write on your listing.

One. Refer the buyer to another agent in your office. You keep the listing side. Your seller keeps the price.

Two. If you stay dual, disclose the Zillow number in writing. Let the seller decide if your second commission is worth the discount.

Three. If you stay dual, renegotiate your buyer-side fee down. Two thousand back to the seller is the floor. Anything less is the conflict tax.

### CLOSE (0:51-0:58)
Two commissions or one client's price. Pick one. The Zillow data just made it the seller's question, not yours.

  - Action 3 (renegotiate the buyer-side fee down) is a compromise play for agents who choose to stay dual. The "$2,000 back to the seller" floor is the script's own benchmark, framed as the minimum to neutralize the documented price effect. Not asserted as a measured market norm.
