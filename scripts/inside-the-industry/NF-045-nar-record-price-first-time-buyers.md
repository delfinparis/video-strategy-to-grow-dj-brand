# Record May Price. First-Time Buyers Back to 2020 Levels. One Report, Two Stories.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + contrarian reframe -- two numbers that aren't supposed to share a report. The May median hit a record high for the month ($429,300, the 35th straight month of year-over-year gains), and first-time buyers jumped to 35% of sales, their highest share since June 2020. Translation for your buyers on the sidelines: the people who can least afford it stopped waiting, and they're closing on the house.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment

## Script (~60 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
The median home price just set a record high for May. Four hundred twenty-nine thousand, three hundred dollars. And first-time buyers haven't been this active since June 2020.

### CONTEXT / MIRROR (0:10-0:30)
Read those two facts again, because they're not supposed to be in the same report. Prices hit a record. And the buyers with the thinnest down payments just flooded back in. Thirty-five percent of May sales were first-timers. Up from thirty-three in April. Up from thirty a year ago.

### THE TURN (0:30-0:48)
So here's the client conversation for this week. You've got buyers parked on the sidelines waiting for prices to drop. Some of them have been waiting since last spring. And while they wait, first-timers are walking right past them and closing on the house. The people who can least afford it stopped waiting. Your client with the bigger down payment is still on the bench.

### CLOSE (0:48-1:00)
The question isn't whether prices come down someday. It's who owns the house while your buyer waits to find out. Call the three clients who told you they're sitting tight. Read them both numbers.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "The median home price just set a record high for May. Four hundred twenty-nine thousand, three hundred dollars... the 35th straight month of year-over-year gains."
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS, "Existing-Home Sales Report Shows 3.2% Increase in May," released 2026-06-09 ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/nar-existing-home-sales-report-shows-3-2-increase-in-may)).
  - Exact figures: "$429,300: Median existing-home price for all housing types," a 1.3% increase from one year ago ($423,700), the 35th consecutive month of year-over-year price increases.
  - Record language: Chief Economist Lawrence Yun's quote, "The new record-high May home price reflects solid fundamentals for homeowners and ongoing supply constraints." This is a record for the month of May specifically, NOT an all-time record. The script says "record high for May" on purpose. Do not let it get edited to "all-time record."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Thirty-five percent of May sales were first-timers. Up from thirty-three a month before. Up from thirty a year ago."
  - Source: NAR, same May existing-home sales report, 2026-06-09 ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/nar-existing-home-sales-report-shows-3-2-increase-in-may)).
  - Exact figures: First-time buyers were 35% of sales, up from 33% in April and up from 30% one year ago.
  - Who was measured: share of existing-home buyers in May 2026.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "And first-time buyers haven't been this active since June 2020 / the highest share in nearly six years."
  - Source: HousingWire, "Existing home sales beat estimates, what it signals for 2026," June 2026 ([link](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/existing-home-sales-beat-estimates-what-it-signals-for-2026/)), corroborated across industry coverage of the same report.
  - Accuracy note: The NAR release itself confirms the 35% figure but does NOT state the "highest since June 2020" superlative. That framing comes from housing-press analysis of the monthly series. June 2020 to May 2026 is five years, eleven months. EP polish standardized every surface on "since June 2020" / "since 2020" and removed the earlier round-ups ("nearly six years" recap in the spoken mirror, "6-year high" in TikTok/YouTube), because "6-year high" overstates an 11-months-short interval. If D.J. wants to stay strictly inside the NAR release, swap the hook's second line to "first-time buyers jumped to 35% of sales, up from 30% a year ago" and drop the June 2020 reference everywhere.
  - Status: confirmed via secondary source; superlative not in primary NAR release

- **Claim:** "Existing-home sales rose 3.2%" (context, not in spoken script but referenced in social).
  - Source: NAR, same report, 2026-06-09. Existing-home sales increased 3.2% both month-over-month and year-over-year.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Production note -- the angle is the contradiction, not the price:** The reel works because two signals that usually move opposite each other (record prices, surging entry-level demand) showed up together. The client-facing payload is the sidelined-buyer conversation, kept in agent vernacular: what to say to a buyer who is waiting for a dip. No mortgage-rate math, no affordability-index jargon. If a viewer needs a spreadsheet to get it, it's the wrong altitude for a 60-second reel.
