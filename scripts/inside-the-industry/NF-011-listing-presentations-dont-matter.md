# Your Listing Presentation Doesn't Really Matter. 81% of Sellers Already Decided.

> **WOW: NAR's Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers has been reporting the same finding for years -- most recently in 2024 -- that 81% of sellers only contact one agent before hiring. The 2025 edition confirmed the broader pattern: 66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or a past relationship, and 91% of all sellers used an agent. The implication is contrarian and uncomfortable. Listing presentations rarely compete against another listing presentation. By the time you are sitting in the seller's living room, the decision is functionally already made. Your job at the table is not to win the listing -- it is to not blow it. The work that actually wins listings happens months earlier, in the network and the sphere, not on the slides.**

## Social Media

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
Eighty-one percent of sellers only talk to one agent. Your fancy listing presentation is not competing with anything.

That number comes from NAR's Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, and it has been consistent across multiple survey years. In 2024, 81% of sellers contacted only one agent before deciding who would sell their home. The 2025 edition confirmed the broader pattern -- two-thirds of sellers found their agent through a referral or a past relationship, and 91% of sellers used an agent overall. Most sellers are not comparison shopping. They are confirming a decision they already made.

Here is what that means for the way most listing agents are spending their time.

The listing presentation as a sales tool was built for a different industry. It assumed sellers would interview three or four agents, weigh the binders, score the marketing decks, and pick a winner. They do not do that anymore. Most do not even do it once.

By the time you are sitting in the seller's living room with the listing binder, the decision is functionally already made. Your job at the kitchen table is not to win the listing. It is to not blow it.

That should change where the work actually is.

Three things every listing agent should do this quarter.

One. Stop optimizing your listing presentation. Start optimizing what gets you in the door. The decision happens before the meeting. Win there.

Two. Audit where your listings actually came from in the last twelve months. If most are referrals, repeats, or sphere -- and NAR says roughly two-thirds are -- your real pipeline is your network, not your slides.

Three. Spend the time you would have spent re-formatting your CMA on staying visible to the people who already know you. That is where the listing decision gets made.

The work that wins listings happens months before the meeting. The data has been telling us that for years. Most of the industry is still acting like the meeting is the contest.

It is not.
