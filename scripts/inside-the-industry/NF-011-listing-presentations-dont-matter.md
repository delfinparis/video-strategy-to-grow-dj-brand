# Your Listing Presentation Has No Competition. 81% of the Time.

> **WOW: NAR's Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers has been reporting the same finding for years -- most recently in 2024 -- that 81% of sellers only contact one agent before hiring. The 2025 edition confirmed the broader pattern: 66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or a past relationship, and 91% of all sellers used an agent. The implication is contrarian and uncomfortable. Listing presentations rarely compete against another listing presentation. By the time you are sitting in the seller's living room, the decision is functionally already made. Your job at the table is not to win the listing -- it is to not blow it. The work that actually wins listings happens months earlier, in the network and the sphere, not on the slides.**

## Script (~56 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:06)
Eighty-one percent. That is how often your listing presentation has no competition.

### CONTEXT (0:06-0:18)
NAR's Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers has been showing this for years. Most recently in twenty-twenty-four. Eighty-one percent of sellers contacted only one agent before deciding who would sell their home. The twenty-twenty-five report confirmed the pattern -- two-thirds of sellers found their agent through a referral or a past relationship. Most sellers are not comparison shopping. They are confirming.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:18-0:30)
Here is the catch. The listing presentation as a sales tool was built for a world where sellers actually shopped agents. They do not anymore. By the time you are sitting in their living room with the binder, the decision is functionally already made. Your job at the kitchen table is not to win the listing. It is to not blow it.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:30-0:50)
Three things this quarter.

One. Stop optimizing your listing presentation. Start optimizing what gets you in the door. The decision happens before the meeting. Win there.

Two. Audit where your listings actually came from in the last twelve months. If most are referrals, repeats, or sphere -- and NAR says roughly two-thirds are -- your real pipeline is your network, not your slides.

Three. Spend the time you would have spent re-formatting your CMA on staying visible to the people who already know you. That is where the listing decision gets made.

### CLOSE (0:50-0:56)
Listing presentations do not really matter. The work that wins listings happens months earlier. The contest is not the presentation. It is everything that happens before it.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "81% of sellers contacted only one agent before deciding"
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS®, "2024 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2024-11/2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2024_2.pdf))
  - Cross-confirmed: 2023 NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers reports the same 81% figure; pattern has been consistent across multiple survey years
  - Status: confirmed (primary source: NAR's own published research)

- **Claim:** "Two-thirds of sellers found their agent through a referral or past relationship"
  - Source: NAR, "2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/blogs/economists-outlook/top-10-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers)) -- "66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with in the past"
  - Spoken delivery rounds 66% to "two-thirds" for cadence; exact figure preserved here per Rule 1
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "91% of sellers used an agent" (context for the WOW gate, not in spoken script)
  - Source: same 2025 NAR Profile; FSBO at 5%, the lowest share ever recorded by the survey
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Listing presentation as a sales tool was built for a world where sellers actually shopped agents"
  - Status: editorial framing -- not a numeric claim. Defensible against the multi-year NAR data showing four in five sellers do not comparison shop. The history of the listing presentation as a 1980s/90s real-estate-coaching-industry artifact is widely understood and not contested.

- **Claim:** "Your job at the kitchen table is not to win the listing -- it is to not blow it"
  - Status: editorial color, not a statistical claim

- **Hook framing note:** The opening line "your listing presentation has no competition -- 81% of the time" is a precise restatement of the NAR data, not a claim about presentation effectiveness. The 81% measures seller comparison-shopping behavior; the spoken hook attributes the resulting absence of a rival presentation, not the absence of presentation function. This distinction matters under Rule 1.

- **Buyer-side parallel (context, not in spoken script):** The 2025 NAR Profile reports 67% of first-time buyers and 76% of repeat buyers interviewed only one agent. The "do not comparison shop" pattern is not unique to sellers.

- **Cross-reference within series:**
  - Connects to the broader pattern that the industry's tactical playbook (presentation polish, CMA design, staging the meeting) is calibrated to a buyer-and-seller behavior pattern that no longer exists. Useful pairing if a future IS synthesis pulls "outdated tactics vs. current behavior" into a longer thread.

- **Action layer (the three actions in WHAT YOU DO NOW):** Each action is a watchpoint or audit the agent fully controls -- a self-review of pipeline sources, a reallocation of preparation time, and a continued investment in network visibility. No prescriptive marketing-product recommendation, no specific brokerage tool plug. The guidance maps directly to NAR's own data on where listing decisions actually originate.

- **Fabrication audit:** Every number in the spoken script traces to NAR's published Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (2024 and 2025 editions). The "81%" is exact. The "two-thirds" is a defensible spoken rounding of the 66% in the 2025 report. No invented percentages. No "studies show." The contrarian framing ("listing presentations don't really matter") is interpretive, not statistical, and rests on multi-year published data.

Your listing presentation has no competition. 81% of the time.

One. Stop optimizing your listing presentation. Start optimizing what gets you in the door. The decision happens before the meeting. Win there.

Two. Audit where your listings actually came from in the last twelve months. If most are referrals, repeats, or sphere, your real pipeline is your network, not your slides. NAR says roughly two-thirds are.

Three. Spend the time you would have spent re-formatting your CMA on staying visible to the people who already know you. That is the room where the listing decision gets made.

The work that wins listings happens months before the meeting. The data has been telling us that for years. Most of the industry is still acting like the meeting is the contest.

It is not.
