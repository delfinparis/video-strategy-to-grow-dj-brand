# The 30-Day Stigma: What Overpricing Actually Costs Your Seller

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + tactical specificity -- Realtor.com's June 2026 release put a number on the price of sitting: homes that close in the first four weeks sell for 1.8 percentage points more than the average sale in the same month. Zillow's research: overpricing by 10% adds a month to time-on-market, and homes sold between days 30 and 60 close 4.75% under list. In Chicagoland right now, 44% of listings go under contract in the first two weeks. The script arms the listing agent for the "we can always come down" seller with a whiteboard action and the compound-tax frame.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Named Stakes | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Avatars:** All, listing agents especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Sellers who test the market aren't taking a risk. They're paying a compound tax on time."

## Script (~68 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:14)
One point eight percentage points. That's the premium for closing in the first four weeks. Your listing hits day thirty. Buyers stop thinking "negotiation opportunity." They start thinking "what's wrong with this house."

### CONTEXT (0:14-0:36)
Overprice by ten percent and your listing sits an extra month. That's Zillow's research. Between day thirty and sixty. That's when homes sell for four point seven five percent under list. And in Chicagoland right now, forty-four percent of listings go under contract in the first two weeks. That's peak attention. Miss it and buyers stop asking if the house is right for them.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:36-0:52)
Overpricing has three compounding costs. Lost offers in week one. The eventual price cut. And the stigma cut when someone finally bites. All from listing a little high and seeing what happens.

### CLOSE (0:52-1:10)
Here's what you do now. At every listing appointment this week, put day thirty on the whiteboard. Then show them the Zillow number and the Realtor.com number. Sellers who test the market aren't taking a risk. They're paying a compound tax on time.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "One point eight percentage points. That's the premium homes get when they sell in the first four weeks."
  - Source: Realtor.com research release, "By Week Four, Your Listing Is Either Getting Offers or Price Cuts," PR Newswire 2026-06 ([link](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/by-week-four-your-listing-is-either-getting-offers-or-price-cuts-302797293.html)). Finding: homes that close at the four-week mark sell for 1.8 percentage points higher (as a ratio of asking price) than the average home sold in the same month. Sellers whose homes go into contract in the first two weeks fare even better.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Precision note: the 1.8pp is specifically the CLOSE-in-4-weeks premium (which implies going under contract within ~2 weeks). D.J.'s original brief said "1.8pp higher for homes that go to contract in the first two weeks" -- close but not exact. Script uses the accurate framing ("premium homes get when they sell in the first four weeks").

- **Claim:** "Overprice by ten percent and your listing sits an extra month."
  - Source: Zillow Research, "The Price of Overpricing: How Listing Price Impacts Time on Market" ([link](https://www.zillow.com/research/overpricing-impacts-time-market-12476/)). Finding: overpricing by even 10% causes a listing to sit on the market approximately one month longer.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Between day thirty and sixty... homes sell for four point seven five percent under list."
  - Source: Same Zillow Research (above). Finding: homes sold between 30 and 60 days on market close at a 4.75% discount from list price. Homes on market 60+ days close at approximately 5% below list.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "In Chicagoland right now, forty-four percent of listings go under contract in the first two weeks."
  - Source: Illinois Realtors / Redfin Chicago Housing Market Update, May 2026 ([link](https://www.redfin.com/blog/chicago-il-housing-market-may-2026/)). Finding: 44% of Chicago-area listings go under contract within the first two weeks. Median Chicago-area days on market: 27 (Illinois Realtors May 2026 print).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (editorial):** "Overpricing has three compounding costs. Lost offers in week one. The eventual price cut. And the stigma cut when someone finally bites."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's synthesized frame drawing on the Zillow and Realtor.com findings.
    - Cost 1 (lost offers in week one): from Realtor.com finding that peak-attention early offers command the 1.8pp premium.
    - Cost 2 (eventual price cut): from Zillow finding that overpricing extends time on market and forces reductions.
    - Cost 3 (stigma cut when someone finally bites): from the 4.75% discount on day-30-to-60 homes -- the buyer who bites late knows the home has sat and negotiates accordingly.
  - Status: editorial framing, tied to the cited data

- **Claim (tactical):** "At every listing appointment this week, put day thirty on the whiteboard. Then show them the Zillow number and the Realtor.com number."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended listing-appointment protocol. The whiteboard action is a concrete visual anchor -- most sellers have never seen the day-30 stigma line drawn out. Consistent with the "run the numbers with the client" ethos of NF-059 (sub-4% seller conversation).
  - Status: tactical guidance
