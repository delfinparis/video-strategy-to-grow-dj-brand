# She Built a Community. Then She Built a $100 Million Business.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + counter-narrative -- a Greenwich, Connecticut agent did over $100M in transactions last year, and the most-cited reason in the Inman profile isn't her brokerage, her marketing budget, or her social presence. It's a community resource she co-founded that grew into The Local Moms Network across dozens of U.S. cities. Her clients used her community long before they hired her. The script reframes "lead-gen" as "be the resource your community needs" and hands the agent a Facebook-group starter playbook.**

**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Script (~70 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
A Greenwich agent did over a hundred million dollars in transactions last year. The reason isn't her brokerage. It's a community resource she built that her clients used long before they hired her.

### CONTEXT (0:10-0:30)
Megan Sullivan. Inman profiled her in March. She left a finance career and moved to Greenwich as a young mom. There were no local resources for families. So she and a friend started Greenwichmoms.com. It grew into The Local Moms Network across dozens of U.S. cities. By the time families in town needed a real estate agent, Megan was already the woman they trusted with their schools, their pediatricians, and their summer camps.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:30-0:48)
You don't need a national network. You need one community in your market with a need nobody's filling. A free Facebook group that actually helps that community is the most undervalued lead-gen tool in real estate. It's also the one Compass and Zillow can't compete with. Trust earned over schools doesn't transfer to a portal.

### CLOSE (0:48-1:10)
Here's what you do now. Pick one underserved community in your market this week. Maybe it's relocating families. Maybe it's empty nesters downsizing. Start a free Facebook group named after that community. Post one helpful answer every week. No listings. Just answers. By month six, you're the agent they call.

## Social Copy

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
A Greenwich agent did over $100 million in transactions last year. The reason isn't her brokerage. It's a community resource she built that her clients used long before they hired her.

Megan Sullivan. Inman profiled her in March.

She left a 10-year finance career in New York and moved to Greenwich as a young mom. There were no local resources for families. So she and a friend started Greenwichmoms.com. It grew into The Local Moms Network, an online resource that now spans dozens of U.S. cities.

By the time families in town needed a real estate agent, Megan was already the woman they trusted with their schools, their pediatricians, and their summer camps.

Here is the read for the rest of us.

You don't need a national network. You need one community in your market with a need nobody is filling. A free Facebook group or page that actually helps that community is the most undervalued lead-gen tool in real estate.

It's also the one Compass and Zillow can't compete with. Trust earned over schools doesn't transfer to a portal.

Here is what you do now.

1. Pick one underserved community in your market this week. Relocating families. Empty nesters downsizing. Veterans buying their first home. First-time landlords in a specific neighborhood. Pick one.

2. Start a free Facebook group named after that community. "[Your Town] Relocation Q&A." "Downsizing in [Your Suburb]." Make it free to join. Make the rules simple.

3. Post one helpful answer every week. No listings. No ads. Just answers.

By month six, you're the agent they call. By year two, you're the only agent they would call.

The work is small. The compound is enormous.
