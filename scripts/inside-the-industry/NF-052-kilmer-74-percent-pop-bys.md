# She Grew 74% a Year. She Spent Nearly Nothing.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + counter-narrative -- a Kansas City agent grew her business 74% YoY for five years on a near-zero marketing budget. No Zillow. No Facebook spend. The tactic is the one most agents quit using in 2019: the pop-by, a hand-delivered gift to a past client with no ask attached. The script reframes "lead-gen" as "doorstep work" and hands the agent a four-step Saturday playbook.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All, buyer-side and listing agents, agents under five years in the business especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Social Copy

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
A Kansas City agent grew her business 74% a year for five years. She spent nearly nothing on lead-gen. Her tactic is the one most agents quit using.

Rachel Kilmer. Inman profiled her this morning.

No paid leads. No Zillow spend. Her business is built on what the industry calls pop-bys. A small gift, hand-delivered, to a past client, with no ask attached. Coffee on the way to a showing. A pumpkin on a porch in October. A school-supply pack in August.

The reason isn't the gift. It's the doorstep.

Most agents quit pop-bys when CRM automation showed up. Drip campaigns are cheaper. Templates are faster. Click-rates are measurable. None of that is the same as standing on someone's porch with a $4 coffee.

The reason pop-bys worked is the reason they're rare now. It's effort. Showing up at a doorstep is the one marketing move you can't fake with an email blast.

Rachel's 74% didn't come from a tool. It came from showing up.

Here is what you do now.

1. Make a list of your 10 favorite past clients.

2. Pick one cheap seasonal item this weekend.

3. Block four hours next Saturday and hand-deliver each one with a 30-second hello. No pitch. No business card unless asked. Just hello.

4. Do it again in three months.

That's the whole system. No spend. Just the doorstep.
