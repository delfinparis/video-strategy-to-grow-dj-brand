# Everybody's Experimenting With AI. Almost Nobody's Shipping.

> **WOW: Surprising gap plus a mindset reframe. McKinsey's latest State of AI finds 62% of organizations experimenting with AI agents but only 23% scaling one, while McKinsey's real estate work says agentic AI could unlock up to $550 billion in value. The take: experimenting feels like progress but isn't; the jump is shipping one daily task to a tool that runs without you. NOTE: this replaces the original brief's unverified "92% / 5%" stat, which appears nowhere in McKinsey's reports. Classification flag: could also run as AI Tip of the Week.**

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:40-0:54)
Pick one task you do every single day that you hate. Just one. Follow-up texts, CMA prep, sorting new leads. This week, hand that one task to a tool and let it run. Don't dabble in ten things. Ship one. That's the jump from sixty-two to twenty-three.

### CLOSE (0:54-0:57)

## Production Notes

- **Stat-integrity flag (read this first).** The original research brief proposed "92% of firms are experimenting, 5% have made it work." That split does not appear in McKinsey's real estate report or its State of AI surveys and could not be sourced. It was replaced with McKinsey's actual figures: 62% experimenting with AI agents, 23% scaling. Do not revert to 92/5 on camera. This is the same failure class as the IS-002 "6% Club."
- **Cross-industry vs. real estate.** The 62%/23% figures are from McKinsey's cross-industry State of AI survey, not a real-estate-only study. The script deliberately says "companies," not "real estate agents," to stay honest. The real-estate-specific claim is the value figure (up to $550B). Keep those two sources distinct on camera.
- **Classification.** Filed as NF (reactive to a published McKinsey report). It could alternatively run as AI Tip of the Week given the "ship one task" action. If moved, renumber to the AI Tip sequence and adjust the close toward a tool CTA.
- **Avoid naming a specific vendor on camera** unless D.J. wants to. "A tool" keeps it evergreen; the AI Tip series is the place for a named tapthis.co-style recommendation.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Sixty-two percent of companies... experimenting with AI agents. Twenty-three percent have actually scaled one."
  - Source: McKinsey, "The State of AI" ([link](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)). 62% of respondents say their organizations are at least experimenting with AI agents; 23% report scaling an agentic AI system somewhere in the enterprise.
  - Note: cross-industry survey, not real-estate-specific. Spoken script says "companies," not "agents," to preserve accuracy.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "agentic AI... could unlock up to five hundred fifty billion dollars in value" (real estate)
  - Source: McKinsey, "How agentic AI can reshape real estate's operating model" ([link](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/real-estate/our-insights/how-agentic-ai-can-reshape-real-estates-operating-model)); McKinsey Global Institute estimate of $430B-$550B in annual value across real estate, construction, and development.
  - Spoken delivery uses "up to five hundred fifty billion" (the top of the $430B-$550B range).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "agentic AI, tools that run a workflow instead of just writing you a paragraph"
  - Editorial plain-language definition of agentic AI vs. first-wave generative AI. Supported by McKinsey's framing of the shift from content generation to workflow automation.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by source

- **Claim (illustrative):** "Follow-up that fires on its own. Leads sorted before they wake up."
  - Illustrative examples of agentic workflows for an agent, not cited statistics.
  - Status: illustrative / coaching color

- **Fabrication audit:** 62% and 23% trace to McKinsey's State of AI survey and are framed as cross-industry ("companies"). The up-to-$550B figure traces to McKinsey's real estate / MGI work. The original brief's "92% / 5%" was removed because it could not be sourced to any McKinsey report. The "ship one task" action and the workflow examples are coaching color, framed as such, not statistics.
