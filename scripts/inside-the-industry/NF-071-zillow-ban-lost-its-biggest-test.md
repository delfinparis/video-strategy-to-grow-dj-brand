# Zillow's Ban Just Lost Its Biggest Test.

> **WOW: Contrarian take plus tactical specificity. For seventeen months agents have sequenced listings around one private company's policy, because breaking it got your seller's home banned from the biggest search site in real estate. On September 15 that policy lost in federal court, and the analyst who has covered this case hardest read the opinion and called the policy dead. The script does not ask the agent to celebrate or to pick a side. It asks them to open the marketing plan they are about to run and find the part that only exists to dodge a rule that just lost.**

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Zillow's ban on privately marketed listings just lost its biggest test."
  - Source: Zillow's Listing Access Standards, introduced April 2025, bar a home from Zillow if it is not available on IDX or VOW feeds within one business day of being publicly marketed (HousingWire, 2026). On 2026-09-15, U.S. District Judge John J. Tharp Jr. denied Zillow's motion for a preliminary injunction against MRED and dissolved the temporary restraining order, in *Zillow Group, Inc. et al v. Midwest Real Estate Data LLC et al*, N.D. Ill., Dkt. 138.
  - Who was measured: n/a.
  - Status: confirmed. "Its biggest test" is accurate: this is the first federal ruling on the merits posture of the Standards dispute. The earlier Compass action ended in a voluntary dismissal on 2026-03-18 after a February 2026 preliminary-injunction denial, so it produced no comparable ruling.

- **Claim:** "The court lifted the order. MRED can cut Zillow's Chicago feed again today."
  - Source: The 2026-09-15 order dissolved the TRO that had required MRED to restore Zillow's IDX and VOW feeds. Rob Hahn, Notorious ROB, 2026-09-16: "the court ruled against Zillow's motion for a preliminary injunction and removed the temporary restraining order on MRED to not cut off Zillow's data feed. As of today, MRED is free to go back to cutting off Zillow's access to IDX and VOW and other data feeds, though as of this writing, there has been no word of such an action."
  - Status: confirmed. **Note the second half of that quote.** As of 2026-09-16 MRED had not acted. The script says MRED *can* cut the feed, not that it has. Do not upgrade to "MRED cut the feed" without checking first.

- **Claim:** "MRED was enforcing its own rule, not crushing a competitor."
  - Source: Opinion, 2026-09-15: "On the present record, Zillow has not sufficiently shown that MRED's threatened termination of the feed constitutes exclusionary maintenance of monopoly power rather than enforcement of its data distribution rules." Quoted in Hahn, Notorious ROB, 2026-09-16.
  - Status: confirmed against the opinion text as quoted. "On the present record" is a real limit, which is why the spoken line describes what the court found about MRED rather than announcing a general rule for all MLSs. Hahn's caution, worth knowing on camera: an MLS that changed rule after rule to target one party "is going to hit differently than MRED, which had the same rule for over 20 years and clarified it once."

- **Claim:** "The court said MRED going national could be good for competition."
  - Source: Opinion, 2026-09-15: "The record also identifies plausible procompetitive effects from the challenged conduct. MRED's national expansion could permit it to compete with other MLSs for members and listings, potentially increasing the choices available to brokers, agents, and sellers. Reffkin testified that 'the more MLSs are competing with each other, the better it is for agents and sellers,' and Jensen likewise testified that agents, sellers and buyers benefit from competition among MLSs." Quoted in Hahn, Notorious ROB, 2026-09-16.
  - Status: confirmed, quoted from the opinion. **The hedges are in the original.** "Could permit" and "potentially increasing" are the court's words, which is why the spoken line says "could be good for competition." Do not tighten it to "is good for competition."

- **Claim:** "Every other MLS can now copy that rule with the lawsuit risk gone."
  - Source: This is Hahn's analysis, 2026-09-16: "other MLSs will now become emboldened. A federal court just ruled that Zillow has to obey the MLS's rules or face consequences." Supported by the observable record: Realtracs threatened suspension in May 2026 and settled into a new licensing agreement on 2026-08-05 (RISMedia, Inman); Bright MLS took a different route in July 2026, letting sellers suppress price, address, photos, days on market and price history from portals while the listing stays syndicated (Inman 2026-07-09).
  - Status: **analysis, attributed.** The spoken line states a consequence rather than a prediction of any specific MLS's action. If D.J. prefers to keep his own ledger clean, the safe rewrite is "Which is why Rob Hahn thinks every other MLS now copies that rule."
