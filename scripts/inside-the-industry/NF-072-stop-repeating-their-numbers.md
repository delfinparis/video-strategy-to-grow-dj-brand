# Stop Repeating What Zillow and Compass Say About Private Listings.

> **WOW: Surprising specific that almost no agent knows. The opinion establishes on the record that Zillow Preview is itself a private listing network, and that Zillow revised its own Listing Access Standards to permit Coming Soon marketing after determining some of its own Preview listings would otherwise have violated the policy. The company whose ban reorganized how agents market homes wrote an exception that covers its own product. On the other side, the only numbers supporting private-first came from Compass's CEO on the stand. Every agent in America is repeating one of these two stories to sellers as if it were evidence. The script hands over a ninety-day query that replaces both with the agent's own number.**

Compass similarly offers 'coming soon' listings during Phase 2 of its 3PM. Dubbed 'Compass Coming Soons,' these listings are publicly displayed on Compass's website and on Redfin, an online portal competitor of Zillow."   - Status: confirmed, quoted from the opinion. "PLN" is the opinion's abbreviation for private listing network; the spoken line says the words out loud per the general-public rule.

- **Claim:** "The opinion says Zillow rewrote its own ban after it found some Preview listings would have broken it."
  - Source: Opinion, 2026-09-15: "The Standards do not, however, prohibit all marketing before active MLS distribution. Quoted verbatim in Hahn, Notorious ROB, 2026-09-16.

- **Claim:** "Compass says private-first sells for more. That's Compass's own number, from Compass's CEO."
  - Source: Compass CEO Robert Reffkin testified at the 2026-07-02 preliminary-injunction hearing that Compass Private Exclusives and Coming Soon listings showed a 2.9 percent higher sales price and 20 percent faster days to contract, and that homes marketed the traditional way "look like damaged goods." - **Claim:** "They both sell one. Both have money riding on your answer."
  - Source: Zillow adopted its Listing Access Standards in April 2025, barring a home from Zillow if it is not available on IDX or VOW feeds within one business day of being publicly marketed (HousingWire 2026). Compass's three-phase marketing strategy, including Private Exclusives and Coming Soon, is the model those standards land on (HousingWire 2026; RISMedia 2026-07-02). Both companies are parties to this litigation. Reffkin testified that Zillow "does not contribute a single listing to the MLS system" while extracting agent-created content.
  - Status: confirmed. "They both sell one" is supported directly by the opinion, which describes Zillow Preview as a PLN and Compass Coming Soons as its Phase 2 equivalent. Stated as a structural fact about the parties, not as an accusation of bad faith against either.

- **Claim:** "Pull ninety days of closed sales. Compare the homes that started out private against the ones that didn't."
  - Source: MLS participants have access to closed-sale data and to listing-status history in their own MLS. NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers policy, announced March 2025 alongside the retained Clear Cooperation Policy, created delayed marketing exempt listings and office exclusive exempt listings, both of which are filed with the MLS and both of which require documented seller consent (NAR, 2025; HousingWire 2025). Those statuses are what make the comparison possible where an MLS records them.
  - Status: confirmed as to the policy structure. **Practical caveat, not spoken:** how cleanly an individual MLS tags pre-MLS marketing history varies, and some agents will need to ask their MLS for the field or run the comparison on days-on-market history instead. If D.J. wants to add one line for accuracy, the cut is: "If your MLS doesn't track it cleanly, ask them why not." That line is also a heat-4 beat aimed at a system rather than a person, so it fits.

## Social Copy

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
Zillow runs a private listing network. It is called Zillow Preview, and the court's opinion says so in plain language.

From the September 15 ruling: "Zillow Preview is a PLN, which permits participating brokerages to display 'coming soon' listings publicly on Zillow before active MLS distribution."

Then, four paragraphs later, this: "Zillow revised the Standards to permit publicly accessible 'Coming Soon' marketing, after determining that some listings offered through Zillow Preview would otherwise have violated the original policy."

Read that twice. The policy that has reorganized how agents sequence a listing for the last seventeen months got an exception written into it because the company that wrote the policy runs a product that would have broken it.

I am not calling that bad faith. I am saying it is on the record, and almost no agent knows it.

Now look at the other side. The numbers supporting private-first marketing came into this case through Compass CEO Robert Reffkin's testimony on July 2: a 2.9 percent higher sales price and 20 percent faster days to contract for Private Exclusives and Coming Soon listings. Compass's figures, Compass's listings, Compass's CEO, offered in support of Compass's position.

So after four months of litigation, two CEOs on the stand, and a written opinion, the question a seller actually asks still has no neutral answer anywhere in the record.

And most of us answer it anyway. Half the industry tells sellers a private listing makes a home look like damaged goods. The other half tells sellers it nets more money. Both halves are quoting a company with a balance sheet riding on the answer.

Here is the fix, and it takes about twenty minutes.

Open your MLS. Pull the last ninety days of closed sales in your actual market and your actual price band. Split them: homes that had a pre-MLS or delayed marketing period, and homes that went straight on. Compare two things. Days to contract. Sale price against list price.

If your MLS does not track that cleanly, ask them why not. NAR's delayed marketing exempt and office exclusive exempt statuses are both filed with the MLS by design.

Whatever that query says is a real answer about the market you actually sell in, and neither company gets a vote in it.

Stop handing your seller two companies' marketing and calling it advice.
