# NAR Just Voted Down a Rule That Would've Forced Agents to Be Honest

> **WOW: Contrarian take + permission slip. NAR killed a proposed ethics rule because admitting a knowledge gap supposedly loses client trust. That reasoning is exactly backwards, and the agents who already do the opposite keep clients for life.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Type:** Reactive (NF)
**Primary Platform:** LinkedIn | **Post Date:** Friday, June 19, 2026

> **Shareable Moment:** "Most agents think admitting a gap loses the deal. The best ones know it's what earns the client for life."

## Script (55-60 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:08)
This week NAR voted down a rule that would've required agents to admit when a neighborhood isn't their specialty.

### CONTEXT (0:08-0:28)
It came from NAR's own ethics committee at the midyear meetings in D.C. The idea was simple. If you don't know the area or the property type in front of you, you tell the client. The committee chair called it an ethical duty. The board sent it back. One of the arguments against it. Admitting you don't know something makes a client lose trust in you.

### INSIGHT (0:28-0:48)
That's backwards. In twelve years interviewing top producers, I've never met one who pretended to know every market in the city. Chicago is fifty different markets. A Lincoln Park condo expert has no business winging a land deal in the south suburbs. Most agents think admitting a gap loses the deal. The best ones know it's what earns the client for life.

### CLOSE (0:48-0:60)
You don't need a rule to be honest. Here's what you do now. Next lead that lands somewhere you don't know cold, say so, and refer it to someone who does. You lose one commission and gain a referral source for ten years. The best agents never needed NAR to make that mandatory.
