# NAR Just Published Rules on Who Controls Your Listings

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation + tactical specificity -- two days after the federal lawsuit naming MRED, NAR published three resources clarifying Policy 8.5 and the Clear Cooperation Policy. The script names the exact allowed and prohibited filtering categories agents should test their own MLS against, and the specific check to run on IDX and VOW rules this week.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All, brokers and listing-focused agents especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Script (~66 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:09)
NAR just spelled out the rules for how your MLS can display and filter listings. Two days ago. Most agents haven't read them.

### CONTEXT (0:09-0:31)
Three guides clarifying Policy 8.5. MLSs can filter listings by objective criteria. Geography. Price. Property type. They cannot filter by agent or brokerage name. They cannot filter by compensation. And clear cooperation got a sharper line on when off-MLS broker conversations count as public marketing.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:31-0:48)
This isn't NAR housekeeping. If your MLS uses any of those filters, your MLS just got told to stop. The private-exclusive playbook also got narrower. The moment a broker-to-broker conversation crosses into marketing, the listing has to be in the MLS within twenty-four hours.

### CLOSE (0:46-1:06)
Here's what you do now. This week, pull your MLS's IDX and VOW rules. Check two things. Whether users can filter by agent or brokerage name. And whether they can filter by compensation. If either, flag it to leadership. And if you've been running private exclusives, read the clear cooperation clarifier.

  - Source: Real Estate News 2026-05-29 and NAR MLS Policy 8.5. Objective criteria must "not explicitly and/or directly target any particular brokerage and/or agent by name."
  - Status: confirmed

  - Source: Real Estate News 2026-05-29. The third NAR resource explains when a one-to-one broker-to-broker communication would and would not trigger NAR's Clear Cooperation Policy. CCP requires listings to be entered into the MLS within one business day (commonly framed as 24 hours) of being publicly marketed.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Note: NAR's clarifier acknowledges some one-to-one broker communications do NOT trigger CCP. The script captures the agent-relevant edge ("the moment it crosses into marketing") without overclaiming a blanket prohibition.
