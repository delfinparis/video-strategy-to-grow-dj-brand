# Your "Be Careful" Speech Isn't Enough

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + cost-of-inaction -- real estate wire fraud just hit a record $275 million across 12,368 FBI-reported victims last year, AI is making the scam emails more convincing every month, and the average agent's "be careful" warning at the start of a transaction is the only protection most buyers ever get. The script gives the exact-words protocol every agent should be handing buyers at onboarding.**

## Script (~70 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:11)
Wire fraud in real estate closings just hit a record two hundred seventy-five million dollars last year. One in five buyers gets a suspicious message during closing. Most agents say "be careful" and assume that's enough. It isn't.

### CONTEXT (0:11-0:28)
The FBI logged twelve thousand victims last year, up sharply from the year before. Average loss per victim is over twenty thousand dollars. AI is making the scam emails more convincing every month. CertifID calls the trend epidemic.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:28-0:44)
The scam doesn't target you. It targets your client. They lose the down payment. They lose the closing. Then they call you asking why you didn't warn them harder. And the answer is, you said "be careful" once and moved on.

### CLOSE (0:44-1:08)
Here's what you do now. At every new buyer onboarding this week, hand them a one-page wire fraud sheet. Say this. "I will never send you wiring instructions by email or text. If you ever get any, call me first at this number before you move money." Then give them your number. Written. That's the only protocol that actually protects your client.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Wire fraud against home buyers just hit a record two hundred seventy-five million dollars last year. The FBI logged twelve thousand victims."
  - Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) annual report (2025 data, released spring 2026), as covered by NAR magazine "Online Real Estate Fraud Climbed to $275M in 2025, FBI Says" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/online-real-estate-fraud-climbed-to-275m-in-2025-fbi-says)) and Florida Realtors "Real Estate Fraud Losses Hit $275M" ([link](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/04/real-estate-fraud-losses-hit-275m)). FBI-reported real-estate-related fraud losses in 2025: $275M+, 12,368 victims (up from 9,359 victims / $173M in 2024 and 9,521 victims / $145M in 2023).
  - Who was measured: real-estate-related cybercrime complaints filed to the FBI IC3 in calendar year 2025.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "One in five buyers gets a suspicious message during closing"
  - Source: CertifID, "2026 State of Wire Fraud" report. More than 1 in 5 consumers reported receiving suspicious communications during a real estate closing. Coverage corroborated via NAR magazine "1 in 5 Targeted: How to Prepare for Rising Scam Threats" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/1-in-5-targeted-how-to-prepare-for-rising-scam-threats)).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Average loss per victim is over twenty thousand dollars"
  - Source: Computed from FBI IC3 2025 figures above ($275M / 12,368 victims = $22,232 per victim). Standard math on cited inputs.
  - Status: confirmed (derived from sourced data)

- **Claim:** "AI is making the scam emails more convincing every month"
  - Source: FBI / NAR coverage notes AI enables more convincing synthetic content (impersonation emails, voice cloning, deepfaked attorney calls). Standard cybersecurity-industry framing.
  - Status: confirmed (general framing across the cited sources)

- **Claim:** "CertifID calls the trend epidemic"
  - Source: CertifID "2026 State of Wire Fraud" report uses the word "epidemic" to characterize the rise in real estate cybercrime. Corroborated in NAR magazine coverage.
  - Status: confirmed (direct word from CertifID report)

- **Claim (the exact-words client script):** "I will never send you wiring instructions by email or text. If you ever get any, call me first at this number before you move money."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended client protocol. The "verified phone number" approach is the industry-standard wire-fraud prevention practice recommended by FBI IC3, NAR, ALTA, and CertifID.
  - Status: tactical guidance / industry-standard protocol

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
Wire fraud against home buyers just hit a record $275 million last year. One in five of your closing buyers will get a suspicious message during the transaction. Most agents say "be careful" once at the start and assume that's enough.

It isn't.

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center logged 12,368 real estate fraud victims in 2025, up from 9,359 in 2024 and 9,521 in 2023. Average loss per victim is roughly $22,000.

CertifID's 2026 State of Wire Fraud report calls the trend "epidemic." AI is making the scam emails more convincing every month. Some of these now include voice-cloned calls impersonating closing attorneys.

Here is the part most agents miss.

The scam does not target you. It targets your client. They lose the down payment. They lose the closing. Then they call you asking why you did not warn them harder. And the answer is, you said "be careful" once and moved on.

Hand every new buyer a one-page wire fraud sheet at onboarding. Say this.

"I will never send you wiring instructions by email or text. If you ever get any, call me first at this number before you move money."

Then give them the number. Written. On paper. Saved in their phone as a contact.

That is the only protocol that actually protects your client. And it is the only protocol that protects you when their attorney comes asking what warning you gave.
