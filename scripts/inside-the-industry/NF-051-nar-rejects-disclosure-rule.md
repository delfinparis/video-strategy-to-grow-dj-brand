# NAR Just Voted Against Telling Clients What You Don't Know

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation -- NAR's Professional Standards Committee spent two years drafting a code-of-ethics rule that would require agents to disclose when they don't know a property type or geographic area. NAR's Executive Committee debated it for an hour on June 17 and sent it back to committee. Translation: it's dead. The script reframes the vote as the worst possible governance signal at the worst possible consumer-protection moment, and hands the agent the voluntary version they can use at their next consult.**

## Script (~68 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
NAR's Executive Committee just blocked a rule that would require agents to disclose when they don't know a market or property type. Two years to draft it. One hour to kill it.

### CONTEXT (0:10-0:30)
The Professional Standards Committee spent two years building this. The rule was simple. If you don't have the knowledge or skill for a property type or geographic area, tell your client. NAR's Executive Committee debated it for an hour on June seventeenth and sent it back to committee. Translation. It's dead.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:30-0:48)
The post-Sitzer consumer-protection era is the worst possible moment for the trade association to kill a transparency rule its own committee drafted. The argument against was that new agents would be hurt. The argument for was that clients deserve to know. The clients heard you.

### CLOSE (0:48-1:06)
Here's what you do now. Be the agent who makes the disclosure NAR wouldn't mandate. At your next buyer or listing consult, say this. "I haven't sold a condo in the last twelve months. If you want someone who has, I'll refer you. If you want me, here's how I'm going to get up to speed." That's the rule NAR voted not to make. It's still the right one.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "NAR's Executive Committee just voted down a rule that would require agents to disclose when they don't know a market or property type."
  - Source: Real Estate News, "NAR rejects proposal to disclose Realtor knowledge gaps," 2026-06-19 ([link](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/19/nar-rejects-proposal-to-disclose-realtor-knowledge-gaps)). The NAR Professional Standards Committee proposed adding a Standard of Practice under Article 11 of the Code of Ethics requiring Realtors to "disclose to their clients if they do not possess the knowledge, information, or skills about a property type or geographical area to protect and promote the interests of their clients." NAR's Executive Committee referred the proposal back to committee on 2026-06-17 after roughly an hour of debate, the procedural equivalent of a no vote.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "The Professional Standards Committee spent two years building this."
  - Source: Real Estate News (above). Article notes the committee "spent two years" crafting the proposal. Committee chair B.J. Harris and immediate past chair Todd Beckstrom both spoke in favor.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Debated it for an hour on June seventeenth."
  - Source: Real Estate News (above). 2026 NAR President Kevin Brown confirmed the Executive Committee "spent nearly an hour debating the proposal on June 17."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "The argument against was that new agents would be hurt."
  - Source: Real Estate News (above). Opponents argued the proposal was too vague on when and how disclosure would be required, and that new agents would be at a disadvantage.
  - Status: confirmed (paraphrase of the article's framing)

- **Claim:** "The post-Sitzer consumer-protection era is the worst possible moment..."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's editorial reading. The Sitzer / Burnett verdict (Oct 2023, $1.78B) and the NAR settlement (March 2024, $418M plus buyer-representation reforms) put the industry in a multi-year consumer-protection cycle. State AGs, the DOJ, and private-listings legislation all sit downstream. Voting down a transparency rule in this window is a defensible reading of "bad optics."
  - Status: editorial framing, not a stat

- **Claim (tactical):** "I haven't sold a condo in the last twelve months. If you want someone who has, I'll refer you. If you want me, here's how I'm going to get up to speed."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended voluntary-disclosure protocol. Mirrors the spirit of the proposed Standard of Practice and matches the industry-standard "specialist referral" practice already common at high-producing teams.
  - Status: tactical guidance
