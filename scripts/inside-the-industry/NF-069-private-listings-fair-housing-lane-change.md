# You're a Fiduciary. Here's What's Actually at Stake.

> **WOW: Contrarian take + surprising statistic -- for over a year this fight has been argued as antitrust, which makes it a corporate problem with corporate lawyers. In the first week of August it changed lanes. The NAACP's president called private listings the next fight for fair housing in Time on August 4, and Warren told Compass and MRED on August 6 that their partnership "increases risks of civil rights violations." Zillow's own survey is the receipt: almost three in four Black and Hispanic sellers were steered toward a private listing network versus about one in four white sellers. Antitrust liability sits with the company. The duty to the seller sits with the licensee, and NAR's own guidance says failing to explain what a limited marketing plan does can violate state license law and the Code of Ethics.**

## Script (~78 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
You're a fiduciary. Nobody's used that word on you since your licensing class. And it's about to matter more than the lawsuit Compass is fighting.

### THE LANE CHANGE (0:12-0:34)
All year, private listings have been an antitrust fight. Zillow's lawsuit. A House subcommittee. Corporate problem, corporate lawyers. Then it moved. August fourth, the NAACP's president wrote in Time that private listings are the next fight for fair housing. Two days later Senator Warren told Compass and MRED their deal raises the risk of civil rights violations.

### THE RECEIPT (0:34-0:54)
The numbers underneath that are Zillow's. Almost three in four Black and Hispanic sellers said their agent recommended a private listing network. Among white sellers, about one in four. Off-MLS sellers in those zip codes lost around $9,850 a sale. In majority-white zip codes, $3,700.

### WHAT'S AT STAKE FOR YOU (0:54-1:10)
Here's what's at stake for you. Antitrust names companies. Fair housing and your license name you. And NAR's own guidance says it plainly. Failing to promote your client's best interest can violate state license law and the Code of Ethics. So can failing to explain what a limited marketing plan actually does.

### CLOSE (1:10-1:26)
Here's what you do now. Pull every listing where you recommended private or delayed marketing. Look for written proof, in the seller's own words, that they saw the exposure tradeoff and chose it anyway. Not a company form. If it's not in the file, it didn't happen. Your brokerage has lawyers for the antitrust case. You have a fiduciary duty. Nobody can hire that out for you.

You are a fiduciary. Nobody has used that word on you since your licensing class, and it is about to matter more than the lawsuit Compass is fighting.

All year, private listings have been argued as an antitrust fight. Zillow's federal suit against MRED and Compass. The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee's July letters. Those are corporate problems, and corporations have lawyers.

Then it changed lanes.

On August 4, NAACP President Derrick Johnson wrote in Time that private listings are the next fight for fair housing. On August 6, Senator Elizabeth Warren wrote to Compass CEO Robert Reffkin and MRED CEO Rebecca Jensen and said their partnership "increases risks of civil rights violations." She posed 23 questions and set a response deadline of August 21.

The evidence underneath that is Zillow's own research, published March 2025. Among sellers surveyed, 74% of Hispanic sellers and 73% of Black sellers said their agent recommended listing on a private listing network. Among white sellers, 24% said the same. Homes sold off the MLS in majority-Black, Hispanic, Asian American, Pacific Islander and Native American ZIP codes lost $9,850 per sale, compared to about $3,700 in majority-white areas.

Nobody is saying those agents meant harm. That is not how this works. Antitrust liability attaches to the company. Fair housing liability and license discipline attach to the individual.

And NAR's own guidance on pocket and coming-soon listings already says it. A failure to promote the client's best interest, or to explain the effect of a limited marketing plan, can violate state real estate license law and the REALTOR Code of Ethics.

Meanwhile New York's Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listings Act (S.10274) passed both houses in June and sits on Governor Hochul's desk. It would require a signed seller acknowledgment that limiting public exposure "may reduce the number of offers." When a state that size moves, Springfield notices.

Here is what to do this week.

Pull every listing where you recommended private or delayed marketing. Look for one thing in the file: written proof, in the seller's own words, that they saw the exposure tradeoff and chose it anyway. Not a company form with a signature line. The seller's decision, documented.

If it is not in the file, it did not happen.

Your brokerage has lawyers for the antitrust case. You have a fiduciary duty. Nobody can hire that out for you.
