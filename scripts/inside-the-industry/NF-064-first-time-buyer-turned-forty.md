# The First-Time Homebuyer Just Turned 40

> **WOW: Surprising statistic (criterion 4) plus a frame shift (criterion 7). NAR's 2025 Profile put the median first-time buyer age at a record 40, and their share of the market at 21 percent, the smallest since NAR started tracking in 1981. Almost every buyer presentation in this business was built for a thirty-year-old. The stat isn't the payload. The payload is that a forty-year-old first-timer isn't nervous, they're outbid, and the pitch built to calm nerves solves the wrong problem.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Named Stakes | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Avatar:** Buyer's agents

## Script (~72 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
The first-time homebuyer just turned forty. That's NAR's newest number, and it's a record. If you're still running the nervous-rookie pitch, you're pitching somebody who doesn't exist.

### MIRROR (0:10-0:18)
You know the pitch. Somebody built it for a thirty-year-old. Explain earnest money. Slow it down. Point them at a starter home.

### TURN (0:18-0:32)
Here's where it gets worse. They're not nervous. They're outbid. First-timers put ten percent down. The repeat buyers beating them put down twenty-three, and three in ten pay cash.

### FIX (0:32-0:52)
Most agents lead with how the process works. The ones winning these buyers lead with the competition. Pull the last ten sales in their price band. Show what each sold for, what they put down, and whether it was cash.

### REFRAME (0:52-1:00)
First-time buyers are down to twenty-one percent of the market, the smallest share NAR has recorded since nineteen eighty-one.

### CLOSE (1:00-1:12)
Here's what you do now. Open the last email you sent a first-time buyer. If it led with a process explainer, rebuild it before your next showing. Forty-year-olds don't need the process explained. They need to know what they're up against.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "The first-time homebuyer just turned forty. That's NAR's newest number, and it's a record."
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS, 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, released 2025-11-04. Median first-time buyer age 40, described by NAR as a record high.
  - Who was measured: consumers. Household survey of people who bought a primary residence between July 2024 and June 2025.
  - Status: confirmed. https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40

- **Claim:** "Somebody built it for a thirty-year-old."
  - Source: NAR 2025 Profile coverage. First-time buyer median age has climbed incrementally from age 30 in 2010.
  - Who was measured: consumers, same survey series.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "First-timers put ten percent down."
  - Source: NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers. Median down payment for first-time buyers, 10 percent.
  - Who was measured: consumers, July 2024 to June 2025 buyers.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "The repeat buyers beating them put down twenty-three, and three in ten pay cash."
  - Source: NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers. Repeat buyer median down payment 23 percent; 30 percent of repeat buyers paid all cash.
  - Who was measured: consumers, same survey. Both figures come from the same publication, stated as two separate facts about the same cohort, not combined into a causal claim.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "First-time buyers are down to twenty-one percent of the market, the smallest share NAR has recorded since nineteen eighty-one."
  - Source: NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers. First-time buyer share 21 percent, the lowest since NAR began tracking in 1981.
  - Who was measured: consumers, same survey.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Note on the hook framing:** the original brief line "still pitching them like a nervous millennial stretching their budget" was cut. A 40-year-old buyer in 2026 is a millennial, so the line contradicts itself. The script uses the thirty-year-old pitch reference instead, which is what the NAR age series actually supports.

- **Editorial claim (not a stat):** "They're not nervous. They're outbid." This is D.J. Paris's read on the data, stated as a view, not as a survey finding. It rests on the down-payment and all-cash gap cited above.

**Pattern interrupt:** Push-In. Hold normal framing, then bring the stick in a foot closer on the word forty and hold it through "somebody who doesn't exist," then settle back. Breaks the recent Stop / Walk-Toward run and puts the physical move on the number, which is the whole hook. One-handed, mid-stride, no rig.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): "just turned forty" reads as a person, not a data point, which opens the Huh before the Aha. The Curse of Knowledge risk was assuming agents know the number moved. They don't, so the first line delivers the fact and the consequence lands second.
- Share/save driver (Berger): high Social Currency. An agent who reshares this looks like the one who reads the NAR profile. Retells in one sentence without losing the point, which is the valuable-virality test: "first-time buyers are forty now and they're getting outbid by cash."
- Retention move (MrBeast): the "here's where it gets worse" turn at 0:18 lands right at the documented drift point, and it escalates rather than restates. One payoff, delivered at 0:32, abrupt end at the loop-back.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Hormozi says the fix is one beat too abstract. "Show three columns" is a task, not a reward, and the viewer never sees what the columns do to the conversation. The experiment: replace the FIX beat with the literal sentence an agent says while showing it, and cut the REFRAME stat to make room. Same hook, same close, tactical payoff swapped for verbatim language. Hypothesis: the verbatim version saves better on Instagram, the current version reshares better on LinkedIn.
