# I've Interviewed 700 Real Estate Agents. The Top Producers Never Say This One Word.

**Pillar:** Inside the Industry | **Type:** Synthesis (Type B)
**Primary Platform:** LinkedIn | **Post Date:** Wednesday, May 20, 2026

> **Strongest Line:** "Top producers have stopped talking about their job and started talking about their clients."

## Script (45-60 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:08)
I have interviewed over 700 real estate agents on my podcast. After a while, you start to notice patterns. Here is the one that changed how I listen to everything. [ON-SCREEN: "700 interviews. 1 word top producers never say."]

### SETUP (0:08-0:20)
I pay attention to pronouns. It sounds small, but pronouns are a tell. When I ask an agent to describe their best deal of the year, there is a word the top producers almost never use. [ON-SCREEN: "Watch the pronouns."]

### REVEAL (0:20-0:40)
The word is "I."

Mid-producers say things like, "I negotiated a great price. I handled the inspection issues. I closed it in thirty days."

Top producers say things like, "They had a baby on the way. She walked into the house and I watched her exhale for the first time in two months. The sellers were grieving a loss. We figured out a way through it."

Same transaction. Completely different subject. [ON-SCREEN: "Same deal. Different subject."]

### INSIGHT (0:40-0:52)
Top producers have stopped talking about their job and started talking about their clients. The shift from "I" to "they" is not humility for its own sake. It is a symptom of where their attention goes during the work. And where your attention goes during the work is what the client actually feels when they refer you.

### CLOSE (0:52-0:60)
That's one pattern from 700 interviews. There's a whole catalog.

**Estimated Duration:** ~58 seconds

## Data Source
- Synthesis from 700+ episodes of Keeping It Real Podcast
- Pattern observed across Paul Salter, Kristee Leonard, Carrie McCormick, Garrett Maroon, Bari Mill, Jay Cannone, and dozens of other top-producer guests
- Counter-pattern observed across Stuck Intermediate and Forgotten Middle guests
- D.J. Paris direct observation on language and pronoun usage in client-story framing

## Social Media

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
I have interviewed over 700 real estate agents on the Keeping It Real Podcast. After a while, you start to notice patterns. Here is the one that changed how I listen to everything.

I pay attention to pronouns.

It sounds small, but pronouns are a tell. When I ask an agent to describe their best deal of the year, there is a word the top producers almost never use.

The word is "I."

Mid-producers say things like, "I negotiated a great price. I handled the inspection issues. I closed it in thirty days."

Top producers say things like, "They had a baby on the way. She walked into the house and I watched her exhale for the first time in two months. The sellers were grieving a loss. We figured out a way through it."

Same transaction. Completely different subject.

Top producers have stopped talking about their job and started talking about their clients. The shift from "I" to "they" is not humility for its own sake. It is a symptom of where their attention goes during the work. And where your attention goes during the work is what the client actually feels when they refer you.

This is why top producers get referrals and mid-producers get occasional repeat business. It is not because their skills are dramatically different. It is because their clients remember feeling seen, not watched.

Here is the exercise. Record yourself describing your best deal of the last year. Count the "I"s. Count the "they"s. The ratio is a diagnostic.

That is one pattern from 700 interviews. There is a whole catalog.
