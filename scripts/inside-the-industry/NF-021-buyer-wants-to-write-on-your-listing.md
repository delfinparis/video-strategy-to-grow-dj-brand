# Your Buyer Wants to Write on Your Listing. Here's the Exact Line.

> **WOW: Tactical specificity plus surprising statistic. Zillow's May 14, 2026 study put a dollar number on dual-agency seller harm: $2,165 per home, $1.49 billion across three years. Most agents will face the dual-agency situation -- buyer wants to write on listing agent's listing -- multiple times this year. Most don't have a script for it. The deliverable in this script is the exact line the agent says when the situation comes up. The line protects the seller (no documented price loss), the agent (no fiduciary conflict), and the deal (the buyer still gets the home with separate representation). Builds on NF-018 and NF-019, but the deliverable is the verbatim words.**

## Script (~57 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:08)
Your buyer wants to write on your listing. Here's the exact line that saves you and your seller.

### CONTEXT (0:08-0:18)
Zillow released a study on May fourteenth. Sellers in dual-agency deals lose two thousand one hundred sixty-five dollars per home. One and a half billion across three years. Most agents will face this situation a dozen times this year. Most don't have a script for it.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:18-0:28)
The "I'll just bring my own buyer" pitch is the most expensive favor a listing agent does for a seller. The buyer is happy. The agent gets two commissions. The seller pays. With this study on the record, the seller will eventually find out.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:28-0:50)
Here's the exact line. Memorize it.

"I represent the seller. To protect both of us, I'm going to refer you to a buyer's agent in my office. They'll work with you. I'll work with the seller. Same deal. Cleaner protection. The data shows sellers in dual deals leave money on the table. I won't put my client in that position."

That's the script. Three sentences. Refer the buyer out. Keep the listing. Document it.

### CLOSE (0:50-0:57)
Two commissions or one client. Pick one. Then write the line down before the next call comes in.

**Estimated Duration:** ~57 seconds

Compressing the verbatim shortens the segment but loses the actual asset the agent walks away with. The 15-second re-engagement lands on "Most don't have a script for it" closing the CONTEXT beat. The close re-callbacks the title's binary choice and adds a planning prompt ("write the line down before the next call comes in"). This script is intentionally tactical-first; the data context is supporting evidence for the line, not the segment's main beat.

## Production Notes

- **The verbatim line is the deliverable.** This is the same structural pattern as NF-016 (the Fed-chair-question script). The agent should be able to repeat the line verbatim from memory after one viewing. If a beat in the line trips the agent's tongue in delivery, simplify it before filming.
- **Optional adjustment for solo agents.** The drafted line assumes the listing agent has buyer's agents in their office to refer to. If filming the script for an audience of solo agents or small-team brokerages, swap "in my office" for "in my referral network" -- same logic, broader application.

  - Source: same Zillow research release ($1.49B aggregate seller losses 2023-2025)
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "Most agents will face this situation a dozen times this year"
  - Editorial framing of how often the dual-agency scenario presents itself for an active listing agent. The "dozen" is illustrative shorthand, not a measured frequency. An agent doing 20-40 listings per year will face this more often; a part-time agent less often. The framing is intentionally directional, not numeric, and is stated in the context as "most" to allow for the variance.
  - Status: editorial color, not a measured stat

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "Most don't have a script for it"
  - Editorial observation about the gap between how often the situation arises and how systematically agents prepare for it. Defensible against the broader pattern that agency-disclosure conversations are typically improvised at the table rather than scripted in advance.
  - Status: editorial framing, not a measured stat

- **The verbatim line itself:** "I represent the seller. To protect both of us, I'm going to refer you to a buyer's agent in my office. They'll work with you. I'll work with the seller. Same deal. Cleaner protection. The data shows sellers in dual deals leave money on the table. I won't put my client in that position."
  - Status: editorial / coaching script, not a quoted statement from a third party. The line is constructed for the script and tested against the standard agency-fiduciary framework (listing agent's primary duty is to the seller; dual representation requires informed consent in most states; the Zillow data provides the documented basis for the "won't put my client in that position" framing).
  - Editorial note: the line is deliberately direct. An agent who softens it ("I'd really love to help you, but...") loses the protection it provides. Filming the line should match the line's directness.
