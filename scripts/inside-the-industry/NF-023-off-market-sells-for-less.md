# Off-Market Sells for Less. The Number Is $1.3 Billion.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic plus mindset reframe. Zillow's May 14, 2026 study analyzed 6.2 million qualifying U.S. transactions from 2023 to 2025 and found that homes sold off the MLS -- private listings, pocket listings, "coming soon" exclusives -- consistently sold for less than homes that hit the MLS first. Total seller losses: $1.3 billion over three years. The pattern showed up every year of the study. The mechanism is structural: off-market marketing limits the buyer pool, which limits competition, which limits price. The take: the "exclusive" framing brokerages use to pitch private listings is marketing. The math is a discount. This script is the consumer-and-agent-facing data educator take, complementary to NF-020's combined dual-agency/off-MLS thesis and NF-022's regime-change framing.**

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "$1.3 billion in seller losses over three years"
  - Source: Zillow research release, "Selling with one agent on both sides cost home sellers $1.49 billion over three years," 2026-05-14 ([link](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-14-Selling-with-one-agent-on-both-sides-cost-home-sellers-1-49-billion-over-three-years))
  - The study reports two parallel findings on the same dataset: $1.49B dual-agency aggregate and $1.3B off-MLS aggregate. NF-023 focuses on the off-MLS finding.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Zillow analyzed six-point-two million transactions"
  - Source: same Zillow research release. The off-MLS analysis used 6.2 million qualifying transactions (the dual-agency analysis used 6.8 million; both drew from the broader 15-million-transaction dataset).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Off-MLS homes sold for less than MLS-first homes. Every year."
  - Source: same Zillow research release: "The price penalties from dual agency and off-MLS listings have appeared in every year Zillow has analyzed."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "The mechanism is simple. Off-market limits the buyer pool. Fewer buyers, lower price."
  - Editorial reframe of the documented price effect. The buyer-pool restriction mechanism is consistent with the multi-year, large-dataset price differential and is the explanation most often cited in industry coverage of the study. The script presents this as the explanation, not as a separately-proven causal claim.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by source data

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "The 'exclusive' framing is marketing. The math is discount"
  - Editorial reframe / shareable moment. Defensible against the documented price differential. The "exclusive" pitch is widely used by brokerages with private listing networks; the math from the Zillow study contradicts the premium implied by that framing.
  - Status: editorial color, not a quoted statistic
