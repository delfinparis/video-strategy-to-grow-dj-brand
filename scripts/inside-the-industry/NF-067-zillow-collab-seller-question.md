# Your Seller Isn't Going to Ask Who Won

> **WOW: Mindset reframe + memorable one-liner. Reframe -- every Chicago agent has spent three months braced for a ruling, and the question their seller actually asks has nothing to do with who won. One-liner -- "Your seller isn't going to ask who won. They're going to ask if buyers can still find their house." Designed to move a listing agent from following a court case to fixing a page in their listing presentation.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Mirror | **Heat:** 2.5 | **Avatars:** Chicago listing agents first
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Stop (stop walking mid-stride on the first line). No demo.

## Script (~70 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:07)
Your seller's going to text you about this before you've finished reading it yourself. The Chicago listing ruling just landed. Here's what you say back.

### STORY (0:07-0:28)
I've been covering this case since May. Every agent question I get sounds the same. What happens if it goes the wrong way.

Your seller's question is different. Can buyers still find my house?

### BRANCH -- shoot both, use one (0:28-0:38)

**[BRANCH A -- if the ruling goes Zillow's way]**
Today, the answer is yes. Your listing goes out, buyers see it, nothing changes for the person who hired you. But that question doesn't disappear when the headlines do.

**[BRANCH B -- if it doesn't]**
Today, that answer has more moving parts than it did yesterday. Which means the conversation you have with your seller this week does more work than it's ever done. That question doesn't disappear when the headlines do.

### TURN (0:38-0:45)
And most listing agreements in this market have never answered it in writing.

### PAYOFF (0:45-1:03)
Here's what you do now. Before your next listing appointment, add one page. Every place their home shows up. Portals. Syndication. Private networks. Named, on the agreement they sign.

Not because a court said so. Because that's the question they were always going to ask.

### LOOP-BACK (1:03-1:08)
Your seller's going to text you about this. Now they've had your answer since the day they hired you.

**Estimated Duration:** ~69 seconds (Branch A, 165 spoken words) / ~72 seconds (Branch B, 173 spoken words). Both under the Inside the Industry 75-second hard cap.

## Council Review

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, A/B options mapped to hook families):**
1. "Your seller's going to text you about this before you've finished reading it yourself." [Mirror | emotion: recognition plus mild dread] -- current pick
2. "Your seller isn't going to ask who won." [Mirror / Forbidden blend | emotion: correction, "wait, they're not?"] -- the sharpest line in the script, used as the title. Testing it as the opener trades the loop-open for a harder stop.
3. "The Chicago listing ruling landed this morning. Your seller has one question and it isn't the one you've been worrying about." [Named Stakes | emotion: time pressure plus redirect]

**Pattern interrupt:** The Stop. D.J. is mid-walk and stops on "Your seller's going to text you about this." The physical interruption is the same shape as the thing being described, which is a text landing mid-day. Break from Push-In (NF-064, NF-066). Selfie-stick friendly: stop, hold the frame for the hook, resume walking on "I've been covering this case since May."

**Why it should work:**
- **Hook mechanism (Heath curiosity gap):** the hook opens the gap on the *viewer's* behalf, not the news's. "You're about to get a text you don't have an answer for" is a personal-consequence loop, and it closes with a page they can actually write. No prior arc knowledge required, which matters because Zillow amplification puts this in front of people who've never seen NF-057 through NF-066.
- **Share/save driver (Berger):** Practical Value, which is the strongest share driver for professional audiences. "Add one page to your listing presentation" is forwardable broker-to-agent with no context needed. The one-liner carries Social Currency separately, and the two travel on different surfaces, which is why the video can be saved on IG and shared on Facebook by different people.
- **Retention move:** three redirects fire at roughly ten-second intervals. The wrong-question reveal at 0:20, the branch at 0:28, and the listing-agreement gap at 0:38. Each one moves the viewer somewhere they weren't expecting, which is what holds a news piece past the point where the news itself is delivered.

**The dissent (A/B test next):** The strongest line in this script is the title, and it never gets spoken. "Your seller isn't going to ask who won" is doing the work the hook is only gesturing at, and burying it in the frontmatter to protect the loop structure may be over-engineering. The experiment: open cold on "Your seller isn't going to ask who won," let it sit for a beat, then "They're going to ask if buyers can still find their house," and rebuild the ruling context into the second beat instead of the first. Hypothesis: the harder line wins on completion and saves; the current version wins on comments, because the loop-open invites a "wait, what ruling?" reply. Same script, different first eight seconds.
