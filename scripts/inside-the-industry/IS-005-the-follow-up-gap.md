# You Don't Have a Lead Problem. You Have a Follow-Up Problem.

> **WOW: Pattern reveal (Rule 0 criterion 3) -- after 700 interviews, the gap between top producers and everyone else isn't talent or lead volume. It's that the best agents keep following up long after everyone else has quietly filed the lead under "dead." The reframe shifts where the viewer thinks their problem lives.**

**Pillar:** Inside the Industry | **Type:** Synthesis (Type B)
**Primary Platform:** LinkedIn | **Post Date:** Saturday, May 30, 2026

## Shareable Moment
> "You don't have a lead problem. You have a follow-up problem. The leads are already in your phone. You just stopped texting them."

## Full Script (Spoken) (~60 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:09)
I've never practiced real estate. But I've interviewed over 700 agents who have. And the one thing that separates the top producers from everyone else isn't talent. It's that they keep following up long after the rest of us would've quit.

### MIRROR MOMENT (0:09-0:21)
Here's the part that stings. You get a lead. You reach out once, maybe twice, you hear nothing back, and somewhere in your head you quietly file that person under dead. I do the same thing in my own inbox, so I'm not judging. But that lead isn't dead. You just stopped.

### INSIGHT (0:21-0:43)
After 700 conversations, the pattern is almost embarrassingly consistent. Garrett Maroon told me he runs his whole business off relationships, not new leads. Kristee Leonard, a fifty-million-dollar producer, still answers her own phone. Karina Chavez put it best. Always answer, because you never know what you're going to get. None of that is a tactic. It's a refusal to disappear.

### REFRAME (0:43-0:53)
Most agents think they have a lead problem. They don't. They have a follow-up problem. The leads are already in your phone. You just stopped texting them.

### CLOSE (0:53-0:60)
Here's what you do now. Find five people you messaged once and never circled back on. Send each one a real note today. Not a blast. A note. That's the whole play.

**Estimated Duration:** ~60 seconds
