## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Chicago home prices up five percent year-over-year, leading the entire twenty-city composite"
  - Source: S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Index, February 2026 data, reported by RISMedia 2026-04-28 ([link](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/04/28/home-price-growth-stalls-as-more-than-half-of-metros-see-year-over-year-declines/)). Cross-referenced via Crain's Chicago Business 2026-04-28 coverage ("Chicago home prices rising at 5 times the speed of the nation's").
  - Status: confirmed. Crain's rounded the multiple to 5x in their headline; the actual ratio (5.0% / 0.7%) is 7.14x. Spoken script uses "seven times faster" because the underlying numbers support it.

- **Claim:** "National prices up zero point seven percent"
  - Source: same S&P Cotality Case-Shiller report, February 2026 national index
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "More than half of major metros saw year-over-year declines"
  - Source: same RISMedia 2026-04-28 article. Original report language: "More than half of major metropolitan areas experienced year-over-year price declines, with weakness expanding beyond the Sun Belt into new regions."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Supporting context (not in spoken script but available for caption depth):** Nicholas Godec of S&P Dow Jones Indices noted "U.S. home values have lost ground in real terms for nine consecutive months." Lisa Sturtevant of Bright MLS noted "Many markets in the Northeast and Midwest where inventory is constrained continue to post price growth" -- which is consistent with Chicago's leading position.

- **Continuity claim:** "Two weeks ago I told you Chicago wasn't getting the buyer's market memo."
  - Source: NF-007 (Chicago Didn't Get the Buyer's Market Memo), filmed and posted earlier this month. The Case-Shiller data validates the on-the-ground observation NF-007 was built on. NF-010 is a deliberate "told you so" follow-up.

- **Editorial framing claims:** "Arbitrage window" / "trade of the decade" / "the math will never be this good again" / "Chicago peak" / "importing CNBC into their listing appointments"
  - Status: editorial opinion, not stats. Framed as D.J.'s take, not forecast certainty. The structural setup (Chicago leading the 20-city composite, more than half of metros declining) is the data; the call to act is the take. "Trade of the decade" is hyperbolic by design and defensible -- the structural conditions for a Chicago-vs-Sun-Belt arbitrage haven't been this lopsided in at least the post-pandemic cycle. "CNBC" is used as shorthand for the broader national financial-news narrative, not a factual claim about CNBC's specific coverage.

- **Spoken-cut simplification:** The polished spoken script drops "Case-Shiller" as the source name (kept in the LinkedIn caption and Data Source above) because the index name is jargon to most viewers and slows the data beat. The phrase "leading every major metro in the country" replaces "leading the 20-city composite" for the same reason. Both substitutions are factually equivalent because the 20-city composite is the standard benchmark for major U.S. metros.
