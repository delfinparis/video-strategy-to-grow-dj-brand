# Two Branches of Government Are Deciding Who Controls Your Listings

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation -- as of Aug 6, two separate branches of the federal government are unresolved on the same question at the same time. Judge John Tharp's preliminary injunction ruling in Zillow v. MRED and Compass is under advisement (weeks to months). The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee's Aug 5 briefing deadline for MRED and Compass passed with no public outcome. Almost every agent is watching one front; almost none noticed both are open. The script names the convergence and hands the listing agent the exact sentence to say to a seller while the rules are being rewritten.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Earned Observation | **Heat:** 3 | **Avatars:** Listing agents everywhere, Chicago first
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.
**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat -- two fingers up on "Two branches," one finger on "One question." Physicalizes the count in the first 3 seconds.

## Shareable Moment
> "The rules on your listings are being written in a courtroom and in Congress at the same time."

## Script (~72 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
Two branches of the federal government are deciding who controls your listings right now. A judge in Chicago. A committee in Congress. Neither has answered. Here's what's at stake while you wait.

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:36)
Start with the courtroom. Zillow sued MRED and Compass in May. Judge John Tharp got the final briefs July thirteenth and he's still deciding. Weeks, maybe months. Then, while everyone watched the courtroom, Congress opened a second front. The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee gave MRED and Compass until August fifth to explain the same deal. That was yesterday. No public answer.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:36-0:54)
Two branches. One question. Do private listing networks lock buyers out? They could answer it differently. The court could restrict access. Congress could force everything open. So there's no clean date when this gets simple. Every listing you take until then runs on rules being rewritten right now. Your seller doesn't know that. You should.

### CLOSE (0:54-1:12)
Here's what you do now. When a seller asks to keep their home off the portals, say this. The rules on private listings are being decided right now, in court and in Congress. So we protect you in writing. We spell out exactly where your home will and won't show up, and revisit it the day either one rules. That puts the call on paper before a judge or a committee makes it for you.

**Estimated Duration:** ~72 seconds | **Word Count:** ~223 words

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Zillow sued MRED and Compass in May."
  - Source: Zillow filed its antitrust complaint against MRED and Compass on 2026-05-12 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois. Confirmed in NF-057, NF-058; timeline in HousingWire, "A timeline of Zillow's listing war with MRED and Compass" -- https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-injunction-mred-idx-vow/
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Judge John Tharp got the final briefs July thirteenth and he's still deciding. Weeks, maybe months."
  - Source: Two-day preliminary injunction hearing before Judge John Tharp Jr. ran July 1-2, 2026 (RISMedia, 2026-07-02 -- https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/02/zillow-mred-compass-lawsuit-day-two-testimony-reffkin-jensen/). Simultaneous post-hearing briefs July 9, responses July 13 (RISMedia, 2026-07-13 -- https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/13/mred-and-compass-argue-zillow-not-entitled-to-court-order-in-post-hearing-brief/). Ruling under advisement as of early August; reporting notes it "may take weeks if not months" (HousingWire, "Zillow, MRED, Compass await ruling in Chicagoland MLS fight" -- https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-mred-compass-ruling/).
  - Status: confirmed (ruling pending; no ruling reported as of 2026-08-06)

- **Claim:** "The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee gave MRED and Compass until August fifth to explain the same deal."
  - Source: House Judiciary Committee's Subcommittee on the Administrative State, Regulatory Reform and Antitrust (Chairman Rep. Scott Fitzgerald, R-WI) sent letters dated 2026-07-22 to Compass CEO Robert Reffkin and MRED President/CEO Rebecca Jensen requesting staff briefings by 10 a.m. Eastern, 2026-08-05. Inman, 2026-07-22 -- https://www.inman.com/2026/07/22/congressional-house-antitrust-committee-compass-mred/ ; Real Estate News, 2026-07-22 -- https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/07/22/judiciary-subcommittee-summons-compass-mred-ceos-for-briefing ; The Real Deal, 2026-07-24 -- https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/07/24/the-house-judiciary-committee-has-private-listings-concerns/
  - Status: confirmed (multi-outlet corroboration)

- **Claim:** "That was yesterday. No public answer."
  - Source: The Aug 5 briefing deadline is documented above. As of the 2026-08-06 news brief research and a same-day web check, no outlet has reported the outcome of the briefing itself. The script asserts only that the deadline passed and no public answer has been reported -- it does not assert any outcome.
  - Status: confirmed (absence of reported outcome as of 2026-08-06; do not claim a result)

- **Claim:** "Do private listing networks lock buyers out?"
  - Source: Paraphrase of the subcommittee's stated concern -- that private listing networks may "insulate the biggest brokerages from competition at the expense of consumers" and create "velvet ropes" limiting buyer access. Direct language quoted across Inman, RISMedia, and Real Estate News coverage of the July 22 letters.
  - Status: confirmed (direct paraphrase of the subcommittee's framing)

- **Claim (context, not narrated):** "the same deal" = the MRED-Compass Private Listing Network expansion announced April 2026.
  - Source: In April 2026, MRED announced a deal with Compass to expand its Private Listing Network. That commercial arrangement is what both the Zillow suit and the House letters examine. Consistent with NF-066 sourcing.
  - Status: confirmed (industry coverage)

- **Claim (tactical -- the say-this):** "The rules on private listings are being decided right now... we spell out exactly where your home will and won't show up, and revisit it the day either one rules."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended seller-conversation script. Continuation of the written-disclosure-of-listing-distribution ethos across NF-057, NF-060, NF-063, NF-066, reframed as a spoken value-exchange the agent delivers while both proceedings are open.
  - Status: tactical guidance

**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat -- two fingers up on "Two branches," a single finger on "One question" in the WHY beat. The count is physicalized in the first 3 seconds, giving captions.ai a clean audio-synced beat and giving the eye a second stop signal after the spoken hook. One-handed, selfie-stick friendly. Breaks NF-066's Push-In.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath curiosity gap): "Two branches of government deciding who controls YOUR listings" opens a gap on the first beat -- which two, deciding what, why me -- that CONTEXT closes within 20 seconds. No prior arc knowledge required.
- Share/save driver (Berger): the convergence is genuine information asymmetry. Most agents know about the Zillow case or the Congress letter, almost none are holding both as one story. Sharing it signals Social Currency ("I see the whole board"), and the say-this line is Practical Value that survives the retell test.
- Retention move: new stakes fire roughly every 12 seconds -- court still deciding (0:12), Congress second front (0:24), they could conflict (0:40), the say-this payload (0:54) -- so there is no sitter beat before the close.

**The dissent (A/B test next):** Gary Vaynerchuk holds that "two branches of the federal government" reads as civics class, not a scroll-stop, and abstract-structure hooks under-retain against concrete-consequence ones. His experiment: open on the seller's stake instead -- "A judge in Chicago could make your seller's listing disappear from Zillow, and he could rule any day." Same script body. Hypothesis: the concrete-consequence hook wins first-3-second retention and broad reach; the convergence hook wins saves among agents already following the arc. Test both, measure saves-per-view against 3-second hold.
