# Dual Agency Hurts Sellers. The Number Is $2,165.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic plus mindset reframe. Zillow's May 14, 2026 study analyzed 6.8 million qualifying U.S. transactions from 2023 to 2025 and found that sellers whose buyer was represented by the same agent received an average of $2,165 less than sellers whose buyer had separate representation. Total seller losses: $1.49 billion over three years. The pattern showed up every year. The mechanism is structural: when one agent represents both sides, the negotiation collapses to the middle because the agent can't fight hard for either party without harming the other. The seller absorbs the gap. This is the standalone dual-agency data educator take, complementary to NF-018 (the ethics accusation), NF-019 (the legal-exposure forecast), and NF-021 (the verbatim client script).**

  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Every year of the study"
  - Source: same Zillow research release: "The price penalties from dual agency and off-MLS listings have appeared in every year Zillow has analyzed."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "The mechanism is structural conflict. When one agent represents both sides, they can't fight hard for either."
  - Editorial reframe of the documented price effect. The structural-conflict explanation is consistent with standard agency-fiduciary theory: a dual agent owes equal duties to both parties and cannot advocate maximally for either without breaching the duty owed to the other. This explanation is widely used in academic and legal commentary on dual agency.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by source data and standard agency theory

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "The negotiation collapses to the middle. The seller absorbs the gap."
  - Editorial reframe. The "collapse to the middle" framing describes the price outcome that follows from the structural conflict. Defensible against the documented $2,165 average price differential -- sellers absorb the difference between the price they would have received with adversarial representation and the price they actually receive in a dual-agency deal.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by source data
