# RE/MAX Agents Aren't Happy About Real -- Here's the 23% That Explains Why

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + agent-payroll consequence -- On the day Real Brokerage announced it was buying RE/MAX for $880M, REAX stock dropped 23% (Friday + announcement day combined, close 2026-04-27, $2.02/share). So RE/MAX agents are about to be migrated into a brokerage where part of their compensation is equity that just got marked down a quarter on day one. - **Claim:** "The 880-million-dollar deal for RE/MAX has a cash cap of 60 to 80 million."
  - Source: Wolf Street piece cited above, quoting deal terms: "the aggregate cash proceeds to RE/MAX Holdings shareholders in the transaction will be no less than $60 million and no greater than $80 million."   - Status: confirmed via deal terms

- **Claim:** "Real runs a cap-and-stock model."
  - Source: Public knowledge. Real Brokerage's compensation model is a cap-and-stock structure, well-documented in Real's investor materials and across industry coverage. NF-009 establishes the same point.
  - Status: confirmed (industry-standard description)

- **Claim:** "REAX is down 70 percent from its 2024 peak."
  - Source: Wolf Street, same article. Exact framing: "Down 70% from all-time high (August 2024)."
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "The two most beat-up brokerage stocks in real estate just got married."
  - Source: Wolf Street article framing both REAX and RMAX as "Imploded Stocks." RMAX -85% from October 2017 peak; REAX -70% from August 2024 peak. Editorial framing, but factually grounded.
  - Status: confirmed (D.J.'s editorial paraphrase of Wolf Street's framing)

  - Source: D.J.'s own conversations and industry color. Spoken claim is qualitative, framed as D.J.'s direct read of agent sentiment, not a survey.
