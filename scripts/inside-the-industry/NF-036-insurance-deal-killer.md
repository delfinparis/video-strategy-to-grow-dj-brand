# Your Next Deal Isn't Going to Die at the Appraisal

> **WOW: Contrarian take + tactical specificity -- homeowner's insurance is the silent deal-killer of 2026, with premiums up 20%+ year-over-year in many states and quotes coming back at two to three times what everyone expected. The script gives buyer-side agents the exact systematizing move (insurance quote within 48 hours of any serious showing) that prevents week-six dead deals.**

## Script (~68 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
Your next deal isn't going to die at the appraisal. It's going to die at the insurance quote. And most agents won't see it coming.

### CONTEXT (0:10-0:30)
Homeowner's insurance premiums are up over twenty percent year-over-year in many states. The national average is around nineteen hundred dollars, and in Florida it's closer to six thousand. Quotes are coming back at two or three times what everyone expected. The buyer's debt-to-income flips. The deal dies. Or the buyer walks.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:30-0:46)
Most agents wait to run the insurance until the buyer's under contract. By then your buyer has fallen in love with the house, you've spent two weeks on inspections, and when the quote shocks them, you don't have time to restructure. You have a dead deal.

### CLOSE (0:46-1:06)
Here's what you do now. Get an insurance quote within forty-eight hours of every showing that could turn into an offer. Build it into your buyer onboarding next to lender pre-approval. The agents who run insurance early aren't losing deals at week six. The ones who don't are calling their buyers to explain what happened.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Homeowner's insurance premiums are up over twenty percent year-over-year in many states"
  - Source: Multiple 2026 industry analyses. Brokers Bridge, "How Rising Homeowners Insurance Costs Are Reshaping Real Estate Transactions in 2026" (2026-05-21) ([link](https://brokersbridge.com/latest-news/how-rising-homeowners-insurance-costs-are-reshaping-real-estate-transactions-in-2026-20260521)) notes "some states seeing year-over-year increases of 20% or more." HousingWire, "Rising insurance costs shape homeowner decisions in 2026" ([link](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/rising-insurance-costs-shape-homeowner-decisions-in-2026/)) and "Homeowners Insurance is Reshaping the Real Estate Transaction" ([link](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/homeowners-insurance-reshaping-real-estate-transaction/)) confirm the pattern across hardest-hit regions.
  - Who was measured: state-level annual premium increases (homeowner's insurance).
  - Status: confirmed
  - Note: "Many states" is the qualitative-fallback phrasing in lieu of overclaiming "a dozen" or a specific count.

- **Claim:** "The national average is around nineteen hundred dollars"
  - Source: Bankrate, "Average homeowners insurance cost in June 2026" ([link](https://www.bankrate.com/insurance/homeowners-insurance/homeowners-insurance-cost/)) and HousingWire coverage. National average annual premium hovers around $1,900 (some sources report $1,950 by end of 2025, +8.5% YoY).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "In Florida it's closer to six thousand"
  - Source: Industry reports confirm Florida average homeowner premium 2026 between $5,800 and $7,200 annually, roughly 3-4x the national average. Brokers Bridge 2026-05-21 cites this range.
  - Status: confirmed (the "closer to six thousand" phrasing rounds the bottom of the verified range; spoken delivery as a Florida illustration of the broader risk-zone surcharge)

- **Claim:** "Quotes are coming back at two or three times what everyone expected"
  - Source: Brokers Bridge 2026-05-21: "the insurance quote arrives at two or three times what everyone expected." HousingWire confirms the same pattern in covered transactions.
  - Status: confirmed (the transaction-level dynamic, not a population statistic)

- **Claim:** "The buyer's debt-to-income flips. The deal dies. Or the buyer walks."
  - Source: Standard mortgage underwriting math. When insurance premium triples in escrow, the buyer's monthly PITI rises by hundreds, which can push DTI past lender thresholds and trigger loan-fail or buyer-back-out. Documented in Brokers Bridge and HousingWire coverage.
  - Status: confirmed (mechanism, not a stat)

- **Claim:** "Get an insurance quote within forty-eight hours of every showing that could turn into an offer"
  - Source: D.J. Paris's tactical guidance. Not a stat -- this is the systematizing move the script exists to deliver.
  - Status: tactical guidance, not a stat claim

Your next deal is not going to die at the appraisal. It is going to die at the insurance quote. And most agents will not see it coming.

Quotes are coming back at two or three times what everyone expected. The buyer's debt-to-income flips. The deal dies. Or the buyer walks.

The painful version: you spend two weeks on inspections, you're three days from clear-to-close, and the insurance quote comes in at three times what you projected. The buyer's monthly payment jumps several hundred dollars, the lender pulls back, and the deal is dead.

Most agents wait to run the insurance until the buyer is under contract. By then there is no time to restructure.

Here is the move.

Get an insurance quote within 48 hours of any showing that could turn into an offer. Not at closing. Within two days of touring. Build it into your buyer onboarding next to lender pre-approval.

The agents who run insurance early are not losing deals at week six. The ones who do not are calling their buyers to explain what happened.
