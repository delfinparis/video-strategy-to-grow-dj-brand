# Should I Keep My Listing Off Zillow? Here's What to Say.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation + tactical specificity -- Zillow's chief industry development officer testified under oath this week that a new listing gets almost 180 views the day it hits the market and just 60 by day five. Bright MLS and Drexel analyzed over a million transactions and found on-MLS homes sold for about 17.5% more than comparable off-MLS homes -- roughly $54,000 for a typical seller. The Chicago courtroom made private listings a headline; sellers are asking their agents about it right now. The script arms the agent with the honest, data-backed answer they can give at the listing appointment.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Mirror | **Heat:** 3 | **Avatars:** All, listing agents especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Off-MLS is a service to the brokerage. On-MLS is a service to the seller."

## Script (~68 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
A seller is going to text you this week asking if they should keep their home off Zillow. The Chicago courtroom put private listings on every headline. Here's the honest answer to give them.

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:32)
Zillow's chief industry development officer testified this week. A new listing gets almost one hundred and eighty views the day it hits the market. By day five that drops to about sixty. That's not just Zillow talking. Bright MLS and Drexel analyzed over a million transactions. Homes listed on the MLS sold for about seventeen point five percent more than comparable off-MLS homes. That's roughly fifty-four thousand dollars for a typical seller.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:34-0:52)
Off-MLS gets pitched to your seller as control and privacy. What they don't hear is the price. Fewer buyers see it. That means fewer bids and a winning bid from a smaller pool. That's how a home sells for less. Off-MLS is a service to the brokerage. On-MLS is a service to the seller.

### CLOSE (0:52-1:10)
Here's what you do now. When the text comes, don't give them your opinion. Give them the numbers. Then let them decide. The seller who chooses off-MLS with clear data makes an informed choice. The seller who chose off-MLS because their agent didn't have the data got sold to.

**Estimated Duration:** ~68 seconds | **Word Count:** ~200 words

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "A new listing gets almost one hundred and eighty views the day it hits the market. By day five that drops to about sixty."
  - Source: Errol Samuelson, Zillow Chief Industry Development Officer, testimony at the July 1-2, 2026 preliminary injunction hearing (Zillow v. MRED and Compass, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, Judge John Tharp Jr.). Coverage: Inman, "Zillow-MRED Hearing Reveals Fight Over Who Gets Listings First," 2026-07-06 ([link](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/06/zillow-mred-compass-hearing-chicago-errol-samuelson/)). Direct quote/paraphrase per the article: a new listing gets almost 180 views the day it hits the market and falls to about 60 views per day by day five.
  - Status: confirmed (public court testimony under oath, coverage 2026-07-06)

- **Claim:** "Bright MLS and Drexel analyzed over a million transactions. Homes listed on the MLS sold for about seventeen point five percent more than comparable off-MLS homes. That's roughly fifty-four thousand dollars for a typical seller."
  - Source: Bright MLS in partnership with Drexel University's School of Economics, expanded 2019-Q1 2023 transaction dataset (1M+ transactions). Coverage: Inman, "Private Listings Offer No Clear Advantage For Homesellers: Bright MLS," 2025-04-04 ([link](https://www.inman.com/2025/04/04/private-listings-offer-no-clear-advantage-for-sellers-bright-mls/)); Real Estate News, "Listing off the MLS cost sellers more than $1B, study finds," 2025-02-18 ([link](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/02/18/listing-off-the-mls-cost-sellers-more-than-usd1b-study-finds)); Real Estate News, "Off-MLS transactions cost sellers thousands, new report finds," 2025-11-06 ([link](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/11/06/off-mls-transactions-cost-sellers-thousands-new-report-finds)). Finding: on-MLS homes sold for about 17.5% more than comparable off-MLS homes; translates to roughly $54K more for a typical 2022 seller in Bright MLS's footprint.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Caveat held for transparency (not narrated on camera): Bright MLS is a marketplace with a direct commercial interest in demonstrating the value of MLS exposure. The Drexel partnership adds academic methodology. Corroborating findings: Zillow research (2024-2025) put the aggregate off-MLS loss at $1B+ across 2023-2024 and ~$4,975 per home; San Francisco Association of Realtors and RealReports study (2022-2024 data) found an 18.6% MLS premium. Counter-evidence: The Real Deal 2026-04-08 covered an independent study claiming homes marketed off MLS got better prices; DeLeon Realty has similar advocacy. The mainstream weight of the industry data supports the on-MLS premium framing; the script goes with the dominant finding.

- **Claim:** "The Chicago courtroom put private listings on every headline."
  - Source: Coverage across Inman, HousingWire, RISMedia, Real Estate News, The Real Deal Chicago, Chicago Agent Magazine, all 2026-07-01 through 2026-07-06. Public and industry media saturation with the Zillow v. MRED and Compass hearing story.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Off-MLS gets pitched as control and privacy."
  - Source: Standard marketing framing of Compass Private Exclusives and equivalent off-MLS programs. Documented across NF-027 (Compass billboard), NF-047 (eXp buyer harm), NF-058 (Reffkin/Jensen testimony). "Control and privacy" is the industry-consensus articulation of the seller-facing pitch.
  - Status: confirmed (industry framing)

- **Claim (editorial):** "Off-MLS is a service to the brokerage. On-MLS is a service to the seller."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's shareable moment. Consistent with the load-bearing consumer-protection framing of NF-047 (eXp buyer harm), NF-051 (NAR governance disclosure), and NF-058 (Reffkin/Jensen testimony). Off-MLS programs generate brokerage referral / dual-agency opportunities; on-MLS exposure maximizes buyer competition on behalf of the seller.
  - Status: editorial framing

- **Claim (tactical):** "When the text comes, don't give them your opinion. Give them the numbers. Then let them decide."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended agent-consumer protocol. Consistent with the "informed consent" framing of NF-051 (NAR blocked disclosure rule) and the "written disclosure" framing of NF-057 and NF-058. In the post-Sitzer consumer-protection era, agents documented as providing objective data rather than steering are better protected legally and reputationally.
  - Status: tactical guidance

- **Legitimate off-MLS use cases (held out of spoken script for cadence but agents should know):**
  - Bona-fide privacy needs: celebrity sellers, victims of stalking, litigation-related sales.
  - Test-marketing: seller wants a soft launch to gauge price before full MLS exposure.
  - Active-investor / off-market institutional buyers where speed matters more than price maximization.
  - The script's "informed choice" framing accommodates these cases -- the point is the seller with the numbers can weigh them against the trade-off.
