# Your Biggest Listing Competitor This Summer Isn't Another Agent

> **WOW: Pattern reveal + tactical specificity -- the lock-in effect is keeping 300-500K homes off market, and sellers aren't choosing other agents over you. They're not choosing anyone. The script names the actual competitor (a feeling about a three percent mortgage), the exact-words move (a one-page rate-vs-equity sheet), and the exact action (top ten outgrown-home clients, this week).**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All, listing-focused agents especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Script (~66 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
Your biggest competitor for listings this summer isn't another agent. It's the three percent mortgage your seller already has.

### CONTEXT (0:10-0:26)
NAR puts it at three to five hundred thousand homes that should be on the market right now and aren't. It's called the lock-in effect. Sellers with pandemic-era rates think they can't afford to move. Most of them haven't actually done the math.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:26-0:46)
You're not losing the listing to another CMA. You're losing it to a feeling. The seller looks at six percent on a new loan and thinks "I'll never replace three." Then they shut down the conversation before any agent gets a chance. Including you.

### CLOSE (0:46-1:06)
Here's what you do now. Pick the top ten clients in your database who've outgrown their homes. Build a one-page sheet showing equity gain, target home price, new loan size, and actual monthly delta. Send it. The listing you win this summer is the one where you did the math your seller was afraid to.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "NAR puts it at three to five hundred thousand homes that should be on the market right now and aren't"
  - Source: NAR Chief Economist's office and Economists' Outlook blog. NAR has framed inventory shortfall as needing "an additional 300,000 to 500,000 homes for sale" to bring the market closer to historical norms. Referenced in NAR magazine coverage and economist commentary throughout 2026.
  - Who was measured: U.S. existing-home inventory gap relative to historical normalized supply.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "It's called the lock-in effect. Sellers with pandemic-era rates think they can't afford to move."
  - Source: Widely recognized industry term. NAR Economists' Outlook and Federal Reserve / FHFA research have documented the lock-in effect: homeowners who locked in sub-3% mortgages during 2020-2021 are reluctant to list because doing so requires a new mortgage at current market rates (now ~6.5%+).
  - Status: confirmed (industry-standard framing, not a single-source stat)

- **Claim:** "Most of them haven't actually done the math"
  - Source: D.J. Paris's direct observation from agent conversations about fence-sitting sellers. Presented as a pattern, not a stat.
  - Status: tactical framing, not a stat

- **Claim:** "Build a one-page sheet showing equity gain, target home price, new loan size, and actual monthly delta"
  - Source: D.J. Paris's tactical guidance. Standard real estate consult-prep practice; not a stat. The four-input sheet is the systematizing move the script exists to deliver.
  - Status: tactical guidance, not a stat claim

Your biggest competitor for listings this summer isn't another agent. It's the three percent mortgage your seller already has.

Send it.

The listing you win this summer is the one where you did the math the seller was afraid to.
