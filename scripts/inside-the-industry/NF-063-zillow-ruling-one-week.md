# The Judge Has One Week to Rule on Your Chicago Listings.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry read + cost-of-inaction -- the biggest real estate case in the country is sitting on one judge's desk in Chicago, and the ruling could land within the week. Both sides filed their final post-hearing briefs July 9; response briefs are due July 13; Judge Tharp could rule as soon as next week. The script closes the courtroom arc: it clarifies that this is the preliminary injunction (not the full merits), walks both outcomes neutrally, and hands the agent a three-part apolitical playbook to run before the ruling drops. It stays above the fight while turning 'the ruling is coming' into a this-week workflow decision.**

## Script (~66 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
You know how your Chicago listings just automatically show up on Zillow? One judge is about to decide if that's still true. And he could rule this week.

*(Scroll-stop = the first spoken line. Kane "You know how...?" opens the curiosity gap on something the agent takes for granted, then names the reward. Push-In on the phone showing the Real Estate News headline. Spoken, not on-screen text.)*

### TURN (0:10-0:30)
This is Zillow versus MRED and Compass. And here's what should get your attention. What drops this week isn't even the verdict. It's the preliminary round. One question. Does Zillow's feed of MRED's listings stay on while the real case plays out. That's it. And it's still the most-watched real estate ruling in the country. Because whoever wins this round writes the playbook everybody else copies.

### THE FORK (0:30-0:44)
So here's the fork. If Zillow wins, your listings keep showing up on every big portal. Business as usual. If MRED and Compass win, your MLS can start deciding which portals see which listings. That's the private-listings model. And if it works here, it goes national.

### PAYOFF (0:44-1:06)
Here's the move, and you can run it on your next listing. Before the ruling even drops, put in writing which venues actually see your seller's home. Portals, private networks, all of it. Right on the listing agreement. Confession. I never read that section closely until this case. Now it's the first thing I check. And don't jump online to pick a side while it's still live. You don't know what you don't know. Just be ready. Because whichever way this goes, you want to be the agent who saw it coming. Not the one whose seller asks why their house disappeared from Zillow.
