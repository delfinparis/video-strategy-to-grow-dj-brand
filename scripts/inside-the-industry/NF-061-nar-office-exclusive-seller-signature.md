# NAR Just Made Your Seller Sign for the Private Listing.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry access + tactical specificity -- NAR's new office-exclusive and pre-marketing guidance for brokers landed in D.J.'s inbox this week from NAR's Chief Marketing Officer, Bennett Richardson. The through-line most agents will miss: NAR kept the Clear Cooperation Policy and kept the office exclusive, but the price of going off-MLS is now a signed seller certification. The seller has to acknowledge in writing the MLS exposure they are giving up. The script turns NAR's own policy into the informed-consent conversation that protects the seller and the agent both.**

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
Before you take another listing off the MLS, there's one line in NAR's new broker guidance you need to read. It landed in my inbox this week from their Chief Marketing Officer, and most agents will never open it.

*(Scroll-stop = the first spoken line. "Before you take another listing off the MLS" targets listing agents and opens the loop in the first 6 words. Spoken, not on-screen text: captions.ai builds the caption from the audio.)*

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:38)
Clear Cooperation is still the rule. Publicly market a listing, and it has to hit the MLS within one business day. NAR kept that. But they also gave your seller two ways to stay off the MLS. The office exclusive, and delayed marketing. Here's the part almost nobody is reading. To use either one, you can't just keep it quiet. You have to file it with the MLS, and you have to get your seller to sign a certification. Two things on it. Your relationship to them, and a written acknowledgement that they understand the exposure they are giving up.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:38-0:56)
This drops the same month a Chicago courtroom put private listings on every headline in the country. And NAR's answer is not a ban. It's a signature. That signature is not paperwork. It's the line between a seller who chose to skip the MLS and a seller who got talked out of it. More buyers on the MLS means more bids. That exposure is exactly what the form makes your seller acknowledge they are waiving.

### CLOSE (0:56-1:14)
Here's what you do now. If you take an office exclusive, don't slide the certification across the table at the end for a quick signature. Walk them through it at the start. The exposure they are giving up is the whole conversation. NAR just made informed consent the rule. The agent who documents the choice is protected. The agent who skips it now owns it.

Corroborated publicly by Real Estate News, "Pre-market, office exclusive listings guidance underway, NAR says," 2026-07-03 ([link](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/07/03/pre-market-office-exclusive-listings-guidance-underway-nar-says)), which reports NAR is distributing new PDF resources to MLSs and brokerages on office exclusives, pre-marketing, the broker's role in upholding MLS policies, and the off-market vs on-market distinction.
  - Status: confirmed (direct email + public reporting)
  - Access note (do not overclaim on camera): the email appears to be a broad distribution to brokers and industry leaders, not a one-to-one message. The script says "sent brokers... landed in my inbox," which is accurate and does not claim a private/personal relationship beyond receiving the resource.

- **Claim:** "Clear Cooperation is still the rule. Publicly market a listing, and it has to hit the MLS within one business day."
  - Source: NAR, "MLS Clear Cooperation Policy" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-clear-cooperation-policy)). CCP (adopted 2020) requires a listing broker to submit a listing to the MLS within one business day of marketing the property to the public. Retained in 2026.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "They also gave your seller two ways to stay off the MLS. The office exclusive, and delayed marketing."
  - Source: NAR, "Multiple Listing Options for Sellers" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/multiple-listing-options-for-sellers)); NAR newsroom, "NAR Introduces New MLS Policy to Expand Choice for Consumers" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/nar-introduces-new-mls-policy-to-expand-choice-for-consumers)). The Multiple Listing Options for Sellers policy is effective March 25, 2025, with an implementation deadline of September 30, 2025. It provides two exempt paths alongside CCP: office exclusive (not disseminated through the MLS and not publicly marketed) and delayed marketing (public marketing through IDX and syndication delayed for a period allowed by the local MLS).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "You have to file it with the MLS, and you have to get your seller to sign a certification. Two things on it. Your relationship to them, and a written acknowledgement that they understand the exposure they are giving up."
  - Source: NAR, "Multiple Listing Options for Sellers" ([link](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/multiple-listing-options-for-sellers)). Both exempt listing types must be filed with the MLS pursuant to a seller certification obtained by the listing broker that includes (1) disclosure of the professional relationship between the Participant and the seller and (2) the seller's acknowledgement that they understand the MLS benefits they are waiving or delaying, such as broad and immediate exposure of the listing through the MLS.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "This drops the same month a Chicago courtroom put private listings on every headline in the country."
  - Source: Zillow v. MRED and Compass preliminary injunction hearing, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, July 1-2, 2026, with saturation coverage across Inman, HousingWire, Real Estate News, and The Real Deal Chicago through 2026-07-06. See NF-057, NF-058, NF-060.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (editorial):** "NAR didn't ban the private listing. It made your seller sign for it."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's shareable moment / framing of the Multiple Listing Options for Sellers certification requirement. Consistent with the informed-consent through-line of NF-051 and NF-060.
  - Status: editorial framing

- **Held for transparency (not narrated):**
  - NAR is also clarifying that one-to-one, broker-to-broker communication about a listing does not trigger CCP, while multi-brokerage communication constitutes public marketing that does. Useful for the comment section and the listing-appointment conversation; left out of the spoken script for length and focus.
  - Legitimate off-MLS use cases (privacy needs, test-marketing, speed-over-price) still exist. The informed-consent frame accommodates them: the seller with the signed acknowledgement is choosing with eyes open.
