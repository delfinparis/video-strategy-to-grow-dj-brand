# Stop Calling Your Buyers. Text Them.

> **WOW: Zillow's 2025 buyer survey: text messaging is buyers' most preferred way to hear from their agent (39%), phone is second (33%), and 67% prefer some form of written contact, rising to 73% for buyers in their 30s.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Swap / List | **Heat:** 4 | **Avatars:** Buyer agents, newer agents trained on phone-first scripts
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: Gesture-On-Beat (Palm to camera on 'Stop,' then hold up the phone on 'Text them.')

## Script (30 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:01.5)
Stop calling your buyers. Text them.

### TENSION (0:01.5-0:06.5)
Zillow says two out of three buyers want it in writing.

### THE POINT (0:06.5-0:21.5)
Text is their top pick. Phone calls come in second. Buyers in their thirties want writing even more. About three in four. To them, a surprise call is a chore.

### PAYOFF (0:21.5-0:30)
Ask every buyer on day one. Call, text or email? Then use what they pick.

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, pick one to A/B):**

**Pattern interrupt:** Gesture-On-Beat confirmed. The phone in hand on 'Text them' doubles as One Prop.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): 'Stop calling' violates a belief agents were trained on ('pick up the phone'). That gap is the Huh. 'In writing' is plainer than 'asynchronous.'
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): Social currency for younger agents: they send it to the old-school team lead. Amusement is high arousal.
- Retention move (MrBeast): 'About three in four' is a short, punchy re-hook at about 0:14. The close is an action, not a moral.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Byron Lazine says old-school coaches will fight this in the comments ('the phone is where deals happen'), and that is reach. Chris Do worries the comment fight will drown the tip. **Experiment:** pin a first comment that adds 'calls still win for bad news and offers.' Track whether replies stay on topic.
