## Production Notes

- **Re-verify the number before filming.** Rates move weekly. 6.51% is the Freddie Mac PMMS reading for the week of May 21, 2026. If filming slips a week, pull the current PMMS number and swap it. The script structure holds at any number; only the figure is perishable.
- **Use the Freddie Mac weekly survey, not a daily quote.** Daily trackers (Bankrate, MND) will show a different, often higher number on any given day. The script cites Freddie's weekly PMMS because it is the standard, sourceable benchmark. Do not blend the two on camera.
- **Don't predict.** The script names the drivers (bond yields, Fed posture) but makes no rate forecast. "Waiting for a cut is a bet, not a plan" is a framing of uncertainty, not a prediction that rates will rise.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "The thirty-year fixed jumped to six-point-five-one percent... was six-point-three-six a week earlier"
  - Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, 30-year fixed averaged 6.51% as of 2026-05-21, up from 6.36% as of 2026-05-14. Fox Business, "Mortgage rates rise to 6.51%: Freddie Mac," 2026-05-21 ([link](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/mortgage-rates-may-21-2026)); Freddie Mac PMMS ([link](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Fifteen basis points in seven days"
  - Source: Arithmetic from the two Freddie Mac readings. 6.51% minus 6.36% equals 0.15 percentage points, which is 15 basis points, over the one-week survey interval.
  - Status: confirmed (derived from sourced figures)

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "bond yields reacting to global tension and a Fed that's in no hurry to cut"
  - Editorial summary of the widely reported drivers of the move. Framed as context, not as a quantified causal claim. No specific event is asserted as the sole cause.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by general reporting

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "waiting on a cut is a bet, not a plan"
  - Coaching framing of rate uncertainty for buyer conversations. Not a forecast. It reframes the decision to wait as taking on risk rather than guaranteeing a better outcome.
  - Status: editorial / coaching framing

- **Fabrication audit:** Both rate figures (6.51% and 6.36%) are exact Freddie Mac PMMS readings with dates. "Fifteen basis points" is the verified difference. No forecast is made. The driver language is flagged as editorial context. The brief's "6.65%" and "highest since last fall" were both dropped because neither could be sourced to the Freddie Mac weekly survey.
