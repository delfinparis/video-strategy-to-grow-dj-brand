# Don't Drop the Other Agent After Closing.

> **WOW: Compass Research (Oct 8, 2026), on an RIT / UNC Charlotte study: "A one-standard-deviation increase in counterparty connectedness ... increases average career duration by 37%." Who sat across the table on a new agent's first deal changed how long that agent lasted.**

**Pillar:** Tactical | **Hook Family:** Swap / List | **Heat:** 4 | **Avatars:** All agents, especially newer agents
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: One Prop (hold a coffee cup up to the lens on "Don't drop").

## Shareable Moment

Don't drop the other agent after closing.

You close, you say thanks, and you never talk again.

Compass wrote up a new study on agents' very first deals. Some new agents had a better-connected agent on the other side. Their careers lasted 37 percent longer.

So don't drop them. Within two weeks of closing, take the other agent to coffee.

## Data Source
- **Claim:** "Compass wrote up a new study on agents' very first deals. Some new agents had a better-connected agent on the other side. Their careers lasted 37 percent longer."
  - Source: Compass International Holdings Market Research, "How Important Is Networking to an Agent's Career Success?", Oct 8, 2026, authored by Jonah Coste, economist. https://www.compass.com/newsroom/research/how-important-is-networking-to-an-agents-career-success/ . Underlying paper linked from that page: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5349694 (Rochester Institute of Technology and UNC Charlotte researchers; SSRN returned 403 on 2026-10-14, not opened directly).
  - Exact wording: "New research from the Rochester Institute of Technology and UNC Charlotte disentangles these effects by using a quasi-random source of variation in an agent's network: how connected was the agent on the other side of their very first transaction." And: "A one-standard-deviation increase in counterparty connectedness increases the probability of surviving one year in the industry by 6% to 7% ... and increases average career duration by 37%."
  - Who was measured: new real estate agents and the agent on the other side of their first transaction (transaction data, not a survey).
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "One-standard-deviation increase in counterparty connectedness" said as "a better-connected agent on the other side"; "average career duration by 37%" said as "careers lasted 37 percent longer." The brief's framing ("agents who were better connected with other agents") does not match the page and was not used.
- **Claim (captions only):** "A one-standard-deviation increase in the counterparty agent's network connectedness increases career transaction volume by 12.5%." Same page, exact wording. Confirmed 2026-10-14. Kept out of the spoken script (one number per line, one stat per script).
- **Claim:** "So don't drop them. Within two weeks of closing, take the other agent to coffee."
  - Source: editorial recommendation. Compass's own action step on the same page: "Prioritize Connected Counterparties: Actively seek out and build relationships with well-connected agents." The study measures the first deal only; extending it to every closing is editorial.
  - Status: commentary.
- **Claim:** "You close, you say thanks, and you never talk again."
  - Source: editorial framing of the habit. No statistic implied.
  - Status: commentary.
