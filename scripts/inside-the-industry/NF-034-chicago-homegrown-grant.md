## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Grants up to seventy thousand dollars toward a home purchase. Launches Monday."
  - Source: WBEZ Chicago, "Housing program to give eligible Chicagoans up to $70,000 toward down payment," 2026-06-01 ([link](https://www.wbez.org/housing/2026/06/01/homegrown-purchase-assistance-program-eligible-chicagoans-70-000-down-payment)); Chicago Sun-Times same date ([link](https://chicago.suntimes.com/real-estate/2026/06/01/homegrown-purchase-assistance-program-eligible-chicagoans-70-000-down-payment)); official City of Chicago program page, [chicago.gov/homegrown](https://www.chicago.gov/homegrown).
  - Status: confirmed
  - **Day-of-week note:** Program launches Monday, June 8, 2026. The initial brief framed it as "this Sunday, June 8" -- corrected to Monday. Do not let any caption or read-through revert to Sunday.

- **Claim:** "HomeGrown Purchase Assistance Program. Expected to help three to four hundred buyers in the first round."
  - Source: WBEZ 2026-06-01 and chicago.gov/homegrown. The $21 million program is expected to assist between 300-400 homebuyers depending on individual grant amounts.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Up to seventy thousand in Zone A neighborhoods like Lincoln Park and Lakeview. Up to fifty thousand in Zone B neighborhoods like Englewood and Roseland."
  - Source: WBEZ 2026-06-01 and chicago.gov/homegrown. Zone A: neighborhoods experiencing significant home-price increases (examples named in WBEZ: "Lincoln Park and Lake View"), up to $70,000. Zone B: low-income census tracts where 70%+ of families earn below 80% of statewide median income (examples named: "Englewood and Roseland"), up to $50,000.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Note: Lincoln Park / Lake View / Englewood / Roseland are NAR-cited examples. The interactive map at chicago.gov/homegrown is the authoritative source for any specific property's zone designation.

- **Claim:** "Income cap for a family of four: one-eighty-two, two-fifty. For an individual: one-twenty-seven, six-fifty."
  - Source: chicago.gov/homegrown via WBEZ 2026-06-01. Household income cannot exceed 150% of area median income; for a family of four in Chicago that is $182,250. Individual (household of one) limit is $127,650.
  - Who was measured: applicant household income, gross annual.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Pull every fence-sitting buyer in your CRM who could qualify. Send them one text."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's tactical action prompt for agents. Not a stat.
  - Status: tactical guidance, not a stat claim

- **Eligibility caveats NOT in spoken script but worth knowing for follow-up answers:**
  - Applicants must complete HUD-certified homebuyer education before closing.
  - Property must be a 1- or 2-unit Chicago home occupied as primary residence for at least five years.
  - Existing properties citywide are eligible (not just new construction).
  - Applications go through TRP Lending (The Resurrection Project) or Neighborhood Lending Services (NHS Chicago).
  - These details belong in the agent's follow-up text "the details" referenced in the close, not in the 70-second spoken cut.
