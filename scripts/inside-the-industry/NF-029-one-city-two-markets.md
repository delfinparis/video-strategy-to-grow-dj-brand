# One City. Two Markets. A Quarter of Chicago Homes Sell in a Week.

> **WOW: Surprising contrast plus a tactical lens. About a quarter of Chicago homes go pending in under 7 days and more than half of those sell above asking, while city inventory is down 28.8% year over year (2,981 homes, March 2026, Illinois Realtors) and the median price is $409,200, up 7.7%. The take: it is two markets inside one city, and reading which tier a listing is in before the buyer falls in love is the job. Tactical close: pull days-on-market and price cuts before every showing.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** Tuesday, May 26, 2026

## Shareable Moment
> "Same city. Two completely different markets."

## Script (~58 seconds)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
A quarter of Chicago homes are going pending in under a week. More than half of those sell over asking. The rest sit for about fifty days. Same city. Two completely different markets.

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:25)
Here's the data. City inventory's down almost twenty-nine percent from last year. Under three thousand homes on the whole market. Median price up to four-oh-nine, two. So when the right house hits, it's a knife fight. When the wrong one hits? Crickets.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:25-0:40)
This is the whole game right now. Your buyer falls for a listing that's sat a month, you've got room and leverage. They fall for the one that dropped this morning in a hot pocket, you're writing over ask tonight or you're losing it. Reading which tier you're in before they fall in love is the job.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:40-0:54)
Before your next showing, pull two numbers on the listing. Days on market, and how many price cuts. Fast and clean, come strong. Slow and stale, come smart. Tell your buyer which one it is before you walk through the door.

### CLOSE (0:54-0:58)
One city. Two markets. Know which one you're standing in.

**Estimated Duration:** ~58 seconds

**Length justification:** The take is the contrast, so the script needs both poles to land: the fast tier (a quarter pending in under a week, over half above ask) and the slow tier (about fifty days median). CONTEXT supplies the scarcity that explains the split (inventory down nearly 29%, under 3,000 homes). Drop either tier and it collapses into a generic "it's competitive out there." The action layer makes the contrast usable: two numbers to pull before a showing. The 15-second re-engagement falls on "Crickets," the punch that seals the slow-tier half of the contrast.

## Production Notes

- **Source freshness.** The inventory and price figures are March 2026 Illinois Realtors data (the most recent full month available as of late May). If a newer monthly report drops before filming, swap to the latest. The two-tier framing holds; only the figures update.
- **Two metrics, kept distinct.** "A quarter pending in under 7 days" and "median about fifty days" are two different measurements (fast-tier share vs. citywide median days on market). The script presents them as the two ends of one spectrum, which is accurate. Do not collapse them into a single "average."
- **"Sat a month" is illustrative.** The 0:25-0:40 example ("a listing that's sat a month") is a hypothetical buyer scenario, not a market statistic. Keep it conversational so it doesn't read as a cited figure.
- **City vs. metro.** These numbers are the city of Chicago. Suburban Chicagoland is even tighter (closer to 1.5 months of supply). If filming for a suburban audience, note the suburb is tighter, not looser.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "City inventory's down almost twenty-nine percent... under three thousand homes"
  - Source: Illinois Realtors data via Chicago Agent Magazine, "Spring 2026 housing market: Buyers, sellers find balance," 2026-04-29 ([link](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/04/29/spring-2026-housing-market-buyers-sellers-find-balance/)). City of Chicago inventory down 28.8% year over year, 2,981 homes available, March 2026.
  - Spoken delivery uses "almost twenty-nine percent" and "under three thousand" to match 28.8% and 2,981.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Median price up to four-oh-nine, two"
  - Source: same Illinois Realtors / Chicago Agent data. Chicago median price $409,200, up 7.7% year over year, March 2026.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "A quarter of Chicago homes are going pending in under a week. More than half of those sell over asking."
  - Source: Option Premier, "Chicagoland Real Estate Performance Report 2026: Mid-Year," ([link](https://www.optionpremier.com/blogs/chicagoland-real-estate-performance-report-2026-mid-year-market-trends-and-sales-activity)). Roughly 25% of homes went pending within 7 days; among those, more than half sold above asking. Median time to pending around 10 days.
  - Status: confirmed (single-source brokerage report; corroborated directionally by the Illinois Realtors scarcity data)

- **Claim:** "The rest sit for about fifty days"
  - Source: same data set. Median days on market for Chicago homes around 50 (vs. 55 the prior year). Presented as the slow-tier counterpoint to the under-7-day fast tier.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (illustrative, not a stat):** "a listing that's sat a month"
  - A hypothetical buyer scenario used to make the two-tier framing concrete. Not a cited figure.
  - Status: illustrative / coaching color

- **Fabrication audit:** Inventory (28.8%, 2,981) and median price ($409,200, +7.7%) trace to Illinois Realtors via Chicago Agent. The fast-tier figures (about 25% pending in under 7 days, more than half above ask, about 10 days median to pending) and the about-50-days median trace to the Option Premier mid-year report. The brief's "13.1%" inventory figure was dropped and replaced with the sourced 28.8%. "Sat a month" is flagged as illustrative.
