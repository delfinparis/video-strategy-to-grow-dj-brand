# Clear Cooperation Is Dead in Practice. Every Listing Agent Needs a Position by Friday.

> **WOW: Tactical specificity plus pattern reveal plus earned-observation framing. NAR's Clear Cooperation Policy technically still exists on the books, but the practical regime that backed it collapsed this week. Compass formally declared CCP non-binding in December 2025 (HousingWire). Zillow softened its listing standards in March 2026, allowing pre-MLS exposure (The Real Deal). On May 13-14, BrightMLS -- the largest MLS in the country -- partnered with Compass to distribute private exclusives (Inman, Real Estate News). Yesterday's Zillow study put a $1.3 billion seller-cost price tag on the off-MLS architecture (NF-020 cross-ref). Inman called it: "The Clear Cooperation Policy Is Dead. What Comes Next?" (May 15, 2026). The take: CCP is dead in practice and every listing agent needs an explicit, documented position before their next listing presentation.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Post Date:** Friday, May 15, 2026

### CONTEXT (0:08-0:22)

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:22-0:35)
Coming-soon and private listings used to be banned. Now they're the default for the biggest brokerage in America. Yesterday's Zillow study priced the cost at one-point-three billion. Sellers paid. Your sellers will find out.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:35-1:00)
Three plays this week.

One. Audit your last twelve months. Document why you used coming-soon or off-market. Discovery questions don't take prisoners.

Two. Take written informed consent on every new listing. MLS-first or off-MLS. Let the seller decide. Document the conversation.

Three. Ask your brokerage which side they're on. If they push coming-soon, that's now a measurable seller harm. If they default MLS-first, you have a position to lead with.
