# Half of America's Mortgages Are Under 4%. Moving Costs Them $50,000.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic plus the cause nobody names. More than half of every outstanding mortgage in America carries a rate under 4 percent, and one in five is under 3. To move, the typical locked-in owner would trade that for today's ~6.5% and pay roughly $400 more a month -- a cost the FHFA pegs near $50,000 in present value per household, and $2.4 trillion in aggregate. That is the golden handcuffs. And the FHFA counted the result: lock-in prevented 1.72 million home sales between 2022 and 2024 and cut sales of fixed-rate-mortgage homes 45%. The inventory didn't vanish. It's sitting inside houses nobody can afford to leave. Agents keep calling this a slow market. It isn't slow. It's handcuffed.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "More than half of every mortgage in America is under 4 percent"
  - Source: FHFA National Mortgage Database, Q3 2025, sub-4% share = 51.5% (down from a 65.1% peak in Q1 2022, lowest since Q4 2020). Reported via Redfin rate-lock analysis and WolfStreet, January 2026 ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/rate-lock-q2-2025/), [WolfStreet](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/01/03/locked-in-homeowners-nevertheless-pay-off-below-4-mortgages-their-share-of-all-mortgages-drops-to-lowest-since-q4-2020/))
  - Who was measured: All outstanding US first-lien mortgages
  - Status: confirmed. "More than half" is an exact lower bound on the 51.5% figure, not a rounding.

- **Claim:** "One in five is under 3 percent"
  - Source: Redfin rate-lock analysis, Q2 2025: 20.4% of mortgaged homeowners below 3% ([link](https://www.redfin.com/news/rate-lock-q2-2025/))
  - Who was measured: US mortgaged homeowners
  - Status: confirmed. "One in five" = 20.4% (Rule 5 "X in Y" preference).

- **Claim:** "about four hundred dollars more a month" and "near fifty thousand dollars a household" and "$2.4 trillion in aggregate"
  - Source: FHFA Working Paper 24-03, "The Lock-In Effect of Rising Mortgage Rates" ([link](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/wp2403.pdf)). Direct quote: locked-in owners "give up roughly $400 a month... worth nearly $50,000 in present value and an aggregate value of $2.4 trillion."
  - Who was measured: Modeled across active fixed-rate mortgages, as of 2024Q2
  - Status: confirmed. These are FHFA model estimates (present value), not observed receipts. Spoken script uses the source's own "near/about" language; exact source phrasing preserved here.

- **Claim:** "Lock-in stopped 1.72 million homes from ever hitting the market"
  - Source: FHFA Working Paper 24-03 ([link](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/wp2403.pdf)). Direct quote: lock-in "prevented 1.72 million sales between 2022Q2 and 2024Q2" and "decreased the sales of homes with fixed-rate mortgages by 45% in 2024Q2."
  - Who was measured: US home sales, modeled 2022Q2-2024Q2
  - Status: confirmed. Model-derived estimate, not an observed count. Exact figure, not rounded.

- **Claim (mechanism):** "they'd trade that rate for a six and a half"
  - Source: Market 30-year rate in the ~6 to 6.5% range across 2025-2026; ICE 30-year conforming fixed index hit 6.04% on Jan 9, 2026 ([ICE](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/ICE-Mortgage-Monitor-Early-January-Rate-Decline-Unlocks-Refinance-Opportunity-for-Nearly-5-Million-Homeowners/default.aspx)); ~6.38% at Redfin's Sept 2025 publication.
  - Status: confirmed as a range. "Six and a half" is the upper, conservative end of the 2026 market-rate range used consistently across this series.

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "It's handcuffed"
  - Editorial reframe of the lock-in mechanism. The FHFA's 18.1% per-point sale-probability suppression and the 1.72M prevented-sales figure support "handcuffed" over "slow."
  - Status: editorial color, not a stat.
