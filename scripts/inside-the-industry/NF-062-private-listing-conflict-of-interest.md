# The Private Listing Is a Conflict of Interest With Better Marketing.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry take + tactical proof + honest concession -- most private-listing content either cheerleads it or ignores who it pays. This one names the incentive out loud (off-MLS gives the listing brokerage the best shot at both sides of the commission, plus recruiting inventory), stacks the Bright MLS/Drexel finding that on-MLS homes sold about 17.5% more (roughly $54K on a typical sale), and then concedes the two cases that actually hold up -- safety and documented informed choice -- so the takedown lands as an audit, not a rant. The scroll-stop points friction at the playbook, never at the agent.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** System Indictment | **Heat:** 5 | **Avatars:** All, listing agents and brokers especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Stop (stop walking, turn square to camera on the first line). No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "A strategy that pays the house and costs the client is not a strategy. It is a conflict of interest with better marketing."

## Script (~90 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:14)
Almost every reason you've been given to pull a listing off the MLS was written by someone who makes money when you do. There are maybe two honest reasons to sell a home in private. Let me give you both, and then let's talk about why the rest is a story your brokerage needs you to believe.

*(Scroll-stop = the first spoken line. It points friction outward at whoever wrote the playbook, stands with the agent, and opens the loop in the first ten words. Spoken, not on-screen text.)*

### WHY IT PAYS (0:14-0:36)
Start with who the private listing actually pays. When your listing never hits the MLS, your own brokerage has the best shot at the buyer too. Same firm, both sides of the commission. That is the engine behind the big private-exclusive programs. It is not a seller feature. It is a revenue stream. Inventory nobody else can see also recruits agents and makes buyers call your office. Every one of those wins goes to the house.

### THE PITCH VS THE NUMBERS (0:36-0:58)
So how does it reach the seller? Three words. Privacy. Control. Exclusivity. Here is the problem. Bright MLS and Drexel studied over a million sales. Homes on the MLS sold for about seventeen and a half percent more. Fifty-four thousand dollars on a typical sale. Zillow put the total loss past a billion. Privacy that quietly costs your seller fifty grand is not privacy. It is a price cut they never agreed to.

### THE TURN (0:58-1:16)
Now the honest part. There are two reasons that hold up. One, safety. A celebrity, a domestic-violence survivor, a judge, anyone whose home address in public is a real risk. That is legitimate, full stop. Two, a seller who sees the fifty-thousand-dollar number, understands it, and still chooses privacy. That is their right, and if you documented it, you did your job. And yes, in a narrow luxury and investor lane, off-market buyers really do pay up. That is a handful of deals. Not a default.

### CLOSE (1:16-1:32)
So before you pitch a private listing, ask yourself one question. Is this for my seller's safety, or my brokerage's spreadsheet? If it is not safety, and your seller has not seen the numbers, you already know the answer. A strategy that pays the house and costs the client is not a strategy. It is a conflict of interest with better marketing.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "When your listing never hits the MLS, your own brokerage has the best shot at the buyer too. Same firm, both sides of the commission. That is the engine behind the big private-exclusive programs."
  - Source: Structural analysis of the in-house/off-MLS incentive. Off-MLS listings are disproportionately matched to buyers represented by the same brokerage, which positions the firm for both sides of the commission (dual agency / designated agency) and the associated referral value. The largest branded private-listing program is Compass Private Exclusives / the Compass three-phase marketing strategy; Compass is a defendant in Zillow v. MRED and Compass (N.D. Ill., preliminary injunction hearing July 1-2, 2026). See NF-027 (Compass billboard), NF-047 (eXp buyer-harm framing), NF-058 (Reffkin/Jensen testimony).
  - Status: analysis / industry-structural framing (spoken script says "the big private-exclusive programs," not a single named company; D.J. can name Compass on camera if he chooses -- it is factual and Compass is a litigation defendant)

- **Claim:** "Inventory nobody else can see also recruits agents and makes buyers call your office."
  - Source: Off-MLS inventory is used as a recruiting and buyer-acquisition asset ("list with us for access to exclusive inventory"). Documented in Compass and other brokerage recruiting and marketing materials; consistent with the courtroom testimony arc (NF-057, NF-058).
  - Status: industry framing

- **Claim:** "Bright MLS and Drexel studied over a million sales. Homes on the MLS sold for about seventeen and a half percent more. Fifty-four thousand dollars on a typical sale."
  - Source: Bright MLS in partnership with Drexel University's School of Economics, expanded dataset of 1M+ transactions. Coverage: Inman, "Private Listings Offer No Clear Advantage For Homesellers: Bright MLS," 2025-04-04 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/04/04/private-listings-offer-no-clear-advantage-for-sellers-bright-mls/)); Real Estate News, "Listing off the MLS cost sellers more than $1B, study finds," 2025-02-18 ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/02/18/listing-off-the-mls-cost-sellers-more-than-usd1b-study-finds)). Finding: on-MLS homes sold for about 17.5% more than comparable off-MLS homes, roughly $54K more for a typical seller. Same figures used in NF-060.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Caveat (not narrated): Bright MLS is a marketplace with a commercial interest in MLS exposure; the Drexel partnership adds academic method. Corroborated by Zillow research and the SF Association of Realtors 18.6% premium finding.

- **Claim:** "Zillow put the total loss past a billion."
  - Source: Zillow research on off-MLS sales, aggregate homeseller loss estimated at over $1 billion across 2023-2024 (approx. $4,975 per home). Same source used in NF-060.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (concession):** "In a narrow luxury and investor lane, off-market buyers really do pay up."
  - Source: Counter-evidence held honestly. The Real Deal, 2026-04-08, covered an independent study claiming homes marketed off-MLS achieved better prices; DeLeon Realty has published similar advocacy. The mainstream weight of the data supports the on-MLS premium for the median seller, but a genuine luxury/investor niche exists. Including it is what keeps the script an audit rather than a strawman.
  - Status: contested, acknowledged on camera

- **Claim (concession):** "Safety... a celebrity, a domestic-violence survivor, a judge, anyone whose home address in public is a real risk."
  - Source: Recognized legitimate privacy use cases for off-MLS listings: public figures, domestic-violence and stalking survivors, judges/law enforcement, litigation-related and sensitive circumstances. Consistent with NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers rationale for the office-exclusive exemption.
  - Status: editorial / legitimate-use framing

- **Claim (tactical):** "A seller who sees the fifty-thousand-dollar number, understands it, and still chooses privacy... if you documented it, you did your job."
  - Source: Ties directly to NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers seller-certification requirement (see NF-061) and the informed-consent framing of NF-060. The documented, data-backed choice is the compliant and defensible path.
  - Status: tactical guidance

- **Trigger:** NAR's office-exclusive / pre-marketing guidance for brokers (email to D.J. from NAR CMO Bennett Richardson, week of 2026-07-06; publicly confirmed by Real Estate News, "Pre-market, office exclusive listings guidance underway, NAR says," 2026-07-03 ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/07/03/pre-market-office-exclusive-listings-guidance-underway-nar-says))). NF-062 is the opinion companion to the NF-061 explainer.
