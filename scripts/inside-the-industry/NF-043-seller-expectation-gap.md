# 83% of Sellers Expect Asking Price. The Market Is Giving Them a Discount.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic + tactical specificity -- more than four in five sellers expect to get asking price or better this spring, but homes are closing below list. That expectation gap is the source of most agent-seller friction, and the fix is a specific move at the listing consult, before the agreement gets signed.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Your seller is doing math the market stopped supporting weeks ago."

## Script (~60 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:10)
Eighty-three percent of sellers right now expect to get asking price or more. The market is quietly handing them a discount. Closing that gap is your job, before you sign the listing.

### CONTEXT (0:10-0:24)
That number's from a Realtor.com survey this spring. More than one in three sellers expect offers above asking. A separate Homes.com analysis shows homes closing around one and a half percent under list. So the seller in your car is doing math the market stopped supporting weeks ago.

### THE TAKE (0:24-0:44)
Here's where agents lose. You don't correct that number at the listing table, because you're afraid to lose the listing. So you take it at their price. Three weeks of silence later, you're having the price-reduction conversation instead. Set the real number on day one, and you look slow at the table but brilliant at closing. Agree to the fantasy number, and it's the reverse.

### CLOSE (0:44-0:58)
Here's what you do now. At your next listing consult, bring the last ten comparable sales. Put list price next to sale price on every one. Let that spread set the expectation, not the seller's hope. Set the number before you sign.

**Estimated Duration:** ~60 seconds | **Word Count:** ~150 words

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Eighty-three percent of sellers right now expect to get asking price or more" and "more than one in three sellers expect offers above asking"
  - Source: Realtor.com seller survey, spring 2026, as reported by Chicago Agent Magazine, "Spring 2026 housing market: buyers, sellers find balance," 2026-04-29 ([link](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/04/29/spring-2026-housing-market-buyers-sellers-find-balance/)). 83% of potential sellers expect asking price or better; 37% expect above-asking offers; 46% expect their asking price.
  - Who was measured: potential home sellers (consumer-reported, Realtor.com survey).
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "A separate Homes.com analysis shows homes closing around one and a half percent under list"
  - Source: Homes.com analysis, cited in the same Chicago Agent Magazine article, 2026-04-29 (link above). Homes selling at roughly a 1.5% discount to list price.
  - Who was measured: closed-sale transaction data (market analysis, distinct from the Realtor.com seller-sentiment survey).
  - Status: confirmed
  - Rule 1.3 note: The 83% / 37% figures (Realtor.com seller survey) and the 1.5%-under-list figure (Homes.com closed-sale analysis) are two separate sources. The script names them separately ("a Realtor.com survey" vs "a separate Homes.com analysis") and does not imply one study produced both. The gap between the two is the editorial point, drawn explicitly across two sources.

- **Claim:** "the last ten comparable sales... list price next to sale price"
  - Source: D.J. Paris's tactical guidance for the listing consult. Standard CMA practice; not a stat.
  - Status: confirmed (action step, not a stat)
