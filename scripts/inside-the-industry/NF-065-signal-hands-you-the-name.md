# The Signal Hands You the Name. It Never Makes the Call.

> **WOW: Earned inside-the-industry observation (criterion 8) plus tactical specificity (criterion 1). D.J. runs recruiting at a brokerage that pays for attrition-scoring software, and the internal rule is that you never open a call with the signal the software gave you, because it's creepy and it ends the conversation. That rule is already written down in this repo's own call scripts. The same tool category now points at sellers, so the rule transfers, and almost nobody buying these lists knows it yet.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Defector | **Heat:** 3.5 | **Avatar:** Listing agents
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: The Stop. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Every agent in your market can buy the same list. The signal isn't the advantage. The call is."

## Script (~77 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:11)
I use software that scores which agents are most likely to leave their brokerage. It's called Courted, and it's never recruited a single agent for me.

### CONTEXT (0:11-0:22)
That same kind of tool points at sellers now. Revaluate scores your own database on who's likely to move in the next six to twelve months.

### TURN (0:22-0:33)
Here's the part that should stop you. Every agent in your market can buy the same list. The signal isn't the advantage. The call is.

### PAYLOAD (0:33-0:46)
And there's an easy way to blow it. Our rule at Kale is you never open with the signal. Telling somebody an algorithm flagged them is creepy. It ends the call.

### FIX (0:46-1:02)
On the listing side, don't say "my data says you might be selling." Say "you've owned that place a long time. Two homes on your block closed this quarter. Want what they sold for?"

### CLOSE (1:02-1:20)
Here's what you do now. Pull ten owners from your own database who've been in the same house a long time. Call one today with what sold on their block. No listing ask. The software hands you the name. It'll never make the call.

**Pattern interrupt:** The Stop, confirmed. Stop walking cold, turn square to camera, deliver the whole hook standing still, then resume walking on "That same kind of tool." Youshaei's note: the stillness is the interrupt here, since the line itself is an admission and motion would undercut it. Chris Do agreed on the condition that D.J. doesn't smile through it.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the loop opens on a contradiction the viewer can't resolve without staying. He pays for the tool and says it's never worked, which is a Huh that only the "the call is the advantage" line turns into an Aha. The credential does the stopping and the contradiction does the holding.
- Share/save driver (Berger): high Social Currency for the agent who reshares it, because the naming of a real internal rule is information most agents don't have. The one-sentence retell survives intact: "everyone can buy the same list, the call is the edge." Berger flagged that the emotion is closer to competitive anxiety than anger, which shares narrower but sends more, which suits the Instagram and Facebook send signal.
- Retention move (MrBeast): three escalations at roughly 11-second intervals. The tool exists, the tool points at your sellers, and there's a way to blow it. The "creepy" line at 0:40 is the peak and it lands right after the drift point.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Hormozi holds that the recruiting confession, while the best hook in the script, spends 11 seconds on D.J.'s business before the viewer's business shows up, and listing agents will bail before the payload. The experiment: run variant 2 (Forbidden, seller-side from word one) with the Courted admission moved to a single line inside the body as proof. Same payload, same close. Hypothesis: the current version earns follows and reshares from agents who like being let inside, variant 2 earns higher completion from cold listing agents who don't know D.J. yet.
