# The 3-Year Freeze Is Cracking. Most Agents Will Notice Too Late.

> **WOW: Scoreboard plus a timing warning. The mortgage lock-in that defined three years of weird inventory is measurably loosening. The share of homeowners with a rate at 6 percent or higher tripled from a 7.3% low to 21.2% (Q3 2025) and now exceeds the under-3% group for the first time. The share stuck under 6% has fallen every quarter, from 92.7% to 80.3%. And first-time buyers, the ones with no low rate to protect, were more than half of all purchase loans in March 2026, the highest since June 2020. The trap: most agents will wait for the thaw to finish, then go chase the listings. By then everyone is calling the same owners. The agent who wins builds the relationship now, while the owner still swears they will never move, because the life event that unsticks them is coming and nobody gets to know which one.**

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "The share of homeowners with a rate at 6 percent or higher just tripled, from 7.3 percent to 21.2 percent... bigger than the under-3-percent group for the first time"
  - Source: FHFA National Mortgage Database, via Redfin and WolfStreet. The 6%+ share rose from a 7.3% low (Q2 2022) to 21.2% (Q3 2025), the highest since ~2015, and now exceeds the sub-3% share for the first time ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/mortgage-rate-lock-in-effect-eases/), [WolfStreet](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/01/03/locked-in-homeowners-nevertheless-pay-off-below-4-mortgages-their-share-of-all-mortgages-drops-to-lowest-since-q4-2020/))
  - Who was measured: All outstanding US first-lien mortgages
  - Status: confirmed. Exact figures, not rounded.

- **Claim:** "first-time buyers... were more than half of every purchase loan in March. Highest since 2020"
  - Source: ICE May 2026 Mortgage Monitor: "First-time homebuyers accounted for more than half of all purchase loans closed in March -- the highest share since June 2020 -- supported by improved affordability earlier in the year and insulation from the rate lock-in effect" ([link](https://mortgagetech.ice.com/resources/data-reports/may-2026-mortgage-monitor))
  - Who was measured: Share of mortgaged purchase loans closed in March 2026 (NOT share of all buyers; NAR's all-buyer first-timer figure is a different and lower number because it includes cash buyers)
  - Status: confirmed. The "more than half" is ICE's exact framing.

- **Claim:** "The share of owners stuck under 6 percent has dropped every single quarter, from 92.7 down to 80.3"
  - Source: FHFA NMDB via Redfin: below-6% share 92.7% (Q2 2022) -> 87.7% (Q3 2023) -> 82.8% (Q3 2024) -> 80.3% (Q2 2025) ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/rate-lock-q2-2025/))
  - Who was measured: US mortgaged homeowners
  - Status: confirmed. Exact endpoints; the "every quarter" decline is supported by the full series.

- **Claim (framing):** "The freeze... is cracking" / "the handcuffs are loosening"
  - Source: Redfin attributes rising 2026 for-sale inventory to the easing lock-in effect plus new construction ([Redfin Feb 2026](https://www.redfin.com/news/buyers-vs-sellers-february-2026/)); ICE frames the unwind as gradual, "more likely to ease gradually than unwind at any single rate threshold" ([ICE May 2026](https://mortgagetech.ice.com/resources/data-reports/may-2026-mortgage-monitor)).
  - Status: editorial framing, directly supported by Redfin and ICE.

- **Counter-balance (for integrity, not in spoken script):** The effect persists even as it eases. ICE's May 2026 data still describes lock-in actively constraining existing owners, and Coldwell Banker's April 2026 agent survey found 61% still call it a major or moderate factor. The take stands: thawing, not thawed. That gap between "loosening" and "gone" is exactly the window the action layer tells agents to work.
