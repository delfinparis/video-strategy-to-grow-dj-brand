# Stop Telling Renters They're Throwing Money Away.

> **WOW: The most repeated line in residential real estate is wrong for a big share of Chicago renters. Zillow's own 2026 math says buying in the Chicago metro takes six and a half years to beat renting, once taxes, insurance, upkeep and closing costs are counted. Any renter who would move sooner comes out behind by buying. The agent who says "keep renting" to that person is the one they call when it is finally time.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Swap and the List | **Heat:** 4 | **Avatars:** All, buyer agents most

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "In Chicago, buying takes six and a half years to beat renting."
  - Source: Zillow, "Rent or buy? How long it takes for buying a home to pay off in each metro," press release, 2026-06-04. Metro table: "Chicago, IL 6.5" (buying break-even point in years). United States: 6.0.
  - Who was measured: the Chicago metro (not the city alone), typical home vs. typical rent, over a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage. "Chicago" in the spoken line is the metro; the long captions say "Chicago area."
  - Status: confirmed. Figure extracted from the release PDF's metro table on 2026-10-13.
- **Claim:** "It counts taxes, upkeep and closing costs."
  - Source: same release. Buyer costs include mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, maintenance and closing costs. Renter costs include rent and renters insurance, plus the return on cash not spent on a down payment. Mortgage interest deductions and other tax benefits are excluded.
  - Status: confirmed. Insurance is left out of the spoken line for length; it is in the captions.
- **Claim:** "Buy, then move in four years? You come out behind."
  - Source: follows directly from a 6.5-year break-even: before that point, Zillow's model has buying costing more than renting. The release's own guidance: "Rent if: You might move in less than six years" (national framing).

## Social Copy

### LinkedIn (PRIMARY)
**Caption:**
"Renting is throwing money away" is wrong for a lot of Chicago renters, and Zillow's own numbers say so.

Zillow's June 2026 rent vs. buy analysis puts the Chicago area at 6.5 years before buying beats renting. Nationally it is 6.0. That math counts mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, maintenance and closing costs on the buying side. On the renting side it counts rent, renters insurance, and what the down payment money would have earned if it stayed invested.

So a renter who buys and moves in four years comes out behind. In Zillow's model, renting would have been the better deal.

Most of us were trained to say the line anyway. It sounds like advice. For a client who is likely to move for a job, a relationship, or a bigger place in a few years, it is the opposite of advice.

The better first question is simple. How long will you stay?

If the honest answer is under six and a half years, tell them to keep renting, and tell them why. You lose nothing. You become the one agent who told them the truth when it cost you a deal, and that is who they call when the answer changes.

Source: Zillow, "Rent or buy? How long it takes for buying a home to pay off in each metro," June 4, 2026.
