# Zillow's Own Economist Just Called the Top of the Market

> **WOW: Named stakes + authority admission against interest -- Zillow's Chief Economist Mischa Fisher said July "may represent the peak of what we can expect for the rest of the year." A company that profits from transactions telling buyers the good part may be over. The numbers back him: July closings up 7% YoY (biggest jump of 2026), but newly pending listings down 7.7% from June -- the pipeline is thinning. The script flips the slowdown from a reason to wait into the reason to move: the buyer who closed in July bought the strongest market of the year, and waiting for a better one after a called peak is betting against Zillow's own economist. Ends with the exact text to send a fence-sitting buyer.**

**Scroll-stop variants (spoken, A/B options mapped to hook families):**
1. "Zillow's own chief economist just called the top of the 2026 housing market." [Named Stakes / authority admission | emotion: surprise from the source] -- current pick
2. "The company that profits when you buy a house just told you the good part might be over." [Sacred Cow / who-said-it | emotion: intrigue] -- higher risk, higher ceiling
3. "Your buyer who's waiting for a better market is waiting for something Zillow says already happened." [Mirror | emotion: recognition]

**Pattern interrupt:** The Stop -- D.J. plants mid-stride on "called the top of the market." A walking creator who suddenly stops is a hard visual break, and it maps to the content: the market topped out, so he stops. Selfie-stick friendly. Breaks NF-068's Whip and NF-067's Gesture-On-Beat.

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): the surprise is the source. Zillow benefits from transactions, so Zillow's economist calling a peak is an admission against interest, which reads as more credible and more curious than a bearish take from a permabear. The gap is "why would Zillow say that?"
- Share/save driver (Berger): "The buyers who won this summer are the ones who stopped waiting for a market that already came" is a clean retell line with Practical Value. It survives a DM from agent to client with zero setup.
- Retention move: the flip at 0:34 ("this is where it flips") is the re-hook, and it inverts the viewer's expectation set up by the hook ("bad news") -- so the payoff is a reversal, not a continuation.

**The dissent (A/B test next):** Alex Hormozi argues the reframe is doing too much work in the viewer's head and the video should hand over the text sooner. His experiment: open cold on the send-this line ("Text every buyer who's waiting and send them this...") and back-fill the Zillow proof after, so the tactic is the hook and the authority is the support. Hypothesis: sharper saves and DMs because agents come for the copy-paste asset; slightly lower completion because the surprise-source hook is spent later. Same facts, reordered.
