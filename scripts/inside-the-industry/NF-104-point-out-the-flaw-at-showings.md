# Stop Telling Buyers Every House Is Great.

> **WOW: NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, Exhibit 4-5: the most-cited benefit buyers got from their agent was that the agent "pointed out unnoticed features/faults with property" (54%), well above "negotiated a better price" (35%).**

**Pillar:** Client Management | **Hook Family:** Swap / List | **Heat:** 4 | **Avatars:** Buyer agents
**Filming:** Walk and talk. Visual open: One Prop (hold up a house key or a lockbox on "Stop," lower it on "every house is great").

## Shareable Moment

## Teleprompter

Stop telling buyers every house is great.

Cheering for every house doesn't help them pick one.

NAR asked recent buyers in 2025 how their agent helped. The top answer was spotting things they missed, good and bad. Fifty-four percent said that. Only thirty-five percent said a better price.

So don't cheer. At every showing, name one problem and one good thing they missed.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "NAR asked recent buyers in 2025 how their agent helped. The top answer was spotting things they missed, good and bad. Fifty-four percent said that."
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS, 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (released Nov 2025), Chapter 4, Exhibit 4-5, "Benefits Provided by Real Estate Agent During Home Purchase Process." PDF copy: https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf
  - Exact wording: "Fifty-four percent of buyers reported that their agent pointed out unnoticed features and faults with properties." Table row: "Pointed out unnoticed features/faults with property: 54%" (All buyers), the highest row in the exhibit.
  - Who was measured: recent home buyers (consumer-reported). NAR mailed a 120-question survey in July 2025 to 173,250 buyers who bought a primary residence between July 2024 and June 2025; 6,103 responses.
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). "Unnoticed features and faults" said as "things they missed, good and bad" for grade-5 speech; meaning unchanged. Respondents could choose more than one benefit.
- **Claim:** "Only thirty-five percent said a better price."
  - Source: same exhibit. Table row: "Negotiated a better price: 35%" (All buyers).
  - Status: confirmed (re-opened 2026-10-14). Same study, same question, separate sentence.
- **Claim:** "Cheering for every house doesn't help them pick one." / payoff action.
  - Source: editorial framing and recommendation. No statistic implied.

**Pattern interrupt:** One Prop confirmed. The key in hand reads as "showing" before a word lands. Different from NF-103 (Gesture-On-Beat) and NF-099 (The Whip).

**Why it should work:**
- Hook mechanism (Heath): every buyer agent has said "this one's great" this month. The hook names it and the survey explains why it misses at 0:07.
- Share/save driver (Berger/Hormozi): the 54 versus 35 gap is surprising, and it is a number an agent can repeat at the next team meeting.
- Retention move (MrBeast): "Only thirty-five percent said a better price" at 0:19 is the turn. Most agents assume price is the job.

**The dissent (your next A/B test):** Kane wants variant 2 as the hook because the price comparison is the real surprise. Miller says variant 2 hides the don't, and the brief needs the don't said plainly up front. Decision: keep variant 1. **Experiment:** if saves are high and shares are low, re-cut with variant 2 and keep the same body.
