# The CEO of Realtor.com Just Called Out Your Listing Pitch

> **WOW: Contrarian take + earned inside-the-industry observation. The CEO of Realtor.com publicly framed the private-listing pitch as a fiduciary failure, and D.J. agrees with him while naming the bias out loud. The fresh angle is the "seller choice" reframe and a paper-trail action no other private-listing script in the catalog gives.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Choice only counts when the seller knows the cost."

## Script (~65 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (Act 1) (0:00-0:10)
The CEO of Realtor.com just told agents who push private listings they're on the wrong side of history. He's right. But one word in his argument gives away his real motive.

### STORY (Act 2 -- the conflict unfolds) (0:10-0:38)
His name's Damian Eales. This week he drew a line. A family's home is most of their wealth, and hiding it from the market for a quiet deal isn't serving that family. It's the agent putting commission ahead of the client's price. Then he made it a test. Years from now, you're on the right side of this or the wrong one. Now here's the turn. Eales runs a listing site. Open listings are his business model. So yeah, he benefits from saying this out loud.

### PAYOFF (Act 3) (0:38-1:08)
And he's still right. Because the tell isn't his motive. It's the pitch. Agents call it seller choice. But choice only counts when the seller knows the cost. Fewer buyers, fewer offers, almost always a lower number. So here's what you do now. Next listing appointment, stop offering private as an equal option. Lead with full exposure as your recommendation. If they still want quiet, put it in writing that you advised against it. Because years from now, your file shows which side you were on.

**Estimated Duration:** ~70 seconds | **Word Count:** ~180 words

**Length justification:** Over 45s because this is a three-act story and cutting a beat kills the payoff. The hook opens a loop (the "one word" / real motive). Act 2 needs the turn -- Eales's own bias -- or the story is a flat summary and the viewer never feels the tension. Act 3 has to resolve that loop ("the tell isn't his motive, it's the pitch") before it hands over the action, or the hook's promise goes unpaid. The bias beat is also what keeps D.J. from sounding like he's parroting a portal CEO; it earns the trust the close needs.
