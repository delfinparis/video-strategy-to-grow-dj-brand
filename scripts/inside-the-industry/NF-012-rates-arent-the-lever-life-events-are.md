# Stop Waiting for the Rate Cut. 54% of Homeowners Won't Sell at Any Rate.

> **WOW: Surprising statistic plus contrarian take. Bankrate's mid-2025 homeowner survey found 54% of U.S. homeowners say there is no mortgage rate at which they'd be comfortable selling this year, and 51% say no rate would get them to buy. The industry's dominant narrative says rate cuts reignite the market. Consumer behavior says the opposite. The industry is betting on a lever that doesn't exist. This isn't a rates market. It's a stuck market. The actual lever is life events. Top producers in 2026 are tracking births, jobs, divorces, retirements, and school decisions in their sphere instead of waiting for a rate move that the data says won't move the needle.**

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "54% of homeowners say there is no mortgage rate at which they would be comfortable with selling their home this year"
  - Source: Bankrate, mortgage rates sentiment survey, conducted mid-2025 ([link](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/mortgage-rates-sentiment-survey/))
  - Who was measured: U.S. homeowners (consumer-reported)
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "51% of homeowners say they would be uncomfortable with purchasing another home no matter what happens with mortgage rates this year"
  - Source: same Bankrate mid-2025 survey
  - Who was measured: U.S. homeowners (consumer-reported)
  - Spoken delivery rounds 51% to "half" because the precision in the spoken hook is reserved for the 54% figure (Rule 5: max two numbers per line, plus the "X in Y" preference)
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "This isn't a rates market. It's a stuck market"
  - Editorial reframe of the underlying data, not a numeric claim. The Bankrate finding that more than half of homeowners are immovable on rates regardless of direction supports the framing.
  - Status: editorial color, not a stat

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "The industry is betting on a lever that doesn't exist"
  - Editorial reframe. Defensible against the Bankrate finding plus three years of industry coverage forecasting "rate cut, market reopens" while transaction volume has remained near multi-decade lows.
  - Status: editorial color, not a stat

- **Claim (qualitative framing):** "People aren't moving on rates. They're moving on life"
  - Editorial reframe. The NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers consistently identifies life events as primary motivators (job change, family change, downsizing, upsizing, retirement). Bankrate's lock-in finding is consistent with this -- if rates aren't moving people, life events are doing the work.
  - Status: editorial framing supported by NAR data and Bankrate survey, not a quoted statistic

- **Counter-trend (acknowledged for editorial integrity, not in spoken script):** Inman, "Homeowners Finally Eye A Move As Lock-In Effect Loosens: Intel Survey," 2026-04-27 ([link](https://www.inman.com/2026/04/27/homeowners-finally-eye-a-move-as-lock-in-effect-loosens-intel-survey/)) -- the Intel Survey released in April 2026 showed mortgaged homeowners more interested in buying than a year ago, indicating the lock-in is loosening on the margin. The 54% figure remains the most recent comprehensive national homeowner sentiment number on selling-side lock-in. The take stands: the lever is loosening, but it has not flipped, and the loosening that has occurred has not been driven by rates dropping.
