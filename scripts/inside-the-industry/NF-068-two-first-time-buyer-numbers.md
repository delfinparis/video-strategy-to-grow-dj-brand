# You've Been Quoting the Wrong First-Time Buyer Number

> **WOW: Frame shift (criterion 7) on top of a correction the brief itself got wrong. NAR publishes two first-time buyer shares that disagree by eight points, and the industry has spent nine months quoting the pessimistic one. The 21 percent everybody knows is the November survey of self-identified first-time buyers. The 29 percent from yesterday's July report uses the mortgage-data definition, which counts anyone who hasn't owned in three years, including people buying alone again after a divorce or a death. The stat isn't the payload. The payload is that the gap between the two numbers is a segment nobody markets to, sitting in every agent's own database. CORRECTED FROM THE BRIEF: the brief called 29 percent "one of their lowest market shares on record" and compared it to the 40 percent pre-2008 norm. Both are wrong. 29 percent is UP from 28 percent in July 2025, and the 40 percent norm belongs to the survey series, not the monthly one. Comparing them mixes two measurement systems.**

## Script (~76 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:08)
You've been quoting the wrong first-time buyer number all year. So have I. NAR published the other one yesterday.

### THE TWO NUMBERS (0:08-0:22)
The one you know is twenty-one percent. Record low, smallest since nineteen eighty-one, every headline in November ran it. Yesterday's number was twenty-nine percent of everything that closed in July. Same organization. Both correct.

### THE TURN (0:22-0:43)
They're not counting the same people. The twenty-one is a survey that asks buyers if this was their first home. The twenty-nine is mortgage data. It counts anybody who hasn't owned in three years. That includes somebody buying alone again after a divorce. Or after a funeral.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:43-0:52)
The number that talked this business out of first-time buyers isn't the one describing who actually closed last month.

### WHAT YOU DO NOW (0:52-1:10)
Two things. When you cite the share, name the report. A sharp seller will ask. Then pull every past client who sold three or more years ago and never bought again. The mortgage definition calls them first-time buyers. Your marketing calls them nothing.

### CLOSE (1:10-1:16)
Twenty-one and twenty-nine. That gap isn't a rounding error. It's a call list.

## Production Notes

- **Say the numbers as words, not figures.** "Twenty-one" and "twenty-nine" carry better than reading percentages off a card, and captions.ai transcribes spoken numerals cleanly.
- **The pause is on "Or after a funeral."** Let it sit for a beat before moving. It is the only human sentence in the script and rushing it turns a person into a data footnote.
- **If filming slips past late September,** re-verify the monthly share. The August existing-home sales report lands mid-September and will replace the 29 percent. The 21 percent holds until NAR's 2026 Profile releases in November.
- **Do not run this back to back with NF-064.** Same subject, different number. Space them by at least two posts so the 21 and the 29 don't read as a contradiction in the feed.
- **Editorial framing, not a stat:** "the number that talked this business out of first-time buyers" is D.J.'s read on how the record-low headline was used, not a measured claim. Do not let it drift into a cited figure.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Yesterday's number was twenty-nine percent of everything that closed in July."
  - Source: National Association of REALTORS, Existing-Home Sales report for July 2026, released 2026-08-11. First-time buyers were 29% of sales, down from 33% in June 2026 and up from 28% in July 2025. <https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/nar-existing-home-sales-report-shows-1-7-decrease-in-july>
  - Status: confirmed
- **Claim:** "The one you know is twenty-one percent. Record low, smallest since nineteen eighty-one."
  - Source: NAR, 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, released 2025-11-04. First-time buyer share 21%, described by NAR as a record low; NAR has tracked the series since 1981. <https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40>
  - Status: confirmed
- **Claim:** "The twenty-one is a survey that asks buyers if this was their first home. The twenty-nine is mortgage data. It counts anybody who hasn't owned in three years. That includes somebody buying alone again after a divorce. Or after a funeral."
  - Source: NAR Research, "How NAR Research Collects First-Time Buyer Data and Why It Matters," Economists' Outlook. The Profile survey asks buyers directly (6,103 respondents, July 2024 to June 2025). Mortgage data applies the federal definition: no home ownership in the prior three years, plus divorced and widowed individuals purchasing on their own. Mortgage data also excludes all-cash buyers. <https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/how-nar-research-collects-first-time-buyer-data-and-why-it-matters>
  - Status: confirmed. NAR lists several drivers of the gap, not only re-entrants, so the script says the gap *includes* these buyers and never claims it is only them.
- **Claim:** "the number that talked this business out of first-time buyers"
  - Status: editorial framing, explicitly not a statistic. Flagged in Production Notes.

- **Fabrication audit:** Every spoken number traces to a cited NAR source above. Two figures from the brief were dropped and one framing was corrected:
  - **DROPPED — "the historical norm is 40 percent."** The pre-2008 ~40% norm belongs to the Profile survey series. Setting it against the monthly 29% compares two different measurement systems and would have been the script's one indefensible line. NAR's own framing is that the share "contracted by 50% since 2007," which is a statement about the survey series ending at 21%, not the monthly series.
  - **CORRECTED — the brief called 29% "one of their lowest market shares on record."** It is not. 29% is up from 28% in July 2025. The record low is 21%, and that is the other series. This was the brief's central factual error and the reason the script became a correction piece instead of a decline piece.
  - **NOT USED —** 4.06M SAAR, the 1.7% monthly decline, $434,100 median, and the 4.6-month supply were all verified against the same NAR release and are accurate, but none of them are spoken. They belong to a different story and would have padded this one past its cap.
