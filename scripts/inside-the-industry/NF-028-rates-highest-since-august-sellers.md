# Rates Hit Their Highest Since August. Fence-Sitter Sellers, This Is for You.

> **WOW: Contrarian take -- mortgage rates just hit their highest point since last August, and every fence-sitting seller reads that as a reason to wait. D.J. flips it: waiting for rates to drop means waiting for the exact moment every other seller floods the market. List into the quiet, not the flood.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Avatars:** All
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "List into the quiet, not into the flood."

## Script (~60 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:09)
Mortgage rates just hit their highest point since last August. Most sellers hear that and think wait. For a seller, that's exactly backwards.

### CONTEXT (0:09-0:21)
Freddie Mac put the thirty-year fixed at 6.51 percent last week, up from 6.36. So every fence-sitting seller is telling themselves the same thing. "I'll list when rates come back down."

### THE TAKE (0:21-0:44)
Here's the problem with that plan. The day rates drop, every one of those sellers lists at the same time. You go from low competition to a flooded market overnight. Right now, higher rates are scaring off half your competition before they ever list. And the buyers still shopping at this rate aren't tire-kickers. They're serious. Fewer homes to compete against, and only motivated buyers looking. That's a seller's window. It just doesn't feel like one.

### CLOSE (0:44-0:58)
Here's what you do now. If you've got a seller sitting on the fence waiting for rates to fall, show them what happens to their competition the day it does. Then tell them the truth. List into the quiet, not into the flood.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Mortgage rates just hit their highest point since last August"
  - Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, reading dated 2026-05-21, reported by Fox Business, "Mortgage rates rise to 6.51%: Freddie Mac," 2026-05-21 ([link](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/mortgage-rates-may-21-2026)). The 30-year fixed reached its highest level since late August 2025.
  - Who was measured: Freddie Mac PMMS national average for the 30-year conforming fixed-rate mortgage.
  - Status: confirmed

- **Claim:** "Freddie Mac put the thirty-year fixed at 6.51 percent last week, up from 6.36"
  - Source: Freddie Mac PMMS, 2026-05-21. 30-year fixed averaged 6.51%, up from 6.36% the prior week (a 15-basis-point increase). Corroborated by Fox Business (above) and MPA, "Hopes of 5%-range mortgage rates in 2026 fading" ([link](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/hopes-of-5-range-mortgage-rates-in-2026-fading-after-freddie-mac-latest/576170)).
  - Who was measured: Freddie Mac PMMS national average.
  - Status: confirmed
  - Note: The Yahoo Finance link in the original brief reports a separate daily figure (6.50%, Zillow lender marketplace, May 20). The 6.51% / 6.36% figures used here are the Freddie Mac weekly survey numbers, which is the industry-standard reference and matches the brief.

- **Claim:** "The day rates drop, every one of those sellers lists at the same time... higher rates are scaring off half your competition... buyers still shopping aren't tire-kickers"
  - Source: D.J. Paris's market reasoning, not a cited statistic. The dynamic (rate-driven seller hesitation suppressing current inventory, pent-up supply releasing when rates fall, rate-sensitive buyers self-selecting for seriousness) is presented as analysis. No specific buyer-pool or inventory figure is claimed.
  - Status: confirmed (framing, not a stat). "Half your competition" is rhetorical, not a measured figure -- delivered as a general characterization, not a precise share.
