# Over Half Your Potential Sellers Have a Rate Below 4%. Here's the Conversation That Actually Moves Them.

> **WOW: Tactical specificity + counter-narrative -- 52.5% of mortgaged U.S. homeowners still hold a rate below 4% (Redfin, Q2 2025 print). Most listing agents accept "we can't afford to move" as a hard no. Top producers use a three-question reframe (net equity + life stage + cost of waiting) that turns the same answer into a listing appointment. The script gives the exact conversation script and the one-homeowner-this-week action.**

**Pillar:** Market Intelligence | **Hook Family:** Swap/List (Do/Don't in the room) | **Heat:** 3 | **Avatars:** All, listing agents especially
**Filming:** Walk and talk. No demo.

## Shareable Moment
> "Life doesn't wait for rates."

## Script (~68 seconds, walk and talk)

### HOOK (0:00-0:12)
When your seller says "we can't afford to move," don't hear a no. Hear the conversation you haven't had yet. Half of your database is sitting on a rate under four percent. And there's a three-question reframe that turns that answer into a listing.

### CONTEXT (0:12-0:30)
The stat's from Redfin's rate lock analysis. Fifty-two point five percent of mortgaged homeowners still have a rate under four. Down from sixty-five percent at the pandemic peak, but still a majority. That means most of the sellers you want are sitting on golden handcuffs. And most agents accept it. That's the mistake.

### WHY IT MATTERS (0:30-0:54)
Top producers ask three questions instead. Net equity. Their sub-four rate looks great. But they've probably built over a hundred thousand in equity since twenty-twenty. That's cash they only see if they sell. Life stage. Kids growing, aging parents. Life doesn't wait for rates. Cost of waiting. The target house is going up too. Sometimes the wait costs more than the rate spread.

### CLOSE (0:54-1:12)
Here's what you do now. Pick one homeowner in your database this week. Someone who bought in twenty-twenty or twenty-twenty-one. Send them the three-question worksheet. Then say this. "I don't know if now's the right time to move. But I know if we don't run these numbers, you'll never know either." The conversation top producers have every day. Now you have it too.

## Data Source

- **Claim:** "Fifty-two point five percent of mortgaged homeowners still have a rate under four."
  - Source: Redfin rate lock analysis, Q2 2025 data. Coverage: Redfin News, "Share of Mortgages with Rates Above 6% Climbs to 10-Year High as Americans Adapt to New Normal" ([link](https://www.redfin.com/news/rate-lock-q2-2025/)). Exact figure per the source: 52.5% of mortgaged U.S. homeowners have a mortgage rate below 4% as of Q2 2025.
  - Status: confirmed (Q2 2025 print; verify Q3/Q4 2025 or Q1/Q2 2026 refresh has not yet moved the number below 50% before posting).

- **Claim:** "Down from sixty-five percent at the pandemic peak."
  - Source: Same Redfin analysis. Peak rate-lock share was 65.1% in Q1 2022. Q2 2025 print (52.5%) is a directional decline of ~12.6 percentage points.
  - Status: confirmed (~65% used for cadence per Rule 5 rounding for spoken delivery).

- **Claim:** "They've probably built over a hundred thousand in equity since twenty-twenty."
  - Source: Case-Shiller / FHFA home price index data 2020 through mid-2025. U.S. national home price appreciation cumulative 2020-2025 has run roughly 40-50% depending on market. On a median-priced home purchased in 2020 (~$300K), that's approximately $120-150K in equity growth before any principal paydown. The script's "over a hundred thousand" is a defensible directional median. Individual agents should run the specific number with the specific homeowner (which is the whole point of the "three-question worksheet" tactic).
  - Status: illustrative directional (spoken script frames as "probably" to signal client-specific math)

- **Claim (tactical -- three-question framework):**
  - Net equity: How much has the client built in equity since purchase? This is cash they only realize on sale.
  - Life stage: What's changing that doesn't wait for rates -- kids growing, aging parents, retirement, job pivot?
  - Cost of waiting: If the target home is appreciating faster than the rate savings on holding, the wait costs more than the rate spread.
  - Source: D.J. Paris's synthesized reframe drawing on standard top-producer coaching content (Buffini "Working by Referral," Tom Ferry client-conversation frameworks, Mike Ferry seller-consultation scripts). The three-pillar synthesis is D.J.'s framing consistent with the Golden Handcuffs arc.
  - Status: tactical guidance (D.J.'s synthesized framework)

- **Claim (tactical -- one-homeowner-this-week action):**
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended cadence for implementing the three-question worksheet. One touch per week over 20-30 weeks nets 20-30 seller conversations, of which typical top-producer conversion is 3-5 listings. Consistent with the standard sphere-of-influence nurture cadence in NF-053 (Sullivan community FB group) and NF-056 (Gen Z 5-year pipeline).
  - Status: tactical guidance

- **Claim (D.J.'s exact-words agent script):** "I don't know if now's the right time to move. But I know if we don't run these numbers, you'll never know either."
  - Source: D.J. Paris's recommended agent-to-client script. The uncertainty-framing ("I don't know if...") disarms the seller's guard because the agent is not pushing a listing. The follow-up ("if we don't run these numbers, you'll never know either") makes the next step joint discovery rather than transaction pressure. Standard top-producer consultative technique.
  - Status: tactical guidance
