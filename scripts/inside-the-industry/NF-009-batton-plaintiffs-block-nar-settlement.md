NAR's $52 million Tuccori settlement just got called a "reverse auction" by the other plaintiffs in the same case.

The Batton plaintiffs filed today asking the federal court to block NAR from proceeding with the deal. Their argument, in their own words: NAR abandoned settlement negotiations with them just two days after they proposed a specific amount, then settled with the Tuccori plaintiffs for twelve percent of what the Burnett settlement paid. They are calling it a "cheap release" and a "race to the bottom."

Here is the part most agents will get wrong about what happens next.

The practice changes are not going away. The written buyer agreement, the compensation in writing, the new disclosure language -- those came out of the Burnett settlement, which is already final. Whatever the court does with Tuccori on July 28, those rules stay in force.

What might go away is your brokerage's release from buyer-side liability. The Tuccori structure let brokerages opt in for a release from homebuyer claims. If the Batton motion succeeds and the court enjoins the Tuccori settlement, that release sits in limbo pending appeal.

So tomorrow, walk into your office and ask your broker one question. "Did we opt into Tuccori?" If the answer is yes, your leadership should already be watching the July 28 hearing.

Either way, keep using the buyer agreement. The lawsuits aren't over. Don't act like they are.
