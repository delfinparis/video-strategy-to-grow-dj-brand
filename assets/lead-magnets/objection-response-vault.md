## How to use this

Every response below is built the same way, because that structure is what makes a script usable under pressure:

1. **What they said**
2. **What they actually mean** (the fear under the sentence)
3. **Don't say this** (the trap almost everyone falls into)
4. **Say this** (the exact words)
5. **Why it works**
6. **Where it comes from** (a named guest from the Keeping It Real archive, with a timestamp)

Read the "say this" out loud before you use it. If it doesn't sound like you, change the words but keep the move. The move is the part that matters.

**On sourcing:** the anchors are real guests teaching real principles on the podcast, cited with episode date and timestamp. The scripts themselves are D.J.'s, written from those principles. Where a guest is quoted directly, it is marked as a quote. Nothing here puts words in a guest's mouth that they did not say.

## 1. "Can you cut your commission?"

**What they actually mean:** "I don't yet see what I'm getting for that number." It is almost never about the money in isolation. It is about the value being unclear.

**Don't say this:** "Well, my brokerage sets that." (Weak, and untrue in spirit.) Or worse, "Sure, I can come down a little." The second you discount without being asked twice, you have told them the first number was made up.

**Say this:**

> "I could. Agents do it all the time. But here's what I'd have to take out to get there, and I don't think you want any of it gone. Can I show you what the number actually buys, and then if you still want me to cut it, we'll talk?"

Then show them. Line by line. Photography, staging consult, the pre-market fixes, the negotiation, the hours.

**Why it works:** you did not say no, so there is nothing to argue with. You moved the conversation from *price* to *what the price contains*, which is the only ground where you win. And you gave them a path back to the question, so they don't feel shut down.

**The other half of this one is your posture.** Pamela Marcus put it better than anyone: *"Even if you need that commission check to pay the rent, don't act like you do."* Desperation is what actually loses the negotiation. Not the number.

**Anchor:** Pamela Marcus, Keeping It Real, 2023-02-27, 09:30 and 10:30.

## 2. "We want to list it higher than that."

**What they actually mean:** "My house is special and your number feels like a judgment of it." Pricing conversations are emotional before they are financial.

**Don't say this:** "The comps don't support that." You are technically right and you have just made the data the enemy of their feelings. They will find an agent who agrees with them.

**Say this:**

> "We can. I want to be straight with you about what happens if we do. The first two weeks are when you get the most showings you will ever get. If we price above the market, we spend those two weeks proving to buyers that the house is overpriced, and then we chase the price down anyway, only now with the words 'price reduced' attached. I'd rather use those two weeks to start a competition. Can I show you what that looks like?"

**Why it works:** you agreed to their request, then made the cost of it concrete and time-bound. You are not arguing about value. You are describing a sequence of events, which is much harder to disagree with.

**Anchor:** Steve Shull on tactical empathy in listing presentations, Keeping It Real, 2023-01-12, 44:00. The move is making the client feel understood before you make them feel corrected.

## 3. "We're interviewing a few other agents."

**What they actually mean:** "We want to feel like we did this properly." Sometimes it is a real comparison. Often it is a process they feel obligated to run.

**Don't say this:** "What are they offering?" You just entered a bidding war on someone else's terms. Also don't badmouth the others. It reads as fear.

**Say this:**

> "Good. You should. Can I ask you something? What would have to be true for you to feel great about the person you pick? Not what they'd charge. What would they have to do or say?"

Then listen and take notes. At the end: *"If I can do those things, do you need to take the other meetings?"*

**Why it works:** you handed them the criteria pen, and people trust their own criteria. You also learned exactly what to sell against, which nobody else in the process will bother to ask.

**Anchor:** Steve Shull's "figure out if you're the favorite or the fool before you go on the appointment," Keeping It Real, 2023-01-12, 1:14:00. This question is how you find out which one you are while you still have time to change it.

## 4. "Zillow says my house is worth more."

**What they actually mean:** "I found a number I like better than yours and it came from a computer that has no reason to lie to me."

**Don't say this:** "Zillow is always wrong." They have heard agents say that for a decade and it sounds defensive.

**Say this:**

> "The Zestimate is a really good starting point and a terrible ending point. It has never been inside your house. It doesn't know you redid the kitchen, and it doesn't know the unit two floors down had the same layout and sat for ninety days. Let me show you the three homes a buyer is actually going to compare yours to, and you tell me where you think you land."

**Why it works:** you validated the tool instead of attacking it, which keeps them from defending it. Then you replaced an algorithm with three specific addresses. Specific beats abstract every time.

**Bonus play:** Carrie McCormick used the Zestimate as a *prospecting* tool, not just a rebuttal. Her actual words: *"I would reach out to everybody you know that owns a home and say, 'Hey, I was just browsing around on Zillow and I noticed the Zestimate for your home is X.'"* Same tool, pointed the other direction.

**Anchor:** Carrie McCormick, Keeping It Real, 2022-11-21, 12:00. Chris Linsell on running a detailed Zestimate analysis as a public demonstration of expertise, 2022-11-22, 18:00.

## 5. "We need to think about it."

**What they actually mean:** almost always one of three things. They have an unspoken concern, they are not the only decision-maker, or you have not made the next step small enough.

**Don't say this:** "Sure, take your time, call me when you're ready." That is where listings go to die.

**Say this:**

> "Totally fair. Most people say that when there's one thing still sitting funny with them. If you had to guess, what's the one thing?"

If they name it, handle it. If they say "nothing, really," go to: *"Then let me ask it differently. Is it the price, the timing, or me?"*

**Why it works:** "think about it" is a door closing politely. The question reopens it without pressure, and it gives them permission to say the real thing. The three-option version works because it is easier to pick from a list than to volunteer a criticism.

**Anchor:** Steve Shull's tactical empathy principle, Keeping It Real, 2023-01-12, 44:00. The goal is making it safe to tell you the truth.

## 6. "We're going to try selling it ourselves first."

**What they actually mean:** "I think the commission is the only thing standing between me and more money."

**Don't say this:** "Most FSBOs fail." Even if the data supported you, you just told them they are about to fail. Nobody hires the person who opens with that.

**Say this:**

> "Honestly? Some people should. If you've got a buyer lined up and you're comfortable with the contract and the disclosures, you might not need me. Can I do something for you either way? Let me send you the three things that trip up most for-sale-by-owner deals in this market. If you sail past all three, you didn't need an agent and I'm glad I helped. If you hit one, call me."

**Why it works:** you gave them permission, which removes the fight entirely. Then you made yourself useful with no strings, which is the only way to stay in the conversation. Most of these calls come back.

**Anchor:** Vickey Barron on approaching FSBOs with honesty and genuine curiosity, offering value without an immediate expectation of a sale, Keeping It Real, 2022-12-23, 13:00.

## 7. "Why should I hire you? I've never heard of you."

**What they actually mean:** "Convince me you can handle the biggest transaction of my life."

**Don't say this:** a list of your credentials. Nobody has ever chosen an agent because of a designation acronym.

**Say this:**

> "That's a fair question. Here's the honest answer. You're not hiring me for my name. You're hiring me for what happens on the worst day of this deal, when the inspection comes back bad or the appraisal comes in low. Ask me what I'd do in either of those, and judge me on the answer."

Then be genuinely good at the answer. Have it ready before you walk in.

**Why it works:** you reframed the criteria from reputation, where you may lose, to competence under pressure, where you can win on the spot. It is also true, which is why it lands.

**Anchor:** Shayla Twitt on honing and regularly updating your value proposition so it says something specific instead of something generic, Keeping It Real, 2023-02-22, 12:30.

## 8. "We're going to wait until rates come down."

**What they actually mean:** "I'm scared of the payment and waiting feels free." Waiting is not free, but it feels free, and that is the thing to address.

**Don't say this:** "Rates might go up!" Fear-selling. They can smell it.

**Say this:**

> "You might be right. Here's the part most people don't price in. If rates drop, every buyer who's waiting comes back at the same time, and you're bidding against all of them for the same houses. You'd get a better rate on a higher price, in a competition. Right now you have less competition and a price you can negotiate. And the rate is the one part of this you can change later. The price you pay is permanent."

If it's a payment problem specifically, don't leave it philosophical. Get a lender on the phone about a **2-1 buy down** or a **seller-funded buy down**. Those change the actual monthly number now.

**Why it works:** you agreed with their prediction and still dismantled the conclusion. And you replaced a vague "wait" with a concrete instrument that solves the real problem, which is the monthly payment.

**Anchor:** Joel Schaub (VP of Lending) on 2-1 buy downs and negotiating seller-funded buy downs to lower the initial payment, Keeping It Real, 2022-11-29, 07:00 and 15:17. Leslie Singer's line for the mindset piece: *"Date the rate, marry the home."* 2023-01-20, 49:00.

## 9. "The other agent said they'd do it for less."

**What they actually mean:** "Match it or justify it."

**Don't say this:** "Then go with them." Ego, not strategy. Also don't instantly match. If you fold in four seconds, everything else you said about your value was theater.

**Say this:**

> "They might be the right call. Can I ask what they said they'd do for it? Because there are two kinds of cheaper. One is an agent who runs a leaner operation and passes it on. The other is an agent who's discounting because it's the only thing they've got to offer. If it's the first one, that's a real option. Let's find out which it is."

**Why it works:** you did not defend and you did not cave. You introduced a distinction that makes them go do your due diligence for you, and in most cases they come back having discovered the second kind.

**Anchor:** Pamela Marcus on not negotiating from need, Keeping It Real, 2023-02-27, 09:30. Shayla Twitt on a value proposition specific enough to survive a price comparison, 2023-02-22, 12:30.

## 10. "It's been on the market for weeks and nothing's happening."

**What they actually mean:** "I'm starting to wonder if this is your fault." This is the price-reduction conversation, and the mistake is having it too late.

**Don't say this:** "The market is slow right now." You just blamed something neither of you can act on, and you sounded like every other agent.

**Say this:**

> "I've been thinking about the same thing, so let me show you what the market has told us. We've had [X] showings and [Y] second showings. When a house is priced right, those two numbers move together. Ours aren't. That's not a marketing problem, it's a price problem, and I'd rather tell you that now than in another month. Here's what I recommend, and here's what I think it does."

**Why it works:** you brought it up first, which keeps you in the driver's seat instead of on the defensive. You used their own listing's data instead of an opinion, and you named the tradeoff plainly. Sellers forgive bad news. They do not forgive being handled.

**Anchor:** Carrie McCormick's reframe, quoted directly: *"Let's look at this negotiation as an opportunity to reach an agreement."* Keeping It Real, 2022-11-21, 15:45. Her prep tactic (understand what actually moves the person across the table before you sit down, 12:00) applies to your own seller too.
